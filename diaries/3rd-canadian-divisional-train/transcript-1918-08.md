# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — August 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps (CASC), 3rd Canadian Division (styled "H.Q. 3rd Canadian Divisional Train" on the cover)
- **Month:** August 1918 (Volume 31) — this volume covers 1 to 31 August 1918
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** August 1918, p. 1 – p. 12 (image files page-0305 – page-0316 of the diary)
- **August 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–12): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–31 August, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The Place column of the diary sheets is blank throughout; places in the entries are as written in the text. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. Weekly transport-return tables are reproduced as written, including their arithmetic (see open questions where the diary's stated totals do not match its own figures). Abbreviations as used in the diary: S.S.O. = Senior Supply Officer; O.C. = Officer Commanding; M.T. = Mechanical Transport; H.T. = Horse Transport; R.F.P. = Re-filling Point; A.A.&Q.M.G. = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster-General; D.A.Q.M.G. = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster-General; G.O.C. = General Officer Commanding; A.P.M. = Assistant Provost Marshal; F.P. No. 1 = Field Punishment No. 1; F.G.C.M. = Field General Court Martial; S.A.A. = Small Arms Ammunition; G.S. = General Service; C.A.S.C. = Canadian Army Service Corps; C.C.S. = Casualty Clearing Station; D.A.C. = Divisional Ammunition Column; C.M.R. = Canadian Mounted Rifles; P.P.C.L.I. = Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry; M.O. = Medical Officer; O.R's = Other Ranks; Bde. = Brigade. The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "(A8984) Wt. W12439/M1295. 750,000. 1/17. D. D. & L., Ltd. Forms/C.2118/14."

### Page 4 — 1–3 August 1918 (Sheet No. 1)

> **1 August 1918.** — S.S.O. and Adjt: gone to Railhead, Division and 4th Army. Water Tank opposite being removed by the French. This tank is filled from Light Railway and is the only source of drinking water here, DURY. Town Major just been in and confirmed this. Billet 57 where our Mess is and where we have been living, some of the kitchen utensils, was visited by a man this afternoon who the Interpreter says is the Caretaker. He informs the Interpreter that the French who were in here before us, took a great quantity of stuff away. He may or may not be the Caretaker, only I saw him take out of the building 2 large enamelled wash-bowls we were using in the kitchen. When we leave he may say of us as he said of the French, hence this note in my Diary. Transport could not make the distance from CANDAS and we are staging again. S.S.O. [uncertain] will have M.T. pick up rations at Re-filling Points and deliver at a point they pass where HT will pick up and deliver to units, this second staging is something sprung on us at the last moment and we shall have to do our best to see all get fed, and I don't think there will be many legitimate complaints. All have tried hard to meet the situation. No.1 Company stages tonight at PONT DE METZ, Nos. 2, 3, and 4 at BOIS DE NEILLY. A few bombs around us at 11.00 p.m.
>
> **2 August 1918.** — Raining. No.1 Company pulled in to BACOUEL this afternoon, their final destination, their re-filling point here also. No.2 Company will be at SAINS EN AMIENOIS, re-filling point between this place and ST. FUSCIEN, No.3 at HEBECOURT, also re-filling point, No.4 and re-filling point at PROUZEL. Adjutant gone round all Companies in car informing them about rationing, and ascertained for A.Q.M.G. from the French, particulars about source of water supply.
>
> **3 August 1918.** — Started fine, but turned to rain later. So called caretaker of this billet and a little old man went upstairs, and when they came down I noticed caretaker had a bundle under his arms that looked like clean sheets. This may or may not be alright, only I am making a note of it. Lieut: Hessey, O.C., No.4 Company reported his Company in, so did Capt: Higgins of No.2 report. Wagons with our blankets arrived at 3.00 p.m. also office papers etc Orders for Artillery move came in, therefore No.1 Company will move this evening from BACOUEL to BOVES WOOD, about 9 miles due East.

All Supply Dumps are here also. Fine.
>
> **10 August 1918.** — Yesterday I tried 3 cases in No.2 Company before moving. Today again fine. Saw A.A.Q.M.G. in morning and gathered we were not likely to have to deliver beyond QUESNEL, so will remain in present Camp. Hauls for 7th Bde. and Divisional Troops will be long, but mostly down hill with loaded wagons and up with empty; besides, this is the most convenient and almost the only place for water for horses, and that is over a mile away.
>
> **11 August 1918.** — Mist in early morning. Hun busy bombing all around us during night, but no casualties to us. The D.A.C. close to us got it rather badly. Called at Division H.Q. at BEAUCOURT with S.S.O., but found they had gone on to QUESNEL, where we located them. Quite a number of captured horses coming through, reports say 600 captured by Cavalry at ROYE. Train Headquarters moved into a Dug-out close to No.1 Company. Everything running remarkably smooth. Water is one great difficulty. Besides rations on the supply wagons, we now carry mails and Ordnance to the Units, quite an addition to the weight of the loads, but we can do it, only because Company Officers are most careful in the care of their horses. Care of horses is their first consideration, greasing of wagons, and absolute cleanliness to breast-collars, all other cleaning and polish to be considered for the time and under present circumstances as secondary. Three Officers, viz: S.S.O., O.C., No.1 Company, and the Adjutant are doing most excellent work.

### Page 8 — 13 August (continued) – 18 August 1918 (Sheet No. 5)

> **13 August 1918 (continued).** — Captains de Graves and Fisher volunteered for Siberia. Sent forward their names with recommendation. Besides the ordinary delivery of rations to First Line the Train delivers mails, and certain Ordnance Stores urgently required up to 200 lbs. per wagon forward, kits of reinforcements, and brings back empties and kits of killed and wounded. This, over side tracks telling on the condition of the horses. The average hauls in mileage is not excessive but the bad heavy tracks are equal to double the mileage on hard roads. Have ordered as a little relief that all Baggage teams now with the Units be changed with tired teams from Supply Sections, this will help a little. During this push supplies were delivered right up to the Batteries, and Supply Convoys were more than once right ahead of the Artillery. Weather fine.
>
> **14 August 1918.** — S.S.O. to Railhead. I called with S.S.O. at Division H.Q. Besides Ordnance and Mails etc. we have now to bring back Salvage. Arranged with D.A.Q.M.G., that I was to decide what loads I should bring back in supply wagons and that in no case would I load on any one wagon more than 1000 lbs. Fine but cooler.
>
> **15 August 1918.** — Made rounds of all Companies and found all in good shape. Advantage is being taken of the grazing round about and horses are improving in consequence.
>
> **16 August 1918.** — Fine. Division moved at Noon to BEAUCOURT, they are coming out of the Line for a spell. S.S.O. and Capt: de Graves visited D.H.Q. and Engineers, S.S.O. also to Railhead. Interpreter gone on leave. Capt: Graham reported back late last night from leave. Posted him as S.O. to No.4 Company. Making out applications for immediate awards for good work during the recent operations. Col: MacAlpine called in for a few minutes, spoke to him about Capt: Prikler. Ten Remounts came in, very fair sample.
>
> **17 August 1918.** — Fine, but much colder. Again inspected all Train horses, all picking up, No.2 the poorest in the Train. Visited Division H.Q., and General Stewart of the Artillery. He expressed himself as more than satisfied with the feeding during recent operations.
>
> **18 August 1918.** — Dull, and still colder. Finished preparing my recommendations for immediate awards for work during recent operations. Sent same by hand, (Prestidge), to A.A.& Q.M.G. S.S.O. to Division.

## Open questions / uncertain items (August 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 31" but the contents page reads "Volume 18 - 8." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number.
- The 8 August entry is split across Sheet No. 2 and Sheet No. 3: the sentence "I asked him to keep in mind the haul we are making the condition of the horses and to bring this notice of the Conference, before they come to any decisions." runs across the sheet break; both parts are transcribed as written.
- 1 August: a word after "S.S.O." is overtyped and could not be read with confidence; marked [uncertain]. The sentence is rendered "S.S.O. [uncertain] will have M.T. pick up rations…".
- 6 August: "100 lbs. tea lead to solder [uncertain] incinerator, GOUY EN ARTOIS" — the word read as "solder" is not certain and its sense is unclear; marked [uncertain].
- 6 August: "steps wagon" is as written; it may be "steps, wagon" (two items) but is transcribed as read.
- 13 August: the words "Major McLeod" are typed at the foot of the entry, below the quoted letter signed by W. W. Gibsone; their function is unclear (they may be the sheet signature). They are transcribed as a separate line, as they appear.
- 24 August: the artillery letter names "Major: J.W.McLeod and Captain W.A. deGraves, S.S.O., and O.C. No.1 Company. respectively" — read as McLeod being S.S.O. and deGraves O.C. No. 1 Company. The letter for Captain E. M. Philips spells the name "Philips" (not "Phillips") in both places; the September diary spells it "Phillips" — both spellings are as written in their months.
- 24 August: the 7th Brigade letter is signed "W.Gibson" in the note from Divisional Headquarters dated 21/8/18, while the 13 August letter is signed "W.W. Gibsone" — both as written.
- 26 August: the name in "a replacement for Mr Reed" is read as "Reed"; it is not read with full confidence and is not used elsewhere.
- 31 August: the signature at the foot of Sheet No. 9 is a one-line flourish that could not be read with confidence; it is recorded as [uncertain] and not used as a name. It may read "J. W. McLeod" but this is not certain — and the September diary shows Major McLeod acting as O.C. only from 25 September, so the August O.C.'s identity is left open.
- Place names are as written, including variant spellings: "BACOUEL" (2–3 August), "BOVES WOOD", "PLACHY BUYON", "BOIS DE NEILLY", "BOIS DE GENTELLES", "QUESNEL", "RIVRY", "HONVAL", "FIEFFES MONTRELET", "AGNEZ LES DUISANS".
- No Train casualties are recorded in August 1918. The 13 August figures (76 officers and 1,487 other ranks) are Divisional casualties for the recent operations, not Train casualties; the 11 August bombing caused no casualties to the Train, though the D.A.C. nearby "got it rather badly".
- Names read as: Lieut. Hessey (O.C. No. 4 Company); Capt. Higgins (No. 2 Company); Capt. Bawlf; Capt. de Graves; Capt. Fisher; Capt. Graham (posted as S.O. to No. 4 Company 16 August); Col.
