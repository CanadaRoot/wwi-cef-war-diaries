# 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, CASC — war diary

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps, 3rd Canadian Division (Headquarters, 3rd Canadian Divisional Train)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 — "War diaries - 3rd Canadian Divisional Train" (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Dates:** 28 January 1916 – 18 March 1919
- **Pages:** 370 page images (38 monthly volumes, Feb 1916 – Mar 1919), all downloaded from the LAC IIIF manifest `https://digitalmanifest.bac-lac.gc.ca/DigitalManifest/1/2005004` (`manifest.json`, `download-pages.sh`)
- **Transcripts:** 38 monthly files, `transcript-1916-02.md` … `transcript-1919-03.md`
- **Index:** 1,144 dated entries in `INDEX-D.md` (merged from `index-train-d1.md` … `index-train-d5.md`), covering 1,144 of the 1,146 calendar days 1916-01-28 – 1919-03-18. The only two dates with no diary entry are 13 July 1918 and 22 December 1918.
- **Transcribed:** 2026-10-27, Eighth D run 4 (five parallel subagents, one per period; parent run verified index coverage and spot-checked February 1916 against the page image).

## What the diary is

A candid first-person journal kept by the Officer Commanding, Lt-Col. J. A. (W. A. d'Auvergne) Findlay, D.S.O., on Army Form C.2118 sheets — handwritten through 1916–1917, typewritten with handwritten insertions from late 1917 onward. It records the Train's daily work hauling rations, forage and supplies for the 3rd Canadian Division: refilling points, railheads, horse and wagon states, officers' movements, discipline, and the O.C.'s own opinions. The Train arrived at Bailleul on 28 January 1916 with no wagons, horses, equipment, cooking pots, riding horses or bicycles, and the first months are a struggle to build it from nothing.

## Key findings

- **Command:** Lt-Col. Findlay commanded throughout (28 Jan 1916 – Feb 1919), with Maj. W. A. DeGraves taking command 13 Feb 1919. Findlay was awarded the D.S.O. (announced 3 Jan 1917) and the Croix de Guerre from the 42nd French Division (18 Sep 1918).
- **Moves:** Fletre → Busseboom (Mar 1916) → Steenvoorde (Aug 1916) → the Somme (Sep 1916: Fieffes, Vadencourt, Senlis, Albert) → Bernaville/Frohen-le-Grand → Villers-Chatel → Aubigny → Bethonsart → Bruay → Barlin → La Targette (Apr 1917) → Mont St. Eloy → Vlamertinghe/Caestre (Passchendaele, Oct 1917) → Lillers → Bracquemont → La Targette again (Feb 1918) → Barlin/Beugin/La Goulee → Rely area → Gouy-en-Artois → Wanquetin/Bouquemaison → Dury → Amiens area (Aug 1918) → Arras area → Quéant → Cambrai area → Mons (Armistice) → Nivelles → Tournai → Le Havre → Witley Camp → Bramshott (Mar 1919).
- **Vimy Ridge, 10 Apr 1917:** "During advance on Vimy Ridge supplies went forward without a hitch."
- **Passchendaele, Oct 1917:** at peak the Train fed 58,764 men and 18,732 horses "without any fall down".
- **Amiens, 8–11 Aug 1918:** convoys hauled rations right up to the batteries, at times ahead of the artillery; 600 horses captured by Cavalry at Roye; Division casualties to noon 12 Aug were 76 officers and 1,487 other ranks, 2,915 prisoners taken.
- **Casualties:** Sgt. G. J. M. Pegg killed by shell fire at the Coal Dump 28 May 1916 (Pte. Shields wounded); Lieut. Greer died 29 Sep 1916; L/Cpl. Cook died of pneumonia 28 Dec 1916; two men killed and several wounded by a German aeroplane bombing the night of 3 May 1917; S.S.M. Courtney died in his sleep of heart failure 4 Oct 1917; six men wounded by German bombs 11 Nov 1917 (two Supply men later died); Lieut. Rason died of influenza 6–7 Nov 1918 and Lieut. Lawrence died in England (reported 11 Nov 1918). Gen. Mercer, G.O.C. 3rd Division, was killed in the 2–3 June 1916 fighting; Gen. Lipsett was killed (reported 14 Oct 1918).
- **Armistice, 11 Nov 1918:** Train H.Q. moved Jemappes → Mons and spent the first night after the Armistice there; Gen. Sir Arthur Currie made his triumphal entry into Mons at 3:30 p.m.
- **How the diary ends:** final entry 18 March 1919 at Bramshott — farewell inspection by the G.O.C. 3rd Canadian Division, who "spoke very nicely to the men on parade and bade everybody good-bye and personally shook hands with everybody on Parade"; Nos. 1 & 4 Coys. had sailed on S.S. Olympic on 17 Mar, Nos. 2 & 3 Coys. to sail on S.S. Cedric on 19 Mar; "All Officers, N.C.O.s and Men of the unit struck off the Strength in England on departure for Canada, and Reg'tl Depots in England."
- **Other:** wartime-election soldier voting recorded (Alberta, Saskatchewan, federal, 1917); 3,845 francs subscribed to the Halifax Relief Fund; King George V passed the office and dined with 2 and 3 Companies 5 Dec 1918; the King of the Belgians visited Mons 27 Nov 1918; a pigeon carrying a message in German code found 13 Apr 1918; Capts. de Graves and Fisher volunteered for Siberia 13 Aug 1918.

## Uncertain / open items

- 422 `[uncertain]` occurrences across the 38 transcripts (illegible signatures — mostly the O.C.'s — struck-through words, place names and a few figures).
- Cover vs. contents volume numbers disagree in nearly every month (e.g. Feb 1916: cover "Volume 1" vs. contents "Volume 16 – 2"); both transcribed as written.
- The Place column is blank on most sheets, so index places come from the entry text or the narrative; noted in the transcripts.
- April 1916 sheet numbers jump 1–4 then 25–42 (verified, no content missing); Sheet 54 is used twice (29–30 Jun and 1–7 Jul 1916); December 1916 begins on Sheet 90, the same sheet number November ended on.
- Several entries split mid-sentence across sheets and are given whole under the page where they begin; several dates appear twice across sheet breaks with the entry continuing.
- Weekly transport totals in the diary often disagree with their company figures (e.g. Oct 1918: 6,958 vs. 6,961; Jan 1919: 13,070 vs. 13,270) — transcribed as written.
- The 22 January 1918 move destination was struck through in the diary and could not be read.
- The LAC guide's date range for this diary (28 Jan 1916 – 18 Mar 1919, RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020) matches the record; the September 1918 volume is labelled "Septembre" (French) in the LAC manifest.
