# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — December 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps (C.A.S.C.), 3rd Canadian Division (styled "Hqrs. 3rd Canadian Divisional Train" on the cover)
- **Month:** December 1918 (Volume 35) — this volume covers 1 to 31 December 1918
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** December 1918, p. 1 – p. 9 (image files page-0345 – page-0353 of the diary)
- **December 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–9): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–31 December, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" not struck through on these sheets). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Handwritten insertions and corrections in the original are noted where they occur. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. The signature at the foot of the December sheets is read as [uncertain] (see open questions). "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "S.O." = Supply Officer; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "F.P." = Field Punishment; "D.A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "C.I.Bde" = Canadian Infantry Brigade; "M.G.Bn" = Machine Gun Battalion; "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column; "R.T." = as written; "H.M. the KING" = King George V, as written. The diary's own place column gives the Train's location for each entry; the diary text also names railheads and places as written.

### Page 4 — 1–6 December 1918

> **MONS, 1-12-18.** — Colonel Webb came to lunch. Had great chat over the demobilization of the Canadians. S.S.O. reported that Pack Train was broken into and all the rum, medical comforts etc. stolen on way up
>
> [Remarks column:] S.S.O.
>
> **MONS, 2-12-18.** — Transport return for week ending 1st December 1918 :—
>
> Company No. 1 — Average Mileage 3.7, Total Mileage 934, Longest Haul 7
> Company No. 2 — Average Mileage 4, Total Mileage 462, Longest Haul 7
> Company No. 3 — Average Mileage 1.7, Total Mileage 218, Longest Haul 6
> Company No. 4 — Average Mileage 4.3, Total Mileage 547, Longest Haul 12
>
> Total Mileage for the Train 2161 miles. Hardly enough to keep horses hard and in good condition. Pack Train coming in most irregularly.
>
> **MONS, 3-12-18.** — Pack Trains of the 1st, 2nd, & 3rd not in at Railhead at this 12 noon. S.S.O. away seeing about it. Released de G. from arrest without prejudice and as concurs [uncertain]. Weather — Wet.
>
> **MONS, 4-12-18.** — All three trains in now and we are back to normal. Went to CASTEAU and tried one of the water details, a most argumentative, verbose, and stubborn individual. While there inspected Company, and ordered dumbails [uncertain] to be put on in accordance with new order — why it was ever changed licks me. Weather — Dull & wet. Revising Diary. Lieut. Lowery reported for duty, posted him to No. 4 Company. Weather — Dull.
>
> **MONS, 5-12-18.** — Weather—Brighter. Movement Order in but date not yet fixed. H.M. the KING passed the office this morning. Dines at 2 & 3 Companies — put in an hour. Weather — Fair generally.
>
> **MONS, 6-12-18.** — Move tomorrow. BRUSSELS BALL still going on in spite of move. Submitted immediate awards. Again move put off until further orders probably the 11th. This has upset the arrangements the owner of my billet and the mess have made, and he's not so 'nice' over it that we dont

### Page 5 — 6–11 December 1918

> **6-12-18 (continued).** — want to remain if we can get established elsewhere. He must be a queer man to put up with the Hun for four years and is inconvenienced because we want to stay four weeks instead of three.! Weather — Fine and bright.
>
> **MONS, 7-12-18.** — Interpreter out looking for another billet and mess.
>
> **MONS, 8-12-18.** — Lunched at 9th Cdn. Field Ambulance with Colonel Wiyond, and afterwards inspected his transport. He showed me a report from the D.A.D.V.S. and I am bound to say it was most un-called for and unjust. Moved into new billet at 121 Boulevard de La Hopital and a much better one too. Major McLeod and Lieut. Hessey returned to duty from hospital after their 'flu' and look terribly pulled down. Railhead is now at MONS.
>
> **MONS, 9-12-18.** — Lieut. Hessey posted to go back and take command of his Company No. 4. Major McLeod will go round today and get into touch with Guinea [uncertain]. Captain Graham who has done most excellent work as acting S.S.O. will remain assistant S.S.O. and take charge of Xmas Gifts.
>
> Transport for the week ending 8th December 1918 :—
>
> Company No. 1 — Average Mileage 4, Total Mileage 1159, Longest Haul 8
> Company No. 2 — Average Mileage 4, Total Mileage 479, Longest Haul 7
> Company No. 3 — Average Mileage 2.1, Total Mileage 228, Longest Haul 6
> Company No. 4 — Average Mileage 4.4, Total Mileage 430, Longest Haul 10
>
> Total for Train 2294

> **MONS, 10-12-18.** — Orders are that the Division moves tomorrow, all but the 9th C.I.Bde that goes on the following day. Divisional H.Qrs. and Train H.Qrs. follow later. Weather — Fine.
>
> **MONS, 11-12-18.** — Move began at 1 a.m. Papers for Major de G. came in, ordered his re-arrest and the Adjutant to arrange to take summary of evidence and give all facilities to de G. to collect evidence and prep

### Page 6 — 11–17 December 1918

> **11-12-18 (continued).** — are defence. Called at Divisional H.Qrs. and saw A.A.& Q.M.G. Gave him D.A.D.V.S. report on 9th Cdn. Fd. Ambulance, mine, and that of the Train V.O. which corroborates, and lodged my protest. Weather — Very Wet.
>
> **MONS, 12-12-18.** — Capt. Graham goes to No. 1 Coy to command pending disposal of the Major's case as Capt. Dance is ordered home. Capt. Bonnie will take over duties of S.O. unless he gets his special leave, then Capt. Graham will be S.O. and C.O. of Company. Weather — Wet.
>
> **NIVELLES, 13-12-18.** — Train H.Qrs. left MONS 9. a.m. and after one break-down of the car arrived at NIVELLES just after 11 a.m. Weather — Wet.
>
> **NIVELLES, 14-12-18.** — Sanctioned return as requested to England of Capt. Dance and Lieut. Moore. Personally took Capt. Bonnie's application for leave to Division and urged it as a special case. Capt. Dance asked for to go to England where he is urgently required, despatched him today. Supply Officers gone to forward areas. Weather — Fine.
>
> **LA HULPE, 15-12-18.** — Left NIVELLES at 11 a.m. arrived at LA HULPE 12.45 p.m. Got in behind Engineers on a one way road and could not pass for a couple of miles. Divisional H.Qrs. will not move until tomorrow. Major de Graves came for the evening.
>
> **LA HULPE, 16-12-18.** — All day on Court Martial giving evidence. At 11 p.m. wires from Corps come in dated 14th to take on and feed Units on 17th, which is of course impossible. S.S.O. is out doing his best.
>
> **LA HULPE, 17-12-18.** — Now that the Train Companies are again under my Command and do not take orders from Brigades, I am having their locations changed to facilitate hauling from Railhead. Called at Divisional H.Qrs. and S.S.O. Quite a wintry tang in the air today. General Loomis returned from leave unexpectedly about 4 a.m. Many Units are kicking and are getting disgruntled. So far not one kick from the Train, and all are working well. No crimes either. Weather — Cold and Wet.

> **LA HULPE, 19-12-18.** — Interpreter and Capt. Graham gone to purchase straw. Lieut. Boyd going to BRUSSELS to purchase Xmas Fare for the Train. Capt. Banf [uncertain] returned, will do duty for a couple of weeks at M.G.Bn before returning to Train.
>
> **LA HULPE, 20-12-18.** — Went to No. 1 Company and promulgated sentence of F.G.C.M. and made an inspection of Company — must waken this Company, some of the officers are none too sharp — returned at 3 p.m. Capt. Graham sent for to act as D.D.S., and Lieut. George replaces him as Supply Officer, Divl. Troops. Colonel McAlpine and Colonel Wisby [uncertain] called.
>
> **LA HULPE, 21-12-18.** — Railhead changes to GENVAL today. Called at Divisional H.Qrs. Nothing new. Lieut. QWYNNE returned from leave. Lieut. Boyd back from BRUSSELS, could not get turkeys and everything prohibitive prices. Will have to depend on Y.M.C.A.
>
> **LA HULPE, 23-12-18.** — Lieut. Boyd gone to Y.M.C.A. to try and get Xmas cheer for the boys. Managed to get half turkeys half geese, a few apples, biscuits, and chocolate, so that the boys will after all get a decent dinner. Major McLeod gone to WALLERS. Weather — Wet.

> **LA HULPE, 26-12-18.** — Clear and Bright. Movement Orders in. Submitted recommendations for Peace Honours List. Am much surprised at the meagre allotment for the Train, I cannot help thinking that the splendid work of the Train has not been appreciated as much as it should be. From little things I hear it is keenly felt.
>
> **LA HULPE, 27-12-18.** — Wet and Stormy. Railhead for 29th ENGHIEN. Adjutant and Interpreter out settling up for straw. Adjutant and S.S.O. gone to Divisional H.Qrs. to settle about move of Train H.Qrs.
>
> **LA HULPE, 28-12-18.** — No. 4 Company with 9th C.I.Bde move today into OPHASSELT and tomorrow to RENAIX after this one more stage, and on the 2nd January reach the entraining point at BAISEUX. The Adjutant gone to get in touch with all Train Companies and the location of Re-filling Points, as it is too far

> **28-12-18 (continued).** — for them to get back and report — it is just here where the Train should have a motor cycle to keep in touch.
>
> **LA HULPE, 29-12-18.** — Adjutant only got back at 8.30 a.m. after having been out from 7.30 last night getting in touch with Companies. Weather Wet and Cold.

> **LA HULPE, 30-12-18.** — Pulled out from LA HULPE at 10.4M. [uncertain] and called at ENGHIEN Railhead. The Pack Train for 29th had not arrived at noon and no word of its arrival. Could not get in touch with Fifth Army so jumped into car and beat it back to HAL, saw 3rd Corps, a Colonel Harvey, and we did everything as could [uncertain] to help us. Called at Railhead twice and ascertained they could give us one complete dry ration. So back to ENGHIEN got through on the phone to Army and got sanction to draw dry ration. S.S.O. and Supply Column left for HAL to pick it up and deliver. He will stay at Railhead the night and get information re Pack Trains. Adjutant and I then made our way to TOURNAI to locate chalets, in this direction little or nothing had been done for us. Called and reported situation to Division. Weather Cold and Wet.
>
> **TOURNAI, 31-12-18.** — Railhead today will be TOURNAI and I hope to goodness trains will be on time. Adjutant gone to ENGHIEN to get in touch with the S.S.O. for 100 rations for some men returning from leave.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *[signature — [uncertain]] Lieut. Colonel, / Commanding 3rd Canadian Divisional Train.*

## Open questions / uncertain items (December 1918)
- 3 December: the last words of the de G. sentence read "and as concurs [uncertain]" — the final word is faint and may be a different word.
- 4 December: "dumbails [uncertain]" — the word is faint; it may be a piece of horse equipment ordered to be put on under a new order. The diarist's phrase is "why it was ever changed licks me", as written.
- 9 December: "get into touch with Guinea [uncertain]" — "Guinea" is read as written; whether it is a person, place or unit is not clear from the diary.
- 19 December: "Capt. Banf [uncertain]" — the surname is faint and may read differently (e.g. "Banff").
- 20 December: the second visiting colonel reads "Colonel Wisby [uncertain]".
- 30 December: the departure time is typed "10.4M. [uncertain]" — probably 10.40 a.m. The sentence about 3rd Corps reads "and we did everything as could [uncertain] to help us" — the wording is garbled as typed.
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the December sheets is a flourish and is not read with confidence; it is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name.
- The place column on page 7 (18 December) shows a heavily struck-through entry overwritten with "LA HULPE".
- 22 December has NO entry — the diary jumps from 21 December to 23 December. No date is missing otherwise; 6, 11, 23 and 28 December each appear twice, once at the foot of one sheet and once at the head of the next, with the entry continuing across the page break.
- Transport-return arithmetic: the week ending 8 December company totals (1159 + 479 + 228 + 430 = 2296) do not add to the diary's Train total of 2294 — a discrepancy of 2, as written. The other weekly totals in this month check out (week ending 1 December: 2161; week ending 15 December: 6319; week ending 22 December: 6902).
- Names read as: Colonel Webb; Lieut. Lowery; Colonel Wiyond (9th Cdn. Field Ambulance); Major McLeod; Lieut. Hessey; Captain Graham; Capt. Dance; Lieut. Moore; Capt. Bonnie; Lieut. Boyd; Lieut. QWYNNE; Lieut. George; Colonel McAlpine; General Loomis; Colonel Harvey (3rd Corps); Major de G. / Major de Graves.
- No casualties are recorded in this volume. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only.
