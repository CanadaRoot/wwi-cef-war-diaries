- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)

- **March 1916 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–13): cover, contents, diary text 1–31 March, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The diarist's initials "J.A.S." appear at the end of most entries and are transcribed each time. "Coy." = Company; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "Adjt." = Adjutant; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant & Quartermaster General; "D.D.R." = Deputy Director of Remounts; "D.A.P.M." = Deputy Assistant Provost Marshal; "A.D.M.S." = Assistant Director of Medical Services; "D.T." = Divisional Train; "D.H.Q." = Divisional Headquarters; "C.R.A." = Commander Royal Artillery; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "H.D." = heavy draught (horses); "L.D." = light draught; "Dft." = draught; "G.S." = General Service; "O.R." = other ranks; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "Bde." = Brigade; "Battn." = Battalion; "R.S.P." = as written (not expanded). The unit stamp on each sheet reads "H.Q., DIVISIONAL TRAIN / 3rd CANADIAN DIVISION". The "Remarks and references to Appendices" column is blank throughout. The sheets in this volume are numbered 14–24, continuing February's Sheets 1–13; the numbers on Sheets 14 and 15 are partly hidden under the unit stamp but the sequence is unambiguous.

## Pages 3–13 — Diary text (1–31 March 1916)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed footer reads "T.2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S." on Sheets 14–22 and "1875 Wt. W593/826 1,000,000 4/15 J.B.C. & A. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118." on Sheets 23–24.

### Page 3 — 1–3 March 1916 (Sheet No. 14)

Place: Fletre.

> **Fletre, Mar. 1st.** — With S.S.O & Adjt. trying to locate new refilling points & Billets. Inspected new horse with the V.O. for charger. 1st day of 9th move. Major Webb back. V.O. finished glanders test on No. 2 & started it on No. 3 Coy. Col. Simpson dined with us. Consulted Cameron re new R.S.P's. — Thaw restrictions off 12 to-night. Fine mild all day.
>
> **2.** — A.D.V.S. Inspected horses of Train & expressed satisfaction. V.O. finished testing No. 3 Coy.
>
> **3.** — S.S.O meeting this morning. Orders received for No. 2 Coy to move. S.S.O 1st Div. arranged to take over straw for us. Capt Fortin to lunch. Hargreaves called & received orders about move & disposal of Bricks that were to be used for stands. Capt Church, Hennessy & two Subs (for instruction) on F.G.C.M. 6 am Fine mild 9 am rain, 3 pm storm rain & sleet, very dark. 8 pm, rain colder. J.A.S.

### Page 4 — 4–6 March 1916 (Sheet No. 15)

Place: Fletre.

> **Fletre, Mar. 4.** — S.S.O got message from A.A. & Q.M.G. Corps. He will inspect transport & horse lines next week. Good! — Tried case from 2 Coy. Visited refilling points of 2 & 3 Coys. 20 Drivers arrived for H.Q. Coy. More snow cold & wet.
>
> **5.** — Sent one of my horses to No. 3 Coy & got in exchange one from A.D.V.S. Major Webb & S.S.O. went to see if Coy has started back, found nothing being done & gave Capt Graham what he deserved, & S.S.O. did ditto to Capt Fortin, & I hope they made it hot & strong. Arty. moving back. Sun & Rain — still cold snow gone.
>
> **6.** — A section of H.Q. Coy came in at midnight. The same old game going on, order counter order & disorder. What a blessing an even temper under control is, in such circumstances. Fortin back & only a small portion of H.Q. Coy left to come in to-morrow. Capt Oliphant admitted to Hospital malaria. Snow, Sun & thaw alternately all day — frost at night. J.A.S.

### Page 5 — 7–10 March 1916 (Sheet No. 16)

Place: Fletre.

> **Fletre, Mar. 7.** — All Headquarters Coy back. Arty move and 27 + 28 on. S.S.O. 2nd Div. called & discussed feeding arrangements to take effect after completion of moves with S.S.O. 3rd Divn. 20 men sent back to Base Horse Transport Depot. 6th Bde 'phoned up to express their thanks for the way we carried out all feeding & transport arrangements during time they were attached to 3rd Div. and their regret at being taken away. Snow & rain alternately all day, heavy fall during night — no sun all day.
>
> **8.** — D.A.Q.M.G. out inspecting Coys. Gas notice out. Nothing of moment to relate. Bright sun all day. Frost at night. Snow going fast.
>
> **9.** — Lindsay and Sub. Park arrived. Instructed S.S.O. to have all arrangements come to between S.S.O's put in writing & then blame can be placed where it should be. Fine all day.
>
> **10.** — In office all day — moves on. Fine but dull. J.A.S.

### Page 6 — 11–13 March 1916 (Sheet No. 17)

Place: Fletre.

> **Fletre, Mar. 11.** — Heard case against Driver from No. 4 Coy. Cutting horses attached to limber wagon, remanded for further evidence in afternoon. Case dismissed on grounds that this team are always trying to break, driver has several times reported this bad quality in them, he is a good man, takes great care of his team & makes great fuss over them, above borne out by his Officer & Section Sergeant. Inspected Horses, Harness & Wagons at No. 4 Coy. Wagons — good. Horses — fair. Harness bad condition. Warned all to have a vast improvement for my next inspection. Fine misty afternoon tried to rain.
>

### Page 7 — 13 March (continued)–15 March 1916 (Sheet No. 18)

Place: Fletre.

> **Fletre, Mar. 13 (continued).** — Water details are samples to be remembered. He & M.O. have some disagreement re water details, will see them & straighten matters out. Inspected horses harness & wagons of No. 2 Coy, see decided improvement, but can be much better, & must be. Glanders test No. 4 Coy. 44 horses. Beautiful day sunny & warm — clear.
>
> **14.** — V.O. turned in 6 horses to Mobile Vet. Sec. [uncertain] from No. 2 & 3 from No. 3 Coy. No. 2 Coy arranged with farmer for use of portion of field to park wagons @ 25 francs. O.C. of 3 Trains Conference at Gen Woods, discussed promotions etc. First meeting of S. Captains, T.O.s, S.S.O's & Q.M's addressed by D.A.Q.M.G., S.S.O & Adjt of Train in my absence. Met O.C. H.Q. Coy & M.O. fixed up trouble re water details. With Adjt visited No. 2 & 3 Coys. Very nice & fine all day.
>
> **15.** — 85th Batty Lahore are not to have their rations delivered again to present location by order (verbal) Lt. Col. McBrien & he is taking the matter up re this & their retaining Brakesmen. Sent back correspondence re recommending Sergt.

### Page 8 — 15 March (continued)–17 March 1916 (Sheet No. 19)

Place: Fletre.

> **Fletre, Mar. 15 (continued).** — Mann C.M.R. for his Commission, agreeing with Col. Simpson's remarks. Some rain early morning. Fine all day.
>
> **16.** — Following letter received 3rd Canadian Div: Q/8/30. 3rd Canadian Div Train. C.R.A. 1st Canadian Div: has expressed his appreciation of the most efficient manner in which your H.Q. Coy provided for two brigades during the time that they were with this division — The G.O.C. this Division is very pleased indeed to get this report of your good work, & knows that it will be continued. S.S.O. O.C. H.Q. Coy & O.C. Supply Column gone to new area to get information. Meeting of O.C. Coys. Supply Officers & Q.M's in evening, gave full & definite instructions re move & rations. Fine all day two very light showers.
>
> **17.** — Gave Capt Hargreaves instructions that his new interpreter should mess with the Sergts. Move started. Lt. Plumptree came with half H.Q. Coy 2nd Div. to relieve half ours. He dined with us. Paymaster evacuated to Hospital. A.D.M.S. came to see me re M.O. Orderly. I insisted he must be at all stables. J.A.S.

### Page 9 — 17 March (continued)–20 March 1916 (Sheet No. 20)

Place: Fletre to 18 March; Busseboom from 19 March.

> **Fletre, Mar. 17 (continued).** — and as he is a C.A.S.C. driver, M.O. cannot take him away from those duties, & must arrange his own not to interfere. Fine all day.
>
> **18.** — H.Q. Coy & No. 3 moved out. Lunch & dinner at No. 2. O.C. 24th [uncertain] Train called later his S.S.O. to see mine. Capts. Oliphant & Phillips came in to see about their move. Lieut Blake discharged from Hospital yesterday. My charger came. Fine all day slight shower evening.
>
> **Busseboom, 19.** — Moved with S.S.O. & Adjt. to new area — got fixed up in new offices. All Coys up got pretty well settled in, but found camps in filthy state especially H.Q. Coy.
>
> **20.** — All settling in very well — more lively here. No. 2 Coy will not get into their proper place until to-morrow, owing to the Coy they relieve, not moving out, but can double up with the other two Coys alright. Fine.

### Page 10 — 21–23 March 1916 (Sheet No. 21)

Place: Busseboom (spelled "Buseboom" in the Place column on this sheet).

> **Busseboom, Mar. 21.** — American sacks coming with oats are rotten, & easily tear, causing much unnecessary waste — am reporting same. D.H.Q. move in to-day. Report from O.C. H.Q. Coy & V.O. State of horses & wagons received from 2nd Train, also condition of camp as he took it over. Sent in report re same. A.Q.M.G. & D.A.Q.M.G. called, also Gen. Nairn, he came to see about straw in lieu of hay short. Fine rain all day.
>
> **22.** — Lieut Blake again on sick list fear that boy is very delicate. 28 H.D. from Lahore, kept 27, returned one no good, one other lame but not bad. Wire received to detail men for leave. Craig off back to pay No. 4 Coy. Hennessy & Oliphant looking out short cut to Railhead. Fine rain all day.
>
> **23.** — General inspection all round. S.S.O & I called on D.A.Q.M.G. General discussion, also had a long talk to Genl Nairn re 2 Train Horses. Supply section moved to Railhead. Wet all day. J.A.S.

### Page 11 — 24–26 March 1916 (Sheet No. 22)

Place: Busseboom (spelled "Buseboom" in the Place column on this sheet).

> **Busseboom, Mar. 24.** — First refilling at Railhead, very bad arrangements there for refilling, too much man handling of supplies, & no adequate shelter for cutting up. Took 2½ hours. Can much improve on this. D.A.Q.M.G. came to see us start refilling. Very stormy day — snow & wind all day.
>
> **25.** — Refilling in better time but oats & hay on pack train were at wrong end of train, and had to be man-handled practically whole length of train & took up unnecessary time & labour. S.S.O meeting at Corps. D.D.R. viewed horses L.D. with me & I decided to cast 8 & keep balance as they might be classed as either L.D. or H.D., & he expressed himself as satisfied. That with the 27 already received will make the Train Draught horses practically complete.
>
> **26.** — Pack train later than ever, not only refilling later, but convoy kept standing out in the wet, consequence one horse died. No ill effects on the men, but the whole delay most annoying & should be avoided.

### Page 12 — 26 March (continued)–29 March 1916 (Sheet No. 23)

Place: Busseboom (spelled "Buseboom" in the Place column on this sheet).

> **Busseboom, Mar. 26 (continued).** — Inspected No. 2 Coy. O.C. Coy making great improvements. This officer is doing very good work. Got inoculated in afternoon — more wet.
>
> **27.** — In bed all day feeling effects of inoculation. Must keep an eye on Capt Fortin. Paymaster back of hospital full of praise for the way he was treated. Wet & fine, lot of rain during night.
>
> **28.** — Still feeling very cheap. D.A.Q.M.G. gone with S.S.O. to see about transportation of straw. Adjt. got inoculated this afternoon. All going smoothly well. M.O. ordered hut to be burnt. Fine all day.
>
> **29.** — Refilling Railhead much better time 7.8.9. actual time refilling 14 minutes, time can be saved by better judgement in off loading train so that Supply Sections can be cutting up when oats & hay are being off loaded. Hennessy (Adjt.) feeling as bad as I was after inoculation. Inspected No. 4 Coy in morning. A.A. & Q.M.G. of Corps, & A.A. & Q.M.G. of Division inspected all four Coys. & seemed pleased, but said very little. Visited D.H.Q. afternoon with S.S.O. Wrote Col. MacB. asking him to grant Craig interview. Very quiet all day. Fine & drying winds. J.A.S.

### Page 13 — 30–31 March 1916 (Sheet No. 24)

Place: Busseboom (spelled "Buseboom" in the Place column on this sheet).

> **Busseboom, Mar. 30.** — Refilling done in good time this morning. Cyclists reported their arrival this afternoon. Arranged with their 2nd in command to return supply wagons to H.Q. Coy. Sent him on to O.C. H.Q. Coy to discuss the various items of interest to them, & which should be understood from the first. Investigating complaint from Pioneer Battn re rations. Lot have passed. Interview with D.A.Q.M.G. 7 Horses evacuated to the Mobile Vets. all of them came to us from the 2nd Train on the 8th, one broke its leg & had to be shot. Dr. Tyddlet's [uncertain] diagnosis of Lt. Blake's trouble confirmed by letter to-day. He is admitted to a London hospital & I hope to see him while on leave in a day or two.
>
> **31.** — Quarter of an hour saved on refilling this morning. Col. Anderson called & requested that I leave Supply & Baggage Wagons with him, had to refuse. Made an inspection (General) of H.Q. & 4 Coys to-day. Capt Craig & I had interview with A.A. & Q.M.G. re how he was to be disposed of. Saw A.A. & Q.M.G. again at night re same. Fine & bright all day — ground drying fast.
>

## Open questions / uncertain items (March 1916)
- The cover reads "Volume 2", but the contents page reads "Volume 16 - 3." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number. The contents page's "Diary Text 11 Pages" matches the 11 diary sheets (Sheets 14–24).
- The sheets in this volume are numbered 14–24, continuing February's Sheets 1–13. The numbers on Sheets 14 and 15 are partly hidden under the unit stamp but the sequence (16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24) is unambiguous.
- The new place is spelled "Busseboom" in the Place column on 19 March and "Buseboom" on 21–31 March — both spellings as written; the index uses "Busseboom".
- 3 March: "Capt Church, Hennessy & two Subs (for instruction) on F.G.C.M." — F.G.C.M. is read as Field General Court Martial.
- 9 March: "Lindsay and Sub. Park arrived." — "Sub. Park" is transcribed as written; its meaning is unclear.
- 12 March: "R.S.P. 24th [uncertain] back 4 pm." — the unit figure "24th" is read as written but is unclear; there was no 24th Divisional Train, so it may be a misreading of "2nd". The same figure recurs on 18 March ("O.C. 24th [uncertain] Train called").
- 13 March: "Water details are samples to be remembered." is transcribed as written; the phrasing is odd and "samples" may be a slip for another word.
- 14 March: "V.O. turned in 6 horses to Mobile Vet. Sec. [uncertain] from No. 2 & 3 from No. 3 Coy." — the abbreviation after "Mobile Vet." is not read with confidence (it may be "Sec." for Section, or a section number); the horse numbers in the sentence (6 from No. 2, 3 from No. 3) do not add up to the "6 horses" stated at its start.
- 14 March: "O.C. of 3 Trains Conference at Gen Woods" is transcribed as written; whether "Gen Woods" is a person (General Woods) or a place is not clear.
- 15 March: "85th Batty Lahore" is transcribed as written.
- 17 March: "A.D.M.S. came to see me re M.O. Orderly. I insisted he must be at all stables." — the sense is that the M.O.'s orderly, being a C.A.S.C. driver, cannot be taken from his duties.
- 22 March: "28 H.D. from Lahore, kept 27, returned one no good, one other lame but not bad." — the "28" is corroborated by the 25 March entry ("That with the 27 already received will make the Train Draught horses practically complete").
- 26 March: one horse died after the convoy stood out in the wet — the first animal casualty recorded in the diary.
- 29 March: "Refilling Railhead much better time 7.8.9. actual time refilling 14 minutes" — "7.8.9." is transcribed as written; its meaning (possibly times) is unclear.
- 30 March: "Dr. Tyddlet's [uncertain] diagnosis of Lt. Blake's trouble" — the doctor's name is not read with confidence; it may be "Tyddlett".
- 30 March: "Lot have passed." is transcribed as written; its meaning is unclear.
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the March sheet is not read with confidence; it appears to read "J. A. S. Findlay" but is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name. A second signature at the foot of the sheet reads "Lieut. Col." in a different hand.
- Names read as: Col. Simpson; Cameron; Capt. Fortin; Hargreaves; Capt. Church; Hennessy; Maj. Webb; Capt. Graham; Capt. Oliphant; Lindsay; Lt. Plumptree; Capt. Hargreaves; Sergt. Mann (C.M.R.); Lt.-Col. McBrien; Gen. Nairn; Lieut. Blake; Capt. Phillips; Craig; Col. Anderson; Col. MacB.; Dr. Tyddlet [uncertain].
- Casualties in this volume: Capt. Oliphant admitted to hospital with malaria (6 March); the Paymaster evacuated to hospital (17 March); Lieut. Blake repeatedly on the sick list and admitted to a London hospital (22 and 30 March); two bombs dropped on the H.Q. Company refilling supply point (12 March) with no casualties reported; one horse died in the wet (26 March) and one horse from the 2nd Train broke its leg and had to be shot (30 March).
