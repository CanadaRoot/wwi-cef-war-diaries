## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The Place column is blank on every diary sheet in this volume; no place is entered against any date. The Hour column is likewise blank throughout. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not transcribed against every entry here. Overtyping and handwritten corrections in the original are transcribed as the final readable text, with the correction noted where it matters. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer (as written); "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "D.D. of S. & T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "D. of S. & T." = Director of Supplies and Transport; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "S.O." = Supply Officer (as written); "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "G.C.M." = General Court Martial; "J.A." = Judge Advocate (as written); "F.P. No. I." = Field Punishment No. 1; "L. of C." = Lines of Communication; "H.D.H." / "H.D." = Heavy Draught Horse (as written); "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding. ### Page 3 — 1–4 August 1916 (Sheet No. 61)

> **1.** — Day started with ground fog. O.C. Headquarters Company was at Refilling Point for first time. Called with S.S.O. at Divisional Head Quarters, and again in afternoon on A.A. & Q.M.G. with Adjutant re new Officers coming to Train. Captain Oliphant asked if he might be allowed to exchange back to 1st Train. He is dissatisfied with the way the Authorities are sending up men from the Base to replace casualties, green men, and ignoring the good work of those who came out to the Front, and remained there from the beginning. He is not alone in feeling like this, and I cannot blame them. I feel the same way myself. There seems to be little credit attached to the fact that a man came out on the 1st Call. The way Juniors are jumped up at Home, and then come out here is enough to discourage and kill, the vim of anyone. Very fine and hot.
>
> **2** — Tried case from No. 2 Company, minor case, but as this Company has been going back since Captain Oliphant left it, I wanted to strengthen his hands on his return, and gave punishment of 14 days F.P. No. I. Acting Sergeant Stewart returned from 8th C.F. Ambulance, and sent him to No. 3 Coy as Private, to be watched and promoted if he should warrant it later. Inspected Headquarters Company Harness, not what I desire. Gave demonstration explaining my requirements, and in future will take no excuse. Had to dress V.O. down re care of his horses. He is a poor Vet. and lazy.
>
> **3** — Was Casement executed to day? I hope so. With Adjutant visited Divisional Head Quarters. Saw Headquarters Company at Drill. O.C. not good, lacks experience. Received his letter complaining of my adverse report and will take it to A.A. & Q.M.G. to morrow, but will not write reply unless asked as I prefer to treat it with the contempt it deserves. Franklin dined with us, first time he has. Spring returned. Tried case from No. 3 Company, Corporal Stewart, The Doctor reported on him, and asked for a Board, suspect malingering. Hot and fine again.
>
> **4** — French papers state Casement was hung. Good [uncertain] Adjutant and I saw A.A. & Q.M.G. this morning re Major McGillivray's letter, replied to same and forwarded it. Traffic Control Officer at this post No. 6, lunched with us. Lieut. Glasco by name. Adjutant and I to D.D. of S. & T. at Cassell, he got instructions as to the filling of Army Form B.213a, and I had a conference with the D.D. of S. & T. re how the Establishment could possibly be cut down. On way home inspected Workshops of Supply Column. Adjutant explained how B.213a's are to be filled in in future, specially explained to O.C. Headquarters Company. While at Major Lawson's Captain

### Page 4 — 4–7 August 1916 (Sheet No. 62)

> **4** (continued) — Strathy came in and seemed very surprised to see us. Asked to have a cup of tea and replied no I must get back at once and left. I had an idea he had been drinking — that was the reason of his prompt exit.
>
> **5** — N.C.O. No. 3 Company finished his B.213a at 12-30; at 2 a tommie wakened me up to get his direction Got a report about Stewart and showed it to his O.C. with instructions to bring him before me. Got secret instructions re prospective move. Called all Officers Commanding before me and gave them their instructions. Captain Graham reported to me that Captain Strathy has for some time been most unsatisfactory, and I instructed him to lay a formal charge against him. Got official notification of Captain Stirrett's evacuation. Pointed out to Captain Oliphant certain alteration I wished him to make in the fitting of his traces. Dull in morning, but sunny and fine later
>
> **6** — Adjutant and I visited Divisional Head Quarters. Adjutant, M.O. and Self went to STEENVOORDE and saw Captain Strathy. M.O. examined him and said he should go to hospital, heart and lung affected. I say he had been drinking, and was prepared to place him in arrest, but in face of Medical opinion could not, however he has gone to Hospital. Visited No. 4 Company and warned Lt. Greer he would be in orders as S.O. for No. 3 Company. Captain McLennan reported here for duty, sent him to see Captain Oliphant, and arrange for McLennan to take over command of No. 2 Company, and Captain Oliphant to be posted to No. 4 Company as S.O. Got wire from Head Quarters to say the General would see me and McGillivray tomorrow morning, time to be arranged later. Some scrapping between aeroplanes very high up. Fine all day.
>
> **7** — Called at 1st Divisional Train and went to Corps for a conference of Train Officers re deductions additions, and amendments to Train Establishment. Arranged with Colonel Simon and Massey to meet and confer in future before going to Conferences, so as to do all our arguing beforehand and save time. Called at Divisional Head Quarters, saw A.A.&Q.M.G. Adjutant taking summary of evidence for F.G.C.M. all afternoon. Lieut. Hessey reported for duty. Captain McLennan took over from Captain Oliphant command of No. 2 Company, and he begins S.O. duties. O.C. H.Q. Company issued orders for docks and sheaths of all horses to be cleaned on three days a week, notified him an alteration I wished in this. Fine, but dull and not much sun.

### Page 5 — 8–10 August 1916 (Sheet No. 63)

> **8** — Adjutant and I visited Head Quarters, and on way back looked round Headquarters Company Lines, quantity of loose straw round the roads, wagons very badly parked. The General and A.A.&Q.M.G. visited Headquarters Company. Major McGillivray went with me and had interview with the General and I agreed to slightly alter my report, still keeping the main fact, lack of experience prominent, and re-submit same. Shewed him amended report which he signed, and had a long heart to heart talk, nothing he said made me change my opinion, and I cannot drive it into his head I have not a personal grudge against him. I am inclined to think he has a good opinion of himself, and still thinks he is it. If leave came on and he was second in command I would not dare to go away. Straw shows which way the wind blows, and from what I gather, if I did go there would be trouble with the Officers. They place no confidence in him either. About 9-30 heavy bombardment started, and word came a gas attack on, all stood to by 12-30 all was practically quiet. Fine all day, and beautiful clear cool night with a mild wind blowing towards us.
>
> **9** — Visited and Inspected all Train Companies to day. Paid special attention to the horses gassed yesterday, they all seem to be labouring for breath like a man with an attack of asthma. Reduced a Lance Corporal in No. 3 Company who was up before me to day. Re-distributed between Companies the 56 trained men returned from the Lahore. Very hot all day.
>
> **10** — Visited Railhead and carefully watched all the cutting up, apportioning and loading. Everything done with method and smartness as usual; some traces and breechings I altered notably in Headquarters Company, this Company was five minutes late in pulling in, otherwise all loading, pulling in and out, done with utmost dispatch and no confusion, all know their work and do it. Received orders to dispatch Major Scott home, gave him the necessary papers. Sorry to loose him, and wish I could have him as O.C. Headquarters Company. S.S.O., Adjutant, and self visited Divisional Head Quarters. Had O.C. Headquarters Company and Captain Phillips before me, and gave them a dressing. Major Hogarth of 4th Train called, and I gave him some advice re feeding new arrivals of his Division, also arranged to lend him for three days Staff Sgt. O'Malley so as to get him into the correct way of starting his indents and returns. Meeting of Officers at 8 to review general points that have been noted for some time. Lectured to all Officers. Fine and hot.

### Page 6 — 11–14 August 1916 (Sheet No. 64)

> **11** — Convoy much better after lecture. S.S.O. and I called at Divisional Head Quarters. G.C.M. on and using Office. S.S.O. of 4th called and went into many matters new to him. Court-Martial on all day. Fine all day.
>
> **12** — S.S.O. meeting at Corps. Interviewed a Private from the 60th for his Commission. Visited Divisional Head Quarters with Adjutant, spoke to A.A.&Q.M.G. re Captain Strathy and Major McGillivray. Salvage case before me to day, remanded by accused's choice for F.G.C.M. Disposed of case myself from No. 2 Company. Chaplain called and arranged for a service tomorrow, and he would get a Band. Very hot.
>
> **13** — Re-heard case from Salvage Company, and had to dismiss same. [Handwritten above this line: "Lack of Corroborative evidence".] D.A.Q.M.G. called and he took Adjutant to front line with him. Wire that Captain Strathy would be back again tomorrow. Advised O.C. No. 2 Company to meet him, as he would be attached to him for Transport Duties. Church Parade at 3 p.m., Captain Graham acting as Adjutant in absence of Captain Hennessy, and very well he did it too. The Band of the 58th came and helped us with the hymns. Marching of Headquarters Company and No. 4 Company leaves much to be desired. O.C. Headquarters Coy. and No. 2 Company do not pay enough attention to saluting their C.O. when they pass him at the head of their Companies. Captain Oliphant to lunch, and Pardrie to tea. Not so bright but fine and warm still.
>
> **14** — Dull morning inclined to rain, cleared later to hot and sunny. With Adjutant inspected Nos. 4 and 2 Companies, at latter promulgated sentence of Corporal Stewart and it should be a salutary lesson to others. Saw a run away smartly stopped by one of this Company, in the lines. Visited Divisional Headquarters. Colonel Webb called, he looks very well and is proud of his command of which he gives a good account. At home they tried to get him to take extra Majors but he absolutely refused. All surplus cars, cookers, mess carts, Officers etc. were promptly turned back. They are getting down to real business in the Canadians at last. From what Colonel Webb told the D. of S.&T. at Bramshott, good is likely to result, and he is better aware now of the necessity of co-ordination and co-operation with this side.

### Page 7 — 15–20 August 1916 (Sheet No. 65)

> **15** — Adjutant and I to Divisional Head Quarters. Drawing up scheme for convoy of whole Train necessary road space, and order of march etc. Making notes for O.C. to draw up orders for entraining and detraining. Assembly of whole Train on the Road to night to try them out.
>
> **16** — Measured up Road Space occupied by Train last night, and had about 30 yards to spare. Had Adjutant of 4th Train this afternoon for instruction in rendering returns. S.S.O. and Adjutant gone to 4th Train to give them assistance, and I am going through collection of correspondence and carrying out some destruction. Nice and Fine and cooler.
>
> **17** — Refilling at Railhead this morning was carried on exceptionally well. Visited Divisional Head Quarters, took Colonel McBrien to YPRES and back to VLAMERTINGHE. Saw Grant and heard complaint managed to smooth things over all right, and sent him away happy. Case from Headquarters Coy. and one from No. 3. Colonel Webb called, arranged for one man from each Company to come here and attend at Company Orderly Rooms to see how returns are made out. A good deal of rain.
>
> **18** — Interviewed Captain Strathy re visiting Units, and a visit of 6 hours to HAZEBROUKE, and am most dissatisfied with his answers. I informed him I would send in an adverse report about him and of course let him see it, which I did later on. Had the pleasure of having tea at General Lipsits and meeting General Sir Sam Hughes. I thought he looked tired. I had all the O.C.s up this evening and went over the scheme set, pointing out their mistakes and weak spots. Also talked to them of the Convoy as formed up the other night. Webb called in on his way home. Mostly wet.
>
> **19** — Adjutant away on Court Martial at Divisional Head Quarters. Major Edwards S.S.O. of the 4th Divisional Train called and had a long consultation re taking over from us when we move. Major Hogarth, S.S.O. of the 4th Canadian Divisional Train called and got certain information re his work. Lightened kit, and destroyed unnecessary correspondence. V.O. treating my horse wrong. Partly fine, but mostly wet.
>
> **20** — Packing up what can be packed in readiness for a move. Colonel of No. 4 Train with his S.S.O. and Adjutant called, and made some arrangements with me for their taking over from us. His S.S.O. and mine gone to Railhead and coal dump, also to make preliminary arrangements.

### Page 8 — 20–25 August 1916 (Sheet No. 66)

> **20** (continued) — I visited with S.S.O. and Adjutant, Divisional Head Quarters, and got particulars about our new billets. Colonel McRae with Lieut. Colonel Webb paid me an unexpected visit late afternoon, and arranged for a consultation, and inspection on Tuesday afternoon, he will come to lunch. Fine but dull, some showers.
>
> **21** — I went with Adjutant to see new billets at STEENVOORDE, all very poor, and the boys will now learn a little something of active service conditions, do them good. Called on way back at Divisional Head Quarters. Captain Graham reported unofficially that Lieut. Aldous was not making good, and does not seem to try or want to. S.S.O. and Adjutant again to Head Quarters to ascertain artillery moves, so that I can issue Train Orders. Dull but comparatively fine.
>
> **22** — D. of S. & T. lunched, and then went over transport of Headquarters Company, expressed himself as very well satisfied. Had a long talk about promotions and gave him a letter re Captain Stirrett. Interviewed Captain Strathy, who said he did not want to be paraded before the Genl. Does not excuse himself in any way, but is most anxious to be allowed to remain in France, and given another chance to make good, and I recommended he be sent to L. of C. Inspected Nos. 2, 3, and 4 Companies. Fine all day.
>
> **23** — S.S.O. to Divisional Head Quarters. Busy with moves, and answering questions re same. No more wagons to Engineers, again their guide was useless. S.S.O. 4th Canadians and Colonel McRae called, former in doubt as to coal, coke, and charcoal ration. If artillery would let us know their move intentions, I could get my orders for Train completed. No. 2 Company moved out to day. Greer's brother-in-law Tommey to hospital, kicked by horse, nothing dangerous. Fine all day.
>
> **24** — O.C. Supply came and made all and final arrangements re the new Railhead, and handing over supplies. Signal Company came and removed phone, and Divisional Head Quarters gone, but as far as I know officially they are here yet. Wonder what they would say if they did not get their Staff [uncertain] packing up. Young Magee from N.B. came to see me. Staff left BUSSEBOOM at 1-30 for STEENVOORDE. Headquarters Company left at 1-45 and arrived at 5-50 rather slow travelling, and had it fine all the way. Wet evening.
>

> **26** — One Section of No. 4 Company came in this morning, and came from Railhead just on 3 hours, good time and all the horses looking well, however loads were not heavy. >
> **28** — V.O. Adjutant and myself visited remounts to try and find four chargers; two for Train and two for D.A.Q.M.G. saw nothing fit to throw a leg across. Called in at Divisional Head Quarters. Staff gone to see new area. Very little rain in the morning and very fine later, farmers carrying wheat until dark, others picking their hops, and they look in very good condition.
>
> **29** — I with S.S.O. and Adjutant visited Dumps at Nos. 3 and 4 Companies in time [uncertain] the former to see Supply Column come in and off load, they took 30 minutes, and stacked none too well; they can get time down to 20 minutes. Interviewed C.Q.M.S. Hutchins before he goes to morrow to 9th Field Ambulance, and gave him pointers. Colonel Webb 4th Train called, and we had a long discussion re promotions. He wants me to see him again to morrow, if possible, anyway before I go, and talk this over again. Colonel Peters of No. 9 Field Ambulance called and wanted Smith promoted to W.O. Rank instead of sending him a W.O., and was evidently vexed he had no say in the matter. Slight rain early, heavier in afternoon with thunder and lightning, from 5 p.m. in a deluge and continued during night.

### Page 10 — 30–31 August 1916 (Sheet No. 68)

> **30** — Adjutant and I called at Divisional Head Quarters. Went on to 4th Train, had long talk with O.C., stayed to dinner. Had to put out all lights on way back until we entered ABEELE. Instruction at Gas School postponed owing to wet which continued heavily all day. The horses are standing it well.
>
> **31** — Adjutant all morning acting as J.A. at Court Martial, so hung round lines all day. Had a beastly headache and feeling good for nothing, cannot keep my head up. Some rain, dull but generally fine.

## Open questions / uncertain items (August 1916)
- The cover reads "Volume 7" but the contents page reads "Volume 16 - 8." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number.
