# War Diary — Headquarters 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — June 1916

- **Unit:** Headquarters 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, 3rd Canadian Division
- **Month:** June 1916 (Volume 5) — this volume covers 1 to 30 June 1916
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** June 1916, p. 1 – p. 8 (image files page-0057 – page-0064)
- **June 1916 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–8): cover, contents page, diary text 1–30 June, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). The diary is written in the first person by the Officer Commanding. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. The Place column is blank throughout — no place is recorded in the diary's Place column, so places named in the index are taken from the entry text where a place is named. Words struck through in the original are noted in the open questions, not silently dropped. Entries that run across a page break are given whole under the page where they begin, with the continuation noted. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "D.D.S & T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "R.F.A." = Royal Field Artillery; "H.D." = heavy draught; "L.D." = light draught. The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12."

## Pages 3–8 — Diary text (1–30 June 1916)

### Page 3 — 1–6 June 1916 (Sheet No. 49)

> **1916 June 1** — Paymaster back from leave, reported this morning. Took Petrie to General for interview re his commission, and he signed it. Dined with General and had a long talk about Train and am glad to say he expressed his satisfaction with what I had done so far. Things lively. Charlie came to see me and stayed for tea and dinner. Fine.
>
> **2** — General look round, piles of correspondence. Remanded case for F.G.C.M. Dr. & Head Quarters Company do not seem to be pulling as well as they might, but hope I have straightened things out. Heavy Bombardment. Wild rumours of last night not yet confirmed. Hot and fine all day.
>
> **3** — Dr. Ordered away and No 9 Field Ambulance will carry on for him here. Called in at Headquarters and heard the news about the scrap. We will all miss our General. Called at Railhead and Salvage Company. Pointed out to O.C. Companies what they were to keep in mind when they sent names in as recommended for Military Medal. Only O.C. Headquarters Company had names, 2 transport and 1 supply, and is to submit two in writing, one Supply and one Transport. Very heavy artillery in evening. Fine all day.
>
> **4** — Adjutant and Mr McDonald at Court Martial, Private from [uncertain] Battn. placed by me under arrest for coming into our Billet drunk. Charlie came to lunch. Church Parade for Train at Headquarters Company Parade Ground. Fine but dull, rain towards evening.
>
> **5** — 8th Brigade moved to rest at Steenvoorde this morning. No 3 Company followed this afternoon. The 7th Brigade goes to morrow also No. 2 Company. Lorries will come into use again for supplies. Ambulances, Field Coys. Engineers, Pioneer & Composite Battalions are switched over to Headquarters Company and No. 4 Company for feeding. The Staff do not give us information soon enough to make necessary arrangements, consequently our horses have to do much unnecessary hauling. Report that No. 3 Company left a good quantity of material behind, and camp dirty. M.O. returned to Train. Visited Divisional Headquarters, and had long talk with A.A. & Q.M.G. re moves, coal dump and refilling points etc. Major Lawson called mid-day and Colonel Simson at tea time. Dull then rain, but fine in evening.
>
> **6** — Went with O.C. Headquarters Company to see Colonel Anderson re a report from Shorncliffe that he (Webb) was appointed O.C. 4th Train. It was in orders at Shorncliffe and they were expecting Webb to arrive at any moment. Up to the present I have heard not one word Officially about it. Saw the "Q" Branch at Division. Dull, wet and cloudy all day. [Entry continues on next page.]

### Page 4 — 6–12 June 1916 (Sheet No. 50)

> **1916 June 6** — [Continued from previous page.] expecting Webb to arrive at any moment. Up to the present I have heard not one word Officially about it. Saw the "Q" Branch at Division. Dull, wet and cloudy all day.
>
> **7** — Six men returned one day late with extension of leave from Record Office, and granted on the most flimsy of excuses too. How complete discipline is to be maintained with this sort of thing going on is something to be worried out. Move of 7th Brigade off, have had to recall Baggage Wagons and Supply Section. Called at Headquarters after all this. Counter orders came to move. Oh what glorious muddling. Capt. Graham took over No. 3 Company. Fine.
>
> **8** — Inspected Lines left by No. 2 Company, all very clean. Adjutant and I visited Division Headquarters. Fine.
>
> **9** — Went to STEENVOORDE early. Had Breakfast at No. 2 Company. Inspected new Location then on to No. 3 Company, also the refilling point. Adjutant and I to Headquarters Division. Rain.
>
> **10** — S.S.O. to conference. I took S.S. Petrie to D.D.S & T. for interview re his commission and requested if possible, he be posted to my Train, and I think this can be arranged. Anthony called from 3rd Imperial R.F.A. and stayed to tea. Rain most of the day.
>
> **11** — S.S.O., M.O., and O.C. Headquarters Company visited Nos. 2 & 3 Companies. Self visited Divisional Headquarters and had a talk with the General. Webb came in to tea. S.S.O. and Adjutant adjourned a case for documents. Hughes called in for escort to go for man at baths. Wet morning, fine later.
>
> **12** — S.S.O. visited Headquarters. Adjutant and M.O. to Poperinghe. Capt. Oliphant came up from his Company, and I instructed him to join Headquarters Company to morrow and learn the ropes before Major [uncertain] Webb leaves to take command of 4th Train. What all the delay is, beats me. He should be at home getting them into shape, They will be suddenly ordered out here half ready. Again adjourned case for settlement of accused's rank. Heavy firing for short time at 4 a.m. Wet all day.

### Page 5 — 13–18 June 1916 (Sheet No. 51)

> **1916 June 13** — Headquarters with S.S.O. Colonel Shaw called about taking over two days supply of potatoes from us. Lieut. Anthony called. M.O. looked in and had tea, then returned to Ambulance. Made appointment to get tooth stopped to morrow. No news yet re departure of Major Webb. Wet all day.
>
> **14** — S.S.O. and Adjutant gone to Nos. 2 & 3 Companies. Capt. Oliphant reported to join Headquarters Company. Wrote Simpson sending on letter from Webb and Stirrett re date of their being gazetted Captains. Dentist tooth filling. P.M. back. 2 hours sunshine rest rain and dull. Time put on one hour.
>
> **15** — Never noticed any difference because of time being advanced one hour. S.S.O. and I to Headquarters. Leave cut for train to two per week. Went with D.D.R. to inspect jibbing horse of No. 4 Company, got him cast. O.C. No. 4 on Court Martial. Major Greer and Weir called in afternoon. Casualties pretty heavy last two days. One German wounded prisoner reported by Gas Officer 1st Division, Lt. Beaumont, to have said "God damn the Kaiser". No sun but fair most of the day, little rain early.
>
> **16** — Inspected No. 4 Company. General look round. Visited Headquarters. Furious bombardment. Fine all day.
>
> **17** — Inspected convoy at Railhead, and again on road. Turned back lame horse. All convoy very good with exception of Headquarters wagons, reported same to D.A.Q.M.G. 2nd Division straw pile on fire. Had to gallop past to get to Headquarters. S.S.O. at Corps Meeting. Reported to me that D.O.S. said at Corps I have the best kept horses in Corps. Colonel Simson came at tea time, congratulated me on being mentioned in despatches. So did A.A. & Q.M.G. Very glad Train has been recognised. Simson gave me correspondence re Webb and Stirrett. Aeroplanes busy. Fine, plenty sun.
>
> **18** — Went to Headquarters with S.S.O. In afternoon on to No. 3 Company, heard a case, and remanded same for F.G.C.M. Inspected No. 2 Company, and instructed O.C. what to do about a case he had. Q.M. Capt. John Hutchison called and aired his grievances, gave him a non-committal letter to Major Hutton who will know what to do with him. Webb had long talk about 4th Train and his appointment, it seems to me there must be a lot of wire and political pull going on, [Entry continues on next page.]

### Page 6 — 18–23 June 1916 (Sheet No. 52)

> **1916 June 18** — [Continued from previous page.] and yet to me it all seems so simple, Webb either is or is not the best qualified for the post. I am certain he is far and away above the other two, and Simson agreed. Fine all day.
>
> **19** — All reported back from leave, and tendered their congratulations on my mention. It is good of them for I have never spared them. No. 3 Coming back so went and inspected their camp, and warned the Artillery they have to get out, and leave the place absolutely clean. Webb dined Very cold, wet, but fine towards evening. Quiet all day.
>
> **20** — Started Inspection of the 9th Brigade 1st Line Transport. Machine Gun Section and the 43rd and 52nd. Battalions. The latter two improved, not the first. No. 2 Company returned to same old Billet found it clean and tidy. All the horses are looking well after their journey. Called at Divisional Headquarters with Capt. Webb. Saw A.A.Q.M.G. re his going to 4th Train and had long talk about it. Fine most of the day, warm in afternoon.
>
> **21** — Finished Inspection of 9th Brigade 1st Line Transport. Pioneers, Machine Gun Section and Brigade Headquarters have gone back, much to my disappointment. Heard case of absentee and remanded him for F.G.C.M. Captain Church came to tea, and after went with me to Brigade Headquarters Inspection. I have tried to impress on all Units the absolute necessity of all Transport being perfect in every detail at all times, but much seed has fallen on stoney ground. Things were quiet all day. Sunny and fine.
>
> **22** — Inspected with Adjutant and O.C. Headquarters Company, 8th, 9th and 10th Field Ambulance. I am not satisfied these Units are in as good a shape as they should be. Have clearly pointed out to them where they can improve. Had a chat with O.C. 1st Train. S.S.O. had a meeting of his S.O's and R.O's chiefly about purchase of clover before it is turned into hay and hay price. Aeroplanes very busy to day. S.S.O. and O.C. Headquarters Company visited Divisional Headquarters. Fine all day and warm, dull towards evening.
>
> **23** — Inspected Divisional Headquarters 1st Line Transport with Major Webb and Adjutant, also transport of Divisional Signallers. I find most of the horses in very good hard condition in spite of the short rations. Grooming, care of feet, sheaths and docks seems to be the hardest thing to inculcate. Cleaning of Wagons, and proper use of dubbing is hard to instill into almost all Units, they will not see its absolute necessity and will devote time which should be spent softening harness to burnishing lines. [Entry continues on next page.]

### Page 7 — 23–28 June 1916 (Sheet No. 53)

> **1916 June 23** — [Continued from previous page.] Generals Farmer & Lindsay with Colonel Sutherland Brown came and saw me re the moving of shelters for supplies at Railhead beyond dead end, and I instructed S.S.O. to see the Engineers and get it in hand at once. M.O. left for his leave. Capt McAskill is to act for him. Morning fine, then sultry and about five heavy rain and thunder storm.
>
> **24** — Inspected with Adjutant and O.C. No. 2 Company the 7th Brigade Headquarters, R.C.R., P.P.C.L.I., and 42nd. 1st Line Transport. Attended with Major Stirrett and Adjutant funeral of General Mercer. Everyone seems to rejoice that his body was recovered and had an opportunity of paying last respects to a good man, friend, and soldier. Rain morning fine later.
>
> **25** — Finished inspection of 7th Brigade. Headquarters Company turned out going to remove sheds at Railhead for Supplies, re erect, and make continuation of road. All worked hard and well. Visited Headquarters with S.S.O. Passes renewed. Quiet all day. Fine and warm.
>
> **26** — Took Staff Sgt. Major Bayliffe, Staff Sgt. Brand and C.Q.M.S. Hutchins to General for personal interview re their commissions, they were all recommended. At Railhead this morning 54 wagons of Headquarters Company filled and got away in 31 minutes. Inspected transport of the three Companies and they were good. Warm with some showers.
>
> **27** — Adjutant and Capt. Oliphant all day at a long Court Martial, Poperinghe. S.S.O. went to No. 3 Company with P.M. 10-30, away all day. Lieut. Richards back from leave, late through no fault of his own. Men from No. 2 Company up, drunk on Parade, got 28 days F.P. No. I. Wet day.
>
> **28** — Adjutant and I inspecting 1st Line Transport of 8th Brigade at Steenvoorde. Sent note to Headquarters Company to answer for me during my absence. Tried Case at No. 3 Company, neglect of duty, and gave interview to Wheeler Sergt. with regard to his discharge. Back late and heard case of Corporal and five men of No. 4 Company disobeying orders, and riding on loaded wagons, gave them 14 days C.C. each. Corps phoned to send car for Major Scott who is to be attached to 3rd Train for instruction. Wet all morning and dirty day for inspection, fine towards evening. Dispersed crowd at Estaminet opposite, they gave police some trouble.

### Page 8 — 29–30 June 1916 (Sheet No. 54)

> **1916 June 29** — Went with Webb and S.S.O. to Headquarters. D.A.A. & Q.M.G. on leave so could not speak to him about Webb. Just wrote fixing up programme to use every minute of my leave when I got notice it was cancelled and all leave stopped. Rev. Wood to tea, and arranged for Sunday Service. Estaminet closed. Fine all day.
>
> **30** — S.S.O. gone to D.D. of S. & T. I paid visit to the Dentist. News of Sir Roger Casement gives great satisfaction. Wet dull then fine.
>

## Open questions / uncertain items (June 1916)
- The cover reads "Volume 5" but the contents page reads "Volume 16 — 6." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number.
- 6 June, 18 June and 23 June each run across a page break (Sheets 49–50, 51–52 and 52–53); each entry is given whole under the page where it begins.
- 4 June: the battalion number of the private placed under arrest is a row of dots in the original and is not read; recorded as [uncertain].
- 7 June: "Capt. Graham" is typed with a raised "r" (G^r aham); read as Graham.
- 12 June: an unclear character or initial stands before "Webb" in "Major [uncertain] Webb leaves to take command of 4th Train"; recorded as [uncertain].
- 14 June: the name is written "Simpson" (sending on the letter from Webb and Stirrett); elsewhere the same officer is written "Simson" — both spellings as written.
- 23 June: the sentence about dubbing ends across the page break; the word "time" is typed twice at the foot of Sheet 52 and the second occurrence is struck through, so the sentence reads "will devote time which should be spent softening harness to burnishing lines."
- 26 June: "Staff Sgt. Brand" is typed with a raised "r" (B^r and); read as Brand, the same man later sent to the School of Instruction (20 July).
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the June sheets is not read with confidence; it is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name. The signature is identical in form to the one at the foot of the July sheets.
- The Place column is blank on every June sheet; no headquarters place is stated for the month.
- No casualties in the Train itself are recorded in this volume. "Casualties pretty heavy last two days" (15 June) refers to the Division generally, not to the Train. General Mercer, G.O.C. 3rd Canadian Division, was killed earlier in the month (the "scrap" of 3 June); his funeral was attended on 24 June.
- Names read as: Petrie; Charlie; Major (Webb); Capt. Graham; Capt. Oliphant; Lieut. Anthony; Major Lawson; Colonel Simson; Colonel Anderson; Colonel Shaw; Major Greer; Weir; Lt. Beaumont (Gas Officer, 1st Division); Major Stirrett; Generals Farmer & Lindsay; Colonel Sutherland Brown; Capt. McAskill; Major Hutton; Q.M. Capt. John Hutchison; Captain Church; Major Scott; Staff Sgt. Major Bayliffe; Staff Sgt. Brand; C.Q.M.S. Hutchins; Lieut. Richards; Rev. Wood; Sir Roger Casement.
