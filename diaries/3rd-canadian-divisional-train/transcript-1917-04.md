- **Unit:** Headquarters, 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps, 3rd Canadian Division
- **Month:** April 1917 (Volume 15) — this volume covers 1 to 30 April 1917
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** April 1917, p. 1 – p. 7 (image files page-0144 – page-0150 of the 370-page diary)
- **April 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–7): cover, contents, diary text 1–30 April, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The April sheets are on the lined form (footer "T.124. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."). A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "G.C.M." = General Court Martial; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "F.P.I" = Field Punishment No. 1; "M.T." = Mechanical Transport; "R.S.O." = Railhead Supply Officer (as used here); "D.H.Q." = Divisional Headquarters. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets is the unit name printed at the top left.

## Pages 3–7 — Diary text (1–30 April 1917)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY".

### Page 3 — 1–4 April 1917 (Sheet No. 114)

> **April 1.** — Bright morning, clouding up towards noon, with a heavy fall of snow, accompanied by thunder about 5 p.m. Nothing out of the ordinary happened during the day. Visited D.H.Q. and received copy of Establishment of Divisional Pack Coy. Asked us to supply Officer to command, but advised them we were already short three Officers and could not spare another. Applied for authority to use two trucks per brigade for unloading supplies at railhead to enable us to rest up some of our horses. Dull and stormy to-night.
>
> **April 2.** — Bright during early part of morning, though very chilly. Attended demonstration of pack saddlery at Barlin railhead at 11 a.m., but most of samples produced were anything but simple and easy of construction. The one shown by 5th Divi. Train was most in favour. Went up to forward area to look over some of the roads in neighbourhood of NEUVILLE ST. VAAST as to their suitability and submitted scheme for first two days move of Train in case of an advance, to D.H.Q. Started to snow heavily about 5.00 p.m., being one of the heaviest falls this winter. Still snowing at 10.00 p.m. tonight.
>
> **April 3.** — Snowing still this morning, though it cleared up towards noon and the sun came out. Went to R.S.O. Bruay to arrange about drawing the extra emergency ration for each man, authorised for coming operation, also to draw sufficient ordinary rations to enable Brigades to go into line with two days rations. Visited D.H.Q. but nothing very pressing there. Our work progressing satisfactorily and pack train in by 11.00 a.m. this morning. Dull night again with some slight rainfall.
>
> **April 4.** — Dull morning and some sleet fell about 11.00 a.m., but the weather cleared up considerably towards the afternoon. 49th. Battn moving from BRUAY and 116th Battn from BOUDAIN to VILLERS-AU-BOIS, necessitating slight changes in Supply arrangements. Drew 10,000 rations of Preserved Meat and Biscuit which will be issued in the morning at the rate of 800 to each Battn. and 100 to each M.G. Coy as an extra emergency ration for approaching operations. Court Martial assembled to H.Q. to try A/C. Q. M. S. Landsky and Staff Sergt Wheeler A. J. Smith this afternoon. Night dull and cloudy, though not so cold. O.C., Train arrived back from leave this evening.

### Page 4 — 5–14 April 1917 (Sheet No. 115)

> **April 5.** — Took over duties of O.C., Train this morning. Called in and reported back at D.H.Q. Nothing of importance there. Noticed on my return that horses are showing signs of the hard work and trying weather. As today has been very sunny and warm perhaps it means a change in the weather for the better, if so then horses should pick up. Inspected horses of Nos 1 and 4 Coys, got bad kick at battery my glass case saved me from worse. Sunny and fine.
>
> **April 6.** — Feeling sore and stiff, taking a quiet day. Train in early. Move postponed. Wet all afternoon.
>
> **April 7.** — Fine but cold. Inspected No 3 Coy.
>
> **April 8.** — During night artillery lively. Fine all day. A.D.V.S. had interview.
>
> **April 9.** — Train horses being dipped this morning. Fine early, cold and snow later.
>
> **April 10.** — During advance on Vimy Ridge supplies went forward without a hitch. S.S.O. doing splendid work. Snow and sun alternating all day.
>
> **April 11.** — Major H. O. Lawson came to lunch and had long talk about refilling points as he expects his Supply Column back again in a day or two, with the 3rd Divn. where he should be. Snow and sleet all day.
>
> **April 12.** — Tried case from No 1 Coy attempting breach of censor regulations, Man is a fool but an honest one and willing to make all amends he can. Fined 28 days pay and same to be published in Orders as a warning. Case from No 3 Coy, careless breaking of tail board. He would take an F.G.C.M. and got it.
>
> **April 13.** — Having a little topographical exercise with the Adjt. for practice. Fine all day. Had one of my Hell heads.
>
> **April 14.** — Went with S.S.O. and Adjt. to Hdgrs and Advance Hdgrs. Arranged about Train moving up and advancing Refilling Points. Meeting of Officers to explain matters. Capt. Oliphant

### Page 5 — 14–20 April 1917 (Sheet No. 116)

> **April 14 (continued).** — indisposed. Fine.
>
> **April 15.** — Capt. Oliphant much better and it will not be necessary I think, for him to go to Hospital. Train Hdgrs. Nos 1 and 4 Coys moved from BARLIN this morning, latter to GOUY SERVINS. Hdgrs to VILLERS AU BOIS. On arrival there found there was a doubt as to the certainty of getting Supply Column and ordered, by priority wire, Nos 2 and 3 Coys to follow Nos 1 and 4 Coys at once. If M.T. is not put on then there is a better hope of the Train Horses standing up. They are all showing, in spite of all possible care and nursing, signs of overwork. Railhead changes tomorrow to PREVIN CAPELLE. Only lack of water compels me keeping all the Train out at Gouy Servins, instead of at Villers au Bois. Could hardly have had a worse day for a move, raining hard all day, but all are in grand spirits owing to recent successes and the part Canadians played so successfully. Rained hard all day and plenty of mud.
>
> **April 16.** — All Companies moved up. Supply Details of No 4 moves a little further forward today to LA TARGETTE. Showers, mainly fine.
>
> **April 17.** — Train driver being tried this morning for smashing tail board of wagon in front through neglect, would not take my punishment. Rained all night and wind blowing a hurricane. Capt. Oliphant better and member of Court Martial. Very rough and cold, snow, rain and high wind.
>
> **April 18.** — Supply Sections of 3 and 4 Coys changing over. Wet and windy.
>
> **April 19.** — A.D.V.S. called. Inspected remounts. Lieut. Bell of Reserve Park called re material for repairs of 30 wagons lent to Engineers and used by the Artillery. After repairs only 5 can go on road. Sent list of material required to A. A. & Q.M.G. Trying to arrange wagons for Y.M.C.A. The horses inspected today were on the whole a very good lot. Roads and mud terrible Rain as usual.
>
> **April 20.** — "X" Ration reported off, but can get no confirmation. Morning broke fine, can it keep up?

### Page 6 — 21–28 April 1917 (Sheet No. 117)

> **April 21.** — Inspected Refilling Points and road approach to same. Fine, sun and wind.
>
> **April 22.** — Am not forgetting this day two years ago. Went down to Gouy to No 2 Coy and tried acting Corpl. Countryman for being 35 minutes late on parade, he took my punishment. This being late is growing. Hennessy in bed. Fine all day.
>
> **April 23.** — Hennessy better. Court Martial on man late for parade in No 2 Coy. Would not take my punishment. Had all Company Commanders up re amount of salvaged material and articles reported by Salvage Officer, and it is as I suspected all of it was left behind by previous Units or picked up outside our own lines. All last night a most terrific bombardment on. Great noise and grand sight. Beautifully fine all day and plenty of sun.
>
> **April 24.** — On as member of G.C.M. Hearing case of false swearing under oath. Remanded same for F.G.C.M. Fine all day.
>
> **April 25.** — Remanded man attached to No 1 Coy for F.G.C.M. Charge Sleeping on Post, i.e. Sentry over wagons. Lieut Fisher back from forward refilling point relieved by Capt. Phillips. Inspected method of fixing petrol tins on wagons for carrying water. The best method is that suggested by No 3 Coy, four tins under wagon, 2 on either side. Very little sun but fine.
>
> **April 26.** — Adjt. taking evidence in case of man sleeping on post. Major Freeman and Capt. de Graves called. Capt Oliphant came in and reported that Lt. Richards was not making good as a Transport Officer and requested a change for the benefit of the Company.
>
> **April 27.** — Had Lt Richards paraded and warned him that if he did not make more progress in his transport work, I would not recommend him for promotion, as reports of his work had not been good. Fine.
>
> **April 28.** — F.G.C.M. on attached man for perjury. G.C.M. of which I was a member postponed.

### Page 7 — 28–30 April 1917 (Sheet No. 118)

> **April 28 (continued).** — Y.M.C.A. gave an entertainment to the Train, and a very good one too. Fine.
>
> **April 29.** — Arranging for another move. Os. C. No 1, 2 and 4 called. Tried case of neglect of duty while on Sentry duty. 28 days F.P. No 1. Fine.
>
> **April 30.** — All Companies moved to LA TARGETTE, inspected all companies in new camp. Packing up.
>
> [Signed:] *[signature — appears to read "Findlay" [uncertain] as to first name] Lieut-Colonel, / O.C. 3rd Canadian Divl. Train*

## Open questions / uncertain items (April 1917)
- The cover reads "Volume 15" and the contents page reads "Volume 17 — 4." — both as written.
- The contents page says "Diary Text 5 Pages" but the diary text occupies pages 3–7 (5 sheets, Sheets 114–118); the count is consistent this month.
- 4 April: the O.C. (Lt. Col. Findlay) arrives back from leave; 5 April he takes over duties of O.C. Train from Major Beasley.
- 9–10 April: the Battle of Vimy Ridge. 10 April: "During advance on Vimy Ridge supplies went forward without a hitch."
- 15 April: Train Headquarters and Nos. 1 and 4 Companies move from Barlin — Headquarters to Villers-au-Bois, No. 4 Company to Gouy-Servins; Nos. 2 and 3 Companies follow; railhead changes to Previn Capelle [as written]. 16 April: all companies move up; No. 4 Company's supply details move further forward to La Targette. 30 April: all companies move to La Targette.
- 22 April: "Am not forgetting this day two years ago" — the Second Battle of Ypres, 22 April 1915.
- 14 April and 28 April each appear twice, once at the foot of one sheet and once at the head of the next, with the entry continuing across the break; both are transcribed as written.
- The signature at the foot of the April sheets appears to read "Findlay" [uncertain] as to the first name; the O.C. is identified in the January volume as Lieut. Colonel Findlay (D.S.O.).
- Names read as: Major H. O. Lawson; Capt. Oliphant; Lieut. Bell (Reserve Park); Corpl. Countryman (acting, No. 2 Coy); Hennessy; Major Freeman; Capt. de Graves; Lt. Richards; Lieut. Fisher; Capt. Phillips; A/C. Q. M. S. Landsky; Staff Sergt. Wheeler A. J. Smith.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only. No Train casualties are recorded in this volume.
