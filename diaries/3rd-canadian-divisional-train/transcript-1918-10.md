# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — October 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps (C.A.S.C.), 3rd Canadian Division (styled "Headquarters 3rd Canadian Divl Train" on the cover; "3rd Canadian Divisional Train" on the title page)
- **Month:** October 1918 — this volume covers 1 to 31 October 1918
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** October 1918, p. 1 – p. 8 (image files page-0326 – page-0333 of the diary)
- **October 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–8): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–31 October, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" ("INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" not struck through on these sheets). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. Place names are given as written, even where the spelling differs from the standard form (e.g. "Croisselles", "Moreuil", "Wadignies—Hamaye", "Nelesmes", "Luidgnies", "Horsnain", "Suce", "Hormain", "La.Goul ee"). Map references are given as written (e.g. "C.12.C.2.8.57.C.", "P.21.C.O.5 sheet 44"); no latitude/longitude coordinates are given. "H.T." = horse transport; "M.T." = mechanical transport; "D.H.Q." = Divisional Headquarters; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "D.D. of S.& T" = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "D.A.D.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Supplies; "A.D.O.S." = Assistant Director of Ordnance Services; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "O.R." = other ranks; "S.S.O." = Staff Supply Officer (as written); "C.S.M." = Company Sergeant-Major; "R.A.F" = Royal Air Force; "Bde" = Brigade; "Divn." = Division; "C.I.Bn." = Canadian Infantry Battalion; "C.M.G.C." = Canadian Machine Gun Corps. The signature at the foot of the October sheets is read as [uncertain] (see open questions).

## Pages 4–8 — Diary text (1–31 October 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "(A3945. Wt. W3129/M1559. 750,000. 1/17. D.D. & L., Ltd. Forms/C.2118/14."

### Page 4 — 1–10 October 1918 (Sheet No. 1)

> **QUEANT, 1-10-18.** — Heard today that a move is in the air but no details were available at D.H.Q. today. Fair.
>
> **do., 2nd** — Sent 20 men for remounts as Division were very short of men. Assisted Division to distribute Div Canteen stocks today.
>
> **do., 3rd** — Moved 7th Bde Dump as the R.A.F was using the Area near his former location for stores, etc.
>
> **do., 4th** — Queant is stilled shelled. All Coys were bombed last night. No casualties.
>
> **do., 5th** — Fair, colder towards evening.
>
> **do., 6th** — Division moved to new Area on 9th. Trains Coys will clear Croisselles on 11th and onwards by H.T. 51st (Scottish) Division relieve us in this sector. Cold.
>
> **do., 7th** — All Train Coys are dug in and all horses are partially protected from bombs. Fair. Transport for week ending 6-10-18. Coy. Average miles per week. Total mileages per Coy. Longest distance by any waggon.

> **do., 8th** — Visited O i/c C.A.S.C. Personnel at Corps and arranged to send 20 drivers who are thoroughly incompetent to the Base. The D.D. of S.& T and the D.A.D.S. 1st Army, called today. The A.D.O.S. Canadian Corps called with reference to Ordnance Dumps.

### Page 5 — 11–14 October 1918 (Sheet No. 2)

> **Queant., 11th** — Railhead Queant. Moved No,1.Coy to Bourlon On Bourlon–Anneau Road. D.A.Q.M.G. was asked to clear Railhead by H.T. for the whole Division. Impossible for us to clear for D.Troops and deliver as this would have been 35 miles per day.
>
> **do., 12th** — Moved No,3 & 4 Coy to Queant – Moreuil [uncertain] Area. Railhead now at Croiselles from 13th inclusive. Clearing railhead by H.T. for 2.3 & 4 Coys and D.T's by lorry.

> **Croiselles., 13th** — O.C. returned yesterday afternoon and resumed Command today. During my absence on leave Maj. McLeod carried out the duties of O.C. Train with great credit. Reported back to Divn. Fine.

Taylor of 2nd Div (Arty) came in wounded from dressing station, we took care of him and sent him back happy this morning. Transport for week ending Oct. 13th as under. Coy. Average mileage per wagon. > **do., 14th (continued, no date in date column)** — Capt. Grant left this morning to take up duty in England. I have lost in him a most valuable, loyal and faithful Officer. Following letter received from Acting Chief Engineer Canadian Corps:— "Canadian Corps. C.E. 977/18— Officer Commanding. 3rd Canadian Divisional Train. O i/c CASC. I wish to express my appreciation of the excellent work of these men, whilst employed under me in assisting in the maintenance and repair of roads in the Canadian Corps and during the recent advance. Signed. H.F. Hughes. Colonel. Chief Engineer, Canadian Corps. 13-10-18. Chief Salvage for last week 100 sacks of salt. The sad news has just come in that Gen, Lipset, has been killed. Weather fine.

### Page 6 — 15–23 October 1918 (Sheet No. 3)

> **Croiselles. 15th.** — Gen. Lipset buried this afternoon. 3 Officers and 50 O.R. attending from the Train. All going smooth and well. Wet and raw.
>
> **do 16th** — Wet again. Nothing to relate all going well.
>
> **do. 17th** — Wet. All going well.
>
> **do. 18th** — Visited Railhead and made thorough inspection of all Coys Convoys. With the exception of one team of old horses, all in excellent condition. Wagons clean and well repaired, harness well cared for. As soon as horse rugs are available, trace high clipping will begin. This should start any day now. My groom, Dvr. Labourne [uncertain] got mentioned in Orders for gallant conduct. i.e. the stopping at great personal risk of a runaway team frightened in a bombing raid.
>
> **do. 19th** — Stand to orders come in last night to be prepared for a move forward at short notice. All arrangements made and we are ready. Movement orders in.
>
> **Douai. 20th** — Capts Graham and Barlf gone to new area to locate dumps and billets. Train will move tomorrow. Capt. Gobey of 1st Train on return from leave, slept here last night and went off with Capts Graham and Barlf to join his unit this morning. Capt Phillips gone on months leave, today.
>
> **do. 21st.** — Coys all move today. My recommendation for Capt Graves, much to my regret has been turned down. Ran up to Somain and saw D.A.Q.M.G with S.S.O and arranged to move Train Hd Qtrs up beside them, telephonic communication being impossible to arrange further back. No,1.Coy here also. No,2. Wadignies—Hamaye [uncertain]. No,3 and 4 Nelesmes [uncertain]. No,1.may move to Wallers today or tomorrow.
>
> **Somain. 22nd.** — Rested at Somain all day. Showery.
>
> **Wallers. 23rd** — Moved to Wallers in afternoon No,2.Coy moved to Hasnon 15.180. Civilian Rations are being distributed as under, care of the Mairie, No,1.Coy 1190 to Raisme, No,2,Coy 1000 to Luidgnies [uncertain]. 3500 to Horsnain. No,3.Coy 2000 to Suce [uncertain]. 1150 to Hormain 1280 to Helesmes No,4.Coy 4500 to Wallers. Fine.

### Page 7 — 24–27 October 1918 (Sheet No. 4)

> **Wallers. 24th** — No, 2. Coy from Hasnon to P.21.C.O.5 sheet 44 Col MacAlpine wants to see Capt Graham but this but this cannot be done at present, what with the advance and Officers on leave, there is as much work to do as can be accomplished. Showery.
>
> **do. 25th** — Called with Capt Graham at Corps H.Qtrs to see Col. MacAlpine re Staff appointment for Graham. Met there and took back with me Lt. Moore attached from Pool to Train. Posted him to No,2.Coy letting Lt. Gwynne return to No,1. Called in on M.T.Coy, tried to see O.C. or Adjt to lodge complaint about their very unsatisfactory work. Could see neither, so on my return, put complaint in writing and sent through D.A.Q.M.G. Called at Div Hqtrs, saw Col. Browne and Maj. Hennessey and they also had something to say about the fall down of the M.T. and it was certainly not complimentary. Wet.
>
> **do. 26th** — S.S.O. out all night owing to late information that a Pontoon Section from 2nd Divn would join up in morning and would have to be rationed, and also an alteration in the rationing of civilians. Transport for week ending 19-10-18. Most of hauling done in wet weather, over bad roads and a good deal of it in the dark owing to distance of Railhead and shortage of lorries etc. Coy. Average haul. Total haul. Longest haul.

> Inspected dumps and lorries of 1.7.5 & 4 Coys [uncertain] Train, wrote a note to salvage Officer complaining of the filthy condition of his convoy coming into La.Goul ee [uncertain] today. All have had to remove an immense amount of manure from lines left by the Germans, the swine seem to roll in filth of all kinds. S.S.O. gone to Railhead. Interpreter and Capt Barlf out locating Supplies in the area.

> **do. 27th.** — Called on A.A.Q.M.G. re complaint that S. . 9th Bde [uncertain] gave orders ro S.Column to remove civilians. Absolutely no foundation for such complaint. Civilian ration wagon delivering rations was blown up by shell, with the Trains usual luck, one man only slightly scratched and one horse wounded. Fine and sunny.

## Open questions / uncertain items (October 1918)
- The cover reads "Volume 33" and the title page is hand-numbered "33", but the contents page reads "Volume 18 — 10." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number.
- 14 October has two entries: the dated entry and a second long entry in a row with place "do." and no date in the date column; both are transcribed as written, the second as a continuation of 14 October.
- 6 October: "Division moved to new Area on 9th" — tense as written (the move is described on the 6th as happening on the 9th).
- 12 October: "Queant – Moreuil [uncertain] Area" — the place name is not read with confidence; it may be Moeuvres. Recorded as written.
- 18 October: the groom's name reads "Dvr. Labourne [uncertain]"; the surname is not read with confidence.
- 21 October: the company locations read "Wadignies—Hamaye [uncertain]" and "Nelesmes [uncertain]" — both spellings as written; they may be Wandignies-Hamage and Helesmes.
- 23 October: civilian ration distribution places are spelled as written — "Raisme", "Luidgnies [uncertain]", "Horsnain", "Suce [uncertain]", "Hormain", "Helesmes". "No,2.Coy moved to Hasnon 15.180" — the "15.180" is a map reference as written.
- 24 October: "but this but this" is repeated in the original and kept as written.
- 26 October: "Inspected dumps and lorries of 1.7.5 & 4 Coys [uncertain] Train" — the company numbers are not read with confidence (likely "1, 2, 3 & 4 Coys"). "La.Goul ee [uncertain]" — the place name is not read with confidence. The transport table for the week ending 19-10-18 gives a Train total of 6958, but the four company totals (2220 + 1027 + 2858 + 856) add to 6961 — a discrepancy of 3, as written.
- 27 October: "complaint that S. . 9th Bde [uncertain] gave orders ro S.Column to remove civilians" — the complainant's designation is not read with confidence, and "ro" is as written (possibly "to").
- 31 October: "Lt. Eason" returned from leave; the November diary spells a similar name "Rason" (see November open questions) — the two spellings are recorded as written in each month.
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the October sheets appears to read "J. A. Lindsay" [uncertain] and is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name.
- Names read as: Maj. McLeod; Major de Graves; Corpl Brown; Capt. Taylor (2nd Div Arty); Capt. Grant; H.F. Hughes, Colonel, Chief Engineer, Canadian Corps; Gen. Lipset (spelled "Lipset" throughout; buried 15 October); Capts Graham and Barlf; Capt. Gobey (1st Train); Capt Phillips; Lt. Moore; Lt. Gwynne; Col. MacAlpine; Col. Browne; Maj. Hennessey; Gen Loomis; C.S.M. Niven; Col Scott; Col. Webb; Gen Currie; Lt. Eason.
- Casualties: 4 October — all companies bombed, no casualties. 27 October — one man slightly scratched and one horse wounded when a civilian ration wagon was blown up by shell. No other casualties are recorded in this volume.
- No appendices are listed in the contents and none are present among the October pages. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; locations are given as place names and sheet/map references only.
