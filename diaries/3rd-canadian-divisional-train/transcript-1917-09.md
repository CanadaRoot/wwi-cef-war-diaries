- **September 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 197–206 of 370): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–30 September, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout on Army Form C.2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Handwritten insertions and corrections in the original are noted in square brackets at the point they occur. The Place column is blank throughout; the Date column gives "Sept. 1917" with the day number. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not transcribed against every entry here. The printed footer on each diary sheet reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S." Map references are given as written (F.11, A.9.d. sheet 51b); no latitude/longitude coordinates are given. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer (as written); "T.O." = Transport Officer; "P.P.C.L.I." = Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "C.M." = Court Martial; "D.D.V.S." = Deputy Director of Veterinary Services; "D.A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "D.R.O." = Deputy Returning Officer (as written); "D.S.O." = Distinguished Service Order; "S.S.M." = Squadron Sergeant Major; "C.Q.M.S." = Company Quartermaster Sergeant; "S.M." = Sergeant Major; "M.T." = Mechanical Transport; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "L.D." = as written; "P.P." = as written.

## Pages 4–10 — Diary text (1–30 September 1917)

Army Form C.2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed instruction at the top left reads "Instructions regarding War Diaries and Intelligence Summaries are contained in F. S. Regs., Part II. and the Staff Manual respectively. Title pages will be prepared in manuscript."

### Page 4 — 1–5 September 1917 (Sheet No. 162)

> **1 September 1917.** — Adjutant and S.S.O. [gone — handwritten insertion] to survey new location we move to on 4th. Inspected No.3 Co. & Signallers' horses, fair. Harness should be ever so much better, wagons good running order Lines clean. Got reply from T.O. of P.P.C.L.I. will see Colonel as he requests, reply not satisfactory, have interviewed Capt. Church before coming to this conclusion. Generally fine, some showers. Remanded two cases No.4 Co. for F.G.C.M., refused to take my punishment.
>
> **2 September 1917.** — No.3 Co. moved out to new position at F.11 East of MONT ST. ELOY. Adjt. of 1st Train called. Some rain but mostly fine.
>
> **3 September 1917.** — Paraded Lt. Keyes at Corps H.Q. for interview re desirability of making better use of his abilities as an engineer. After reading evidence of the two men of No.4 Coy. dismissed case as no court could convict on the evidence. I have no doubt in my own mind that they were guilty, but that is not good enough. Just heard we are to have our Artillery back on 5th and S.S.O. is out about their feeding. Fine.
>
> **4 September 1917.** — No.2 & 4 Co's moved out near No.3 Co. vicinity of MONT ST. ELOY. Inspected lines and all left very clean and tidy. No.1 Co. moves out tonight. Warm and fine.
>
> **5 September 1917.** — No.1 Coy. moved last night, were late getting off because O.C. did not give definite instructions [were struck out; "MESS." inserted in handwriting] wagon had to report after being loaded up and it had to be hunted for. This is where Major Freeman falls down. Just heard Artillery could not get into their notified location and as supplies had [gone — handwritten insertion] Adjt. had to chase after them [continued on page 5]

### Page 5 — 5–10 September 1917 (Sheet No. 163)

> **5 September 1917 (continued).** — in car and notify where supplies were to be delivered. Poor staffwork. Drawing from new Railhead at ECURIE first time this morning. Fine.
>
> **6 September 1917.** — Moved Train H.Q. from BARLIN to LA TARGETTE. Inspected all the Coys there, they are making the most of accommodation, mostly in the open. Fine all day, late afternoon thunder and lightning, and torrential rain.
>
> **7 September 1917.** — Called to see Capt. de Graves who has been down with fever for a couple of days, he is coming on all right. Co's lines are bad, very muddy but all that can be done is being done, horses all round in very good shape. Dull wet morning clearing towards mid-day, becoming foggy and close.
>
> **8 September 1917.** — Working on plans for standings. Capt. de Graves came to see me about leave, good reasons and I have forwarded recommendation for same. Fog early, clearing to bright in afternoon.
>
> **9 September 1917.** — Inspected and measured work done on standings etc., of all four Co's. One of the horses used in mower to cut green clover slightly injured. At 4 p.m. heavy shelling on balloon just above us, pieces from 30 to 40 lbs. dropping far too close to be agreeable. Beasley and I dined at 5th Bde. with General Ross. Had long talk with Capt. Hennessy at dinner. Fog early, later warm and fine.
>
> **10 September 1917.** — Inspected 18 remounts. V.O. went with party to get them. One LD. was only a rider and a poor one at that, none were first class but an improvement on the last lot. Col. Massie 2nd Train called, he will take over from us shortly. Inspected progress made with horse standings. Misty, clearing to sunny and fine.

### Page 6 — 11–13 September 1917 (Sheet No. 164)

> **11 September 1917.** — O.C. 2nd Div. Train and his Adjt., lunched here today also Camp Commandant, and we talked over the prospect of our moving out and 2nd taking over. Adjt. and Camp Commandant went to try and locate new positions for us. D.A.D.V.S. called and asked me to parade recent remounts for inspection by authorities on Thursday at 3 o'clock. All are making progress on standings. Arranged with Capt. Church for inspection of 1st Line Transport of 7th Bde. tomorrow 1.30 Dull early, very fine later.
>
> **12 September 1917.** — Morning broke dull. At daylight artillery opened up pretty lively. S.S.O. on Court Martial. Could not inspect 1st Line as arranged. We have to move out and let 2nd Train come in their own area. We have to move nearer MONT ST. ELOY, absolutely no shelters of any kind for man or beast, only bare Mother Earth. Co's moving from here today to A.9.d. sheet 51b. Inspected site for Hd. Qrs. to move to near refilling points and informed Division who gave us two Nissen Huts. Adjt. & S.S.O. gone to see if they can get M.T. to transport them otherwise it means 14 wagons. Mostly fine.
>
> **13 September 1917.** — Inspected the 4 Co's in their new lines. Interviewed No.482080 Dvr. Arsenault A., 8th Field Ambulance re his application for a commission. Do not consider him a suitable candidate and will not recommend him. Captains Hennessey and Chivers lunched here. A.D.V.S., D.A.D.V.S., and Remount Officer [uncertain — word struck out] inspected parade of unsuitable horses for work in Train and ambulances. I put my points before them summarised thus: horses sent as remounts should be such as can carry out the work they are called upon to perform. Personally I don't care one iota whether they are young or old, good looking or ugly so long as they can do their work properly, certainly a good many remounts should have been sold or shot at Base and not sent up to the front. A little sun, poor visibility chilly towards evening. Turned down Lt. Fisher's leave to PARIS. I [have — handwritten insertion] stopped all leave for men why not officers also. Just heard our new refilling points nearing completion after the greatest difficulty have to be pulled down, and put in another place so that light railway can be used. Why this could not have been pointed out when we fixed on these points licks me as the Division knew the site chosen. Dull little sun & some rain.

### Page 7 — 14–19 September 1917 (Sheet No. 165)

> **14 September 1917.** — Got letter from Major Freeman saying he would be willing to exchange with Major Scott Interviewed Lt's Fisher and George re taking positions of instructors. Inspected 9th Field Amb. marked improvement all round. Dull and some heavy rain.
>
> **15 September 1917.** — Inspected existing refilling points just completed and new ones just begun, to be supplied by light railway. Recommended Lt. George and S.S.M. Bayliffe to go to Canada as instructors. Sent [uncertain] to get my new charger, hope to goodness it is not like former ones supplied which I would not ride. Fine.
>
> **16 September 1917.** — Inspected new charger, the only one I have received yet of any use. D.D.V.S. and D.A.D.V.S. to lunch and afterwards inspecting Train horses in Co's lines. D.D.V.S. expressed himself as well pleased. Censured O.C. No.4 Co. as directed by Army Commander Interviewed Junior Officers that came from England, ascertained value of home training Adjt. of 2nd Train came in to tea and arranged taking over from us tomorrow when we move out.
>
> **17 September 1917.** — Moved into new Hd. Qrs. at refilling points. Busy getting quarters and offices ready. Inspected all transport coming to refilling points. Fine.
>
> **18 September 1917.** — Got pretty well settled in. Visited Co's. Have attack of lumbago. S.M. Hartley and C.Q.M.S. Poole dangerously ill in hospital with double pneumonia. A little rain towards evening.
>
> **19 September 1917.** — Lumbago very troublesome all night, feeling very crippled this morning. About 4 o'clock a Hun plane chased one of ours right over our huts not three lengths behind our man, making him land before reaching his aerodrome. He showed some nerve, at one time we could not have been more than two hundred feet from the ground and treated our rifle fire with contempt, only firing about half a dozen rounds to make the rifles keep cover, three of our planes were after him very quickly but he was out of view in the distance and we could not see the end. Dvr. McDonald of No.4 Co. before me charged with disobedience of orders, elected to be tried [by — handwritten insertion] FG.C.M. reason he says he got off once before. I hope they will [continued on page 8]

### Page 8 — 19–23 September 1917 (Sheet No. 166)

> **19 September 1917 (continued).** — soak it to him this time as an example to others. Fine during day, rain during night.
>
> **20 September 1917.** — It is 24 hours since Pte. McDonald was sent up for F.G.C.M. and I have again had him before me and asked him if he still wants to be tried by C.M. and he still adheres to it. I am confident if Courts would be much more careful in their considerations and remember that a C.O. can give up to 28 days and they gave a little more C.M's would go down 50%. The moral effect would be great besides an enormous saving of time and money. It rained hard last night and I don't admire the man who designed the Nissen Hut or the Government for adopting it, for a standing camp it may be all right, but to take up and down a more useless thing I cannot imagine, and it cannot be cheap, to say nothing of it's requiring skilled men for it's erection. Capt. de Graves saw the General and re his leave to Canada and as usual the General was more than nice. Visited dumps and Co's. Fine all day.
>
> **21 September 1917.** — M.O. called in and gave me another fierce rubbing, it certainly eases the pain and has done a lot of good and hope to get about a bit tomorrow. Fine.
>
> **22 September 1917.** — Very heavy fog early morning, a few bombs dropped but no damage to any one near us. Inspected work done at new positions for refilling points so that light railway can come into operation. They should have informed me of their intention to use light railway before sanctioning the present site, it would have saved no end of work which now has gone for nothing. Near new dump I saw a number of heavy steel rails being used for cribbing for horse standings. Perhaps rails torn up in England from permanent ways were intended to be thus used; to me it seems utter useless and cruel waste. Adjt. in bed half yesterday and three quarters of today. Fine.
>
> **23 September 1917.** — Very heavy fog before daylight at which time a Hun plane played us a short visit but no one was disturbed by it. Roads to new refilling points being built slowly. Very few P.P. men at work. Warm and fine during day. DA.D.V.S. called before going round four or five twin and doubtful horses in Co's. Vague rumours floating about that we are likely to lose our General very much to the regret of every one. There is a great desire to see Gen. McP[uncertain]rien [continued on page 9]

### Page 9 — 23–27 September 1917 (Sheet No. 167)

> **23 September 1917 (continued).** — fill the vacancy over every one else. From my knowledge of him he would be an ideal man for the post, every one would work for him with a will. Fine.
>
> **24 September 1917.** — Lt. Grant reported back from leave; he was medically examined and there is practically nothing wrong with him much to my relief. I feared his lung was touched. Capt. Church came to lunch and afterwards we went with me to inspect the 1st Line Transport at 7th Bde. All are in very fair shape except the 49th and their limbers are broken and dirty, after my visit they are sure to improve or there will be wigs on the green. Fine, sunny & hot.
>
> **25 September 1917.** — Train horses are sweating a little after haul from railhead, but they are looking very fit and in good condition, wagons and [uncertain — word struck out] carefully kept and well repaired, breechings not as well fitted as I should like, the tendency is to keep them too high and hip strap too far forward. New refilling dumps getting on very slowly. Fine.
>
> **26 September 1917.** — Capt. de Graves' leave for a month came through, and I inspected his Co., horses, wagons and lines before he turned over to Lt. Hessey and all in splendid shape. For New Years' Honours List have got the huge allotment of one honour and two mentions, particularly mean considering we have to feed the whole Divn. and not once has any single unit had a legitimate complaint. We get no rest like Battns., they feed without atrest just the same. We make roads for ourselves, erect refilling points and are a sort of general delivery agents for the Division, we do the drudgery and get no chance of winning honours, there is only one thing to do — that is grin and bear it. Fine.
>
> **27 September 1917.** — Submitted Major Heasley's name and recommended him for D.S.O. and consider he has well earned it. Capt. Church and S.Sgt. Crabtree recommended for mentions and it is little enough for all their hard work. Supply men busy making roads and erecting shelter sheds at new dumps. While there watching about 4 p.m. the Hun opened with a long range shrapnel gun at a balloon over our heads. After the 3rd shot observer jumped and descended by parachute safely, the balloon then travelled away from the enemy perhaps 200 yards and about 5 o'clock an enemy plane in a most plucky manner came over low down and fired the [continued on page 10]

### Page 10 — 27–30 September 1917 (Sheet No. 168)

> **27 September 1917 (continued).** — balloon, our machines were after him but it seemed as if he got away. About 7 p.m. another Hun plane came over and went for a second balloon, his first rocket missed but turning he got in a second and up went the balloon in flames, luckily it was empty This time he did not get away our planes at once engaged him and a young airman shot him in the head and brought him down at SOUCHEZ corner. This was the first plane to his credit. Our planes were busy early part of the night. de Graves gone on leave. Fine.
>
> **28 September 1917.** — Delivered corrected Honours forms personally to A.A. & Q.M.G. Saskatchewan Election papers came by today's mail and I am to be D.R.O. for the Train. I immediately sent out a letter re same. Lt. Graham called in evening, he seems to thrive in the Balloon Section.
>
> **29 September 1917.** — Refused Capt. Bishop's application to go to the Base and see about a coat he was measured for, have his tooth fixed and do some business, the nature of which was not stated. Refilling Points. Work were progressing slowly, great difficulty in getting slag for roadway. Poor visibility but fine all day. Capt. Phillips reported back from leave.
>
> **30 September 1917.** — Inspected convoys at refilling points and found horses in very fair shape. Clipping has begun in Co's. and if this good weather continues there should be no evil after effects. Wagons & harness in good condition. Owing to recent wet weather some spokes show signs of loosening and must have very careful attention. Lt. Fisher heard his brother in R.H.A.S. badly wounded, am helping him to get leave expedited and arrangements made so that he can go and see him. Capt. Bishop applied for transfer to Balloon Section. I will do nothing to stop him going. Fine.
>

## Open questions / uncertain items (September 1917)
- The cover reads "Volume 20" but the contents page reads "Volume 17 – 9" — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number. The contents page also says "Diary Text 6 Pages" but there are 7 diary sheets (Sheets 162–168).
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of Sheet 168 is not read with confidence; it is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name. The faint print beneath it reads "Lieut.-Colonel / O.C. 3rd Canadian Divl. Train" and appears to be the form's signature block.
- 5 September, 19 September, 23 September and 27 September entries each run across two sheets; each is transcribed in full under the page where it begins, with the continuation noted.
- 13 September: a word before "inspected parade of unsuitable horses" is struck out and illegible; recorded as [uncertain].
- 15 September: the word after "Sent" ("Sent [uncertain] to get my new charger") could not be read with confidence; it is not guessed.
- 23 September: the General's name is split across the foot of Sheet 166 ("Gen. McP") and the continuation ("rien"); it is recorded as "McP[uncertain]rien" and is not read as a name. The sentence continues onto page 9 ("fill the vacancy over every one else").
- 23 September: "four or five twin and doubtful horses" is as written; "twin" may be a typist's error for "train" but is transcribed as written.
- 25 September: a word before "carefully kept" is struck out and illegible; recorded as [uncertain].
- 26 September: "Lt. Hessey" is read as written; "they feed without atrest just the same" is as written (apparently "without a rest" run together).
- 30 September: "R.H.A.S." is as written; it may be a mistyping of "R.H.A." but is transcribed as written.
- McDonald of No. 4 Co. is called "Dvr." on 19 September and "Pte." on 20 September — both as written; the rank discrepancy is in the original.
- No. 482080 Dvr. Arsenault A., 8th Field Ambulance, is named on 13 September in connection with an application for a commission that the O.C. would not recommend.
- Names read as: Capt. Church; Capt. de Graves; Capt. Hennessy; Capt. Chivers; Capt. Bishop; Capt. Phillips; Lt. Keyes; Lt. Fisher; Lt. George; Lt. Grant; Lt. Graham; Lt. Hessey; Major Freeman; Major Scott; Major Heasley; Col. Massie (2nd Divisional Train); General Ross; S.S.M. Bayliffe; S.Sgt. Crabtree; S.M. Hartley; C.Q.M.S. Poole; Dvr. McDonald (No. 4 Co.); Dvr.
