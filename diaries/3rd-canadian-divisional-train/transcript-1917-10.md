- **Month:** October 1917 (Volume 21) — this volume covers 1 to 31 October 1917

- **October 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 207–217 of 370): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–31 October, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). The printed footer on each diary sheet reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S." `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Overtyped or struck-through words are noted as [uncertain] or [struck through] as they appear. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it reads as "J.A.S." [uncertain] and is not repeated against every entry here. The Place and Hour columns are blank throughout; places are given in the diary text itself. Map references are given as written (Sheet 28, Sheet 36b); no latitude/longitude coordinates are given. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "S.S.M." = Squadron Sergeant Major; "S.Q.M.S." = Squadron Quartermaster Sergeant; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "F.P." = Field Punishment; "M.T." = Mechanical Transport; "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer; "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "C.C.S." = Casualty Clearing Station; "A.A.&Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "D.D.D.&S." is written as read [uncertain] (see open questions).

## Pages 4–11 — Diary text (1–31 October 1917)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed footer reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."

### Page 4 — 1–3 October 1917 (Sheet No. 168)

> **1 October 1917.** — Going over months' diary and preparing voting papers for this afternoon. Took most of the votes before night. Fine.
>
> **2 October 1917.** — Revising diary before sending it off. Report of DDD&S. [uncertain] on Train and 1st Line Transport horses, circulated by A.A.&Q.M.G. Train very good. 1st Line very good (7th Bde.) All C.M.R's very good. 123rd. Div. Pack Train, 8th C.M.G.Co., Good. 58th Bn. Good. 116th Bn. Good. 52nd Bn. fairly good. 7th Field Co. and 8th ditto. fairly good. The G.O.C. appends the following remark "The G.O.C. is much pleased with the report as a whole but there appears still to be room for improvement in some cases, and he is absolutely correct. I consider that all 1st Line Transport Officers should be A.S.C. Officers from the Train and under me when I could remove them if they were not giving full satisfaction, as at present. The tendency is to resent any uncomplimentary remarks I may have to make, there is a feeling I am interfering and this is not conducive to the best results. Col. Marks of the 31st Divn. called on me, we agreed that when light railway comes into operation Train should go back to delivering to 1st Line, its normal work, so as to be sure of being mobile in case of an advance. He wants to change his refilling hour with me at railhead and he, as he is nearer, take the 7.30 hour. I advised as the light railway would come into effect so soon to leave it as it is at present. Heavy rain during the night.
>
> **3 October 1917.** — Light railway used today at new refilling point. The supply sections have managed to erect new shelters [uncertain] the old ones torn down and a little new material they certainly have worked hard and well. Discovered I had made a mistake in date of Election. Visited No.1 Co., Just had sprung on me we have to move, particularly rough as all had worked so hard to get lines and dumps into their present splendid shape. Went with Major Beasley to hear the dumbell troupe and they certainly gave me a surprise by the most excellent and refined show they put on, they have some excellent talent; hundreds were waiting for the doors to open and the place was packed, I should say over a 1000 [entry continues on Sheet No. 169]

### Page 5 — 3–6 October 1917 (Sheet No. 169)

> **3 October 1917 (continued).** — were there, a more orderly and appreciative audience would be hard to find anywhere.
>
> **4 October 1917.** — Lt. Spalding came to say "Good-bye" on his departure to join the Machine Gun Section, to which he is transferred at his own request. R.T.O. will not change refilling hour at Railhead as the Light Railway has to clear first to set trucks free at earliest possible moment. Capt. Churca [uncertain] just brought in the sad news that Sergt. Major Courtney passed away in his sleep this morning from heart failure. He was a very faithful servant. His excessive stoutness indicated heart. He will be buried today. I now [one line struck through and illegible] learn Divl. H.Qrs. are moving tomorrow, they cannot get M.T. and have asked me for 25 wagons, at a [uncertain] I might scramble 20 but no more. They must be carting around a fearful amount of junk to want 25 wagons to move it. Lt. H. C. Walton reported for duty here this afternoon and [one word struck through] seems quite a smart officer. Buried S.S.M. Courtney this afternoon AUX RIETZ Cemetery, was covered with the Union Jack, he lies in Row G., grave No.4. Almost every officer of the Train was there to pay their respects to a loyal and faithful comrade. R.I.P. [uncertain] Wrote his wife giving her particulars of his end and conveyed the sympathy of the whole Train. Dull all morning and pouring torrents in evening and during the night.
>
> **5 October 1917.** — Weather cleared to sunshine, good for the move, roads are very sticky, muddy & rain.
>
> **6 October 1917.** — H.Qrs. of Train moved to VILLERS BRULIN this morning in pouring rain, arriving about 10.35 a.m. accommodation very poor. No.2 Co. moved to ROCOURT, NO.3 to MINGOVAL, and No.4 to LA COMTE all in 4, U & V., Map sheet 36b. Railhead at HOUDAIN. J of same map. No.1 Co have no orders to move yet. Very wet all day. All watches put back an hour this night.

### Page 6 — 7–12 October 1917 (Sheet No. 170)

> **7 October 1917.** — Tried Corpl. Brigham of No.3 Co. absent for three hours without leave, severely reprimanded him and afterwards warned him that if he came up before me although he is well up on the list I would not let him be promoted. Very wet and windy all day.
>
> **8 October 1917.** — Walked out and inspected Transport of 15th Machine Gun Co. Fine up to 3.30 p.m. then clouded over and rained, gave short address to Machine Gun Co. on harness, horses and shoeing. They gave on the whole a good showing. Heavy rain during the night. No mails for five days, something gone wrong with the postal arrangements, this is most unusual, they have up to now been extremely good.
>
> **9 October 1917.** — Very dull morning, heavy clouds, wind and only glimpses of sun, looks like more rain. Major Freeman and Lt. Grant called in. Divl. Troops moving. Leave stopped. Rain on & off.
>
> **10 October 1917.** — No.1 Co. moved to COCOURT this morning in pouring rain. Most of the horses will have shelter, and after clipping they certainly need it. We are to keep mobile and ready to move up north at short notice. Lt. Hessey called at tea-time. Leave on again and Adjt., had to go out in car after dinner and warn Co. Commanders not to send their men who were due to go, out on convoy. Rained up to mid-day and then cleared. Capt. Aldous came in before dinner and took $850. off. New Ballot papers came in necessitated by a stupid blunder I made about date of poll. I can now put it all right, Wet morning, clearing, afternoon and evening colder.
>
> **11 October 1917.** — Leave opened up again. Got [uncertain] off. Visited No.1 & 3 Co's. No.1 only Co. that have shelter for horses. Beasley and I lightened our kits to the limit. Major McClelland, Paymaster, joined us at Mess again. Generally fine all day. Wet at night.
>
> **12 October 1917.** — Visited No.2 and 4 Co's. Horses deep in mud. Our Mess cook, Rae, fooling about with gasoline, burnt his left hand badly. He has nothing, directly or indirectly to do with gasoline and we almost deserve the lesson he has got. Rain and sunshine, mostly rain. Doctor joined No.1 Co. today to march with them tomorrow.

### Page 7 — 13–18 October 1917 (Sheet No. 171)

> **13 October 1917.** — No.1 Co. moves up north today marching with Artillery, and a rotten wet day they have for the trip. S.S.O. gone to CAESTRE to view new location and take his bearings. Wet and cold.
>
> **14 October 1917.** — H.Qrs. Train left VILLERS BRULIN at 10.30 a.m. arrived CAESTRE 12.30, a little over 40 miles in the car. As usual no arrangements were made for billeting us and it was 5 o'clock before any were found and it was dark before all kit and office equipment got in. No.1 Co. marching here by road with Artillery. Other Co's entrain at TINQUES and detrain at GODEWAERSVELDE and at SAVY detraining at CAESTRE. They will not all be in until the 17th and on that date railhead changes to WIPPENHOEK. Very bright and fine.
>
> **15 October 1917.** — Took Engineers mess in error and changed this morning to one in some street. Why the devil they did not let the engineers take the one we moved to, both being equally bad, and save a double change, Engineers not yet being in, I fail to see. More and more I notice the Adjutant flares up about the smallest matter talking with anyone. He gives me the idea of being thoroughly fed up and wants a months rest. I find that Railhead will only change on the 17th for Divl. Troops. Dry but cold.
>
> **16 October 1917.** — All Co's will be in by tonight. Information as to move not yet available. Lieut. Hessey called. Fine.
>
> **17 October 1917.** — Major Freeman called. 3rd Imperials pulling out of standings about a thousand yards S.S.W. of POPERINGHE and I told him to move in there at once and get ahead of them. Co. horses stood journey very well indeed. Freeman will not take his leave yet, will try and send someone else, and we can [uncertain] when things are more settled. Rained heavy during night.
>
> **18 October 1917.** — Adjt. went off early in car to fetch S.Q.M.S. White so that he could get his leave. Went to BORR to No.2 Co. to hear a case remanded for F.G.C.M. He has 24 hours to consider whether he adheres to this. S.S.O. and Adjt., off to No.1 Co. Dry and getting colder. Horses in the open and in the mud not looking well.

### Page 8 — 19–23 October 1917 (Sheet No. 173)

> **19 October 1917.** — Visited with S.S.O. Refilling Point of Div. Troops near VLAMERTINGHE. They certainly are roughing it here. Called in at No.3 Co. located at G.4.d.4.8 Sheet 28, only standings with cover for some 60 horses, but material around if collected and properly used will suffice for all. Wet and muddy and accommodation very poor. Very heavy rain.
>
> **20 October 1917.** — S.S.O. gone to show S.O's their new Refilling Points. and finding out what left behind units and supplies we had to take over. Information as to length of time we are to use new railhead very meagre and I am uncertain as to best location for Co's., so as to curtail as much as possible unnecessary travel, this is most essential to conserve horse strength and because of congestion in roads, this is very considerable in VLAMERTINGHE-YPRES Road.
>
> **21 October 1917.** — Divn. [uncertain] altering charge against a loader in No.2 Co. so that I can deal with it myself, at present the difficulties of convening a F.G.C.M. are very great. Visited No.1 Co. and tried Dvr. O'Connor for drunkenness, gave him 14 days F.P. No.1 examined lines &c., and surveyed accommodation in vicinity for other Co's moving tomorrow. Fine.
>
> **22 October 1917.** — Left CAESTRE 9 a.m. Pulled in alongside No.1 Co., Camp none too clean. We have the usual tidying up and re-arrangements to make but should be quite comfortable. Wagons arrived with our kit at 2.30. All Co's now in here. Started in to rain but cleared by noon. Bombs at night, no damage to Train.
>
> **23 October 1917.** — Lt. Richards reported back from leave. Adjt., and I went and saw Area Commandant re moving to move 2 & 3 Co's. Says we must get them in with No.1 Co. at G.4.d. Sheet 28, where there is hardly room for No.1 Co., To help us out he has given G.15.a.8.6. as well. Went and inspected it with Adjutant and Capt. Phillips, it is all one sea of mud, no standings or shelter of any sort and it would be criminal to put horses there, happened to meet Camp Commandant at No.4 Co., who gave them leave to remain where they are until compelled to turn out and he says the camp [uncertain] given to help us out by Area Commandant is [entry continues on Sheet No. 175]

### Page 9 — 23–26 October 1917 (Sheet No. 175)

> **23 October 1917 (continued).** — is useless and we can mark time for the present see Area Commandant and see what can be done and communicate later result of his conversation with him. Very wet.
>
> **24 October 1917.** — Drawing last time today from EDWAARTSHOEK and tomorrow Railhead will be VLAMERTINGHE about 4000 yards due East. Divn suggested my altering charge against loader I remanded for F.G.C.M. on 18-10-17, and I deal with him myself. I had him up this afternoon and gave him 28 days F.P. No.1 He has had one previous F.P.No.1 of 14 days and 2 of 28 and I am sorry owing to the extra amount of duty in this area and at this time, he evidently is a [uncertain] [uncertain] observed at least 90 days. Hear that Major Scott is on his way to relieve Major Freeman. Strongly recommended Capt. Oliphant to apply for a months leave and get away as soon as possible for a rest and shake off his horrible cold. Capt. Bishop had interview re joining Air Service and will be called for in about a week or ten days. More shells in last night at intervals of 6 minutes, 10 to a dozen in number. High wind. Rain and cold.
>
> **25 October 1917.** — Capt. Oliphant gone on leave. Applied for an extension to a month, forwarded same to D.A.A.G.(a) and with it a strong letter of recommendation. No.3 moved in to 40 Camp, same Camp as No.1 Co. Capt. Church takes over duties of Acting Adjt., temporarily, and Lt. Richards will act as O.C. No.2 Co., Major Freeman to remain here until Major Scott arrives to relieve him. This does him out of his leave. Lt. Walton's horse fell with him and he was admitted to Rest Station, not badly hurt, hopes to return in a day or two. Wet and cold.
>
> **26 October 1917.** — Capt. Phillips is making considerable improvements in his new lines, repairing standings, roofs, cleaning out surface water ditches, and mending roads. Guns hard at it last night, enemy planes over camp and searchlights got on them, they had to beat it. POPERINGHE getting shelled hard last evening and again this morning with big stuff, about 50% duds. Lt. Keyes gone to Ambulance suffering from stomach trouble, I am afraid his age is against him and his term of usefulness at an end. Col. Webb called in and saw me, he is looking excellent and informs me he is out of the A.S.C. Now. This is the day we are after [entry continues on Sheet No. 174]

### Page 10 — 26–28 October 1917 (Sheet No. 174)

> **26 October 1917 (continued).** — PASSCHAENDALE RIDGE and it is cold and raining hard all day. Our M.O. gone to assist at Clearing Station VLAMERTINGHE with wounded.
>
> **27 October 1917.** — Morning broke bright and clear the boys taking the ridge are having a hard time, while one German Officer in C.C.S. was telling the Doctor the Germans could not be beaten, one of his own Co. was brought in and on asking about his Coy. the man told him there was no Cy. it was wiped out, somehow that looks like being beaten. Majors Greer and Morrison called to see S.S.O. re [uncertain] feeding them during the move of the 1st Divn. Parade of Train at 3 p.m. for those who have won them to receive the dumbell badge. Addressed parade on the idea of the conception of the badge, its meaning and what was expected of those who have won them, their Co's and names are No.1 Co. 511164 Dvr. Foster R., 511210 Dvr. Goulden W.H., 511202 Dvr. Smith D., 512134 Pte. (Supply Section) Sadler J. No.2 Co. 2008 Cpl. [one word struck through — [uncertain]] Wilkie A., 17203 Dvr. McWilliam G.D., 514806 Dvr. McGregory J.W. No.3 Co. 826 Dvr. Shaver H.L.C., 510484 Dvr. Stratie R.J., 4936 Dvr. Meacock J.W., 510511 Cpl. (Supply Section) Malaloff F.H. No.4 Co. 117174 Dvr. Burwood F.G., 117170 Dvr. Brown W., 510463 Dvr. Wakefield W.S., 16466 Pte. (Supply Section) Powell H.M., and a fine clean smart bunch of lads they are. Cleared up during night and turned to frost. The Arty. (ours) was very lively from and early part of the night until daylight, when it slackened. No planes or bombs over us today.
>
> **28 October 1917.** — Lt. Fisher reported he has to turn out of his Camp and ordered by Camp Commandant to get into Camp 40, No.1 and 3 Co's are crushed there so he and Adjt. (Capt. Church Actg) go to see Commandant to see what can be arranged. He may have been a General and may have had 45 years service, he certainly has a bad temper, possibly a liver too, but he knows mighty little of the requirements of a Train and how hard and necessary it is to have shelter for horses and keep them mobile. All night the Arty. were going at it hard. Some showers

### Page 11 — 29–31 October 1917 (Sheet No. 175)

> **29 October 1917.** — Beautiful clear morning. Capt. de Graves reported back from leave and posted as Acting Adjutant. Capt. Church returns to his Co. Went with Major McClelland to VLAMERTINGHE Burial ground to try and locate Lt. Langley Attwood's grave but failed. Fine.
>
> **30 October 1917.** — Inspected and distributed eleven remounts. I have seen worse but not much or often. Tried again to find Lt. Langley Attwood's grave but failed. Working out move back to rest. S.S.O. of 1st were arranging about supplies. Col. Simpson and his Adjt., also came later to arrange for taking over. Rained hard all day.
>
> **31 October 1917.** — At 4.30 a.m. H.V. big guns [uncertain] to drop shells into POPERINGHE about a dozen at the same time, nine bombs were dropped in close proximity to our lines. During this month we were at one time feeding as much as 58,764 men and 18,732 horses and we did it without any fall down.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *[signature — [uncertain]] Lieut.-Colonel / O.C. 3rd Canadian Divl. Train*

## Open questions / uncertain items (October 1917)
- The cover reads "Volume 21" (and the title page carries a handwritten "21" at the top right), but the contents page reads "Volume 17. - 10." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number.
- The diary sheets are numbered 168, 169, 170, 171, 173, 175, 174, 175 in that order: Sheet 172 does not appear, Sheet 175 is used twice (pages 9 and 11), and Sheet 174 follows Sheet 175. The dates run continuously 1–31 October with no gap, so no diary text appears to be missing — the sheet numbering itself is irregular.
- Three entries run across sheet breaks and are split in the transcription as written: 3 October (Sheets 168–169), 23 October (Sheets 173–175), and 26 October (Sheets 175–174).
- 2 October: the G.O.C.'s quoted remark opens with a quotation mark that is never closed in the text; the remark as typed runs on into the diarist's own commentary ("I consider that all 1st Line Transport Officers should be A.S.C. Officers..."), so where the quotation ends is not marked.
- 2 October: the report is attributed to "DDD&S." [uncertain] — the abbreviation is read as typed but may be a misreading (a veterinary-services report would be expected for a horse report); it is not expanded here.
- 4 October: "Capt. Churca" [uncertain] — the name is read as typed, but the same officer is written "Capt. Church" on 25 and 29 October (Acting Adjutant), so "Churca" may be a typing error for "Church".
- 4 October: one whole line is struck through with x's after "He will be buried today. I now" and is illegible; and one word before "seems quite a smart officer" is struck through and unreadable.
- 4 October: "R.I.P." [uncertain] — the letters between R and P are not read with confidence.
- 4 October: the burial place is written "AUX RIETZ Cemetery" — as written; the cemetery name is not verified against any other source.
- 5 October: "roads are very sticky, muddy & rain." — as written, though the last word reads oddly.
- 6 October: "ROCOURT" and 10 October "COCOURT" are as written; "all in 4, U & V., Map sheet 36b" is as written.
- 10 October: "Capt. Aldous came in before dinner and took $850. off." — as written; the sum and its meaning are not explained in the diary.
- 18 October: "BORR" is as written (possibly Borre, near Caestre, but not verified).
- 24 October: "EDWAARTSHOEK" is as written. In the sentence about the loader's punishment, two words are overtyped beyond reading: "he evidently is a [uncertain] [uncertain] observed at least 90 days."
- 27 October: "No.3 Co." is written in the Place column in the margin beside the badge list, rather than in the text; the four names following it (Shaver, Stratie, Meacock, Malaloff) are placed under No.3 Co. on that reading.
- 27 October: the badge list gives 15 names with regimental numbers, four each for No.1, No.3 and No.4 Co's and three for No.2 Co.; the word before "Wilkie A." in the No.2 Co. list is struck through and unreadable.
- 31 October: "H.V. big guns [uncertain] to drop shells" — the phrase is overtyped and the verb appears to be missing or mistyped.
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the October sheets is not read with confidence; it is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name. The repeated handwritten initial mark at the end of most entries reads as "J.A.S." [uncertain].
- Names read as: Lt. Spalding; Capt. Churca [uncertain] / Capt. Church; Sergt. Major Courtney (S.S.M. Courtney); Lt. H. C. Walton; Major Beasley; Col. Marks (31st Divn.); Major Freeman; Lt. Grant; Lt. Hessey; Capt. Aldous; Major McClelland (Paymaster); Rae (Mess cook); S.Q.M.S. White; Lt. Richards; Capt. Phillips; Dvr. O'Connor; Lt. Keyes; Col. Webb; Major Scott; Capt. Oliphant; Capt. Bishop; Lt. Fisher; Capt. de Graves; Lt. Langley Attwood; Col. Simpson; Majors Greer and Morrison.
- No battle casualties are recorded for the Train itself in this month. One death: S.S.M. Courtney, died in his sleep of heart failure on 4 October and buried the same day at AUX RIETZ Cemetery, Row G, grave No.4. Injuries/illness: Mess cook Rae burnt his left hand badly (12 October); Lt. Walton's horse fell with him, admitted to Rest Station, not badly hurt (25 October); Lt. Keyes to ambulance with stomach trouble (26 October). Bombs fell near the Train's lines on 22 and 31 October with no damage or casualties recorded. The month's peak feeding strength is given on 31 October as 58,764 men and 18,732 horses.
