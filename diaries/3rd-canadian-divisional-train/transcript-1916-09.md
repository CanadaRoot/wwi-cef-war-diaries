- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps (C.A.S.C.), 3rd Canadian Division (styled "Headquarters 3rd Canadian Divisional Train" on the cover)
- **Month:** September 1916 (Volume 8) — this volume covers 1 to 30 September 1916

- **Pages:** September 1916, p. 1 – p. 9 (image files page-0084 – page-0092 of the diary)
- **September 1916 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–9): cover, contents page, diary text 1–30 September, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The Place column is blank on every diary sheet in this volume; no place is entered against any date. The Hour column is likewise blank throughout. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not transcribed against every entry here. Overtyping and handwritten corrections in the original are transcribed as the final readable text, with the correction noted where it matters. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer (as written); "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "D.D. of S. & T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "D.D.R." = Deputy Director of Remounts (as written); "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "H.D.H." / "H.D." = Heavy Draught Horse (as written); "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "C.D.T." = Canadian Divisional Train; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "T.M.B." = Trench Mortar Battery (as written); "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column; "W.O." = Warrant Officer. ## Pages 3–9 — Diary text (1–30 September 1916)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed footer reads "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12."

### Page 3 — 1–5 September 1916 (Sheet No. 69)

> **1** — 30th Battery complained of horses sent by Train for their Baggage Wagons. Pointed out to them that they had one splendid team, and it came in with sore shoulders, and had to change them. The team sent are gibbers and I broke them up, but they were not satisfied with the old grey I sent, and put up the bluff about not being allowed to have greys. I have allowed this to pass and they have a steady pair of blacks, and hope they will take care of them. Any more trouble and I am on them quick. Adjutant at adjourned Court-Martial. D.A.Q.M.G. called. Gas class held. Talked to Captain Graham re Supply Officers and their demands. Colonel Webb called. Dull but fine.
>
> **2** — Tried case of V.O. from No. 3 Company. Visited Divisional Head Quarters, and had a talk about extra forage wagons, and how they were to be distributed on the contemplated move. Adjutant, Assistant Adjutant and self rode out to No. 3 Company and made inspection of Company, and was extremely well pleased with all I saw. Lieut. Grant came on as Assistant Adjutant, and Lieut. Fisher to No. 3 on transport duties. My horse took bad while at No. 3 Coy. and had to be left there with groom. Capt. McLennan from No. 2 Company came in for information. Stayed for lunch. S.S.O. saw D.D. of S.&T. re move, and he expressed his regret that this Division was leaving the Second Army as we had given him less trouble than any other Division. Fine all day, slight showers in the evening.
>
> **3** — Two French Interpreters reported for duty with Train. Posted one to No. 3 Company & the other to No. 4. S.S.O. went and saw Division about move. Adjutant and I called later. Colonel McBrien informed me sanction had been granted for Captain Hennessy to be attached to Staff for instructional purposes. Grant will fill his place allright. Poor old "Pop" had more shells in to day. Dull and Fine.
>
> **4** — Inspected 1st Line Transport of No. 10 Canadian Field Ambulance and while there put two Sergeants through a short test, oral and practical. Considering the weather etc. transport very good indeed, but this always was a good unit. No. 8 very much improved allround. M.O. finished innoculations. Adjutant at Court Martial. Very wet afternoon.
>
> **5** — S.S.O. back from visiting new area, and had a very wet and nasty time, reports roads not too bad, but Billets very poor. Received movement orders, called all Train Officers and explained orders to them. They are all so clear and explicit, the move should go without

### Page 4 — 5–10 September 1916 (Sheet No. 70)

> **5** (continued) — a hitch, provided plans are not altered at last minute. Colonel Webb called and I explained the mode of turning over our Artillery Wagons to him. D.A.Q.M.G. to see about remounts before we pull out. Visited Divisional Head Quarters. Rained all day.
>
> **6** — Refilling for the last time for the Artillery, after refilling they go to the 4th Canadian Divisional Train. V.O. gone for remounts, 3 H.D.H., and 1 Riding. S.S.O. gone to new area this afternoon. I with Adjutant tomorrow morning, and am now all but packed up. Cleared up nicely.
>
> **7** — Just as we were moving out, the V.O. reported two horses kicked and could not move out. Instructed him to get the necessary documents made out, and signed by farmers wife, who is to look after them, and take copy to D.A.Q.M.G. Pulled out of STEENVOORDE at 10-30 a.m. through HAZEBROUCK, S. VENANT, BOSNESS, LILLERS, S. POL FRIVENT, DOULLENS, where we lunched and wasted 1½ hours, FIENVILLERS, DOMONT, SURCAMP, arriving at 3 p.m. Captain Oliphant, Lieut. Richards and Lieut. Greer came to see S.S.O. re Supplies. Killed a dog on the way. Very fine all day.
>
> **8** — Adjutant and I called at Divisional Head Quarters. Called in at LA HAIE Farm for wire Re Rail Head; of no importance as we had already ascertained this information. Met Colonel Bott of 2nd C.M.R., first time I had seen him since my Vernon days. All Companies in, and stood journey well. Fine all day.
>
> **9** — Morning dawned in thick fog, move orders arrived so late it was touch and go that Head Quarters Company got away in time. All feeding arrangements made. Called at Divisional Head Quarters during day. Wire re corrections in Movement Orders, cause of delay in my orders being delayed. Fine all day.
>
> **10** — Day broke in fog. All Companies moved off by 7 a.m. Billets and Lines left very clean. Called at Divisional Head Quarters, and luckily found out destination altered. Arrived at FIEFFES at 10-20. Issued orders to post sentries and prevent bathing in brook. At Refilling and instructed O.C's to get in touch with their Brigades re moves. Fine most of the day.

### Page 5 — 11–15 September 1916 (Sheet No. 71)

> **11** — No. 3 Company moved out at 3 a.m., Headquarters and Headquarters Company at 5-35. Lines clean, considering No. 3 Company moved out at an early hour and in the dark. Horses looking good, and all in by mid-day at VADENCOURT. In evening Barn occupied by C.M.R's caught fire. Ran over to Headquarters Company, which was quite close to Barn, and only fire alarm had been sounded. Turned out party to help remove wagons etc. of C.M.R's, and posted 30 men at 2 paces interval along C.M.R's and Head Quarters Horse Shelters to stamp out sparks coming over on to loose straw. After fire died down called roll, and ordered every man to remain in his lines. Major McGillivray quietly walking round, but taking no precautions. Fine all day.
>
> **12** — Moved out at 10 a.m. to SENLIS arrived there by car at 11 a.m., Headquarters Company coming in later. Visited A.A.&Q.M.G. and D.A.Q.M.G. in dug outs at H.Q. of ALBERT. Lunched with O.C. 1st C.D.T., met O.C. 2nd C.D.T. Major McGillivray asked to be paraded in front of General because I posted him as O.C. No. 2 Company so that I could try Captain McLennan out in Headquarters Company, and be in a position to say if I could recommend him for promotion. Refused to parade him, but [uncertain] told him I would forward any complaint in writing. Fine.
>
> **13** — Took two hours to go to Rail Head at BELLE EGLISE owing to the 25th Divisional Artillery taking wrong rout and blocking the way. Refilling in bulk with horse transport, and all going smoothly. O.C. Headquarters Company had a case of a man come before me, and as his orders were neither signed or dated, I could only reprimand him. S.S.O. working very hard and very well, no hitch yet, and he deserves enormous credit. Major Lawson to lunch. Adjutant and I to dugouts. D.A.Q.M.G. called re 8 limbers for Engineers. Some rain at intervals.
>
> **14** — S.S.O., Adjutant and I called at Rail Head to see refilling, and went on and called on D.D.S.&T.. Major McGillivray changed command with Captain McLennan. Called and saw Colonel Simon, 1st Train, and showed him and discussed letter from Major Scott. Lahore all except D.A.C. and Trench Mortar move up here to day. S.S.O., Adjutant and Assistant Adjutant called at dugouts and Divisional Head Quarters (at ALBERT), and I stayed in camp. About 6-30 Artillery opened and continued all night, towards day light the noise became intense and continuous. Cold but fine.
>
> **15** — S.S.O. to Rail Head. Artillery almost ceased which is a good sign. Visited Nos. 2, 3, and 4 Companies at BRICKFIELDS. Saw first German aeroplane up over us since we left YPRES area, he had to keep up so high that he looked a mere speck. Went on to dugouts. Saw about 50 prisoners coming in, all looked well fed and clothed, but the majority did not impress one as being possessed with much brain power, looked a low type of humanity, dull and dour, but they did not look downhearted being taken prisoners. They will perhaps learn now how they have been duped. S.S.O. to Rail Head. Had a long ride to get the lay of the country better into my head. Quite a wintery feeling in the air after sun down but fine.

> **15** (continued) — 2, 3, and 4 Companies at BRICKFIELDS. Saw first German aeroplane up over us since we left YPRES area, he had to keep up so high that he looked a mere speck. Went on to dugouts. Saw about 50 prisoners coming in, all looked well fed and clothed, but the majority did not impress one as being possessed with much brain power, looked a low type of humanity, dull and dour, but they did not look downhearted being taken prisoners. They will perhaps learn now how they have been duped. S.S.O. to Rail Head. Had a long ride to get the lay of the country better into my head. Quite a wintery feeling in the air after sun down but fine.
>
> **16** — S.S.O., Adjutant and self to Rail Head, inspected convoys and can see signs of the long haul in the Horses and wagons. After lunch we went up to Divisional Head Quarters, saw A.A.&Q.M.G., spoke to him of the impossibility of Train Horses lasting if the mechanical transport is removed, and we have to do all the hauling by horse transport. Our greatest haul will be 28 miles, and this will kill any horse. Had a good look at one of the land ships damaged in yesterday's advance, they certainly are some ships. Turning very cold at nights.
>
> **17** — S.S.O. to Rail Head. Working out scheme for O.C. Companies if the three Echelon transport comes into operation. Visited Divisional Head Quarters, and tried to get on to Divisional Artillery Head Quarters, but too many troops on road. Delivered rations to T.M.B. with Train wagon, but told them they must find the wagon I turned over to them, and come to the dump and draw their own rations. Fine all day.
>
> **18** — Very quiet all night and very wet too. In wet weather the mud here reminds one of Salisbury Plains. Horses so far standing it well. Captain Church reported that Lt. Hessey came into camp drunk, and I ordered him to go back and place him under arrest at once. 15th Reserve Park came and reported that his wagons were at our disposal now. Continued wet and cold all day.
>
> **19** — Wet all night again, and mud worse than ever. 6 wagons turned over to us from 15th Reserve Park, and 6 of ours sent to Salvage for duty. Am wondering what they will be like when returned, certainly not first class. Went to No. 4 Company and saw Lt. Hessey who is charged with drunkenness, remanded him for F.G.C.M., went on and called at Divisional Head Quarters. Met Colonel Sutherland Brown who is returning relieving Colonel McBrien who goes to 72nd as Brigadier General. [Handwritten insertion below "72nd": "12½" [uncertain].] Raining most of the day, and furious bombardment all night.

### Page 7 — 19–23 September 1916 (Sheet No. 73)

> **19** (continued) — Brigadier General. Have worked with Colonel B. before, and though sorry Colonel McBrien is going, glad Colonel Sutherland Brown is taking over duties of A.A.&Q.M.G. Raining most of the day, and furious bombardment all night.
>
> **20** — Bombardment still continues. Took summary of evidence in case of Lieut. Hessey. S.S.O. gone to Divisional Head Quarters and Rail Head, and gone away this afternoon to see that all his feeding arrangements are correct, he certainly is doing good work. Forwarded long letter of complaint from M.O. to Divisional Head Quarters. I pointed out to him he had made very strong statements, and he had better be prepared to back them up, and he said he could, so I sent his letter forward. Cleared up late afternoon.
>
> **21** — Visited Rail Head and inspected all horses of convoy. In spite of the long hauls they are now doing, and the bad weather and standings, they are in very good condition. Went on to D.D. of S.&T., saw Major Gillespie and spoke to him about changing of Officers from one Company to another or from Transport to Supply duties, and he concurred with my views, but to write in my desires in this matter and they would in all likelihood be approved. Captain McCallum can be appointed S.S.O. without rank if I write and recommend it. M.O. gone to AMIENS to get some special medicine for me, S.S.O. and Paymaster went with him, they were to get some grub, and then meet at Hotel, have a good dinner and return, but poor old Paymaster got lost and they came away without him. Not fine and not altogether wet but a plethora of mud.
>
> **22** — Car gone back to try and pick up Paymaster. Day broke very fine. 7 remounts came in late last night, one riding and 6 H.D. The Riding is a poor hollow backed skate and a kicker. The others are poor things, lean and soft with no stamina, and one a bad cribber. Paymaster back and went to No. 4 Company direct before coming here and lost no time. He evidently went back to what he thought was the meeting place, and I considered after hearing him that there was a grievous mistake, so let the matter drop. S.S.O. and Acting Adjutant gone to Divisional Head Quarters. Beautifully fine all day, and comparatively quiet.
>
> **23** — Trying to make one of the H.D. horses work that came as remount on evening of 21st. This horse is absolutely useless, and is not broken to harness. I saw him tried for over an hour in every possible way — a splendid looking horse too. Another is a regular rearer but can work. It is simply disgusting that useless horses should be sent out as remounts. Tried him again in roler as he was spoiling his mate when hitched as a pair, but it is useless spending any more time on him. Lahore coming back, and No. 2 Company remains at 1st move and does not go on. I seem to spend most of my time running from place to place, no sooner locate my Companies than they are changed. I don't know when I will ever get in a 1st Line Transport Inspection again. If we could only settle for a few days in one place it would be O.K., still from what I see of them on the march, they are holding up well. Early fog but clear and bright later.

> **24** — S.S.O. and I to Rail Head, met D.A.Q.M.G. on way who told S.S.O. that 9th Brigade would remain at HERRISART, and not go on to LA VICOGNE, and no lorries would be given. Inspected transport and only two horses showing signs of fatigue. No. 4 looking particularly well. Called in at Divisional Head Quarters, saw Majors Hughes and Cameron, former wants estimate of timber wanted for standings, but I told him it would be useless to deliver it during all this moving, as it would in all probability be pinched and used for other purposes. As soon as I know we are making any stay in one place I will let him have it. Headquarters Company is the only one at present making any headway with standings, and they have made no more. Had a talk with G.O.C. re Major McGillivray and horses. Captain Oliphant came with a grievance about the move without lorries. If he would take as much pains to carry out his orders, as to formulating grouches, it would be all for the good. At 9 p.m. the Division wired for return of Officers and men by Ranks and numbers to be in by 10 p.m., luckily a car was available or it certainly would not have been in by 10 p.m. McCallum confirmed as S.S.O. Early fog but very fine all day.
>
> **25** — Captain Hennessy gone to Division for Staff work, Lieut. Grant carries on as Adjutant, and Lieut. Fisher comes in as understudy. S.S.O. seeing about a claim put in for damage to potato patch at HARPONVILLE. No. 3 Company shelled pretty hard last night, only one casualty Pte. Davies got fragment in the calf, clean wound but a nasty one. Fine all day.
>
> **26** — Adjutant and I motored to Divisional Head Quarters. Called in at No. 4 Company to get Captain Church to go with me for 1st Line Transport Inspection of 9th Brigade. As most of the transport of this Brigade was out, could not make a complete inspection, but from what I saw and heard, the transport is in good shape. Went on to FIEFFES and MONTRELET for inspection of 7th Brigade, caught them and all but 49th Battn. in very good shape. Machine Gun Company devoting most of its time to burnishing chains etc. at expense of leather by command of their O.C., which is absolutely wrong. Leather, especially breast collars, must be kept perfect or galls will be the result, and all the brightening in the world of links will not obviate this. This catch the eye business is not horsemastership. Adjutant of 1st came in as I got back, and had a cup of tea. Fine all day.

>
> **28** — Called at Divisional Head Quarters at WARLOY, but they had just moved back. Called in on dentist and had tooth fixed. S.S.O. and Mr. Greer saw Town Major, and tried to fix up claim, but Town Major is now not certain No. 3 Company is responsible although he was positive when S.S.O. interviewed him first time. S.S.O. and I went to TARA HILL and visited Divisional Head Quarters. No. 2 Company coming back to BRICKFIELDS. No. 3 Coy. will remain where it is at BOUZINCOURT as it is central. >
> **29** — I did not go on leave. Buried Lieut. Greer at ALBERT Cemetery. Nothing to report. Generally got rid of correspondence. S.S.O. to Head Quarters and nothing for me there. Heavy firing began at noon. Very wet all day.
>
> **30** — Tried case of N.C.O. from No. 2 Company and gave him severe reprimand. Detailed five wagons for SAUSAGE VALLEY Tramway., two from Headquarters Company and one from each of the other three Companies with double Drivers, and trying to get them replaced from 15th Reserve Park. Called with Acting Adjutant at TARA HILL, Divisional Head Quarters "Q", discussed supply of wagons and drivers to Sausage Valley. Also the indenting for four wagons for the Lahore. All day heavy Artillery Bombardment. Fine and clear.

## Open questions / uncertain items (September 1916)
- The cover reads "Volume 8" but the contents page reads "Volume 16 - 9." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number.
- The Place column and the Hour column are blank on every diary sheet in this volume; no place or hour is entered against any date. Index places for this month are therefore taken from the entry text.
- 5 September is split across Sheets 69 and 70, 15 September across Sheets 71 and 72, 19 September across Sheets 72 and 73, 23 September across Sheets 73 and 74, and 26 September across Sheets 74 and 75; these are continuations of a single entry, mid-sentence in each case, not duplicate dates.
- 4 September: "No. 8 very much improved allround" is as written; there is no No. 8 Company in the Train, and the reference is left as written rather than corrected.
- 6 September: "I with Adjutant tomorrow morning" is an incomplete sentence as typed; transcribed as written.
- 7 September: the route is given as HAZEBROUCK, S. VENANT, BOSNESS, LILLERS, S. POL FRIVENT, DOULLENS, FIENVILLERS, DOMONT, SURCAMP, arriving at 3 p.m. — the destination at the end of the march is not named in the entry, and the spellings BOSNESS, FIENVILLERS, DOMONT and SURCAMP are as typed and not corrected. "S. POL FRIVENT" is as typed (St. Pol / Frevent).
- 12 September: one overtyped word in "Refused to parade him, but [uncertain] told him I would forward any complaint in writing" could not be read; recorded as [uncertain].
- 19 September: a handwritten insertion below "72nd" reads "12½" [uncertain]; its meaning is unclear, and the entry is transcribed as written, with the insertion noted. Colonel McBrien is recorded as going to the 72nd as Brigadier General.
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of Sheet 75 is not read with confidence and is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name.
- The repeated handwritten initial mark at the end of most entries is not read with confidence and is not transcribed against every entry.
- "Land ships" (16 September) are the tanks first used in action on 15 September 1916; the diary's own term is transcribed as written.
- Names read as: Captain Graham; Captain Hennessy; Captain McLennan; Captain Oliphant; Captain Church; Captain McCallum (confirmed as S.S.O. on 24 September); Major McGillivray; Major Lawson; Major Scott; Major Gillespie; Major Hughes; Major Cameron; Colonel McBrien; Colonel Webb (4th Train); Colonel Simon (1st Train); Colonel Bott (2nd C.M.R.); Colonel Sutherland Brown; Lieut. Grant; Lieut. Fisher; Lieut. Richards; Lieut. Greer; Lieut. Hessey; "Pop" (a horse, as written); Pte. Davies.
- Casualties in this volume: Lieut. Greer died and was buried at Albert Cemetery on 29 September; no cause of death is stated in the diary. Pte. Davies of No. 3 Company was wounded in the calf by a shell fragment on the night of 24/25 September — "clean wound but a nasty one". No other human casualties are recorded. Two horses were kicked and left behind at Steenvoorde on 7 September, and a number of remounts received 21–22 September were judged unfit for harness work.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given.
