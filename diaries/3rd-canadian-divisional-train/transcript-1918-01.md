## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading; the cover and title page are partly handwritten. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. The Place column is blank throughout the diary sheets; places named in the text are given as written. Abbreviations as written: "D.H.Q." = Divisional Headquarters; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "A.A.& Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "M.V.S." = Mobile Veterinary Section; "D.A.D.V.S." / "A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director / Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "A.M.C." = Army Medical Corps; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "H.E." = high explosive; "G.S." = General Service; "inst:" = instant (of this month). The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."

## Pages 4–10 — Diary text (1–31 January 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The diary sheets are numbered "Sheet 1." to "Sheet 7."

### Page 4 — 1–5 January 1918 (Sheet No. 1)

> **1 January 1918.** — Dull and cold all day. Called at D.H.Q. first thing and went into one or two matters re rations with D.A.Q.M.G. Went on to No.1 Company afterwards and had a look over their lines etc. finding everything alright. Called on Nos. 2, 3, and 4 Companies during the afternoon. All horses of No.4 Company put through anti-Mange dip at BARLIN this afternoon. Dull night.
>
> **2 January 1918.** — Dull mild day again and thaw setting in. Called at D.H.Q. but nothing new there. Passed on to No.2 Company and had a look over their horses. During the afternoon, inspected horses of Nos. 3 and 4 Companies. Mange gradually disappearing and the Dip evidently did much for the horses of No.4 Company. Nothing else new and all quiet round here.
>
> **3 January 1918.** — Bright day, though fairly mild again. Visited Nos. 2 and 3 Companies during day and looked over the horses. Mange rapidly disappearing under treatment and we hope to eradicate it before long. Nothing else of any importance happening and all our work progressing favorably.
>
> **4 January 1918.** — Bright mild day again. Visited refilling points with S.S.O. this morning, also coal dumps, finding everything in good shape. Had enquiry into loss of bicycles from refilling points and though all have now been recovered, had the Staff-Sergeant concerned before me and warned him as to consequences of carelessness in future. Visited Nos. 1 and 2 Companies during afternoon. Nothing new happening around here and all fairly quiet. Advised that Col: Booth, Chief of Staff of American Forces, will inspect this unit on 12th inst:
>
> **5 January 1918.** — Dull, raw kind of a day. Visited all companies this morning and made arrangements with V.O. to send all suspect mange cases to the Div at M.V.S. BARLIN. Attended meeting at Corps H.Q. this afternoon to discuss further economies in rations, clothing etc. Suggestions were noted for submission to Corps Commander. Nothing else of importance, and all quiet here.

### Page 5 — 6–12 January 1918 (Sheet No. 2)

> **6 January 1918.** — Dull and mild today. Visited all the Companies during the day to see how the "Dip" affected the horses, but found them all looking as fit as ever. Mange steadily decreasing and all suspects have now been clipped and dipped twice. D.A.D.V.S. was around twice also and inspected all our horses and expressed satisfaction at way things are going. Raining tonight. Mileage covered week ending January 6th, 5,267.
>
> **7 January 1918.** — Very mild again today and roads in bad condition. Visited Nos. 2, 3 and 4 Companies and found everything in good shape. Lieut-Col: d'Auvergne Findlay returned from leave and re-assumed Command. Major Heasley returns to duty as S.S.O., Captain Oliphant as Adjutant, and Captain de Graves to command of No.4 Company. All quiet around here and nothing out of the ordinary happening.
>
> **8 January 1918.** — Resumed duties of O.C. Looking up and reading back Orders and correspondence to again get in touch with things. Visited D.H.Q. nothing new and all going smoothly. Fall of snow in morning, bright and cold.
>
> **9 January 1918.** — Inspected No.1 Company. Sick and Mangey horses doing well and receiving good care and attention. All in good shape. Capt: Oliphant confined to bed, nothing serious. Cold and snowing.
>
> **10 January 1918.** — Clear morning, slight thaw. Majors Heasley and Scott gone to ascertain if any truth in rumour Robinson of 4th Train is to be promoted Colonel, and if so lodge a protest. Inspected Nos. 2 and 4 Companies, all in good shape, especially No.4 and great credit is due to Mr. Hessey, who for three months has been acting O.C.
>
> **11 January 1918.** — Called in at D.H.Q. and had long talk with A.A.& Q.M.G. re Simson's appointment, rumoured promotions, and visit of Col: Booth of U.S. Army, glad to hear his flattering remarks of the Train Horses and general turn-out of convoy. Very wet and cold. L. Grane called late afternoon.
>
> **12 January 1918.** — S.S.O. and Capt: Grane gone up to front line to see if they can locate certain reserve rations. [Entry continues on Sheet No. 3.]

### Page 6 — 12–15 January 1918 (Sheet No. 3)

> **12 January 1918 (continued).** — Inspected No.3 Company, all in very good shape, drew Capt: Phillips' attention to unnecessary work in Cook-House, this was a small thing, but waste of any kind must cease, be it big or small. Capt: Fisher gone on leave. A.D.V.S. called yesterday. I have now made a thorough inspection of all Train Companies and am well pleased with things in general, especially good working condition of the horses, and I attribute their present condition to the fact that early in the summer I instilled into every one the vital necessity of starting them to build up a reserve of energy which could be called upon now. Of course good shelter and a full ration have materially contributed, also good and systematic inspections and grooming. Quite a lot of H.E. and shrapnel came in this morning from 6.00 a.m. to 11 a.m. Some damage done and some casualties, but not to the Train; we have had great luck and I touch wood. Cold and dull.
>
> **13 January 1918.** — Morning opened fine and clear with frost, turning again into snow in the night. Visited No.4 Company again; all mange cases doing well and receiving every attention. Letter from Officer i/c P.s. [uncertain] re correspondence he had about a commission for Corporal Landsky. Major Greer, 1st Train, his S.S.O. and Capt: McDougall called to go over our lines to be ready to take over at a not distant date.
>
> **14 January 1918.** — Called on O.C. No.4 Company re Corporal Landsky, and went round Company's lines. Replied to O.C. i/c C.A.S.C. P. [uncertain], saying if Corporal Landsky applied through usual channels for a Commission, I would interview him and say whether I recommended him; up to the present have not been asked to. A most unfortunate accident occurred today, the Mayor was knocked down by a motor car and killed; from what I can gather it was a pure accident owing to the ice on the roads. S.S.Os of 1st and 3rd out looking things over. Had Sergeant Cooksey up before me re his being passed over for promotion, pointed out to him he had been tried out and found not to have the necessary weight behind him to warrant promotion. Mileage last week, 5,610.
>
> **15 January 1918.** — All the snow gone, a fine rain falling, a high wind and result, mud, mud, mud. Saw the convoys on the road and they looked very well. Sergeants had their New Year's [entry continues on Sheet No. 4.]

### Page 7 — 15–18 January 1918 (Sheet No. 4)

> **15 January 1918 (continued).** — dinner last night. They called themselves "The Ginks". Menu and Programme attached. 22 shells came in and little damage done.
>
> **16 January 1918.** — Went round with M.O. I inspected No.1 Company's lines; all in good shape, they escaped all shells yesterday. Capt: Hennessy called, talked about move, then A.S.C. things in general. Donaldson and Macavity [uncertain] called after lunch to take him back. While at lunch, a big bit of shell came plump into the wall of house next door, it must have come three hundred yards, and spent its force on the way, for it did no damage. Raining all day.
>
> **17 January 1918.** — Morning opened with a snow storm, later turning to rain. Heasley's leave to Canada is likely to come through, but he will be struck off the strength of the Train. Had a long talk with him and also with Capt: de Graves about re-posting of Officers to fill vacancy. I have almost made up my mind what is the best arrangement to make. I may take Major Scott from No.1 Company to act as S.S.O., thus giving him a chance on Supplies. Send Captain Oliphant to No.1 Company as O.C., and take Capt. de Graves back as Adjutant perhaps leaving Lieut: Hessey to run No.4 Company; he did it before for three months and very well he did it too. Working out the totals for mileage of Train for first half month of year, comes to 9.58 miles per wagon per day, the greatest distance travelled by any one wagon on any day was 18 miles, one of No.3 Company's.
>
> **18 January 1918.** — Notification that Heasley's leave to Canada had come through. Discussed with Adjutant re-postings. He does not wish to go to No.1 Company, so will remain as Adjt. Capt. Church will go to No.1 Company. Major Scott take up temporary duties as S.S.O. So many Officers are away or are due for leave, it is sure to leave Companies short, but this will test out the younger Officers. Heasley will not go until about the 26th and by that time Major Scott should be familiar with his duties. Capt: Phillips can relieve him and let him away on leave. Capt: Phillips and Lieut: Higgins go on leave tomorrow, the former to get married and the latter to act as his Best Man. Cold and wet and windy. No shells today.

### Page 8 — 19–24 January 1918 (Sheet No. 5)

> **19 January 1918.** — Much milder. Capt: Phillips and Lieut: Higgins gone on leave. Inspected No.2 Company to make sure of its condition, when Capt. Church left it for No.1 Company. Expressed my desire that if anything, Capt. Church on his return should find it if possible in still better condition; all expressed a hope that they may meet my desire. S.S.O. of 1st Division came in to see my S.S.O. Major Scott going round with Major Heasley getting put wise.
>
> **20 January 1918.** — Shelling again started at 7.30 a.m. Paymaster reports that up to date for Halifax Relief Fund the Train has subscribed 3,845 francs, this works about 9 Francs per man. Inspected No.1 Company. All sick horses doing very well. All others in good condition and well cared for. All lines, huts, etc. clean and tidy. Harness good, clean and fairly soft. Drew the attention of a/O.C. to certain points I wanted attention paid to re care of wagons. Inspected Transport of 9th Field Ambulance. Drew the Sergeants' attention to the wearing of horse rugs when weather was mild, lines in small details untidy. Explained how to get harness softer and in better condition. Horses all round in good workable condition. Told Sergeant to give Colonel Gunn the O.C. my compliments and let him know what I had said. Shelling all over before lunch, and no damage done. Distance for week ending today for Train is 9.52 miles per wagon per day.
>
> **21 January 1918.** — Mild and damp, later rain. Quashed sentence of Corporal from A.M.C. We had not had notification of his promotion prior to sentence. Because of dispute over billet at NOEUX-LES-MINES deposited money with Town Major and obtained receipt for Francs 104.
>
> **22 January 1918.** — Train moved to [uncertain — destination struck through in original], and after some trouble got billeted. Rain.
>
> **23 January 1918.** — Damp. S.S.O. out chasing R.Park wagon delivering rations to 8th Field Ambulance which had got astray.
>
> **24 January 1918.** — Paid surprise visit of inspection to 9th Field Ambulance and found all in a much improved condition. Horses with two exceptions in particularly good working condition. Some sun and fine.

### Page 9 — 25–29 January 1918 (Sheet No. 6)

> **25 January 1918.** — Some sun and fine. Major Heasley dined with General last night. He is out with Major Scott today for last time before he leaves tomorrow. We are all sorry to lose him. Sent letter to D.A.D.V.S. re hoof-plates, and also re two suspected cases of mange, and that I had isolated them and ordered the V.O. to be informed.
>
> **26 January 1918.** — Morning opened dull with fog, later cleared to bright. Major H.J. Heasley took his departure at 11.00 a.m. Captains de Graves and Richards going on leave went with him. Major Scott now takes over duties as S.S.O., and from what I can see is more than likely to make good. Paid a visit to No.1 Company and found everything in good shape. A.D.V.S. called but I missed him.
>
> **27 January 1918.** — Told off man from Ambulance for returning from leave to PARIS without seeing he had a return ticket. Captain McLeod of 1st Train called to see me, and later Colonel Webb of 47th Battalion with Major Robinson. We had a long and interesting talk on things in general pertaining to the Train.
>
> **28 January 1918.** — Distance travelled by Train Companies for week ending 27/1/18:— No.1 Company 10.8 miles per day, No.2, 9.1 miles, No.3, 10.4 miles, No.4, 8.1. Average per wagon for the Train of 9.62 miles, longest distance for any one wagon 20 miles. Rode into BARLIN and inspected No.2 Company. All in very good shape. Wagons clean, well repaired, neatly parked, a good deal of mud all round. A.D.V.S. arranged to go with me to Nos.3 and 4 Companies tomorrow on my inspection. Told him I strongly disapprove of hoof-plates, and think they will do more harm than good. He will not report on them until the end of February, and I sincerely hope his report will be an adverse one. Fine and a good deal of sun after misty morning.
>
> **29 January 1918.** — Light switched off three times last night while planes were over dropping bombs; have not heard of any damage done. Night was very clear and frosty. [Entry continues on Sheet No. 7.]

### Page 10 — 29–31 January 1918 (Sheet No. 7)

> **29 January 1918 (continued).** — Visited Nos. 3 and 4 Companies with A.D.V.S. and made inspection. Several cases of old mange in No.3 Company, and about 25 horses ordered to be clipped and dipped as a precautionary measure. In No.4 Company all clear, one case of Ophthalmia to be evacuated. Called to have interview with A.A.Q.M.G. at Corps re new establishment; he wanted my views before answering. Bright and cold.
>
> **30 January 1918.** — Going over notes and work for month. Nothing to relate today. Colder and crisp.
>
> **31 January 1918.** — Hard frost last night and slippery roads this morning. Rode over to D.H.Q. where I was informed Capt: McLeod from 1st Train was posted to me as S.S.O. Called on A.A.Q.M.G. to object, my contention being that as I am short on the establishment of one Major, my establishment should be filled and leave me to post him to No.1 Company or as S.S.O. whichever post I considered he would fill with best results to the Division. Next they will be posting Captains and Subalterns to my Companies and they are not in a position to say what postings would make the best combination. Again so that all my Officers can get experience in both transport and supplies, I am continually changing them and thus only can I get at their best qualities. The A.A.Q.M.G. rang up General Farmer and put these points before him to consider. Average daily ration strength for month:— Men 30,039, Horses 5,692.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] d'Auvergne Findlay, Lieut-Colonel, O.C. 3rd Can. Div. Train [the last words are partly cut off at the page edge]

## Open questions / uncertain items (January 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 24" but the contents page reads "Volume 18 — 1." — both as written.
- The contents page says "Diary Text 7 Pages", which matches Sheets 1–7 on pages 4–10; Appendix "A" is the 11th page.
- The contents page calls the appendix the "Program of annual dinner of the Ginks", while the appendix itself is headed "FIRST ANNUAL DINNER OF THE GINKS." — both as written.
- 12 January: the entry begins at the foot of Sheet No. 2 and continues at the head of Sheet No. 3 ("certain reserve rations" is split across the page break); both parts are transcribed as one entry.
- 15 January: the entry begins at the foot of Sheet No. 3 and continues at the head of Sheet No. 4; both parts are transcribed as one entry.
- 29 January: the entry begins at the foot of Sheet No. 6 and continues at the head of Sheet No. 7; both parts are transcribed as one entry.
- 22 January: the destination of the Train's move is struck through in the original and cannot be read with confidence; it is recorded as [uncertain]. On 28 January the O.C. "rode into BARLIN" to inspect No.2 Company, which implies the new location after the 22 January move was somewhere other than Barlin, near enough to ride in.
- 13 January: "Officer i/c P.s." [uncertain] — the abbreviation after "i/c" is not read with confidence.
- 14 January: "O.C. i/c C.A.S.C. P." [uncertain] — the final letter/abbreviation is not read with confidence.
- 16 January: the second caller is read as "Macavity" [uncertain] — the surname is not read with confidence.
- 28 January: the typed figure for No.1 Company reads "10.8 miles per day", but a handwritten "13.8" appears in the left margin beside the entry, possibly a correction; both are noted here as written.
- 4 January: Col. Booth, Chief of Staff of American Forces, was advised to inspect the unit on 12 January, and the 11 January entry refers to his visit, but no inspection by Booth is recorded in the 12 January entry itself.
- 10 and 17 January: "Mr. Hessey" / "Lieut: Hessey" ran No.4 Company for three months as acting O.C.; he is a different person from Major Heasley (S.S.O.), though the names are similar.
- 11 January: "L. Grane called late afternoon" — the initial is read as "L."; Capt. Grane is named on 12 January.
- The signature at the foot of Sheet No. 7 is read as "d'Auvergne Findlay", Lieut-Colonel, O.C. 3rd Can. Div. Train; the last words are partly cut off at the page edge. The appendix header gives his name as "W.A. d'AVERGNE FINDLAY, D.S.O." with a small superscript "N" above the line before "W.A." that is not explained.
- No entry is missing: every day 1–31 January 1918 has an entry. The Place column is blank on every diary sheet.
- Names read as: Lieut-Col. d'Auvergne Findlay (O.C.); Majors H.J. Heasley, Scott and Greer (1st Train); Captains Oliphant, de Graves, Phillips, Fisher, Church, Hennessy, McDougall, McLeod (1st Train), Grane and Richards; Lieuts. Hessey and Higgins; Corporal Landsky; Sergeant Cooksey; Col. Booth (Chief of Staff, American Forces); Col. Gunn (O.C., 9th Field Ambulance); Col. Webb (47th Battalion); Major Robinson (4th Train, also with Col. Webb on 27 January); General Farmer; Simson; Donaldson; L. Grane. Appendix "A" names: S.S.M. Proctor and Vicks; C.S.M. Bowden, Ridgeway and Slader; C.Q.M.S. Malthouse and Gumbley; S/Sgts. Nicholls, Gibson, Coutts, Henshaw, Burrell, O'Malley, McKenzie, Bullen, Crabtree, Cooksey, Haurault and Lynas; Sgts. Smith, Reardon, McCurdy, McMillan, Trottsch, Hutchins, McDonald and Blair; Capt. Moppatt, M.O.
