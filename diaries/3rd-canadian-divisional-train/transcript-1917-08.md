## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. A repeated handwritten initial mark ("J.F.") appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. Words struck through in the original are noted as [struck word] or [struck: word]. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only. The Place column of the diary sheets is blank throughout; no place is entered against any date. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "D.A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "G.S." = General Service; "R.F.C." = Royal Flying Corps; "F.P. No. 1" = Field Punishment No. 1; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "S.S.M." = Staff Sergeant-Major; "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column; "A.D.O.S." = Assistant Director of Ordnance Services; "M.T." = Mechanical Transport; "Rfg." = Refilling; "Bde" = Brigade; "Fld. Amb." = Field Ambulance; "P.B." = Permanent Base; "A.D.S." is as written and is not expanded. The diary sheets are on the lined form (footer "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S.").

## Pages 4–14 — Diary text (1–31 August 1917)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY".

### Page 4 — 1–4 August 1917 (Sheet No. 151)

> **1 August 1917.** — S.S.O. and Adjt. [struck word] went around Coys. Adjt. to speak to Capt. De Graves and ascertain if he will take over duties of Adjutant if and when Oliphant goes. Salvage Officer dined with us or came and saw me re same. Rained all night and most of the day.
>
> **2 August 1917.** — Conference with S.S.O., D.A.Q.M.G. and O.C. at 10th Fld. Amb. to settle uniform marking on ambulances, G.S. wagons, watercarts &c. Major Freeman finds his lines so wet and muddy he is anxious to move in to vacant horse standings at BARLIN and with it in [uncertain] can find accommodation for his men. Town Major agrees. Wet and nasty [uncertain] all day.
>
> **3 August 1917.** — Capt. & Lt. Graham ordered to report to R.F.C. Went out and tried Sergt. Reardon, [uncertain] by Capt. Graham. Arranged Lt. Fisher take command pro tem of Coy. when Graham goes tomorrow morning. Wet and raw all day.
>
> **4 August 1917.** — Day broke fine. The two Grahams left this morning to join R.F.C. Turned down Lt. Richards' application for special leave. S.S.O. and Adjt. [uncertain] to [uncertain]. Had another fever attack, dosed myself with quinine and turned in early. Mostly dull and rainy.

### Page 5 — 5–8 August 1917 (Sheet No. 152)

> **5 August 1917.** — No. 1 Coy moved to Barlin today where they will get better standings and protection for their horses. Saw the D.A.Q.M.G. and submitted my requirements if coal lorries are taken off and H.T. [uncertain] substituted. Sent Capt. C. P. McCallum a copy of his adverse report as he addresses the [uncertain] in England. Mostly fine.
>
> **6 August 1917.** — Day opened with heavy mist. S.S.O. and D.A.Q.M.G. gone to BRUAY to see if room can be found for D.A.C. coming to replace coal lorries. Began duty as Recording Officer for the Alberta soldier voters. 9th Fld. Amb. complaining of the inefficiency of S.S.M. Hutchins. Asked for adverse report in writing. I will then make inspection and take action.
>
> **7 August 1917.** — Out all day on election duty, but managed to reach three Coys. and look round. Back again in the evening to take other votes. Fine.
>
> **8 August 1917.** — Visited No. 4 Coy. Finished the taking of their and the Salvage Coy's votes. Inspected No. 9 Fld. Amb. and found their horses and harness going back. Decided to replace their S.S.M. who has not made good. Arranged to deliver lecture here on Friday at 1.30. A.D.V.S. will accompany me. Afternoon with a deluge with heavy thunder and lightning.

### Page 6 — 9–11 August 1917 (Sheet No. 153)

> **9 August 1917.** — 2nd Section of 4th Reserve Park left this morning. Sorry to lose Lt. Bell of this Section, he has done admirable work while attached here. Last night went personally and tried to find N.C.O. i/c 10 [uncertain] wagons attached for duty failed, but gave written instructions to stable picket to inform his N.C.O. he had to turn out his wagons [uncertain] at 8 a.m. and continue to do until further orders. Capt. B. B. Bishop reported for duty at 3.30 today. Told him what I expected of him and if after a reasonable time he did not make good I would take steps to have him removed. Lt. Keyes came with a complaint but told him I could not listen to it unless he was paraded [uncertain] in the ordinary way. Capt. Hennessy came and had lunch. Rained afternoon.
>
> **10 August 1917.** — Major Morrison called in passing. Lunched with 9th Fld. Amb. then delivered lecture to the [uncertain] on duties, care of horses, harness. Called Sergt. Smith up and told him he would take over duties of S.S.M. and in a month's time call for report. Impressed on him the necessity of being absolutely firm and of [uncertain] up hard and quick for faults big or small and not to be familiar with juniors.
>
> **11 August 1917.** — Saw A.A. & Q.M.G. and requested him to see that any request coming in for wagons reach me as early as possible and not as has been lately at 9 p.m. Also [continued on next page]

### Page 7 — 11–13 August 1917 (Sheet No. 154)

> **11 August 1917 (continued).** — that wagons are not to be requested to do an abnormal amount of work, they should all be back by 3 p.m. if horses, harness and wagons are to be kept in condition. I saw him make a note. He said he had seen the Adjt. last night on this subject. Had S.S.M. Hutchins up in front of me and pointed out to him that he had badly fallen down and gave him choice of applying for reversion to ranks or being paraded for purpose of reversion. Sun and rain alternately all day.
>
> **12 August 1917.** — Visited Bruay where I tried case of 329 Corpl. Bell and severely reprimanded him. Inspected Train horses &c. (No. 4 Coy) work telling on horses. Also labour [uncertain] attached, their harness dirty and hard. Ordered O.C. No. 4 Coy to procure dubbing and explained to Sergt. in charge method of using same. He said he had orders [uncertain] nothing else but saddle soap and I replied so long as he was under me he would use what I told him to. Just inspected No. 1 Coy. Horses of this Coy best in the Train. Why? because they have a normal haul against abnormal for other Coys. and our head shelter while other Coys have none. Mostly fine.
>
> **13 August 1917.** — Inspected horses of No. 3 Coy. Horses showing signs of hard work and straining [uncertain] especially the old horses. They are busy cleaning out ditches and excavating [uncertain] [continued on next page]

### Page 8 — 13–15 August 1917 (Sheet No. 155)

> **13 August 1917 (continued).** — or slag standings. Some 30 loads already laid down. Making preliminary arrangements for move of 8th and 9th Bdes with Train Coys. Fine generally.
>
> **14 August 1917.** — Lt. Fisher came in about move of his Bde. Paraded S.S.M. Hutchins before G.O.C. who is recommending to the Army [struck: that] his reduction to Sergt. Adjt. & S.S.O. out arranging about move. Sale of mobiled unfit horses at Barlin, prices extraordinary high, quite 50% over value. Fine until evening, then rain and thunder storm.
>
> **15 August 1917.** — No. 3 and 4 Coys. changed camps because of manoeuvres. Called on D.A.D.V.S., talked over condition of Train horses, he expressed himself as well satisfied he is more satisfied than I am. I hope I am not expecting too much. Inspected the labour attached here, animals in good condition wagons very fair considering they have no leaders to clean them. Harness very hard, they use too much soap and water, and no dubbing. Have ordered the latter to be used while they are attached to us, explained to N.C.O. in charge how it was to be used. Instructed him to go to O.C. No. 1 Coy. to help him out in anything he may find he has difficulty with. Rain again.

### Page 9 — 16–19 August 1917 (Sheet No. 156)

> **16 August 1917.** — Got neuralgia at back of head, making me feel rotten. S.O. 8th Bde came to lunch. Great discussion with S.S.O. re a surplus of tea not reported. Fine.
>
> **17 August 1917.** — Visited Railhead, all the refilling points and inspected closer [uncertain] everything going smoothly. Casey of 7th M. Coy. dined, he and Paymaster got into an argument and caused great amusement. Very fine.
>
> **18 August 1917.** — Major Morrison, Capts. Hooper and Aldrich called and asked me to advise them what was right to do under circumstances stated. Lt. Fisher called to ask me to try case this afternoon. Confirmation of the reduction of S.S.M. Hutchins came in, will post him to No. 3 Coy and send Sergt. McHouse [uncertain] to 9th Fld. Amb. Mid day wire for move of 8th Bde to line just came in, which means a rush to arrange transport and supplies as Pack Train is late. Saw Sgt. Hutchins and told him of his reduction and posting to No. 3 Coy. Tried three cases at No. 3 Coy, dismissed two and gave one 28 days F.P. No. 1. Lt. Petrie came to tea. Wet late afternoon. Late at night movement order for 7th Bde came in.
>
> **19 August 1917.** — Went with S.S.O. out to No. 2 Coy to see what arrangements he had made for move, found all satisfactory. New M.O. Dr Moffat reported for duty. Fine.

### Page 10 — 20–22 August 1917 (Sheet No. 157)

> **20 August 1917.** — S.S.O. gone to 1st Div. to arrange supply details of move and taking over. Visited all new refilling points with S.S.O. Just heard Div. H.Qrs are not moving out and have decided to keep Train Hd. Quarters here (BARLIN) for the present. Col. Massie, O.C. 2nd Train called at tea time. Very fine and hot.
>
> **21 August 1917.** — Visited dumps again, all fine. S.S.O. 1st. called in. The Raymond [uncertain] Corps Commander's request has been sent home with object of taking up clerical work. Age 60. Fine.
>
> **22 August 1917.** — Visited Railhead, found pack train had not been increased in spite of fact S.S.O. had given figures to A.D.O.S. If he knew so much better than S.S.O.? Visited small attached units in forward area and S.S.O. arranged for their feeding. O.C. 6th Div. Train called re taking over one of our Coy lines for his [uncertain] Coy. but told him it could not be done, and he would have to go into the open as we have had to do so often. Col. Massie, O.C. 2nd C.D.T. called about his [uncertain] Coy and wanted me to take them over with all their wagons but nothing doing, he refills from our pack train 11.15 a.m. and I have only to see things arranged so that he can do it. Hot and fine.

### Page 11 — 23–24 August 1917 (Sheet No. 158)

> **23 August 1917.** — Lt. Lawrence reported for duty in afternoon. Col. Massie called again and is kindly holding us out to haul, until our railhead changes 26th or 27th. Capt. De Graves reported back from leave. Advised coal in at Railhead must be cleared at once. After great difficulty got M.T. for this purpose, coolly informed coal not for us. Windy and dusty, evening shower, otherwise fine.
>
> **24 August 1917.** — Went with A.D.V.S. & V.O. to railhead and inspected Train horses. With exception of 5 or 6 old ones, all are hard in good condition and well looked after, wagons and harness in good shape. At 8 a.m. this morning notified coal was for use to all. On Pack Train no petrol or oil of any kind came up. Hay 900 lbs short, oats 8000 lbs short, 75 tons of coal, coke and charcoal to clear, on top of wagons having to return from railhead empty and draw from BARLIN. Is it any wonder I am anxious about condition of my horses when extra and unnecessary work is put upon them because someone blunders and does not know his job. Meeting 2.30 Corps H.Q. today. Discussed replacements by P.B. men, as far as Train is concerned they must be able bodied, able to lift weights and march, for loaders. For drivers they must know something about care of horses, hear and see well, although [continued on next page]

### Page 12 — 24–27 August 1917 (Sheet No. 159)

> **24 August 1917 (continued).** — not able to march far. S.S.O. and Adjt. to SAINS-BOUVIGNY to see new Railhead and Rfg. Points. Fine but dusty.
>
> **25 August 1917.** — Inspected lines to be taken over by Train Coys and made inspection of No. 1 Coy. 2, 3 & 4 Coys moved in to here (BARLIN) and went around with V.O. inspecting all horses. Lines entered by No. 3 Coy. left in a filthy condition, filth, tin cans, wire, old stumps, sacks and straw all over the place. Inspected new Railhead at SAINS-BOUVIGNY, and a poor place it is, tramways and dumps are well constructed, but it is evident no A.S.C. Officer was consulted, otherwise planning would be different. Fine.
>
> **26 August 1917.** — Called in at Divn and had talk with A.A. & Q.M.G. Called with S.S.O. on A.D.S. re his scheme for posting in trains by Senior N.C.O. Officers raised strong objection to this and suggested a Senior Officer to do this work and take charge of all postings, he not to be attached but independent of any Train. Left 6 postings to be done by him too including N.C.O.'s.
>
> **27 August 1917.** — Adjt. and I to railhead but no train in and no word of it. Section and a half of 6th Reserve Park reports to me today for duty. Note. Col. Anderson Cdn. Corps phoned to congratulate the Adjt. 3rd Canadian Div. Train on his return to [continued on next page]

### Page 13 — 27–30 August 1917 (Sheet No. 160)

> **27 August 1917 (continued).** — G.S. wagons surplus on reorganization [uncertain] Artz. "This return is exactly what is required and very satisfactory." This most gratifying to Oliphant and self. Held meeting of O.C.'s re award of dumb bell. Formulated a plan which I put before A.A. & Q.M.G. to submit to General for his approval. Wind and wet.
>
> **28 August 1917.** — Train in early. Arranged to meet A.D.V.S. to go round horses tomorrow. Windy and wet.
>
> **29 August 1917.** — 1½ Sections of 6th Reserve Park reported for duty. 76 wagons. Inspected horses with A.D.V.S. who reports horses are well looked after and well cared for. Bar old horses all in good condition. Was rather complimentary. Showed him 4 of yesterday's [uncertain] remnants which were very nearly as bad as the ones cast, one of the four is now cast. I hope the Remount Department are proud of themselves, personally I am disgusted and it takes one all one's time from getting disheartened. Saw G.O.C. at Div. Hd. Qrs. told him result of A.D.V.S.'s inspection. He told me he approved of my suggestion re dumb bell and to submit names. Wet cold and raw.
>
> **30 August 1917.** — Saw D.A.Q.M.G. re application from 1st Line Transport to have Train deliver from dump, and satisfied him they had not made out a case. Rich. [continued on next page]

### Page 14 — 30–31 August 1917 (Sheet No. 161)

> **30 August 1917 (continued).** — Communication from 9th Bde re overloading. Rain & shine alternating.
>
> **31 August 1917.** — Forwarded to O.C. No. 4 Coy. correspondence re overloading for remarks of O.C. before replying. Figuring out move. S.S.O. of 1st called to see my S.S.O. re clover. Pea straw is to be an issue to augment hay, but it is stated that it should be chaffed. Very good but are chaff cutters an issue, and if they are where is the extra transport for hauling same provided for, this sure is a grand game, and played slowly may have advantages I cannot see. When I read the complete history of this war perhaps I shall see clearly and understand many things I fail to do now. Replied to letter 2208/H.Q. S-5-24 re complaint of acting O.C. No. 4 Coy. re transport of 58th Battn re march from RAIMBERT to MAISNIL-BOUCHE 28th Aug.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *[signature — appears to read "James Findlay" [uncertain] as to first name] Lieut-Colonel, / O.C. 3rd Canadian Divl. Train*
