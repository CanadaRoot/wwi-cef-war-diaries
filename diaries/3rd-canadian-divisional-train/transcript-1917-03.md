## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The March sheets are on the lined form (footer "T.124. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."). A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "F.P.I" = Field Punishment No. 1; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "H.Q." = Headquarters; "C.A.M.C." = Canadian Army Medical Corps; "A.G." = Adjutant General; "D.D.S. & T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "O.S.C." = Officer in charge of Supplies (as used here); "M.T." = Mechanical Transport; "C.C." = Confined to Camp (as used here). The diary's own heading on the diary sheets is the unit name printed at the top left.

## Pages 3–8 — Diary text (1–31 March 1917)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY".

### Page 3 — 1–6 March 1917 (Sheet No. 108)

> **March 1.** — Called in at Divl. Hd. Qrs. and had general talk. Asked to parade O.C., Hd. Qrs. Coy. for giving 7 days C.C. for offences which should have gone to a F.C.M. Cols. Simson, Massey, Webb and self met and discussed promotions and the officering of any further Trains coming to France. On the basis of discussion Col. Simson was to draw up a report and submit a copy to each of us, to correct and amend before a further meeting was called. Pack train not in at 10.00 p.m. Dull but dry.
>
> **March 2.** — Pack train a day late, offloaded at 7.00 a.m. today. Paraded O.C., Hd. Qrs. Coy before G.O.C. Have to submit a report in a months time of any improvement or otherwise. Completed Diary for last month. Pay Office paid up O.R. Submitted recommendations for Birthday Honours.
>
> **March 3.** — Yesterday's train only offloaded this morning. O.C., Supply Column called and wanted more M.T. turned out. Showed him the utter impossibility of his request. Lieut Ferg reported for duty from Reserve Park. Went with D.A.Q.M.G. to fix parade ground for inspection of Transport by Army Commander. Cold but fine.
>
> **March 4.** — Called at Division. Went with Adjt. to view proposed Parade Ground. Drawing up order of Parade. Original scheme had to be modified. Dry but cold.
>
> **March 5.** — Fall of snow. Called into Div. Hd. qrs. to see G.O.C. who has altered parade from field to road. Drew up new scheme. Col. Webb and his S.S.O. called to make arrangements about feeding when move is on. Adjt. and I walked over parade road and found there would be room enough.
>
> **March 6.** — Inspected lines etc. of Hd. qrs. Coy at PERFEY. Saw nothing to boast about. I have purposely left the O.C. a free hand and all alone for about a month to see if he would make an improvement in his Coy., but I cannot see it yet. Staked out resting points on the BRUAY-DIVION Road, where head of convoy of Units would rest. General Sir H. Horne inspected the Transport and expressed himself as highly pleased with the parade, and said must have worked hard to get such results. Major Prince Arthur of Connaught was there. Transport

### Page 4 — 6–11 March 1917 (Sheet No. 109)

> **March 6 (continued).** — assembled in following order, 3 Engineer technical wagons, 2 Signal, space of 20 ft. then 9th Bde Transport, composed of R.C.R., P.P.C.L.I., 42nd., 49th., 7th Bgde Machine Gun Coy, Space 20 ft., 8th Bde, 1st., 2nd., 4th., 5th., C.M.Rs., Space 20 ft. 9th Bgde. 43rd., 52nd., 58th., and 60th., 9th Bgde Machine Gun Coy. Space 20 ft. Pioneers, Divl. Train, Field Ambulances, taking up a space of close on 1000 yds. Some sun, cold but fine.
>
> **March 7.** — Adjt. gone to Barlin to look over 1st Train Billets prior to us taking over. S.S.O. gone to meet S.S.Os of 8th and 1st. to make final arrangements for taking over attached units. Re-arranging N.C.O.s of No 1, 2, and 3 Coys. Bitterly cold wind and frosty.
>
> **March 8.** — Quite a fall of snow. Sun came out and morning clear. No 1 Coy moved today from FERFAY and No 2 Coy. from BRUAY to BARLIN. Sun and snow alternately. Very cold.
>
> **March 9.** — O.C., No 1 Coy sent in complaint about the way the Artillery give location of their batteries and when the convoy with their rations arrive there, they find they have gone somewhere else. This happened last night and three hours were wasted locating them. This is alright if they are moving their battery positions for tactical reasons, but where it is bad arrangements and want of forethought on their part, my horses and men should not be called upon to suffer It is taking all my time and every effort, now that the M.T. is removed to cope with circumstances and keep my horses in good workable condition. I can now see why the Artillery horses are in such a shocking poor shape.
>
> **March 10.** — On F.G.C.M. all morning. Detailed 2 wagons for baths, with instructions to keep a register of distances travelled, time occupied, nature of duties. If these are excessive and the horses are overworked, they will be withdrawn. Damp and raw.
>
> **March 11.** — Lt. Col Simson's suggestions came for consideration and it will have it. Called at No 1 Coy and tried Sergt. McDonnell for neglect to comply with an order. Severely reprimanded him and warned him not to come up before me again. Inspected No 1 and 2 Coys horses, harness and lines. No 2 making considerable improvement. ### Page 5 — 11–15 March 1917 (Sheet No. 110)

> **March 11 (continued).** — attention before being of much use, they all show signs of neglect. Dull but hardly any rain.
>
> **March 12.** — Today's Supply train in at 1.30. There might be a chance now of getting it in on time, and not, as has been so often, a day late. Tried a man from Salvage Coy (F.P. C.C. I.) for four days absence without leave in England. Gave him 28 days F.P. No 1. and wish I could have given him more. Q.M. of 123rd. Battn. came in, 905, roughly 1000, with no transport and no dixies, and of course called on the Train to supply it. They were ordered to come with 5 tons officers stores, baggage etc. and this without transport. Called at Divl. Hd. Qrs. and requested A.A. & Q.M.G. apply for half or quarter section of a Reserve Park to help me out if I am to keep transport in good shape. Muggy and some rain.
>
> **March 13.** — Pack train in a little earlier, but not in position to unload until 1.30. Inspected Train Transport at Railhead and some teams show signs of overwork and are getting thin. Harness and condition of wagons, generally very good, are clean and well cared for, and in view of the late and long hauls recently, this condition reflects great credit on those responsible. Col. Webb called to ask if, when the Lahore were attached to us, they got extra hay and oats. Of course I said certainly not, why should they? Fine and mild, but no sun.
>
> **March 14.** — Find the greatest difficulty in getting vegetables of any kind, and when found prices are prohibitive. The Salvage Officer, Lieut Breckenridge, gone on leave to day and Lieut. Higgins relieves him during his absence. He got car to Bethune with strict orders to send it back at once and at 1.30 it had not reported. Called in at Divl. Hd. Qrs. Put name down for leave on 24th. inst. Lieut Breckenridge took car on without authority and there is likely to be trouble over it. Mild and wet.
>
> **March 15.** — Headquarters of Train moved this morning from BRUAY to BARLIN. When No 3 moves tomorrow, all the Train Companies will be in. Tried case at No 4., Disobeying an order given by an N.C.O. and considered it would be waste of time to refer case for a F.G.C.M. and gave 28 days F.P. No 1. Fine but cold. Capt. Taylor, V.O., reported for duty.

### Page 6 — 16–20 March 1917 (Sheet No. 111)

> **March 16.** — Interviewed V.O. and gave him to understand what I expected of him. A.D.V.S. called and he and Adjt. gone to inspect 5 horses turned over to us from Artillery. I have doubts if they answer the description of them. M.O. reports that there too many cases of diarrhoea and has called in Sanitary Officer. Lieut Day reported for instruction, pending posting. Inspected No 4 Coy., room for improvement. Fine and cold.
>
> **March 17.** — Morning broke very bright and clear. Inspected No 3 Coy and found it in very good shape, also went round the other three Coys., and when at No 1., inspected three Train wagons from the Artillery and their state was beyond description. Reported news of Russian trouble come to hand and causing much speculation and food for consideration and debate. Lieut Petrie called in and saw me. I had not seen him since he got his commission. He looks very well and is most anxious to get back to this Train. Next move will be by half Battns. with intervals of three days between moves, this sure is going to be a difficult matter to arrange feeding and transport, but why worry we can do harder jobs than that. Beautifully fine day.
>
> **March 18.** — Divl. Hd. Qrs. moved today from BRUAY to VILLERS-AU-BOIS, On way D.A.Q.M.G. called in here. Fine but cold.
>
> **March 19.** — Interviewed three men from No 2 Coy. applying for their commissions. Turned all three down as unsuitable, with so many better men could not recommend them. Two asked if they might be transferred as privates. Train reported in very early. Called in at Divl. Hd. Qrs. with S.S.O. and Adjt. Interviewed candidate from No 4 Coy. for commission and turned him down. Too old and not sufficiently well educated. Completed history of 3rd Canadian Divisional Train. as requested and sent it off. Cold and wet.
>
> **March 20.** — Working out on paper, re-organisation of Transport. Adjt. explained same to representatives from No 1 Coy. Interviewed candidate for commission from No 4 Coy. Clean young fellow quiet but energetic and well educated. Instructed his O.C. to parade him before his own Brigadier. After all feeding and transport arranged for 7th. Bgde., move postponed. At 4.00 p.m. it came on to snow, later turned to rain.

### Page 7 — 21–27 March 1917 (Sheet No. 112)

> **March 21.** — Pack Train at BARLIN arrived late and Coys. got back to lines at 5.00 a.m. S.S.O. and self at conference at Divl. Hd. qrs. Told General was going to put Major Beasley in command of Train during my leave of absence. Said alright. Some rain and sleet, cold, snow later.
>
> **March 22.** — Quite a fall of snow in early morning, about 1½". Cold and raw.
>
> **March 23.** — Meeting of Staff Captains, Quartermasters and Transport Officers addressed by A.A. & Q.M.G. D.A. & Q.M.G., S.S.O. and myself, on assumption there will be a forward move. In evening meeting of Train Officers for same purpose. Told Major Freeman S.S.O. would be acting O.C., Train during my absence.
>
> **March 24.** — Col. Findlay proceeded to England on 10 days leave. Major Beasley acting as O.C. Train, in addition duties as S.S.O. Weather bright but cold. Supply train late.
>
> **March 25.** — Dull day but warmer. No 1 Coy, 63rd. R. N. Division with Artillery to be attached to this Division. Billets arranged in Barlin for the Train Coy. Supply Train late again, nearly fourteen hours, but cleared same tonight, holding loaded wagons on the lines of 7th and 8th Brigades to save horses.
>
> **March 26.** — Raining. Supply train late again, as usual. 63rd Divisional artillery arrived in this area this a.m. No 1 Coy. of 63rd Div. Train came into BARLIN and are situated at CITE LIAUTEY. A Section (11 lorries) of their Supply Column has accompanied them & have located a refilling point for them on the FRESNICOURT — GRAND SERVINS road. Drew 4,000 rations of Preserved Meat and Biscuits for supporting points to-day.
>
> **March 27.** — Raining and snowing. S.S. Major Waters transferred to England, for Infantry Commission. Supply Train failed to report arrive. Wonderful Railway system we have these days. 2nd Division ordered No 1 Coy. 63rd Divl. Train to remove dump from present position as being in their area, but advised "Q" office, who wired late to-night that 2nd Division were agreeable to allow same to remain.

### Page 8 — 28–31 March 1917 (Sheet No. 113)

> **March 28.** — Fine Bright Morning. Yesterday's Supply Train turned up early this morning. Possibility of to-day's train also arriving to-day. Supply Train actually came in about 10 a.m., so made arrangement to have same cleared by M.T. D.A.Q.M.G. came up to inspect pack saddlery and water carriers we have prepared and expressed his approval of same. Captain C. A. Graves, C.A.M.C. reported for duty and Major Myblett [uncertain] received orders to report to A.G. Canadians, London, for duty at Kitchener Hospital, Brighton.
>
> **March 29.** — Miserable day, raining hard all day long. Pack Train late again. Operation orders came in moving the 9th Brigade back to the line relieving the 7th & 8th, though the two latter Brigades are remaining in the forward area. Arranged change of refilling point as we will no longer have any cause to use BRUAY, only one Battalion and some details remaining there. No 2 Section, 4th Reserve Park came in this afternoon. Horses look pretty well used up and will endeavour to rest them up for a day or two. Received word at 8.p.m. that Pack Train would not be unloaded until morning.
>
> **March 30.** — Raining again at intervals during the day, roads are in a fearful condition. Yesterday's Train unloaded at 7.a.m. and to-days came in about noon, causing great difficulty with Transport, which was overcome to some extent by double hauling with available M.T. O.C. No 2 Coy. paraded A/Lce Corpl. Lawrence for inefficiency and he was ordered to be reduced to ranks.
>
> **March 31.** — Bright day, though dull at times with a rain and hail storms towards 5.p.m. Attended meeting of O.Cs Trains and Transport Officers at BARLIN station to see a demonstration of Pack Saddlery, but after waiting an hour received word that D.D.S. & T. could not attend and meeting was postponed until April 2nd. Nothing out of the ordinary happening and our work progressing favourably. Two N.C.Os — Staff Sgt Wheeler A. J. Smith and A/C. Q. M. Sgt. J. G. Landsky — were paraded on charge of "Drunkenness" and both remanded for F.G.C.M. Received orders from Divisional H.Q. to turn over 30 wagons of the Reserve Park — without teams or drivers to the D.A.C. for three or four days.
>

## Open questions / uncertain items (March 1917)
- The cover reads "Volume 14" and the contents page reads "Volume 17 — 3." — both as written.
- The contents page says "Diary Text 6 Pages" and the diary text occupies pages 3–8 (6 sheets, Sheets 108–113); the count is consistent this month.
- 6 March and 11 March each appear twice, once at the foot of one sheet and once at the head of the next, with the entry continuing across the break; both are transcribed as written.
- 6 March: General Sir H. Horne inspected the Transport; Major Prince Arthur of Connaught was present. The parade of the whole Division's transport took up close on 1,000 yards.
- 19 March: the O.C. completed a history of the 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, as requested, and sent it off.
- 24 March: Col. Findlay proceeds to England on 10 days' leave; Major Beasley acts as O.C. Train (in addition to his S.S.O. duties). The entries from 24 March onward are in Beasley's voice; the 23 March entry says Major Freeman S.S.O. would be acting O.C., but the 24 March entry says Major Beasley is acting as O.C. Train — both as written.
