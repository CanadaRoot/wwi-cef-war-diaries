## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The May sheets are on the lined form (footer "T.124. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."). A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "G.C.M." = General Court Martial; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "F.P.I" = Field Punishment No. 1; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "M.T." = Mechanical Transport; "D.D. of S. & T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "A.P.M." = Assistant Provost Marshal; "R.C.R." = Royal Canadian Regiment; "R.G.A." = Royal Garrison Artillery; "A.D.O.S." = Assistant Director of Ordnance Services. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets is the unit name printed at the top left.

## Pages 4–10 — Diary text (1–31 May 1917)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY".

### Page 4 — 1–6 May 1917 (Sheet No. 119)

> **May 1.** — Headquarters of the Train moved up to La Targette this morning.
>
> **May 2.** — After waiting nearly two hours the F.G.C.M. was postponed until tomorrow. Beasley, Hennessy, Grant and self dodging shells on Vimy Ridge. Three close calls. They plugged about thirty-five in on us during the night. Many duds and no casualties. Some luck! Came round 3 & 4 times. G.C.M. postponed until tomorrow. G.C.M. now on here. Heavy Artillery fire all night. Fine.
>
> **May 3.** — Self and Capt. Hennessy at G.C.M. Heard case of attached man Corpl refusing to obey any order, remanded him for F.G.C.M. Paymaster back to Headquarters Mess. Office now in a Nissen Hut. Train got narrow squeak of being shelled to pieces on way to Railhead. Grant had close call from bomb. Fine.
>
> **May 4.** — Visited Railhead. Inspected No. 3's Convoy before it moved off. Inspected all Cookhouses and inspected meals being prepared and went into future meals and variation. No 3 and 2 Coy's especially good. Refilling at Railhead advanced from 11 to 9 a.m. the change will be welcomed. Invited O.C. No 1 Coy, 1st. C. D. T. to dine tonight. Fine.
>
> **May 5.** — With Brigade Transport Officer of 7th. Bde. inspected 1st. Line Transport, considering the hard winter trials the horses are in very creditable form. Shower of rain towards afternoon, thunder and cold wind.
>
> **May 6.** — Promulgated sentence of Pte. Doyle for perjury, 60 days. Windy and cold. Supply train late again. Convoy had to turn back from Railhead.

### Page 5 — 7–10 May 1917 (Sheet No. 120)

> **May 7.** — Supply train late again and had to turn back after nearly reaching the Railhead and this immediately after Traffic wired complaining of delay in the unloading. I will not turn out my Convoy five minutes after horses have started feeding, let the train come in on time and I can regulate hours of stables. Last night German aeroplane came over these lines 150 yards N. N. W. of La Targette after all our planes had gone home by 8.30 p.m. and tried to set fire to one of our balloons. He missed his aim three times with his flares, on leaving he turned on his machine gun on objects on the ground but as far as I can ascertain did no damage. The same thing happened on the night of the 3rd when the plane dropped bombs and did damage, killing two men, wounding several others and killed three horses. If the Germans can fly in the dusk and by moonlight so can we I ask therefore why dont they do it. Cold.
>
> **May 8.** — No notice of pack train being late and convoy had to turn back. Called at Div. H.Q'rs nothing to relate. Cold but fine.
>
> **May 9.** — Convoy had to wait at Railhead for arrival of Pack Train. D.A.Q.M.G. called here. Rained all night but cleared towards 4 o'clock. Heard evidence of case from Salvage Company and remanded him for F.G.C.M. Our Artillery lively during early part of night.
>
> **May 10.** — Adjt. taking summary of evidence of case from Salvage Coy. As Pack Train came in on time Football and Baseball games were able to be played and I am anxious to encourage games of this nature to keep the men fit and contented and all Officers understand they are to encourage games so long as they do not clash in any way with any duty. They are all keeping their teams & etc. etc., in such splendid condition, working willingly week in and week out with no rest. When Brigades are out at rest they must be fed just the same and the allotment of honours for the Train is very meagre that they deserve every consideration. Capt. MacCullum reported back from his leave in Paris. Sunny and fine all day.

### Page 6 — 11–14 May 1917 (Sheet No. 121)

> **May 11.** — Col. St. John Parker D.D. of S. T. called and made arrangements for me to clear one train of straw at a date to be arranged later. Fine and warm.
>
> **May 12.** — About 9 a.m. the Germans threw in some 9 or 10 shells just over the Camp, one got within 20 yards of a balloon at anchor and one close to an ammunition dump. As usual their range was some 200 yards too long for any damage to the Train. No. 1 & 3 Convoys went out in very good shape this morning. Wire came for Capt. Hennessy to report as Staff Captain to the 5th. Brigade. Glad of his advancement but very sorry to loose him. He has been a splendid Adjutant and done most excellent work and was with me when I took over Command on 28 January 1916, have never once had occasion to find fault with him. Capt. J. Oliphant will take over his duties and Captain Church will be posted as O.C. No. 2 Company, he has done his turn as Supply Officer. After consultation with the S.S.O. will see who to post as Supply Officer in Church's place probably Lt. Higgins. Capt. Hennessy left to take on his new duties this afternoon and Capt. Oliphant took on his new duties as Adjutant, Capt. Church replacing him as O.C. No. 2 Coy and Lieut. Higgins replacing Capt. Church as S.O. Called in at Divisional H.Qrs. Fine all day.
>
> **May 13.** — At 2.30 a.m. one of the German long range guns, "Louise" as the boys have christened her dropped in about half an hour in the vicinity of NEUVILLE ST VAAST some twelve shells shot at varying intervals from 2 minutes to 10. Had rehearsal of Parade for the General's inspection for tomorrow and if they do as well then as they did today I will have no reason to be ashamed of them. Colonel Simson, O.C. 1st C.D.T. phoned me up and asked me to go and judge horses on Monday at 11.30 a.m. Train had a good football game in the evening against the R.G.A. beating them by 2 to nil. Hot and fine.
>
> **May 14.** — Pack train late just the very day one wishes it to be on time. "Louise" started at 7 a.m. to pay her compliments but, dear soul, she is doing no harm. G.O.C. Division inspected Train in afternoon spending two hours at it. He presented Meritorious Medals to S.S.M. Proctor and S.S. Wade. For the March Past the R.C.R. kindly lent their fife band. The G.O.C. expressed much satisfaction at all he saw and said many complimentary things. Fine all day.

### Page 7 — 15–17 May 1917 (Sheet No. 122)

> **May 15.** — Louise still reminding us of her presence. Owing to break down in car was unable to go and judge 1st. Brigade Line Transport at HERSIN. Col. Webb (one of the judges, other judges) called in evening and informed me of result of competition adding he had told Gen. Greenback [uncertain] they had a long way to go to beat the Transport of the 3rd and that the 4th. were quite out of it, I know the 2nd. are a long way out. G.O.C. asked me to dine this evening. G.O.C. referred case of Salvage L. Corpl to me to deal with instead of F.G.C.M. and I dealt with him by reverting him to the ranks. While at BARLIN today inspected Section 2 of 4th. Reserve Park, attached, and found condition of horses much improved and they were busy repairing their wagons. About 4.30. "Louise" came in three times [uncertain — "three times" or "three times"] splattering the nut with mud but no damage done. Tried Driver Flannigan of A.F.A. Bde. neglect to obey an order given by a superior. 14 days F.P. No. 1. and Private Willis, loader, from No. 3 Coy, filthy language to S.M. said the language referred to himself and the man he used it too, backed him up. He named his own standard thereby and although dismissing the case ordered him back to his unit. I have no use for such a man in the Train. Fine and cool.
>
> **May 16.** — Dined with G.O.C. last night and had most enjoyable and instructive conversation. He had several complimentary things to say about the 3rd. Train, all of which is most gratifying. One charger and three riders came up today, none of them are worth boasting about. Horribly wet and cold.
>
> **May 17.** — Allotted the riders this morning. No 1 Coy. ran in two men from Pioneers trying to break into Canteen, ordered them to be charged and marched back under escort to their Battn. V.O. gone to [uncertain] BARLIN to see horses of 2nd. Reserve Park attached. Last night we off loaded in pouring rain, train of 100 tons of straw, in 1 hour 20 mins. Lieut. Keyes and Laing of 3rd. Pioneers called to see me and try to get transferred to the Train, they neither of them want to go back to England, after being so long here. The breaking up of this good unit has created much soreness in many quarters and much sympathy is felt for them. V.O. reported on his return from BARLIN suspected mange in Reserve Park. Rain early cleared later and cool. Train playing Flying Corps football, and when I left five minutes ago the Train were 5 love.

### Page 8 — 18–23 May 1917 (Sheet No. 123)

> **May 18.** — "Louise" pretty lively and close early part of last night. Spring gone back as batman to Staff Captain Hennessy pending confirmation. BARLIN with D.A.Q.M.G. and V.O. to show former transport of 2nd Reserve Park. Doctor Graves left this afternoon and Doctor Pratt replaced him. Fine.
>
> **May 19.** — "Louise" dropped 52 shells into or close to NEUVILLE ST. VAAST last night and only one dud. Inspected all horses at watering parade this morning and all show marked improvement. Tried case of Pte. South. Forfeits 28 days pay.
>
> **May 20.** — Quiet day. Nothing doing, sunny and fine.
>
> **May 21.** — Heard complaint by A.P.M. on conduct of Lieut. Graham to Provost men. A.P.M's men could not adhere to the same statement or yet to the one put in writing and any other charge except watering with the bit in horses' mouth. I felt bound to dismiss but reprimanded him for this. Provost men must be upheld in the exercise of their duty. 2nd Train played us footer in evening, hard fast game, we scored the only goal. Fine but dull, rain during night.
>
> **May 22.** — Morning opened with downpour of rain and in consequence will not go to judge transport of 2nd Infantry Brigade. Our Pack Train given to the 5th Imperials and we had to wait for another. Lieut. Higgins gone on leave to England. Cleared up afternoon and night.
>
> **May 23.** — "Louise" between 3.45 a.m. and 4.15 dropped about 30 shells in the neighbourhood of NEUVILLE ST. VAAST and LA TARGETTE and a little to the south of these places. At 4.10 a German aeroplane circled around this area and dropped three bombs a little south of LA TARGETTE and then beat it home. Pte. Anderson of No. 1 Coy. overstaying his leave in PARIS by 5 days. Forfeits 28 days pay plus the 5 by Royal Warrant. O.C. 4th C.D.T. called in and we had a long comparison of notes. Lieut. Aldous called in and [uncertain] to see S.S.O. and had long confab. Applied for leave to go to ABBEVILLE to see CAREY. 18 Remounts came in and distributed, not the same good class as the last lot. Fine.

### Page 9 — 24–29 May 1917 (Sheet No. 124)

> **May 24.** — Pack train off loaded at 6 a.m., a good time, gives the boys the cool of the morning for for hauling and a long time day for work and play. Sports went off splendidly. Brigadier Genl. Hill gave away the prizes and it kept cool and fine all day. Told off a man from Postal Corps for abuse of green envelopes. Genl. Hill gave away the prizes at Sports and made very complimentary remarks about the transport entries especially. Every thing went well and without a hitch. Plenty to eat and drink and no case up for drunkenness. Beautifully fine all day.
>
> **May 25.** — Loader from Artillery got 28 days F.P. No. 1 and to be returned to his unit. Aeroplanes very busy in this area during the night and at dawn morning of 25–6. Hot and fine all day.
>
> **May 26.** — Started for Abbeyville 9 a.m. Met Capt. Carey and his sister. Started back at 5. and arrived 8 p.m. Football [uncertain] team played 2nd. Train again. No score. Very fine and hot.
>
> **May 27.** — Aeroplanes busy at daylight this morning dropping bombs and at 10.30 a.m. "Louise" started her little game dropping shells on Fort George and over their ridge, they dont seem to be shooting at any particular target. Football 4 vs 1. 3 to one in favour of 4. Fine and Sunny.
>
> **May 28.** — Hun plane over us 4 a.m. and of course ours came out after he had gone. Mr Sanders reported back from leave last night. Rev. Major Wood called. Major Freeman on F.G.C.M. all day. Bad water turned off and all drinking horse troughs scoured out. D.A.Q.M.G. went round with me and inspected wagons, discussing what parts should and should not be burnished. He seems to favour eyewash. I do, not, and have sent a circular letter re this to the three other Senior Transport Officers to see if we cannot agree on and recommend an Active Service standard to be adopted. Lieut. Graham's batman reported Lieut. Graham had been evacuated to England. Officers vs Sergeants first cricket match this evening ending in favour of the former. Hot and fine.
>
> **May 29.** — Adjutant gone to rear Headquarters re- leave pass for Capt. Graham. Particularly quiet night and morning no planes. Shells about. Dined at 5th. Divisional Headquarters. Fine.

### Page 10 — 30–31 May 1917 (Sheet No. 125)

> **May 30.** — Capt. Graham gone on leave. Went with Adjt. to Corps Headquarters to show General Farmer a G.S. wagon fitted with water carriers. Col. Murray was there with him and they approved and considered the method exhibited the best they had seen, yet he would not order its adoption but not to do so seems to me a sign of weakness, wrote Col. Murray, A.D.O.S. the remarks I made at meeting re- the use of straw in wheels and my reasons for not approving of same, I have yet to learn good reasons why I should change my mind. S.S.O. on Board of Enquiry re loss of reserve rations in forward area. Cool but fine.
>
> **May 31.** — Made personal inspection of all four Coys. and refilling points. Bright and fine.
>
> [Signed:] *[signature — appears to read "Findlay" [uncertain] as to first name] Lieut-Colonel, / O.C. 3rd Canadian Divl. Train*

## Open questions / uncertain items (May 1917)
- The cover reads "Volume 16" and the contents page reads "Volume 17 — 5." — both as written.
- The contents page says "Diary Text 7 Pages" and the diary text occupies pages 4–10 (7 sheets, Sheets 119–125); the count is consistent this month. Page 3 is a separate title page ("WAR DIARY for MAY 1917. 3rd CANADIAN DIVISIONAL TRAIN."), which the other months in this range do not have.
- 7 May: the entry records a German aeroplane bombing on the night of 3 May that killed two men, wounded several others and killed three horses. The men are not named in the diary, and no unit is given for them; the entry does not state whether they were Train men. This is the only fatal incident recorded in this eight-month range.
- "Louise" is the name the men gave to a German long-range gun shelling the Neuville-St. Vaast / La Targette area through mid-May.
- 12 May: Captain Hennessy leaves to become Staff Captain to the 5th Brigade; Captain J. Oliphant becomes Adjutant, Captain Church becomes O.C. No. 2 Company, Lieut. Higgins becomes Supply Officer. Hennessy had been with the O.C. since he took over command on 28 January 1916.
- 14 May: the G.O.C. Division inspects the Train and presents Meritorious Medals to S.S.M. Proctor and S.S. Wade.
