## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The February sheets are on the lined form (footer "T.124. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."). A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "A.D.M.S." = Assistant Director of Medical Services; "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column; "A.P.M." = Assistant Provost Marshal; "M.T." = Mechanical Transport; "G.S. wagons" = General Service wagons; "C.C." = Confined to Camp (as used here, "14 days C.C."); "C.-in-C." = Commander-in-Chief; "O.S.C." = Officer in charge of Supplies (as used here). The diary's own heading on the diary sheets is the unit name printed at the top left.

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY".

### Page 3 — 1–5 February 1917 (Sheet No. 102)

> **February 1.** — Much milder this morning. Pack Train came in at 11.00 a.m. but not in position for unloading until 1.00 p.m. Convoy waiting in cold for nearly 2 hours. When Train was got at, no tea, sugar or bacon on board. Finished First Line Transport Reports. Major Harris and Staff Capt. McKinnon came and saw me about number of lorries we use and I convinced them horse transport was worked to limit. Transport of Companies 3 and 4 looked in splendid condition. Weather cold again.
>
> **February 2.** — Tried case of sentry replenishing fire in vicinity of [uncertain] post and thereby exceeding his duty. His O.C. gave him an excellent character. 14 days C.C. Pack Train in at 7.15 p.m., midnight before Coys. turned in. Great difficulty in getting telephonic communication. Mr Bayliffe and party back from leave five days late, owing to the fact that no leave trains were coming up. Clear, cold and fine.
>
> **February 3.** — Capt. Church came in and I took up with him the report sent in on state of billets left by 4th Train and showed him Col. Webbs letter. Pack train in at 7.30. p.m. Late again before Coys got to bed. Capt. Oliphant gone on leave. Clear and cold.
>
> **February 4.** — Tried two men for missing their train on way up. Charge sent in by A.P.M. Havre very vague. Men reported to him they had missed train and it was not correct therefore to say they were leaving the train and there was the temptation of Hot Tea at the Y.M.C.A. hut close by, during the bitterly cold weather travelling in coaches with broken windows and doors. I considered 14 days C.C. met the case. Called with S.S.O. at Div'l. Hdqrs. Had long interview with A.A. & Q.M.G. re proposed move. We three then went and viewed new refilling points to be used when we come back after rest period. M.O. reported suspected case of smallpox, amended his instructions re precautions, with his concurrence. Pack Train in at 7.00 p.m. Very cold and raw.
>
> **February 5.** — M.O. gone to see A.D.M.S. re case reported above. S.S.O. gone to see S.S.O. of 2nd. to make final arrangements about feeding each others units during move. Pack Train in at 9.30 p.m.

### Page 4 — 5–11 February 1917 (Sheet No. 103)

> **February 5 (continued).** — Capt. Chambers, V.O., 8th Can. Arty. Bgde. reported to take charge until a permanent V.O. is appointed, this is in addition to his other work. S.S.O. of 2nd Divn, Major Mitchell called and talked over the feeding during the pending move and have come to a satisfactory and amicable arrangement. Very cold.
>
> **February 6.** — Major Beasley acting as Judge Advocate on case today. Capt. Church one of the members of Court. Early this morning bombs reported to have been dropped in this vicinity but no damage reported. Train in at 9.30 p.m. Col. Simson and Major Corrigan called to get information from us about the roads near their new location. Bitterly cold.
>
> **February 7.** — Answered O.C. No 3 re his recommendation for awards (not good enough) S.S.O and Adjutant gone to look over the ground we take over from the 2nd. Train. Train in at 10.30 p.m. Cold and frosty.
>
> **February 8.** — S.S.O and Adjutant to Div. Hdqrs. Major McClennan from Second called. Col. Hughes A.A & Q.M.G. of 2nd. came to talk over move. Train in at 1.30 p.m. Clear and cold.
>
> **February 9.** — Col Webb called in early this morning, talked to him about Simson's letter to me and my reply. He agrees I gave the right answer. Straightening up correspondence etc. ready for move. S.S.O. to Divn. Two years today since I, with the First Division, left England. Capt. Hennessy returned from leave. Train in at 1.00 p.m. Dined with Col. Webb, 4th Train. Clear and cold.
>
> **February 10.** — Lieut. Grant, Capt. Graham and Major Freeman gone to look over roads and billets in new area. S.S.O. 2nd. came to make final arrangements about feeding with my S.S.O. Not so cold.
>
> **February 11.** — Pack Train only came in at 2.00 a.m., only 17 hours late, and this when a move is on too. The sooner some head takes the railways in hand the better for every one. FERFAY is available for the Train and this simplifies matters considerably. S.S.O. and Lieut Grant gone there this morning to settle refilling point. S.S.O. then goes on to D.D.S. & T. to

### Page 5 — 11–16 February 1917 (Sheet No. 104)

> **February 11 (continued).** — confer about underdrawals. Recommended, after interviewing him, Mr Sheard's leave to Canada. Adjutant and I visited and inspected Nos. 4 and 3 Coys today. Pack Train came in at 5.00 p.m. Much milder and very fine.
>
> **February 12.** — Orders issued for Headquarters to move off at 11.00 a.m. sharp. Lieut Grant went and fixed up all office and billeting for Headquarters. Called in at Divn. and arranged for Headquarters Coy to billet in Ferfay. To do this No. 8 Coy, C.E. have to move. Evening raining, soft and mild.
>
> **February 13.** — Hdgrs, Train left Cambligneul for Bruay at 12.25 p.m., arriving there at 1.20. Found it very difficult to get into billet for Office. Civil population most disagreeable and their one and only desire seems to be to get as much money out of the troops as possible. Ordered Adjutant to parade all H.Q. Batmen and Car Drivers to answer charge of neglect to leave their quarters clean when they moved out. S.S.O. working himself off his feet and Second Train have been of little assistance. He called at their H.Q. Coy for wagons to turn out to unload Pack Train and could not find even the Orderly Officer on duty. Column stepped in and did it. This place is a dirty hole. We are supposed to be out in rest, but where does the rest for the Train come in. Rest or no rest troops have to be fed. Inclined to frost again.
>
> **February 14.** — Got all Officers established at last, no thanks due to the Town Major, The Train is evidently too small for him to worry about. Serve him right if he had to go hungry. Hd. Qrs. Coy. comes in tomorrow, then things will be easier. Cold and hard frost.
>
> **February 15.** — The 2nd. are likely to ball things up if they keep on making alterations on their own, without consulting this Train, especially with regard to Artillery moves and coal supply. Yesterday our C.-in-C. made inspection. 1st. and 3rd. represented, We come in tomorrow. Train in 10.30 a.m. Re arranging the distribution of Officers and N.C.O.s in Train when complete all the Officers will have done Supplies and Transport. A little milder.
>
> **February 16.** — No 3 Coy paraded to represent Train at inspection by Gen. Neville. Tried Sergt McLeod for leaving his billet dirty when he moved out, Severely Reprimanded. Lieut Grant gone

### Page 6 — 16–20 February 1917 (Sheet No. 105)

> **February 16 (continued).** — as S.O. to H.Q. Coy. Lieut Richards to No 2 Coy. Wrote Lieut. Grant an appreciation of his work as Acting Adjt. for four months. Thaw set in.
>
> **February 17.** — Wrote 42nd. Battn. re super-structures on limbers, contrary to letter Q.598. Capt. Oliphant reported back from leave. Pack Train in at 12.00 noon. Thaw precautions in force. Called at Divl. Hdqrs. and arranged with A.A. & Q.M.G. to get wagons detailed from D.A.C. for delivery of rations from Divl. Troops Dump. to Artillery Units, 7 wagons per Bgde and one to Artillery H.Q. Mild.
>
> **February 18.** — Several Officers have most horrid colds which seem most difficult to cure. S.S.O especially bad. Made several changes in the distribution and re-arrangement of Train Officers. H.Q. Coy had to be strengthened. Dull but mild.
>
> **February 19.** — Court Martial of Sergt Barker, No 2 Coy. He was acquitted but only on account of weak evidence. Capt. McCallum reported back and reports expense at Mentone is fierce and the returning him to Base instead of direct to his Unit a waste of time and a few other uncomplimentary remarks. Capt. Oliphant reported a satisfactory reason why bradoon bits are used on several of his teams. Lieut Spalding's evidence at F.G.C.M. shows him to be what I always suspected, a very weak Officer. Major Freeman has neglected to prefer a charge against the Batmen and Motor Drivers, who left their billets in a dirty condition, this is a very weak spot in him, he will not be firm. Damp and mild.
>
> **February 20.** — Hurried message came in late last night that two Bgdes of Artillery were to go to the 1st Division, but S.S.O. got no notification from D.A.Q.M.G. consequently no indent could be put in for them to go off with rations in the ordinary way and the S.S.O had to make other and hurried arrangements and transport had to be arranged to go off with them when they move. Confidential letters handed to M.O. in this office. He was required to sign them. Lieut Underwood reported from Base for duty and sent him to H.Q. Coy, to whom he was posted New charger arrived for me to try out. Am having difficulty in getting spare hind wheels

### Page 7 — 20–24 February 1917 (Sheet No. 106)

> **February 20 (continued).** — for G.S. wagons. Col. Simson, Majors Corrigan and Grear called to discuss and arrange for the feeding of our two Bgdes of Artillery, which have gone to the First, until their Pack Train is increased. Weather open and mild.
>
> **February 21.** — Train in early. No 2 Coy has the extra hauling to do from railhead, including hauling of wood. Capt. Oliphant reports Lieut Spalding is of little use to him. Always excuses for not having things done, for example two days to find billets for a few men and the Interpreter did it in 50 minutes. Also identification roll of horses, he knew little or nothing about, and he was left in command of Coy. during Capt. McCallum's absence in hospital No initiative to him. Mild and small rain.
>
> **February 22.** — Pack train in early at station but convoy had to remain waiting for it to be in position for off-loading for nearly three hours. Worse management it would be hard to beat anywhere. The waste of time, the unnecessary strain on men and horses, owing to lack of business management is appalling. Pity we could not take over the line from the French then there would be something doing.
>
> **February 23.** — Sergt. Curran requested me to sign recommendation for commission, could not see my way to do it. Inspection of 7th Bgde, 1st Line Transport by G.O.C., who expressed great satisfaction at the good condition. Called in at Div. Hdqrs. and asked A.A. & Q.M.G. to draw attention in orders to G.R.O. re trotting of teams and riding on loaded wagons, also had talk over transport and refilling points when we move. Lieut Wheatley reported for duty, posted him to No 3 Coy. Train only came in last night at 8.00 p.m., therefore H.Q. Coy had a 13 hour day. It is 8½ hours late again today. This bad management is most disheartening and gives my Train no chance to get into shape. Lieut Spalding sent to Hospital with, M.O. says, Possibly grip. Dull and mild.
>
> **February 24.** — Capts. Oliphant and Hennessy on F.G.C.M. H.Q. Coy 13 hours waiting and had to return home because train not in. They will have to do a double trip tomorrow which means 36 miles in one day. Sun came out for a few minutes. Col. Simson called in evening. Dry and mild.

### Page 8 — 25–28 February 1917 (Sheet No. 107)

> **February 25.** — Loading at Railhead early this morning. Convoys will have to come a second time today. Tried case from No 4 Coy, Refused to double when ordered, Refused to take my punishment and took a F.G.C.M. Called at Divn and saw D.A.Q.M.G. and asked him to see that the D.A.C. wagons were not taken away from Artillery, so that H.Q. Coy would get a rest after their 36 mile haul. Second haul completed alright. The Train was in Mid-day. Quite spring-like and some sun.
>
> **February 26.** — Col. Simpson, Massey and self met at First Train last night and discussed the mode of promotions in vogue at present. Decided to meet again tomorrow at this office and ask Col. Webb to attend, again discuss and if necessary take the matter to the Corps or even higher up. S.S.O. out seeing how many lorries can be turned out, so that we can estimate how many wagons can be released for hauling ammunition to Gun positions. Fine.
>
> **February 27.** — Pack train in about 10.00 a.m. Adjutant got slight chill. Train not in position for off-load until 1 o'clock. Tried case of Dr. Scales, No 2 Coy, late for parade, many similar similar [as written]. Forfeits 28 days Pay. O.S.C., Trains never came and meeting postponed. Fine and mild. Capt. de Graves reported for duty.
>
> **February 28.** — M.T. on, and Train horses released, all except No 2 Coy. Lieut Sheard returns to Canada today. Lieut Fisher goes on Supplies in Sheard's place. Spoke to P.M. re my allotment not being paid into my wife's account and about the men's deferred pay for War Loan. It does seem a shame that they cannot draw it and yet get no interest on it. Turn out judging today. Pioneers took First prize and No 3 Coy's turnout second. The Competition Turns-out were extremely good, all of them, but I should just like to see those they left in their lines. I rather fancy there would be many surprises. Cold but fine.
>
> [Signed:] *[signature — appears to read "Findlay" [uncertain] as to first name] Lt. Col. / O.C. 3rd Canadian Div. Train*
