# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — September 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps (CASC), 3rd Canadian Division (styled "H.Q. 3rd Canadian Divl. Train" on the cover)
- **Month:** September 1918 (Volume 32) — this volume covers 1 to 30 September 1918
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** September 1918, p. 1 – p. 9 (image files page-0317 – page-0325 of the diary)
- **September 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–9): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–30 September, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The Place column of the diary sheets is blank throughout; places in the entries are as written in the text. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. Weekly transport-return tables are reproduced as written, including their arithmetic (see open questions where the diary's stated totals do not match its own figures). Abbreviations as used in the diary: S.S.O. = Senior Supply Officer; O.C. = Officer Commanding; M.T. = Mechanical Transport; H.T. = Horse Transport; R.F.P. = Re-filling Point; A.A.&Q.M.G. = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster-General; D.A.Q.M.G. = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster-General; G.O.C. = General Officer Commanding; A.D.M.S. = Assistant Director of Medical Services; D.D. of S. & T. = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; D.A.D.S. = Deputy Assistant Director of Supplies; F.P. No. 1 = Field Punishment No. 1; F.G.C.M. = Field General Court Martial; S.A.A. = Small Arms Ammunition; G.S. = General Service; C.A.S.C. = Canadian Army Service Corps; C.C.S. = Casualty Clearing Station; M.O. = Medical Officer; O.R's = Other Ranks; Bde. = Brigade; L.E. = Lee-Enfield. The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "(A8984) Wt. W12439/M1295. 750,000. 1/17. D. D. & L., Ltd. Forms/C.2118/14." From 26 September the entries are written by Major McLeod acting as O.C. (see 25 September entry), and the 30 September sheet is signed by him.

### Page 5 — 3 September (continued) – 7 September 1918 (Sheet No. 2)

> **3 September 1918 (continued).** — S.S.O., 3rd C.D.T., Called at Division H.Q., saw A.A.Q.M.G. and explained why Capt: Phillips wanted to be paraded. Went on with Capt: Graham and dropped him off at Re-filling Points, while I tried a case at No.1, giving 28 Days F.P.No.1. Asked Capt: Graham to warn Capt: Phillips I might send car for him this evening, if General would see him. Went to AGNEZ to try and find Lieut: RASON re dispute with Purchase Board about Green Feed. They claim we had 40,000 kilos, we say only 15,000. Capt: Graham after lunch, gone to Army to see if they will take over some coal which we cannot use, being so far back, and have to keep and feed a guard on. Railhead tomorrow changes back to ARRAS. Car returned 4-30.
>
> **4 September 1918.** — No.1 Re-filling Point moved forward to 1200 yards West of MONCHY le PREUX. M.T. for Divisional Troops and today for 9th Bde. Tomorrow H.T. haul and deliver at Dumps, First Line Transport coming there to fetch supplies, we haul 2/3rds of distance, and they 1/3rd. Fine.
>
> **5 September 1918.** — Headquarters Train moved today from AGNEZ les DUISANS to G-14.c.4.4., Sheet 51B. Called on G.O.C. by appointment to parade Capt: Phillips who had a complaint to make, but G.O.C. did not arrive. Fine.
>
> **6 September 1918.** — G.O.C. wired he would see me at 9-30, reported and saw him, when he told me he had seen Capt: Phillips. Said he had no grounds for complaint, but that I was to be careful and always put up the next Senior to an acting position, but when confirmation was required, I could put in an adverse report. He could not have been nicer. Called at Dentist's. S.S.O. gone to move No.1 re-filling point. Where it is at present, lorries have to come in against the traffic arrows, and this might lead to trouble. Went over Divisional Farm with S.S.O. already they have lifted 150 tons of potatoes, and ploughed up the ground. All winter seed beds looking very good, and plants healthy. Turnips very good colour but thin and backward, carrots need thinning badly, leeks are poor and for the work entailed, I should say, does not pay to grow.
>
> **7 September 1918.** — Visited railhead with S.S.O. All re-filling points and Rear and Advanced Divisional H.Q. all going well. Lieut:Col: GREER called at tea time, and we compared notes. Heavy shower about 4.00 p.m. Feeling seedy.

### Page 8 — 21–25 September 1918 (Sheet No. 5)

> **21 September 1918.** — Very wet and stormy night. Not feeling any too well. Writing out Honours and Awards. Note came that my Immediate Awards had been turned down by Army. Wet.
>
> **22 September 1918.** — Am contemplating getting leave to BIAERITZ, am getting groggy and weak, and cannot get proper medicine or food here. Artillery made a short move back. ## Open questions / uncertain items (September 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 32" but the contents page reads "Volume 18 - 9." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number.
- The 3 September entry is split across Sheet No. 1 and Sheet No. 2: the salvage list ends mid-sentence on Sheet No. 1 ("135 lbs. Dripping turned in to") and Sheet No. 2 opens with the words "S.S.O., 3rd C.D.T., Called at Division H.Q.…". The connection between the two is unclear; both parts are transcribed as written.
- 3 September salvage list: the figure read as "1385, 5.9 charge cases" is marked [uncertain] — it may be "138 5.9 charge cases". The entry "72 18-lbr. shells" is as written ("18-lbr."). The list's rifle counts ("48 L.E. Rifles, 30 French, 6 German") are as written.
- 8 September: the table's longest haul for No. 3 Company is written "29x" — as written. The stated weekly total is "7,937 miles", but the four company totals (3,443 + 1,228 + 3,494 + 1,772) add to 9,937 miles; the diary's own figures disagree and both are transcribed as written.
- 10 and 14 September: the word read as "reaching" is marked [uncertain] both times; it appears to be a mis-typing of "retching" (the O.C. was ill with suspected ptomaine poisoning), but it is transcribed as written.
- 19 September: a word between "violent and" and "painful indigestion" could not be read with confidence (it appears to be "burning", over a correction); marked [uncertain].
- 22 September: "BIAERITZ" is as written (the O.C. spells it "BIARRITZ" on 25 September). The Adjutant's name is read as "Ferg" ("Adjt:Ferg on leave to England"); it may be short for Ferguson, but is transcribed as written.
- 24 September: the weekly total is stated as "7216 miles", but the four company totals (2,204 + 1,347 + 2,204 + 1,425) add to 7,180 miles; both are transcribed as written. Note also that No. 1 and No. 3 Companies are both given the same total, 2,204 miles.
- 24 September: "about 60 casualties" from the two bombs dropped in the camp — the diary does not say whose camp or whether the casualties were Train men; it is recorded as written and not attributed to the Train.
- The O.C.'s identity: the diary is written in the first person throughout, but the O.C. is never named in the text. Major J. W. McLeod is the S.S.O. (1 and 3 September: Graham acts as S.S.O. during McLeod's leave; 17 September: McLeod back from leave), and from 25 September McLeod acts as O.C. during the O.C.'s 14 days' leave to Biarritz. The 30 September sheet is signed "J. W. McLeod, Major, O/C. 3rd Can. Div. Train." — the first initial is a flourish and the signature is read as "J. W. McLeod".
- Captain Phillips is spelled "Phillips" throughout September; the August diary spells the same name "Philips" in the quoted 7th Brigade letter — both spellings are as written in their months. Captain de Graves is "Captain W.A. deGraves" in August and "Major deGraves" on 19 September — as written; no promotion is stated in the diary.
- Place names are as written, including variant spellings: "CROISELLLES" (26–28 September), "MONCHY le PREUX", "G-14.c.4.4., Sheet 51B", "WARLUS", "QUEANT", "PRONVILLE", "INCHY EN ARTOIS", "BOURLON", "BARLIN".
- The only casualties recorded in September 1918 are the "about 60 casualties" from the 24 September bombing (see above) and the "serious casualties" suffered by units next to Major deGraves's company on 19 September, which are not quantified and are not Train casualties. No Train man is named as a casualty.
- Names read as: Capt. V. H. Graham (acting S.S.O., then acting Adjutant); Major J. W. McLeod (S.S.O.); Capt. Phillips; Lieut. Rason; Lieut. Hessey; Sgt. Hawkes; Sgt. Holland; Sgt. McMillan; L/Cpl. Smith; Dvr. Prestidge; General L. J. Lipsett; General Lomas; Lieut.-Col. Greer; Col. Horrace Greer; Major deGraves; Col. McAlpine; Capt. Prikler; Adjt. Ferg; Lt. Berg; Col. T. A. Simpson (D.D. of S. & T., Canadians).
- No appendices are listed on any sheet; the "Remarks and references to Appendices" column is blank throughout.
