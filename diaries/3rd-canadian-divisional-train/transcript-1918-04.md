- **April 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 267–276 of 370): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–30 April, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading; the cover is partly handwritten and the title page is handwritten. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. The Place column is blank throughout the diary sheets; places named in the text are given as written. Abbreviations as written: "D.H.Q." = Divisional Headquarters; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "A.A.& Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "S.A.A." = small arms ammunition; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "F.P." = Field Punishment; "N.C.O." = non-commissioned officer; "D.A.D.O.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Ordnance Services; "D.D.S.& T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "H.T." = horse transport; "Bde." = Brigade; "inst:" = instant (of this month). The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."

## Pages 4–10 — Diary text (1–30 April 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The diary sheets are numbered "Sheet 1." to "Sheet 7."

### Page 4 — 1–4 April 1918 (Sheet No. 1)

> **1 April 1918.** — Working out returns. I think it is worth recording the salvage work this Train is doing over and above its ordinary work and detail of teams etc. for farming and Engineer's work. The return I sent in yesterday shows 14,875 Rounds of S.A.A., 12-18 pdr. shells, 75 lbs 18 pdr. shells, 1 trench gun and 40' nose fuzes, 137 parts of wagons, 3 wagon loads of empty tins for shelter, 30 sacks old horse shoes, 1 sack of cogs, 6 horse rugs, 746 sheets corrugated iron, 2 ploughs, 2 wheel barrows, 150 sand-bags, 47 snipers plates, 2 felling axes, 8 shovels, 5 pick-axes and 3 heads, 11 petrol tins, 5 oil drums, 2 leg-irons, 1 pair gum boots, 1 bell tent, 1 French and 1 English rifle. Besides numerous other items we have saved 795 lbs Dripping and 496 lbs waste paper. Hun took down 4 Balloons in succession and it is a marvel he got through such a hell of shot and shell doing it. Fine.
>
> **2 April 1918.** — For week ending 31st March, Train covered a distance of 7,442 miles. Average per Company No.1, 8., No.2, 9., No.3, 7.4., No.4, 8.9., or 8.13 miles per day per wagon of Train. Longest distance any one wagon 30 miles, shortest 1 mile for coal. 394 miles of the total distance was on Farm work, 234 Corps Farm, 160 on Divisional Farm. This afternoon potato planting began on Divisional Farm. Fine.
>
> **3 April 1918.** — Dull with showers. Things very quiet today on Farm. Artillery livened up considerably during evening. Lieut: Blake to rejoin me. He sent for a car to & fetch him but none forthcoming; told to find his way here.
>
> **4 April 1918.** — Morning opened very wet. Clearing later, sufficiently to enable some potatoes to be planted on Divisional Farm. A.A.& Q.M.G. will not for a few days allow Supply Sections to return to old dump. Interviewed Supply Sergeant re his incapacity, going to give him a fair and further trial, then if necessary, have him reduced. Interview with Lieut: Blake on his rejoining.

### Page 5 — 5–9 April 1918 (Sheet No. 2)

> **5 April 1918.** — Very wet. Most of No. [uncertain] Company Officers at Court Martial of D.A.D.O.S., Major Adams as member, others as witnesses. S.S.O. reported Lieut: Lawrence has not weight enough to hold down job of S.O. 7th Brigade; there have been numerous complaints lately that should not have arisen. Have decided on a change. Bde. have asked for Capt: Phillips back again.
>
> **6 April 1918.** — Fine, ground mist but sunny. Told Adjutant to post Capt: Phillips to 7th Bde. as S.O., Capt: Graham to command No.3 Company. Capt: Robinson to be tried out in Graham's place, and Lawrence to Capt. Robinson's place. Court Martial of man from No.1 Company today, Adjutant, Lieut: Guynne and Lieut: Blake gone to it. O.C. No.1 Company gave wrong unit of accused, and omitted to have him medically examined. It is this want of attention to small details that emphasises the weak points in an Officer Commanding. Had Capt: Phillips up and told him I was taking him away from his Company and he behaved very nicely over it, this is all to his credit. Rain in evening.
>
> **7 April 1918.** — Fine but poor visibility, and later rain. Lively gun-fire at night. Remanded Edward Drape of No.1 Company for F.G.C.M., threatening language to a N.C.O. Sentence of 90 days F.P.No.1 on man of No.1 Company (attached), for striking a N.C.O., most inadequate punishment, but had to promulgate it.
>
> **8 April 1918.** — Cold and very wet. All planting stopped. Owing to Artillery move, ordered Capt: Fisher and Lieut: Blake to move, latter to act as S.O. besides other work, to gain experience in Supply Work. Major Adams and Capt: Fisher came and complained about order and I had to call the former for it.
>
> **9 April 1918.** — Heavy cold wet fog. Mileage for Train for week ending April 7th, total distance 7,229 miles. Company averages per wagon, No.1 9 miles, No.2 8.7, No.3 7.3, No.4 11.3 per wagon. On Corps Farm 2 teams mileage travelled 224, on Divisional Farm 20 teams 93 miles and on Train Farm 6 teams covered 32 miles. Very heavy shelling began about 3.00 p.m. to search the back area and continued until about 4.00 a.m. One shell hit right into [uncertain — word struck through] wheeler's shop of No.4 Company only slightly hurt Company S.M. and Staff Wheeler, how it did [Entry continues on Sheet No. 3.]

### Page 6 — 9–13 April 1918 (Sheet No. 3)

> **9 April 1918 (continued).** — not kill the whole lot is a marvel. The whole place was wrecked but fire squad was instantly on duty and prevented any outbreak of fire. Although shells were falling thick fast and close all carried on with the utmost coolness, no flurry, no confusion, and I have a right to be proud of my Train. Other units in the near vicinity got the wind up and looked very much like stampeding.
>
> **10 April 1918.** — Quiet during day. Colonel Marshall, A.A.& Q.M.G., 4th, paid me a complimentary call. From 11-45 p.m. to 1-15 a.m. continuous shelling but no damage. Dull but fine. Capt: Graham left to take up Staff work at Army Headquarters.
>
> **11 April 1918.** — Went to Railhead with S.S.O. Called at Divisional H.Q. delivered to Major Brook application with my recommendation for Major Adams to return to Canada. Issued orders for Train to stand to and be prepared to move in an hour. Division informed me orders are on the way to move to Chateau de la Haie. Ordered O.C. No.4 Company to deliver rations to Battalions of his Brigade who have not moved, and in the event of their refusing to take them to dump them off in their lines. These rations are for consumption tomorrow and they must carry them. Distributed 17 Remounts between Companies, these were of a rather better class than usual. Colonel Greer of 4th Train and his S.S.O., (they take over from us when we move), called. He gave a clearance receipt to No.4 Company which moved out this afternoon to SAINS-EN-GOHELLE.
>
> **12 April 1918.** — Fine and bright. S.S.O. and Adjutant kept busy arranging for move and taking over from unit we are relieving. No.1 Company moves today to ABLAIN ST:NAZAIRE, No.2 moves today to CARENCY, No.3 joins No.4 at HERSIN. 1 AND 2 Companies will haul supplies by light railway, 3 and 4 by Horse Transport. Not decided yet where Train Headquarters will be but am trying for BARLIN as that will be our Railhead.
>
> **13 April 1918.** — Dull but fine. Quiet night. Train Headquarters moved today and established themselves in BARLIN after some little trouble in billeting. Everything going smoothly. Yesterday Capt: Fisher observed a hawk fighting a pigeon and when pigeon landed he went to [Entry continues on Sheet No. 4.]

### Page 7 — 13–16 April 1918 (Sheet No. 4)

> **13 April 1918 (continued).** — inspect it, found a message on it in German code. He promptly delivered pigeon and message to nearest Divisional Headquarters which happened to be the 46th.
>
> **14 April 1918.** — Dull and colder but fine. So far things working smoothly. Light railway slightly balled things up by delivering all hay and oats to one siding, instead of to two.
>
> **15 April 1918.** — Again cold and dull. Transport return for week ending 14/4/18, total distance covered by Train, 8,473 miles, or 10.15 miles per day per wagon. Average per Company No.1, 15.4, No.2, 8.2, No.3, 8.4, No.4, 9.3. 218 miles travelled on Divisional Farm. Train Farm, nil.
>
> **16 April 1918.** — M.O. called in yesterday to inform me his relief was on his way out here from England. This morning is inclined to rain. Letter from Colonel Simson yesterday, advising formation of Committee of Officers Commanding Trains, and that Major MacAlpine was to take his place as Officer i/c C.A.S.C. Personnel at Corps. Sent out notices to all Officers Commanding Trains to meet me here 23rd inst: McAlpine will be of little or no help to us here, to begin with he is not a C.A.S.C. man, and has done no Train work in the Field.

### Page 8 — 17–23 April 1918 (Sheet No. 5)

> **17 April 1918.** — S.S.O. and Adjutant went over cross country routes in the event of Light Railway being stopped and main roads blocked. Called in at D.H.Q. V.O. came and looked over horses of Nos. 3 and 4 Companies. Fine but dull.
>
> **18 April 1918.** — Sketching Company routes on maps and making copies for Corps. Order posted for old women and children to evacuate BARLIN. Again dull, slight rain.
>
> **19 April 1918.** — Ordered wagon to report tomorrow at 9.00 a.m. to take away Lady of the house and her grand-child. Wrote by request, confidential report to A.A.Q.M.G., re Major Adams. Cold, hailstorms.
>
> **20 April 1918.** — Lady of house and child, joined by 5 others gone to entrain at BRUAY. Visited No.1 Company at ABLAIN ST.NAZAIRE. and No.2 Company at CARENCY, and inspected same. They were in very good shape. Rain towards evening.
>
> **21 April 1918.** — Morning broke very fine, milder. Made out confidential reports on Major Adams and sent them to him to read and sign as having read. Heard that Paymaster is recalled to England.
>
> **22 April 1918.** — Fine. Mileage for train for week ending April 21st, No.1 Company 951 miles, or 4 per wagon. No.2, 500 miles, or 4.8 per wagon. No.3, 1727 miles or 11.10 per wagon. No.4 1260 miles, or 7.5 per wagon. No Train Farm work, but 4½ miles on 4th Divisional Farm. Major Adams returned report initialed and I forwarded same to Division. Drawing out a revised route for Nos. 1 and 2 Companies from Railhead.
>
> **23 April 1918.** — Fine. Held a meeting of O: C. Trains and discussed certain matters and made recommendations to Officer i/c C.A.S.C. Personnel. Just heard Colonel Webb had his leg taken off by a shell. S.S.O. and Adjutant gone to D.D.S. & T. and Divisional H.Q. See Major Stirrett's promotion in Gazette. Capt: Hennessy called in afternoon. He [Entry continues on Sheet No. 6.]

### Page 9 — 23–28 April 1918 (Sheet No. 6)

> **23 April 1918 (continued).** — had been to the Hospital Colonel Webb was admitted to, but was not allowed to see him, however he was informed the leg was amputated above the knee, and he was going on as well as could be expected, and with his good constitution had every chance of a good recovery. Dr. Moffat also looked in on me. They dropped several shells into BARLIN towards evening, no great damage done.
>
> **24 April 1918.** — Dull with fog. Reported on Capt: Richards (5 copies), returned to Canada after operation on knee. An American Captain called and had tea. If all specimens are the same, they certainly will be top hole.
>
> **25 April 1918.** — Wet. Started shelling this town at 4.00 a.m. and intermittently through the day. Met Colonel Budgen of my old Regiment, and heard quite a lot about the old boys. Provost Marshal came in and complained Blind was not thick enough to keep in the light, asked him if he had just come in and on his replying "Yes", said I thought so, and asked him to give his orders himself to the lady of the house. He was very resplendent in red and looked to me as if he had the wind up. S.S.O. and Adjutant gone to Division and to look over areas for refilling points if the move back is put into force.
>
> **26 April 1918.** — Dull. Very few shells today, all very quiet. Dr. Moffat called in to say "Good-bye".
>
> **27 April 1918.** — Dull but fine. At 3.00 a.m. and 5.00 a.m. some shelling of BARLIN and BRUAY. New M.O., Dr. Cochrane came and reported. Dr. Moffat gone back to England. Asked if I could recommend Grant, Higgins and Hessey for promotion, and replied "Yes, especially the the first and last Officers". Recommended accelerated promotion for Hessey, who has done most excellent work. Lieut: Grant called in to see me.
>
> **28 April 1918.** — Dull with fog. Four shells came in in pairs at 5.00 a.m. but as far as can be learnt no damage. The civil population are getting their wind up and clearing out. Why any are allowed to remain here beats me, they are only in danger and a nuisance. Yesterday in the School square while an inspection was on of Lieut: Underwood's Supply Column Section, a [Entry continues on Sheet No. 7.]

### Page 10 — 28–30 April 1918 (Sheet No. 7)

> **28 April 1918 (continued).** — shell hit in the square; no one hurt, but it is a wonder they were not all blown to bits pieces; this was late afternoon. Colonel Villand called re the exchange of three of his transport men; I can help him out in this.
>
> **29 April 1918.** — Brighter. Shelling again. Very heavy bombardment started about 11-30 tonight, ours. Inspected 1st Line Transport of 9th Brigade. All but 11th good. Train mileage for week ending 28th inst: total distance 5480 miles, average per Company 1370, mileage per wagon per Company, No.1, 4.3, No.2, 7.6, No.3, 11.2, No.4, 7.7. Teams on 4th Divisional Farm 17 miles. Longest distance any one wagon, 26, for canteen stores, shortest, 1, water cart.
>
> **30 April 1918.** — Rain. Judged H.T. of 9th and 11th Can. Field Ambulances. General Currie turned up at judging. Advance orders for move came in. Only two shells during the last 24 hours.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] d'Auvergne Findlay, Lt. Col., O.C. 3rd Canadian Div. Train.

- Names read as: Lieut-Col. d'Auvergne Findlay (O.C.); Majors Adams, Brook, Greer (4th Train) and Stirrett; Captains Phillips, Graham, Robinson, Fisher, Church, Hennessy and Richards; Lieuts. Lawrence, Blake, Guynne, Higgins, Grant, Hessey and Underwood; Edward Drape (No.1 Company); Colonel Marshall (A.A.& Q.M.G., 4th Division); Colonel Simson; Colonel Webb; Colonel Budgen; Colonel Villand; Major MacAlpine/McAlpine; Dr. Moffat; Dr. Cochrane; General Currie.

Shelling of Barlin and Bruay on 25, 27 and 28 April caused no damage or casualties to the Train.
