# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — April 1916

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, 3rd Canadian Division (styled "Headquarters 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, 3rd Canadian Division" on the cover)
- **Month:** April 1916 (Volume 3) — this volume covers 1 to 30 April 1916
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, microfilm reel T-10904, file 769; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** April 1916, p. 1 – p. 20 (image files page-0029 – page-0048 of the 370-page diary)
- **April 1916 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–20): cover, contents, diary text 1–30 April, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The diarist is Lt-Col J. A. Findlay, O.C. 3rd Canadian Divisional Train; his initials "J.A.F." appear at the foot of every sheet. "Hd. Qrs." = Headquarters; "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "F. P. No 1." = Field Punishment No. 1; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "S.C." = Supply Column; "S.C.O." is used as written and is not expanded in the diary; "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "R.F.P." is used as written. The place throughout is Busseboom, as written in the Place column on every diary sheet. Diary sheets are numbered 25–42, continuing the numbering from the earlier volumes; the sheet number on page 3 is partly obscured by the unit stamp. The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "T.134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S." Each diary sheet also bears the oval unit stamp "H.Q., DIVISIONAL TRAIN, 3rd CANADIAN DIVISION" and the handwritten words "Original" and "Confidential" at the top.

> **BUSSEBOOM, 6/4/16 (continued).** — the Supply Column was only a worker and a pusher, sadly a slacker, would fall down daily if we did not push ourselves and thereby save his skin, its too bad such chaps have to be tolerated. ## Open questions / uncertain items (April 1916)
- The 1 April entry refers to Lt-Col Findlay in the third person ("Lt. Colonel Findlay went on leave to day") and then says "I went with him" — although Findlay was on leave 1–9 April and Capt. R. A. Webb was acting O.C., the handwriting and the "J.A.F." initials at the foot of every sheet are the same throughout the month, so the diary is treated as Findlay's throughout; the third-person opening is as written.
- The sheet number on page 3 is partly obscured by the unit stamp; it is read as Sheet No. 25 from the sequence 26–42 on the following sheets.
- 6 April appears at the foot of Sheet No. 28 (page 6), across the whole of Sheet No. 29 (page 7) and at the head of Sheet No. 30 (page 8), with the entry continuing across all three; all parts are transcribed as written.
- The place name is written "Busseboom" in the Place column on every diary sheet.
- Names with variant spellings as written: Lougheed (8 April, reported as R.S.O.) / Loughead (11 April) / Lawhead (12 April, re School of Instruction) — these may be the same man or different men; recorded as written. La Croix (5 April) / La Croie (9 April) / Lt. Croix (11 April); Roach (3, 4, 5, 8 April) / Roche (9 April). McCallum is written "McCallum" throughout April.
- "Gen. Sir Williams" (2 April) is as written; the name may read "Sir William".
- "his [uncertain] Company" (3 April) — the word before "Company" may read "this" rather than "his".
- "Captaincy's [uncertain]" (6 April) — the word after "Church," is not read with confidence; the sentence also reads "so also Major Findlay's and Capt. Lawson of the Supply Column" as written, the object of the possessive not being stated.
- "being an of N.C.O." (8 April) is as written; the phrasing is odd and may be a slip for "being [that] of an N.C.O."
- The small mark between "Major" and "Webb" (10 April, "Major Webb carried on splendidly") is not read with confidence; it may be an initial. The diarist calls Webb "Major" on 10 and 22 April although the May diary shows him still a Captain awaiting promotion to Major (see May open questions).
- "went around No 3 Series" (5 April) — the word "Series" is as written; its sense is unclear.
- "dropped out to A.Q.M.G." (28 April) is as written; it may read "dropped in to".
- The signature at the foot of the April sheets reads "J. A. Findlay" with the middle name not read with confidence; it may read "Andrew" but is recorded here as [uncertain] and the middle name is not used as a name.
- No unit move is recorded in April; the Train stays at Busseboom all month. No battle casualties are recorded in April. Injuries: the No. 3 Coy interpreter dislocated his shoulder in a fall from his horse (23 April, admitted to hospital); the V.O. was injured in a fall from his horse (29 April) and was found on 30 April to have a dislocated thumb, badly shaken, but not evacuated. A case of diphtheria was reported (20 April). German shells fell 500 yards behind H.Q. billets on 6 April and roads were shelled on 5 April, with no casualties stated.
