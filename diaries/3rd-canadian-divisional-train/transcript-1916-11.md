## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The November sheets are fainter and more heavily overtyped than October's, so several words are flagged [uncertain] where the type is illegible. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "D.D. of S. & T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "D.D.S. & T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "G.C.M." = General Court Martial; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "A.D.M.S." = Assistant Director of Medical Services; "D.A.A. & Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "F.P.I" = Field Punishment No. 1; "A.D.S. & T." = Assistant Director of Supplies and Transport (as used here); "W.O." = Warrant Officer. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets is the unit name printed at the top left. The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the November sheets is read as [uncertain] (see open questions).

## Pages 3–9 — Diary text (1–30 November 1916)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed footer reads "2469 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12." on Sheets 84–85 and "1875 Wt. W593/826 1,000,000 4/15 J.B.C. & A. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118." on Sheets 87–90.

### Page 3 — 1–5 November 1916 (Sheet No. 84)

> **November 1.** — Clearing up correspondence and generally straightening up work of the month. 2-30 gave a lecture to all Officers and W.O's of Train on Care of Animals, specially during winter. Gave preliminary statement of funds collected for Lord Kitchener National Memorial Fund. Adjutant saw D.A.Q.M.G. about getting prisoners for fatigue duties. Settled little trouble between Adjutant and O.C. No. 3 Company about detailing men from this Company for fatigue duty on Coal Dump. Still wet and nasty.
>
> **November 2.** — S.S.O. to Railhead. Adjutant to Divisional Headquarters. Paymaster to Field Cashier. Called with S.S.O. at Divisional Headquarters re Coal and Baths, and to find out about G.C.M in am on. Called on Engineers re their departure. Cold and wet.
>
> **November 3.** — Morning same as usual. Made careful inspection of No. 4 Company's, horses in good condition two with greasy hocks are being attended to by the V.O. and I do not think he can do much for them, the ailment seems of long standing; stables are rainproof but standings bad although they are being repaired. Cook house clean and tidy; mens quarters dark but very good. Much to be done round buildings to get surface water away and mud. Harness very good and kept in good shelter. Wagon Park muddy in the extreme, but I do not see how this can be avoided.
>
> **November 4.** — Arranged to take over stock of 60th Divisional Train Canteen, they are certainly a hard lot to drive bargains with, and place a high value on their own possessions; they evidently think Canadians have money and will throw it away, but will they ?. General look round. Phone installed in Office. Wind and some rain.
>
> **November 5.** — At last got Paymaster a room, his former billet was trying to a man of his age. Ordered Headquarters Company to take over billets and lines vacated this morning by 60th. The 56th will take over theirs at AUBIGNY when they come in. The 56th came in this morning. D.A.Q.M.G. called about feeding the 56th Artillery when they come in, and transport for them. Captain Graham not looking at all well though he does not complain. Reply to letter requested that Captain Strathy be taken out of the Train and returned to Canada. O.C. Supply Column called to get 10 loaders at Railhead in morning, ordered 4 from Headquarters Company and 2 each of others. Captain McCallum talked to me about his N.C.O's. 56th came in minus rations. S.S.O. fixed them up. Another case of lack of foresight. Heavy

### Page 4 — 5–11 November 1916 (Sheet No. 85)

> **November 5 (continued).** — gale, rain in evening.
>
> **November 6.** — S.S.O. gone to see about hay, straw and coal, Bedding. Adjutant to Railhead. Inspected No. 3 Company, much work has been done in this Company's lines, all are very clean outside mud of course.
>
> **November 7.** — All day at F.G.C.M. Two 6 wagons came from 1st Army Auxiliary Coy.. Very wet all day, some very heavy rain.
>
> **November 8.** — Saw Captain Oliphant re my recommending him for attachment to "Q" Branch, I believe he would make an excellent D.A.Q.M.G., and he is agreeable. Pack Train not left St Pol yet 9-30, so everything will be late. Again on an adjourned Court Martial at ECOIVRES. Major Wood rang up to say we could draw roofing for recreation hut. Recommended Captain Oliphant for course at "Q". 6 wagons from Advanced Horse Transport Depot arrived to day. Wet, windy and stormy.
>
> **November 9.** — S.S.O. went to 56th Artillery to arrange about rations. Y.M.C.A. saw me about their rations, also coal oil, carbide, coal etc., explained situation. Saw Senior Chaplain re Recreation Hut, and indented for repairing material. Tried to arrange for a service on Sunday. Had a long talk with Captain Hennessy on his return from leave where he saw Acting Minister of Militia. S.S.O. and I had long conversation re the shortage of coal, especially taking into account the excessive number of Baths coal has to be supplied to. Very clear and fine all day.
>
> **November 10.** — Saw Captain Church re damage to railway gates claim, also Captain Oliphant, and answered repudiating any liability on any one as it was a pure accident, owing to the absence of any lights on the gates. I am disgusted at the way the French put in any and every claim, and the grasping way they go after it, by such means they do not rise in our estimation. Lecture by V.O. on common ailments, how to treat and cure them. Spoke to Major Freeman and all Officers of Headquarters Company, pointing out that letters from the Adjutant came as from me and are not to be treated as personal letters from him.
>
> **November 11.** — Reason Lahore detachment could not be located, was that they moved off without being relieved

### Page 5 — 11–15 November 1916 (Sheet No. 86)

> **November 11 (continued).** — and without proper orders, they therefore cannot expect us to chase them with rations. S.S.O. gone to D.D.S. & T.
>
> **November 12.** — Saw Sgt. Curran re unpaid bill, gave it to him to settle and return same to me reporting action taken. Gave Tomney an interview, can [uncertain] sanction his being transferred back, and advised him to submit another application for his commission. Saw Major Freeman re qualification to sit on F.G.C.M. before wiring his name to Divisional Headquarters. Sanctioned long boots to be worn on Church Parade instead of puttees previously ordered — low boots had been returned. Capt. LeCroix came to dinner and I gave him a letter to take back to Colonel Simson. Dull but fine.
>
> **November 13.** — Wrote Jaques and Colonel McRae. Called at Divisional Headquarters saw Capt. Hennessy re position he and Capts. Church and Oliphant are shown in Army List, wrote about same. S.S.O. gone to meet Lt. [uncertain] to see report of his detached duties. 20,000 kilos of straw somewhere on the line and none in at Railhead to day, this is some management? and in view of the fact that horses are only getting 6 lbs. of hay is serious. I cannot imagine why the hay ration was not cut in the [uncertain] time when we could get green feed at roadside instead of waiting until now when horses have much more trying conditions to put up with. Are there good reasons or is it again "someone has blundered" Tried case from No. 4 Company, 14 days F.P.I. Fine.
>
> **November 14.** — S.S.O. gone to BETHUNE, asked him to call in at Division for me. Visited Headquarters. Inspected remounts, received quite a good class. Distributed same. Fire Alarm sounded at Headquarters Company to test out arrangements. I will test them out again this week and time it. Colonel Simson came and we had a long talk on matters, he agrees with me about the inadvisability of clipping horses. Fine but cold.
>
> **November 15.** — Railhead and on to Division. Took up Acting D.A.Q.M.G. and he, I and S.S.O. went to trenches to inspect Stores, must have walked between 12 & 15 miles, and then did not do all I wanted. Home in time to have a lunch tea, expect to feel a wee bit stiff in morning. M.O. neglected to deliver his lecture, and called him for his neglect. Asked all O.C's to meet me at 2-30 tomorrow and talk over Xmas. Captain McCallum asked for 24 hours leave, but refused pending his reason. Weather still holding up.

### Page 6 — 16–19 November 1916 (Sheet No. 87)

> **November 16.** — Major Freeman to hospital for slight operation on his toe, ingrowing nail. Captain McCallum gone on errand for S.S.O., and will be back tomorrow. Called O.C's and conference how to arrange for men to spend their extra 50 francs pay at Xmas. Chaplain called about a permanent troop for entertainment of Division. Sent for S/Sgt. O'Malley and he interviewed him re taking charge. Fine and clear all day, frost at night.
>
> **November 17.** — Inspected Headquarters Company, a great deal of good work has been done since this Company took over from the 60th. All the horse shelters have been erected, and most of it roofed over. Was particularly pleased at the cleanliness and tidiness of all Billets, Cook Houses, & lines. Sounded a surprise fire alarm, and every man was smartly at his post. This Company has, from old material constructed a very neat and handy cart with fire escape, which was quickly manned and on the supposed fire centre. It took just one minute for every man to be on his post with escape and water buckets. Paraded Major Freeman in front of General re a Court Martial he presided over. This good fellowship and soft weariness is not soldiering, and it is the hardest thing in the world to drive into the minds of O.C's. Frost and cold wind.
>
> **November 18.** — Railhead changed to day to AUBIGNY and refilling at 9 a.m. Called at Divisional Headquarters with S.S.O., and discussed with the A.A. & Q.M.G. the taking away of the 21st Reserve Park. Arrangements made to turn over six motor lorries to haul the bulk stuff from Railhead, and by this means we can just manage; 1st Line calling at Refilling Point for their rations. Coal seems to be the great problem, and the S.S.O. is kept worrying. Horses look none too well on their reduced rations, and if continued must somehow avoid long hauls or excessive work. Sir George Perley, Acting Minister visited front. Early morning frost, later snow which turned to rain.
>
> **November 19.** — As morning opened very wet I ordered gum boots to be worn at Church Parade. Captain McCallum reported Captain Strathy again in trouble. He will come before me again tomorrow. S.S.O. gone to Corps to see about Coal Supply. A.D.V.S. called, he and V.O. went round lines during the parade. Choir first time performed and service greatly improved. Wet and cold.

### Page 7 — 20–23 November 1916 (Sheet No. 88)

> **November 20.** — Had Captain Strathy before me on charge of disobedience of orders, evidence was so conclusive and his excuse so weak, I decided to put him up for Court Martial, and detailed the Adjutant to take a summary of evidence forthwith. Adjutant of 1st Train called and talked over certain matters. A.A. & Q.M.G. called and walked over the advisability of changing refilling points nearer Brigades. Strongly advised no change on the ground that 1st Line Transport should get a longer haul than they already have, to keep horses in condition, shortening their haul means lengthening Train haul, and on short rations this is not advisable. Train transport at Railhead looking very good. Inspected 1st Line Transport of 8th and 10th Field Ambulance, former much improved, latter has put in a great amount of work on stables and standings. Dry and cold.
>
> **November 21.** — Six wagons from 2nd Reserve Park to report to Train for duty, this will be a help. Ordered [uncertain] to Headquarters Company. Called at No. 2 Company looked through their order book; inspected where canteen was to be set up. Lines and Billets very clean and tidy. Called at Divisional Headquarters. Talked over objections to moving refilling points, Captain Strathy's Court-Martial; claim for damage to Railway gates etc., Consultation re purchase of Canteen stores suitable for Xmas gifts. Officers put up some 5,000 francs, and Captains Graham and Church got Corps permission to go and purchase outside area. I am glad of this because populace squeeze the boys dry, some things like lace handkerchiefs the boys like to send home to their wives have gone up 250% over the price they were two years ago. Fine all day.
>
> **November 22.** — Captains Graham and Church gone on their purchasing expedition. Order for vaccination republished in Train Orders to day, also M.O. to hold two sick parades to day, in early morning and 2 p.m. A.D.V.S. gone home to take up an appointment at Shorncliffe. Sorry, he served the Train well. Mr Sanders returned from his Paris leave and duly reported. He says there are very few British Officers in Paris. Service good at Hotels and meals reasonable. The Grand Opera was packed the night he went. M.O. delivered his lecture to day and think as I may I cannot collect anything in his twenty five minutes of advantage to remember. Clear and fine.
>
> **November 23.** — Captains Graham and Church back from making their purchases of Xmas stores for Canteen suitable for Xmas gifts.

### Page 8 — 23–29 November 1916 (Sheet No. 89)

> **November 23 (continued).** — No. 4 Company was paid at 1-30 and by 2-15 Captain Church reported every article sold out, and he paid back money borrowed for the original purchase. Divisional Headquarters. Heard case from No. 3 Company, acquitted same.
>
> **November 24.** — S.S.O.'s car skidded into roadway crossing post, and smashed, had to be sent to workshop, no fault of driver. Captain Oliphant reported back and said there were crowds of Officers at Shorncliffe of C.A.S.C., seemingly doing little or nothing. Tried A/Cpl. (sanitary man) of No. 4 Company overstaying his leave, soaked him 21 days, and reduced to Private. S.S.O. got horrid cold and looking cheap.
>
> **November 25.** — A.A. & Q.M.G. rang me up and asked me what sort of an A.D.V.S. Captain Hamore [uncertain] would make, and I gave it as my opinion that he would be no good as such. Had a case of a man from parade and drunk; must stop drunkenness and gave him 14 days F.P.I and forfeits 20 days pay. This is the third case in a week. Wet and cold.
>
> **November 26.** — S.M. Courtney came before me to day on charge of drunkenness, no evidence sufficient to convict and dismissed case. The General called, inspected Office, Horses, Harness, and wagons also lines of Headquarters Company and No. 3 Company and expressed himself as pleased with what he saw. Morning wet but cleared towards evening and inclined to freeze.
>
> **November 27.** — Tried one of Headquarters men, wrong charge and that one not proven. Spoke to O.C. about it not happening again. S.S.O., Adjutant and I went to BETHUNE saw Purchasing Board. Went round to Officers Clothing Department for shirts and handkerchiefs, none of the latter in stock and wrong sizes in shirts. Fine all day, rain in evening.
>
> **November 28.** — S.S.O. and I to Railhead and Divisional Headquarters. Convoy in very good shape, and much pleased with the whole turnout. Asked for leave of absence to get married, got papers to fill in for months leave, it will be nine months since my last. Filled in and forwarded papers. Disposed of case remanded for me to deal with. Dull and foggy.
>
> **November 29.** — Cleared out of Office for G.C.M. to be held there. Owing to late arrival of one of the Court it did not open until 12-30 or there about. Tried case for No. 2 Company, severe reprimand

### Page 9 — 29–30 November 1916 (Sheet No. 90)

> **November 29 (continued).** — met the case. Everything upset owing to so many illnesses hanging about and lateness of Court sitting, had to go out for meals, and mess round in case I was called for evidence. No inspection of Dumps by General as was expected. Case finished 8-15 and 9 p.m. before President left and we could get a meal. Heavy fog and rain all day.
>
> **November 30.** — Major Freeman asked if he wishes to take his leave before me. Captain Oliphant came in about claim, and I advised him to wait and see what move other side will make. Six remounts came in and of a good class. Interviewed Mr Bayliffe W.O. re reason for his not having his commission after passing all the tests, and being kept away from his unit for months. Month's leave not put forward one for 15 days was. Cold and raw.
>

- 5 November, 11 November, 23 November and 29 November each appear twice, once at the foot of one sheet and once at the head of the next, with the entry continuing across the break; all are transcribed as written.
