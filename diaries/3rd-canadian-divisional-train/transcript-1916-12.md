## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "F.P.I" = Field Punishment No. 1; "C.R.A." = Commander Royal Artillery; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer; "W.O." = Warrant Officer. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets is the unit name printed at the top left. The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the December sheets is read as [uncertain] (see open questions).

## Pages 3–8 — Diary text (1–31 December 1916)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed footer reads "1875 Wt. W593/826 1,000,000 4/15 J.B.C. & A. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118." throughout.

### Page 3 — 1–6 December 1916 (Sheet No. 90)

> **December 1.** — Promulgation of Captain Strathy's sentence, reprimanded. Filling in many papers and documents, going over and correcting Lecture. Drawing up orders for the Orderly Officers. Lecture on Russia by Pardrie, and a very good lecture too. Cold, dull and raw. Lieut. Paul Sweard in train accident on 24th at GEZAINCOURT, escaped unhurt.
>
> **December 2.** — Lieut. Reed not yet arrived, but he keeps phoning up from different and extraordinary places for a car to be sent to fetch him. I am curious to see what sort of a bird he really is. Captain Phillips reported back from leave last night, and gave me all sorts of Canadian news from England. Caught a chill at lecture and cannot get warm to day, feeling out of sorts, but why worry. Cold and raw.
>
> **December 3.** — Wet and muddy, ordered long boots for Church Parade, a good Church Parade, Parson, Sermon and Song also good. Colonel Webb, O.C. 4th Cdn. Train, his S.S.O. and Capt. Burnett came in at 10 p.m. starving, gave them a good hot feed to cheer them on their way. Had long talk with Webb re successor to his S.S.O. going home. Will spare Capt. McCallum if it means promotion, he will make an excellent S.S.O. Long letter from W.O. Bayliffe re non granting of his Commission after passing test, will take his case up. O.C. Headquarters Company gone on leave, Capt. Phillips acting for him. Cold, inclined to rain.
>
> **December 4.** — Answered in full claim of 1st Line Transport re their excessive work. Asked by letter that Bayliffe's case be again considered. Divided up a great lot of gifts dried fruit, magazines socks, and several personal parcels delivered. Usual routine. Wet and cold.
>
> **December 5.** — Forwarded to A.A. & Q.M.G. report how I proposed to carry on when frost regulations come into force. More gifts arrived, socks as usual. O.C. No. 3 Coy., 2nd Train came to lunch, fetched down one of our men. Inspected wagon and team Convalescent Camp, and much surprised at bad condition. It has been doing an average of 21 miles per day, and hours so long that Driver has had no time to care for his Horses and Harness or wagon. Uncensored letter sent back from England for me to deal with, am getting information. Very cold, wet and windy.
>
> **December 6.** — Saw Driver L. Spencer for taking letter home and posting it, he admitted it and I placed him in arrest. Tried funny little Irish man from No. 3 Company for overstaying pass and breaking

### Page 4 — 6–9 December 1916 (Sheet No. 91)

> **December 6 (continued).** — arrest, admitted everything. When asked if he had any excuse or anything to say, replied "no I don't wish to tell any lies, I am guilty" 28 days F.P.No.I. Admonished Spencer and recommended his Commission be not granted, the man swore there was only private communications of a family nature to his wife. All things considered this punishment should meet the case. The man giving him the letter I gave 28 days F.P.No.I. Had guard on reserve rations up and gave them a dressing. Fog, cold and raw.
>
> **December 7.** — 1st Train rang up and informed us move of Artillery to us postponed indefinitely, good work this, when we have their supplies already on the dump. Sanitary Officer called, and got from me particulars of the refilling points. Colonel Simson came and had tea, discussed several points of importance, and later in private discussed others. Cold, raw and misty.
>
> **December 8.** — Capt. Graham and Lieut. Sweard reported back from leave, all the time they had good weather, and reports at home are common that surplus Officers are to come out here as Lieutenants or go back to Canada and resume their civil life until called for. Lieut. Sheard looks none too well after his railway accident, and his account of it is not good, having not even an axe on the train to get out those pinned down, had there been men would have been saved a horrible death. A.A. & Q.M.G. phoned up about laundry shutting down because of no coal, and S.S.O. says he had none to send, they use 17 tons a week, and from all accounts do little or nothing for such expenditure, their system simply eats coal, and it is so badly needed by the men at the present moment. Someone should take hold of the coal supply and see we get a full quantity and not 30% dross as at present supplied. We are paying (I presume the French do not give it to us for nothing) for coal and not dust, and the shunting charges should be cut or eliminated. Weather just the same.
>
> **December 9.** — Captain McCallum and Lieut. Franklin whose leave began to night started yesterday. Got in touch with them at Boulogne and ordered them back, they will not now be allowed on leave if I can stop it, cannot tell what possessed them, they will spend all the leave they get travelling up and down on the train, and I hope they enjoy it. Our General saw Capt. McCallum's convoy on the road and wrote me congratulating me on its good condition. O.S.C. have purchased live turkeys for Xmas at 25 frs. a piece. McCallum got late of leave altered. Franklin reported back, and
>
> I asked for him to be paraded in front of me in morning.

### Page 5 — 10–18 December 1916 (Sheet No. 92)

> **December 10.** — Lieut. Franklin paraded and gave me his reason for going away before the specified time. Recommended he be paraded in front of A.A. & Q.M.G. and leave cancelled, wrote to this effect. I honestly think he did not think he was doing a serious act, and in consideration of the frank way he owned to his error, and his continued good work I think I have taken the correct course. Got a bad touch of Flue. Wet.
>
> **December 11.** — Feeling rotten. Colonel Webb called.
>
> **December 12.** — Still feeling rotten, horrible running at the nose and very weak. Put one man up for F.G.C.M. and gave another 14 days F.P.I and 20 days pay. Glad to go to bed. Snowing.
>
> **December 13.** — Feeling a little better to day except for a harsh dry irritating cough. Capt. Strathy gone to hospital. Dull.
>
> **December 14.** — Cough worried me all night and feel rotten. Cold and wet.
>
> **December 15.** — Had much better night and feeling better. Court Martial here this morning. Capt. Graham back from leave. Sent in report re Train wagons not pulling rations up to C.R.A. Barn. Wet.
>
> **December 16.** — Cough so bad that I was terribly sick middle of night, and feel much shaken. Major H. Greer rang me up from 1st Division where he is back again about Tomney. Rang Division up about a letter from P.M. which most mysteriously disappeared. Major Freeman reported back from leave. Clear morning, aeroplanes active, clouded and dull later. F.G.C.M. of yesterday squashed because of some irregularity. Cold, raw and damp.
>
> **December 17.** — Paymaster and Adjutant gone on leave, Lieut. Sweard will act as Paymaster and Lieut. Eric Fisher for Adjutant. Major Freeman took Church Parade for me, did not think it wise to turn out, cough still giving me trouble, and weather foggy. Cold and raw, and there is a good deal of this influenza cold running through the Train. Pardrie called in to see me after service. Dull, cold and misty. Leave postponed for 24 hours.
>
> **December 18.** — Had best night for some time, cough less dry and hacking. Instructional demonstration at refilling points for the benefit of a party coming for that purpose. Inspected team of horses

### Page 6 — 18–23 December 1916 (Sheet No. 93)

> **December 18 (continued).** — sent here by 2nd Reserve Park to be shod. They were thin, poor, unclipped, ungroomed and uncared for, hair off in patches and in places sore. Reported condition to A.D.V.S., who came and inspected them, Train V.O. says nothing infectious but will take no chance on this, and inspected them, consequences might be serious. Tried case from No 4 Company, "Drunk on Duty", clean sheet and O.C. gave him the best of characters, first offence of any kind, 14 days F.P., 21 days pay should freshen up his desire to do right in future. A.D.V.S missed seeing horses, they had been shod and gone so we will try and look them up tomorrow. Cold, raw, dull.
>
> **December 19.** — Turned out of office for F.G.C.M. on the little Irishman to be held here. Really feeling better today. Railhead and Divisional Headquarters, also looked in on No 3 Company. Must be feeling effects of flue for I feel tired out after so little. Morning broke fine clear and frosty but turned to snow in afternoon.
>
> **December 20.** — Had O.C., Hd Qrs Coy and Capt. Phillips in and went over question of iron rations, bandoliers rifles etc. of men returned from 4th Train. Sent reply to A.A. & Q.M.G. re the desire of Y.M.C.A. to take over the running of Canteens, am not in favour of it. Refused to parade Capt. Strathy before G.O.C. unless he gives me good and sufficient reason for doing so. Clear and bright all day.
>
> **December 21.** — Day opened dull. Made arrangements for Capt. Oliphant to act as S.S.O., Capt Church and Capt. Phillips as S.S.Os during the absence of the regular officers on leave. Asked Major Wood to hold no service here on Sunday to enable me to get all the necessary work done for the men to have an easy day on Christmas Day. Dull and wet all day and very cold.
>
> **December 22.** — Ordered that no man doing F.P. No 1 be tied up on Xmas eve, day or Boxing day. Capt Church reported non-return of wagon detailed to Town Major, AUBIGNY. M.O. made arrangements for his work to be carried on during his absence on leave. He has an influenza cold badly. Weather cold, wet and raw.
>
> **December 23.** — Capt. Strathy reported back from hospital. I explained to him why I could not parade him before the General. He was under the impression, so he said, that the General could return him to England as medically unfit, this I pointed out could only be done on a Medical Certificate. The R.T.O is agreeable that for Xmas Day refilling at Railhead be expedited one hour, this

### Page 7 — 23–27 December 1916 (Sheet No. 94)

> **December 23 (continued).** — I have put in orders. Explained to Major Freeman his duties during my absence on leave while he is acting O.C. Train. Capt. McCallum reported back from leave. Major Nyblett [uncertain] M.O. and Lieut. Richards gone today for their leave. Replied to D.A.A. & Q.M.G. re the two wagons left at Sausage Valley. Cold, wet and high wind.
>
> **December 24.** — Leave out. Convoys at Railhead looking extremely well, wagons, harness and horses all in splendid condition and well cared for. The Train is certainly responding to my call and they are a credit. Escort and deserter returned. Called in at Divisional Hd. Qrs. and had chat to all heads on various matters, especially A.A. & Q.M.G. re Capt. Strathy. Harrods parcels for Xmas dinner arrived. The sooner I get my leave and a rest the better, am not satisfied with my slow recovery. Day opened bright but clouded over towards evening.
>
> **December 25.** — Xmas Day opened blustery, high wind and cold rain. Very excellent arrangements made in all Companies to give the men as much an easy day as possible and an enjoyable Xmas, which they have well earned. Following Xmas wires received. "To 3rd Canadian Divisional Train The Corps Commander wishes all ranks the best of luck" Also from Major General A. E. W. Turner, V.C., C.B., D.S.O., "All ranks in England send Seasons Greetings to all ranks in the Canadian Corps". Visited all the boys and saw them at their dinners, Gave a short address to each, reviewing the past eleven months work and the progress of the Train. Much credit is due to all those who were responsible for the admirable arrangements made. The unity and harmony throughout the whole Train is very marked and fills me with admiration and pride in my Train.
>
> **December 26.** — In spite of all the licence allowed, not one single case before me and the whole Train has vindicated the trust I placed in them to keep within reasonable bounds. Everyone at their posts and duties, some look a bit cheap but all cheery. Fairly clear in morning, wet later.
>
> **December 27.** — Driver Moran, No 2 Coy, came up before me on a charge of desertion, after hearing evidence I remanded accused for F.G.C.M. and instructed the Adjutant to immediately take a summary of evidence. Arranged about Leave Warrants for self and Lieut Fisher. Lieut. Franklin gone to hospital with slight lung trouble, nothing serious and he should be back to duty in a week. Weather on the whole fine turning to frost in evening.

### Page 8 — 28–31 December 1916 (Sheet No. 95)

> **December 28.** — Sergts Blair and Curran to report to Division for clerical work, ordered limber from No 3 Coy for 10 a.m. tomorrow. Paymaster and Lieut Grant returned from leave. Early hours of morning cold and wet.
>
> **December 29.** — Had interview with Lieut Graham re special leave and recommended it. Paymaster taken over his duties again from Lieut Sweard who did acting Paymaster very well. Adjutant going over evidence in Moran's case, and fixed it up alright. Very cold and wet. Lance Corporal Cook who died yesterday in No. 4 [uncertain] Casualty Clearing Station from pneumonia was buried [uncertain] afternoon at Aubigny cemetery. Officers and men from Train attended.
>
> **December 30.** — Lieut. Franklin's condition not serious and he is improving. Major Freeman in orders as taking over from me while on leave and Capt. Phillips assumes his duties for same period. Heard case of Farrier Staff Sergt. of No. 3 Coy. and severely reprimanded him. Paraded Capt. Strathy in front of G.O.C., was struck with the easiness of his manner. Poor Strathy, he seems to think me is it and that the Train will loose one of its most competent officers if he goes. However the G.O.C. happened to have all the correspondence and supported my opinion. Rain, off and on.
>
> **December 31.** — Have everything in good order to turn over to Major Freeman and can go on leave feeling easy in my mind. But pity the weather is wet again.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *[signature — [uncertain]] Lieut-Colonel, / O.C. 3rd Canadian Divl. Train*
