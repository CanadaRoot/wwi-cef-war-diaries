# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — February 1918

- **Unit:** Headquarters, 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, 3rd Canadian Division (Canadian Army Service Corps)
- **Month:** February 1918 (Volume 25) — this volume covers 1 to 28 February 1918
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** February 1918, p. 1 – p. 9 (image files page-0247 – page-0255 of the diary)
- **February 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–9): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–28 February, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S." The Place and Hour columns are blank throughout; the place of the Train is given in the text (Bruay until 20 February, La Targette from 20 February). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. Abbreviations as written: "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "D.D.S. & T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "A.A.Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "F.P. No. 1" = Field Punishment No. 1; "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "a/" = acting; "C.S.M." = Company Sergeant Major; "C.Q.M.S." = Company Quartermaster Sergeant; "S.S.M." = Squadron Sergeant Major (here, senior warrant rank in the Train); "Farr." = Farrier.

## Pages 4–9 — Diary text (1–28 February 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed footer reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."

### Page 4 — 1–5 February 1918 (Sheet No. 8)

> **1/2/18.** — Cold raw morning. Major Scott and Capt: Graham gone on leave. Visited No.2 Company, all going well.
>
> **2/2/18.** — a/CSM. Ridgeway and a/CQMS. Malthouse paraded before me and I explained to them reason for my order to change places. they expressed themselves as satisfied and would do their best to make things go. Had one young Officer before me re a dishonoured cheque. He explained how it happened and satisfied me, so I wrote forwarding his answer in writing strongly recommending that the matter be now allowed to drop. Fine.
>
> **3/2/18.** — Called in at No.2 Company with M.O., whose horse went lame and has to be re-shod. Fine and sunny.
>
> **4/2/18.** — Fogely [uncertain] morning. Rode out and inspected Divisional Headquarters transport. Wrote A.D.V.S. re an order given them to rug up day and night. Had Lieut. Reed up for absenting himself from his Dump without permission. Ordered him back to Transport duties and change with Lieut: Ferg.
>
> **5/2/18.** — Fine morning. Distance travelled by Train wagons in use for week ending Feby.3rd, 4373 miles. No.1 Company 12.3, No.2 Company 8.5, No.3 Company 13.0, No.4 Company 8.2 average for week per wagon in use 8 miles. Leaving out water carts would add another 2 miles to distances travelled. Longest distance any one wagon, 20 miles. Capt: Phillips and Lieut: Higgins back from leave. Inspected No.3 Company they are busy building splinter protection, without lumber. Inspected 4th C.M.R. transport. Horses clean and in good working condition. Harness clean, but too hard, wagons good running condition, but should be cleaner and more tidy and better swept out. Went on to 5th C.M.R. Although horses are in fair working condition, stable management is bad. Horses in small numbers in billets all over the place, which is hard to supervise; harness very hard. Horses not well groomed, dirty docks. Empty nose-bags left on horses, and had to hunt for line orderly. The horse lines I visited were in a dirty untidy state, paper, tins, clippings of horses lying around.

### Page 5 — 5–9 February 1918 (Sheet No. 9)

> **5/2/18. Ctd.** — The Corporal I saw seemed more anxious to make excuses than to remedy defects. Wagons need more attention.
>
> **6/2/18.** — Dull morning. Adjutant inspected No.4 Company for me yesterday and reports Company in a very satisfactory condition. All the Train convoys I saw on the road yesterday and inspected looked extremely well turned out, very clean, smart, and the march discipline was very good indeed. I have inspected more of the new hoof plates and am more than ever of opinion they are a mistake; cannot find anyone who has a good word for them. I inspected No.1 Company and all in very good shape. Wagons from Artillery units kept in by O.C. to repair, and they certainly required it.
>
> **7/2/18.** — Small fine rain but mild. Applied for seats for Capt. Oliphant and self at re-union dinner on 14th inst: Inspected 4 remounts came up to-day. I would not call them HD but LD, and very poor specimens at that, 1 I sent to No.2 Company, and 3 to No.3 Company.
>
> **8/2/18.** — Had a return of Sciatica and am keeping quiet to-day. Very wet.
>
> **9/2/18.** — Sent Capt. Church to 43rd Battalion to judge at Transport competition as car was not available and M.O. would not let me ride. Copy of letter sent from General for my information, Begins — "3rd Cdn. Division A.G—26, Canadian Corps A. 3rd Cdn. Divisional Train. "Major H.J. Heasley has acted as Senior Supply Officer of this Division since I have been in Command, over 1½ years. I have found him in every way an excellent Officer, pleasant to work with, energetic and capable. I consider him well fitted to command a Train. He was very loath to leave for Canada and it was only very urgent reasons that compelled him to do so. I would like to place on record my appreciation of his services, and I consider it in the interests of the Corps that his services be made use of in France as soon as it is available. Signed J.L. Lipsett, Major General Commanding 3rd Cdn. Division, 31/1/18". Ends. I am glad the General wrote this letter. Major Heasley is well deserving of it. Dull, wet and muddy.

### Page 6 — 10–15 February 1918 (Sheet No. 10)

> **10/2/18.** — Another dull wet day. Visited No.4 Company and heard charge against a/CSM. Coward and a/Farr. S/Sgt. Goodrich. Dismissed the former and reverted the latter to his permanent rank of Corporal. Adjutant called in at Supply Dump to see Mr. Ferg and get some information from him. Capt. Fisher and Lieut: Grant called and we discussed several matters of moment. Cleared to fine afternoon.
>
> **11/2/18.** — M.O. has just informed me that Capt. Clapp the V.O. has had a slight paralytic stroke and he is taking him to Hospital. Capt. de Graves and Capt. Richards returned from leave. Arranged to parade one of my Captains before General tomorrow afternoon.
>
> **12/2/18.** — Received letter from O.C. 43rd Battalion thanking me for lending Captain Church as judge in his transport competition, and complimenting him on the efficient way he carried out the duties. For week ending 10th, mileage covered by the Train, 3700 miles, average per wagon per day, 11 miles, greatest distance any one wagon, 13 miles. Paraded my Officer before the General; he loses three months' leave. The General could not have been nicer.
>
> **13/2/18.** — Recommended SSM. Kelly, E., No.3083, W.O.Class 1 for the Medal Militaire, in reply to wire A.652 from 3rd Cdn. Division '4:' [uncertain]. No.3 Company called. Very wet.
>
> **14/2/18.** — Hear Capt. Clapp is likely to be evacuated to Canada. Again my inspection of 1st Line transport of 43rd postponed. The re-union dinner presided over by our G.O.C. at which about a hundred sat down went off most successfully, the General making a capital speech. All five coloured patches were observed, showing how the 1st Division have helped to build up successive Divisions. Captains Oliphant and Church came with me. We dined at 8.00 p.m. and got back to our quarters at BRUAY about 1.15 a.m. Weather fine.
>
> **15/2/18.** — Got a note from Major Heasley and from it I judge he sailed on the 9th or 10th. Getting ready to move back into the line. Fine but colder.

### Page 7 — 16–21 February 1918 (Sheet No. 11)

> **16/2/18.** — Hard frost last night. Visited and inspected Transport Lines of the 43rd Battn. and delivered a lecture on Care of Horses, Harness and Wagons. Afterwards inviting questions On the whole, the transport was in very good condition, had only to find fault with very minor points. Colonel Urquhart congratulated himself on having his transport in such good shape.
>
> **17/2/18.** — Cold and frosty. Nothing to relate except all arrangements for pending move going on smoothly, and a bombing Hun came over us at night.
>
> **18/2/18.** — Adjutant gone to LA TARGETTE to look after things at that end until move is completed. Major Scott and Capt. Graham reported back from leave. Capt. McLeod called in, he expects any minute to get orders to report to me for duty. Cold, hard frost.
>
> **19/2/18.** — For week ending February 17th distance travelled by the Train Companies was as follows:— in average miles per wagon per day, No.1 Company 8.8 miles per day, No.2 Company 8.7, No.3 Company 5.8, No.4 Company 7. Average for Train for week 7.7, miles per day. Capt. McLeod reported for duty. Sent note to Capt. Church No.1 Company telling him Capt. McLeod would take over Command and he was to show him everything in his power, remain with him for a day or two until he got the grasp of things. Had a long confidential talk with Capt. McLeod, and expressed my wishes about excessive drinking, gambling etc. and anything in this line that was in any way detrimental to the best interests of the service, I would without compunction, much as I would hate to do it, step on with a heavy hand. He knows at starting where he stands, and I am sure will bear my talk in mind and give me of his best.
>
> **20/2/18.** — Left BRUAY with last two Companies of the Train at 8-15 am. and got established at LA TARGETTE same afternoon where we took over from the 2nd Train. Cold frosty morning, turning to rain in afternoon.
>
> **21/2/18.** — On receipt of wire from DDS. & T. First Army, reported at their H.Q., one hour's run by car, to be received with — "Oh, didn't you get the wire post-poning conference until 22nd"

### Page 8 — 21–24 February 1918 (Sheet No. 12)

> **21/2/18. Cntd.** — Two solid hours wasted, plus driver's time and petrol.
>
> **22/2/18.** — Meeting of Officers Commanding Trains at 1st Army H.Q. Discussed ways and means of economy, but my impression on leaving was that no suggestion of any importance was advanced, and some very foolish ones were propounded. For example, Train N.C.O's should get bicycles instead of horses, or that they should ride on wagons with drivers, and it surely is obvious to do either would seriously handicap them performing efficient work. Called in at Corps H.Q. on Colonel Simpson but found him out. Adjutant and S.S.O. gone to Division H.Q. and on their way back will call in on Col. Simpson, who I hear, is off to England again tomorrow. Wet, windy and cold.
>
> **23/2/18.** — Capt. W.G. Walks, V.O., reported for duty. Gave him the same kind of talk I give to every new Officer joining the Unit, so he knows where he stands. Inspected Nos. 2 and 4 Companies and noted the land they have broken up. In my opinion they are breaking up more than they can keep in good cultivation if all is planted. However, ploughing will sweeten the ground even if it is not planted and will do the land good. Word came through that Major Scott is transferred from my Train where he has been S.S.O. to 2nd Canadian Divisional Train as O.C. and that Major Adams comes from 4th Train to me. I propose therefore to post him to No.1 Company and put Capt. McLeod in as S.S.O. He has done this work before and I know he can fill the bill.
>
> **24/2/18.** — Dull cold but dry, and ploughing going on steadily on Divisional Farm. The Agricultural Officer and A.A.Q.M.G. came and inspected land ploughed up. Major Scott left mid-day to take Command of 2nd Train, Capt. McLeod takes over his duties as SSO. Mileage for week ending today, ground covered by Train 6011 miles for the week, average distance per wagon per Company, No.1, 8.0, No.2, 13.8, No.3, 15.6, No.4, 10.2, average per train wagon for week, 11.9 miles. This includes move to LA TARGETTE from BRUAY area. Total distance, 6,011 miles covered. For month of January, mileage covered, all duties, 22,354.

### Page 9 — 25–28 February 1918 (Sheet No. 13)

> **25/2/18.** — Major Adams reported for duty and will be posted to No.1 Company as O.C. Interviewed men re claims for working pay. Wet stopped cultivation of land all morning but a little was able to be done late afternoon.
>
> **26/2/18.** — Night cold and frosty. Lieut. Gwynne reported back from leave. Sanctioned Adjutant taking a man from No.1 Company into office to understudy his and S.S.O.'s clerk. Notification that SSM. O'Connor is posted here vice Bayliffe invalided. Tried a/Cpl. Milliken of No.3 Company for neglecting to obey an order and reduced him to his permanent rank as Driver. It is a strange thing, but it seems more than hard to get good N.C.O's. I have tried out some good drivers but all to no purpose, and it is the same all the way up to rank of W.O's.
>
> **27/2/18.** — Fine and mild, but poor visibility. Inspected 1st Line Transport of 9th Brigade accompanied by O.C., No.4 Company, Train. With the exception of Trench Mortars, Brigade Headquarters, and the Water Carts of 43rd Battalion, all in very good condition. 43rd have benefited by my short lecture. Tried case at No.3 Company, and accused elected to be tried by F.G.C.M., detailed Capt. Phillips to take down the evidence. Inspected cultivated land of the Train.
>
> **28/2/18.** — Dirty day. Tried case at No.3 Company and man elected to be tried by F.G.C.M. Inspected farm work. Met Major Scott of 2nd Train at No.1 Company, and had chat on things in general.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *[signature — [uncertain]] Lieut.-Colonel, / O.C. 3rd Canadian Div. Train*
