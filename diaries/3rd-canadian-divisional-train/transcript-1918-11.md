# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — November 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps (C.A.S.C.), 3rd Canadian Division (styled "H.Q. 3rd Canadian Divl. Train" on the cover)
- **Month:** November 1918 — this volume covers 1 to 30 November 1918
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** November 1918, p. 1 – p. 11 (image files page-0334 – page-0344 of the diary)
- **November 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–11): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–30 November, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" ("INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" not struck through on these sheets). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. Place names are given as written, even where the spelling differs from the standard form (e.g. "ONNAING", "JEMAPPES", "RAISMES", "VALENCIENNES", "CASTEAU", "NIMY"). The 'Flu is written in the original with quotation marks, as "'Flu'". Map references are given as written; no latitude/longitude coordinates are given. "C.I.Bn." = Canadian Infantry Battalion; "C.M.G.C." = Canadian Machine Gun Corps; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "A.A.Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "A.P.M." = Assistant Provost Marshal; "H.Qrs." = Headquarters; "S.S.O." = Staff Supply Officer (as written); "N.C.O." = non-commissioned officer; "D.S.O." = Distinguished Service Order; "C.S.M." = Company Sergeant-Major; "H.P.B." = initials as written. The signature at the foot of the November sheets is read as [uncertain] (see open questions).

## Pages 4–11 — Diary text (1–30 November 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "(A3945. Wt. W3129/M1559. 750,000. 1/17. D.D. & L., Ltd. Forms/C.2118/14."

### Page 4 — 1–7 November 1918 (Sheet No. 1)

> **WALLERS 1-11-18** — Now the news of an Austrian Armistice has come in, but the news has created no surprise, everyone is taking it very quietly. Lunched with O.C., 43rd C.I.Bn., and inspected his transport. Found horses, wagons, and the harness in very good shape indeed. Horses are scattered all over the town in different stables, but signs are not wanting that Transport Officer takes a lively interest in his duties. Went on afterwards to 58th C.I.Bn., and inspected them, they are better housed all in one building. They also are very fair but not as good as the 43rd, leather work in decidedly inferior.
>
> **—do— 2-11-18** — All afternoon inspecting 1st Line Transport, 116th C.I.Bn, 52nd C.I.Bn, & 3rd C.M.G.C. The 1st is decidedly inferior in all other transport, the 52nd very good indeed, and the 3rd C.M.G.C. good. Surroundings of stabling might be kept cleaner.
>
> **—do— 3-11-18** — Inspected transport of No. 1. Train. Excellent shape. 12 Remounts came in and distributed, only one team anything to look at, others very second class. Sent to G.O.C. 9th C.I.Bde, A. Ad. Q.M.G., and O.C. No. 4 Company Train, copies of my report on 1st Line Transport of 9th Brigade.
>
> **—do— 4-11-18** — Meeting of O.s C. [uncertain] Trains at Colonel McAlpine's Office G.H.Q. re the return of Officers to fill establishments in England. Called at 1st Canadian Division Headquarters and brought back A/Sgt Horan, posted to this Train. Office Clerk gone to hospital with the 'Flu'.
>
> **—do— 5-11-18** — First case from No. 4 Company — Gave extra Guards. S.S.O., Car Driver, and Runner, down with 'Flu' Mr Hessey and the Paymaster seriously. S.S.O. of 2nd Canadian Division had car break-down and lunched here.
>
> **—do— 6-11-18** — Three more with 'Flu' sent to hospital. Major McLeod much better and likely to be up in a day or two. I have it now [uncertain] but it seems a mild form and I do not anticipate complications, am taking every precaution. Pay Master bad. Just got a wire that Mr. Rason had died from it. Train H.Qrs. moved from WALLERS to RAISMES, where railhead is. Left WALLERS for RAISMES, S.S.O. left behind with 'Flu'. I got the 'Flu' so has the Paymaster.
>
> **RAISMES 7-11-18** — Moved to VALENCIENNES, on arrival, as certain that Divisional H.Qrs., are likely to move tomorrow to making one bite at a cherry' [uncertain] and went into ONNAING, No. 1. Company putting us up. Major de Graves has the 'Flu' and Capt. Danger, both in a mild form. Received wire that Lieut. Rason died from it and wrote to Lady Rason. Major McLeod, Lt. Hessey, Lt. Boyd, Lt. Day, and Lt. Gunter, all

### Page 5 — 7–11 November 1918 (Sheet No. 2)

> **ONNAING 7-11-18 (continued)** — all evacuated with 'Flu'. Captain Barlf, Pay Master, is now evacuated. Lieut. George, and Lieut. Glassco, gone to run No. 4 Company, all that Coy's Officers in hospital. Wired Colonel MacAlpine to postpone the calling away of Capt. Graham and Lieut. Gwynne. Can still carry on. I am much better.
>
> **—do— 8-11-18** — All companies move forward again. Damp and wet.
>
> **—do— 9-11-18** — Called at Divisional Headquarters, saw A.A.Q.M.G., and went into the question of transport with him in the event of lorries not being able to get more than a certain distance from Railhead and Railhead getting any further back. Owner of this Chateau came to lunch, a very common man to own such a place. He told us he buried a good stock of wine in the garden, the Germans found it, confiscated it, and fined him 300 francs for hiding it. Captain Graham came in as S.S.O. Captain Wake and I sent cheques for £205.9.7. each for Canadian Victory War Loan, 5½ %. Colonel MacAlpine sent one Subaltern from each of the other Trains to help me out. Major McLeod in hospital.
>
> **ONNAING 10-11-18** — Train H.Qrs. left ONNAING at 10.a.m. and arrived at JEMAPPES 11.15.a.m. and got settled in when a few shells lit in very close. Roads and houses not so bad as I expected. We are only about 4000 yards from MONS. News of the German Emperor's abdication, and his Sons relinquishing all claim to the Throne, just came in, this is surely a sign of the end. Sent H.P.B. a letter to come and see me re returned cheque.
>
> **JEMAPPES 11-11-18** — At 8.a.m. this morning we got the news that the Germans would sign the Armistice at 11.a.m. today. The great strain has been released and the inhabitants are laughing crying and dancing with joy. MONS was captured before daylight and at 3.30.p.m. General Sir Arthur CURRIE made his triumphal entrance. About a Division was drawn up on the Square before the TOWN HALL and as the General rode in, General STEWART who was in command of the Parade, gave the General Salute, the General sitting on his horse facing the boys and proud he must have been of them. He then dismounted and the Mayor, Corporation, and other Dignatories were presented, after which he

### Page 6 — 11 November 1918, continued (Sheet No. 3)

> **MONS 11-11-18 (continued)** — he addressed them and presented as a souvenir his lance with the Canadian Pennant. This brought forth great cheers, at the conclusion of his address he presented some of his Generals and Staff to the Mayor, and with them took up a position and the parade marched past in quarter-column. The enthusiasm of the inhabitants was great and the behaviour and control of themselves wonderful. I was the only one that could be spared to represent the Train. Captain Graham acting S.S.O. and the Adjutant, Lieut. Ferg were busy getting billets as Train H.Qrs. were to stop in MONS. We got well settled spending the first night after the signing of the Armistice in No. 3 Avenue de Mons where the Huns in their advance made use of women and children as a screen between themselves and us. How they had made themselves bitterly hated by all. They have removed all the brass handles and ornaments off the stores.

### Page 7 — 12–18 November 1918 (Sheet No. 4)

> **MONS 12-11-18** — Nothing to relate, settling down. Weather fine.
>
> **—do— 13-11-18** — G.O.C's meeting of Brigadiers and C.O's. He informed us we were going into Germany and told us what he wanted every Officer to do. These views I conveyed to my Officers at a Meeting in the afternoon, adding my own and giving instructions the way I wanted them to set about carrying the General's wishes. Weather foggy, but fine.
>
> **—do— 14-11-18** — No. 1 Company moved from JEMAPPES into MONS. The Division gave a tea and reception in the Town Hall to the inhabitants, and afterwards we took our guests to the Theatre where the 'DUMBELLS' put on "Pinafore" and most creditably they did it. Everyone was highly delighted. Weather fine and sunny, but cold.
>
> **—do— 15-11-18** — General HORNE entered MONS and held a Grand Review in the Square. Many have told me how much they were impressed by the spectacle. The Divisions gave us a reception in the evening.
>
> **—do— 16-11-18** — Thanksgiving Service today and most impressive, another tomorrow in the Cathedral. A fine old Cathedral in the Flamboyant architecture period and took two hundred years to build. A service in it for soldiers should be grand. Reception and Dance at JEMAPPES. Home 3.a.m.
>
> **—do— 17-11-18** — A Meeting at the G.O.'s C. Again he impressed on us some views as at last meeting. He can see an improvement, but I fancy he thinks that we are slow. Paraded one of my Officers (Captain) before the G.O.C. He was to have a pay book and forfeits six months Leave. The General was very fair and could not have been nicer. Captain Fraser returned from leave and looks pale, and pulled down after his attack of 'Flu' when going on Leave. Weather slight frost and fine.
>
> **—do— 18-11-18** — Inspected all the transport of the M.G.Bn., pointed out to them where they could effect much improvement. The care of headstalls and breast collars shows signs of ignorance and neglect, but all are anxious to learn and improve.

> **—do— 19-11-18** — Morning dull and rain. All train Companies getting into very good shape for the 'trek' into Germany. Horses, harness, and wagons looking well, all this done on top of our regular daily work of delivering supplies. This is possible because all Units are in close proximity to Train Companies. Horses doing on an average of 5 miles a day, usually they do just double this mileage.
>
> The G.O.C. unexpectedly called on me to go with him to inspect Train Companies. He gave me rather a surprise at the things he wanted done. However that is not for me to criticise, all that I can do is to try and carry out his wishes. Brass polish is almost impossible to obtain and I want see why [uncertain] I should put my hand in my pocket to pay for it, if it were, and yet I cannot see how I am to carry out orders unless I do. Every paper and polish are not an issue and to carry on without them is an utter impossibility. I dont want to draw on the men's Canteen Fund, as they have been saving for their XMAS Dinner, and already from this fund much saddle soap, polish, and vermilion paint has come. I suppose I must be fleeced and dig into my own pocket, orders are orders, however hard, and my duty is to obey, so that is all there is to be said at present. The G.O.C. did say that the horses were in very good shape, and after all that is the main thing.
>
> **—do— 20-11-18** — Heavy fog all night and morning. General Routine. Inspected Nos. 2. & 3. Companies. In the evening Mr. Estievenart [uncertain], the artist, gave a small reception to us, as a memento of MONS. He gave me two pattern de bon secours jugs [uncertain], one for each of my daughters, and is making a sketch for my wife also as a memento.

### Page 10 — 26–30 November 1918 (Sheet No. 7)

> **MONS 26-11-18** — Weather dull all day. Captain Graham, interpreter, and I went to WATERLOO. Spent a most enjoyable and instructive couple of hours there, and then went on to BRUSSELS. There was little time to go round and see all the places of interest before dark, but even when electrically lit up BRUSSELS is beautiful, many of the buildings from an architectural point of view are gems, and their principal streets are wide, airy, many planted with trees, some of the streets very long and straight. In the art of town planning many lessons can be learnt here. It took us nearly 3½ hours to get home roads in places bad and slippery. At 1.a.m. we managed to reach our billets safely and well, a bit tired and cold but we were glad we went.
>
> **—do— 27-11-18** — No. 4. Company moved out of NIMY to CASTEAU. Before they went tried a minor change. H.M. the King of the Belgians visited MONS today. Stopped just outside the window and I had a good view. He looks well and every inch a soldier. His two sons were with him, the elder a private in the Army, and looked as if he wore issue clothes — what a grand schooling for the future King.
>
> **—do— 28-11-18** — Capt. Fraser, Capt Higgins, and Lieut. Ferg, gone to WATERLOO and BRUSSELS this afternoon, and will not be back until tomorrow. Officers of the 4th Can. Divisional Train coming to the Theatre and afterwards to dinner. Major de Graves reported back. As no papers came with Major de Graves, and no charge against him for the time being, I sent him to his Company under open arrest. Weather dull and wet.
>
> **—do— 29-11-18** — The three Officers returned from BRUSSELS on Time. Weather dull.
>

## Open questions / uncertain items (November 1918)
- The cover reads "Volume 34" and the title page is hand-numbered "34", but the contents page reads "Volume 18 — 11." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number.
- The 7 November entry is split across two sheets: it begins on page 4 (place RAISMES) and continues at the top of page 5 (place ONNAING), ending mid-sentence on page 4 and resuming on page 5; both parts are transcribed as one entry under 7 November. The 11 November entry is likewise split across pages 5 and 6 (place JEMAPPES, then MONS), and the 30 November entry across pages 10 and 11.
- 3 November: "A. Ad. Q.M.G." is as written; it is presumably A.A.Q.M.G. (Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General), but the original is transcribed literally.
- 4 November: "Meeting of O.s C. [uncertain] Trains" — the abbreviation is not read with confidence (presumably "O.C.s Trains", the Officers Commanding Trains).
- 5 November: the sentence "S.S.O., Car Driver, and Runner, down with 'Flu' Mr Hessey and the Paymaster seriously." is as written; the sense is unclear (whether Hessey and the Paymaster are among those down with 'Flu, and how seriously).
- 6 November: "I have it now [uncertain]" — the word is faint and could read "saw"; the sense of the sentence and the closing line ("I got the 'Flu' so has the Paymaster") support "have".
- 6–7 November: the name is spelled "Rason" ("Mr. Rason", "Lieut. Rason", "Lady Rason"); the October diary spells a similar name "Eason" (31 October, "Lt. Eason returned last night from leave"). The two spellings are recorded as written in each month; whether they are the same man is not resolved here.
- 7 November: "to making one bite at a cherry' [uncertain]" — the phrase is as written but its sense is unclear; the entry appears garbled at this point ("on arrival, as certain that Divisional H.Qrs., are likely to move tomorrow to making one bite at a cherry' and went into ONNAING").
- 9 November: "Captain Graham came in as S.S.O." — as written. "Captain Wake" — as written.
- 11 November: "other Dignatories" is spelled as written. "No. 3 Avenue de Mons" is as written.
- 17 November: "He was to have a pay book and forfeits six months Leave" — as written; the officer paraded before the G.O.C. is not named, only "(Captain)".
- 19 November: "and I want see why [uncertain] I should put my hand in my pocket" — the word reads "want" but the sense suggests "can't see why"; recorded as written with the flag.
- 20 November: "Mr. Estievenart [uncertain]" — the artist's surname is not read with confidence. "two pattern de bon secours jugs [uncertain]" — the wording is not read with confidence.
- 22 November: "Capt. Lomas" — as written. The A.P.M.'s wire asked for an escort for an officer who is not on the Train's strength; the diarist complies and sends Capt. Lomas by rail.
- 28 November: Major de Graves, back from leave, is sent to his Company "under open arrest" although no papers came with him and no charge is laid "for the time being" — as written; no reason is given.
