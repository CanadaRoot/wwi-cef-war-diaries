# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — July 1917

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps (C.A.S.C.), 3rd Canadian Division (styled "Headquarters 3rd Canadian Divisional Train" on the cover; "H.Q. 3rd Canadian Divisional Train" on the contents page)
- **Month:** July 1917 (Volume 18) — this volume covers 1 to 31 July 1917
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** July 1917, p. 1 – p. 9 (image files page-0174 – page-0182 of the 370-page diary)
- **July 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–9 of 370): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–31 July, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The Place and Hour columns are blank throughout; the date is given in the Date column only. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "D.D.S.T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "D.A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "R.F.C." = Royal Flying Corps; "M.T." = Mechanical Transport; "H.M." = His Majesty. Place names are given as written, in capitals where the original capitalises them.

## Pages 4–9 — Diary text (1–31 July 1917)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed footer reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."

### Page 4 — 1–7 July 1917 (Sheet No. 145)

> **1.** — About 1 a.m. Artillery opened up, at 3 a.m. after a lull it opened up again with intensity rain coming on. O.C., 1st Train and his S.S.O. called to see about moving into our lines, he wants to remain where he is and we where we are, this being nearer to Railhead, later Capt. McDougall called re same, but nothing definite yet. Most of Staff are away at unveiling of Canadian Cross erected to the memory of those who fell at the taking of VIMY RIDGE. Train was represented. Capt. Clapp V.O. reported back from leave. Fine, but windy.
>
> **2.** — S.S.O. of 1st called to arrange about taking over from us when they relieve us. No's 2 and 3 Coys. moved to CARENCY today also Supply Sections of 2,3,&4 Coys. No.1 Coy. remaining and not moving.
>
> **3.** — M.O., V.O. and Paymaster move today and join the three Train Coys. No.4 Coy. moving at same time. No.1 Coy. remaining will call on M.O. of 1st Train in case of emergency. Fine.
>
> **4.** — H.Q. Train left LA TARGETTE for new H.Q. at CHATEAU DE LA HAIE. Called No.2 Coy. and had lunch there. Wet and dull all day, very little sun.
>
> **5.** — Called in and saw A.A. & Q.M.G. who will arrange for me to get a NISSEN HUT and when I do I will move up so as to be nearer the Coys. Inspected 2,3,& 4 Coys. all busy improving their lines by carting broken brick &c., the water supply seems to be their greatest trouble. Fine.
>
> **6.** — Nothing to report. Fine and sunny all day. Granted leave to England 7th to 20th. Major Heasley will act as O.C. in my absence.
>
> **7.** — O.C. left at 6 a.m. for BOULOGNE en route for England on leave. Visited the Companies this a.m. and issued orders re keeping roads clear of our convoys during visit of H.M. King on the 9th. Went to BARLIN this p.m. and inspected lines etc., of Reserve Park Section attached to us and ordered 6 turn out to report for duty to No.4 Coy. Fine and clear all day.

### Page 5 — 8–13 July 1917 (Sheet No. 146)

> **8.** — Raining heavily this morning, though it cleared up later in the day and evening was fine. Together with Adjutant around No's 2,3, and 4 Coys. and gave them further instructions regarding keeping roads clear for King's visit tomorrow. Convoys will park on south side of ARRAS-BETHUNE road west of LA TARGETTE until the King's party has passed. Rehearsal of proceedings in Chateau grounds this evening witnessed by Corps and Army Commanders. Work progressing satisfactorily and nothing new to comment upon.
>
> **9.** — Raining this a.m. until 9 a.m. remaining dull and chilly during balance of day. Went around Coy. and refilling Points this morning and thence to Railhead, but found everything going along well. Went in to D.D.S.T. this afternoon regarding complaints re unfit issues etc., which he promised to investigate. Visit of H.M. the King postponed so all ceremonies for today cancelled.
>
> **10.** — Another dull windy day. Visited all Companies and refilling points during the morning finding everything quite satisfactory. Went up to BOUVIGNY in the afternoon to see into the green fodder situation and found we still had about 10 tons to come. Orders out today taking away supply of green fodder from Purchase Board and putting it in our hands again. Very awkward as all first crops pretty well used up and only second and third available.
>
> **11.** — Bright day, but rather on the cool side. Nothing out of the ordinary happening and Kings visit did not materialize. Received advise of replacement of 11 loaders by men from Divl. Employment Coy., Lieut. George reported back from leave this p.m.
>
> **12.** — Weather fine and getting quite hot again. Visited all Companies, refilling points and railheads this morning but nothing to report as everything running very smoothly. Leave allotment cut to three this week.
>
> **13.** — Beautiful bright day again. Visited the Companies and passed on to Railhead with A.A.Q.M.G. [entry continues on page 6]

### Page 6 — 13–18 July 1917 (Sheet No. 147)

> **13. (continued)** — and D.A.Q.M.G. Found on arrival that enemy was shelling there and with fair accuracy so on returning ordered convoys to remain parked on side of main road until safe to enter the railway yard. Capt. Oliphant proceeded on leave this evening and is relieved as Adjutant by Capt. W.H. Graham.
>
> **14.** — Fairly decent weather with a heavy shower toward late afternoon. Nothing happening out of the ordinary and our work progressing favourably. Rained heavily tonight.
>
> **15.** — Raining during early part of morning, but cleared about 10 a.m. and remained fine rest of day. Wade left this morning for England with a view to obtaining leave to Canada. Together with Capt. Phillips went up to inspect rations held in supporting points but after searching supposed locations succeeded in unearthing only some 200 complete rations. Nothing else to report upon.
>
> **16.** — Fine bright day. Visited all companies, refilling points and railhead finding everything quite satisfactory. Lieut. Sanders ordered to report to the R.F.C. for training as an observer. Nothing else to report upon.
>
> **17.** — Fine day again. Visited all companies, refilling points and railhead, but nothing to report upon. Train coming in much earlier so changed loading time to 10.30 a.m.
>
> **18.** — Raining heavily practically all day. Went around the Companies but nothing to comment upon. Visited railhead and saw R.S.O. regarding expected shipment of straw but he states that he has no advise of arrival of same. D.A.Q.M.G. advised us of probable date of move into rest area and location of various units.

### Page 7 — 19–24 July 1917 (Sheet No. 148)

> **19.** — Dull rainy day, Went with D.A.Q.M.G. this morning to look over area which Brigades will occupy on going in to rest and select refilling points etc. Went over with O.C. Companies and S.Os. this afternoon advising them as to probable locations. Nothing else of importance happened and everything going well. New charger received from Base for O.C.
>
> **20.** — Beautifully fine clear day. Operation order in for moves into the rest areas so made all arrangements with D.A.Q.M.G. regarding change of railhead and use of M.T. Vehicles. O.C. reported back from leave at 3.00 p.m. and took over command.
>
> **21.** — Visited three Companies and found everything running smoothly and well. Capt. Oliphant on leave and Capt. Graham doing duty as Adjutant and doing it well too. Gave Major McClelland the confidential report to sign as having read it. He proposes to write a protest and I agree with him. Major Freeman called, reported that Capt. McCallum is admitted to 10th Canadian Field Ambulance. Capt. de Graves, Lts. Richards and Ferg got car from S.S.O. from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. to go and make all arrangements for settling into new area. Kept it until 6.30 p.m. preventing S.S.O. from carrying out other arrangements car needed for. Asked to explain in writing. Fine.
>
> **22.** — Laid up all day with fever and ague, feeling rotten. Warm and fine.
>
> **23.** — Feeling rotten but not allowed to do anything. Quinine is having its effect Now have the singing in ears. Very fine and bright.
>
> **24.** — Feeling much better. Had long conversation with A.A. & D.A.Q.M.G. Col. Hogarth, Col. Webb, and Major Fry came to dine. We had a conference about promotions and I looked over his scheme. It must not be forgotten that any scheme evolved now must of necessity work a hardship on someone and his scheme seemed to me the one that will do the most good with the least harm and I gave Col. Hogarth to understand he would have my support. [entry continues on page 8]

### Page 8 — 24–30 July 1917 (Sheet No. 149)

> **24. (continued)** — I gathered Col. Simpson was not of my way of thinking, he seems sore over something. Capt. W. Graham today to be interviewed for entry into air service. He will be sent for in a week or two. His brother Lt. Graham will go to balloon section. I can spare him much easier than his brother.
>
> **25.** — Capt. Oliphant returned from leave and resumed his duties as Adjutant, which Capt. Graham had been performing for him so well. Feeling ever so much better. Some rain and thunder.
>
> **26.** — Left CHATEAU DE LA HAIE 9 a.m. for BARLIN, some trouble getting billet for myself. O.C. No.1 Coy. and V.O. called. Later Major James Morrison and Lt. Grant. Adjt. & S.S.O. gone round Coys. Called with S.S.O. in Divl. H.Qrs. re flour for paste. Very fine.
>
> **27.** — D.A.D.V.S. came to inspect horses with vice [uncertain] and horses failed to parade. O.C. of 2 & 3, Coys. asked to explain. Bale of hay fell off wagon of No.4 Coy killing a child who was hanging on behind the wagon. Visited and inspected 2,3 and 4 Coys. and R. Points. found all running smooth and well. Fine hot and close.
>
> **28.** — Pack Train in at 11 a.m. a much better time than 2 p.m. Thinking over the wording of adverse reports, one of a C.O's least pleasing jobs. Adjt. gone to see O.C. No.1 Coy. Fine.
>
> **29.** — Adjt. gone with Major Dennison to inquire further into the fatal accident by No.4 Coy. to civilian child. Went with S.S.O. and inspected N.1 Coy. They are aa bit cramped but doing alright. Thunder, lightning and heavy rain.
>
> **30.** — Bshores [uncertain] left us today. Lt. Richards called and had a long interview about his adverse report which I agreed to slightly modify. Freeman also came and saw me about my report on him and I think more of him than ever. Fine generally.

### Page 9 — 31 July 1917 (Sheet No. 150)

> **31.** — All going smoothly, nothing to report. Fine.
>
> [Signed, centre:] *[signature — [uncertain]] Lieut.-Colonel, / O.C. 3rd Canadian Divisional Train.*

## Open questions / uncertain items (July 1917)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 18" but the contents page reads "Volume 17 — 7." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number.
- The contents page says "Diary Text 6 Pages." — the diary text does run to 6 pages (pages 4–9, Sheets 145–150), but the printed diary page numbers at the top right run 3–8 while the sheet numbers run 145–150.
- The 13 July entry begins at the foot of Sheet No. 146 (page 5) and continues at the head of Sheet No. 147 (page 6); the 24 July entry likewise begins at the foot of Sheet No. 148 (page 7) and continues at the head of Sheet No. 149 (page 8). Both are transcribed as single entries.
- 27 July: "D.A.D.V.S. came to inspect horses with vice [uncertain]" — the word is read as "vice" but its sense in context is unclear; it may be a typing error in the original.
- 30 July: "Bshores [uncertain] left us today." — the name/word at the start of the entry is not read with confidence; it may be a surname.
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of Sheet No. 150 is not read with confidence and is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name.
- The repeated handwritten initial mark at the end of most entries is not read with confidence and is not transcribed against every entry.
- "They are aa bit cramped" (29 July) and "N.1 Coy." (29 July) are transcribed exactly as written; "aa" appears to be a typing error for "a".
- "ordered 6 turn out to report for duty to No.4 Coy." (7 July) is transcribed as written; the sense appears to be six men turned out for duty.
- Names read as: Capt. McDougall; Capt. Clapp (V.O.); Major Heasley (acting O.C. 7–20 July); Capt. Oliphant (Adjutant); Capt. W.H. Graham (relieving Adjutant; also Capt. W. Graham); Lieut. George; Wade; Capt. Phillips; Lieut. Sanders; Major McClelland; Major Freeman; Capt. McCallum; Capt. de Graves; Lts. Richards and Ferg; Col. Hogarth; Col. Webb; Major Fry; Col. Simpson; Lt. Graham; Major James Morrison; Lt. Grant; Major Dennison.
- Casualties: no military casualties are recorded in this volume. One civilian child was killed on 27 July when a bale of hay fell from a No. 4 Coy wagon; the accident was still being inquired into on 29 July. Capt. McCallum was admitted to 10th Canadian Field Ambulance on 21 July (illness, not stated as a wound).
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only.
