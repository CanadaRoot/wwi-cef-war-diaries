## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The Place column on the diary sheets is blank throughout; places are given in the text of the entries. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S." Abbreviations as written: "D.H.Q." = Divisional Headquarters; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "G.C.M." = General Court Martial; "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer (as written); "H.T." = horse transport; "M.T." = mechanical transport; "M.S.M." = Meritorious Service Medal; "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column; "R.F.C." = Royal Flying Corps; "D.A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "D.D.S.& T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "Q.M." = Quartermaster; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "a/O.C." = acting Officer Commanding; "S.SGT-Major" = Staff Sergeant-Major (as written); "N.C.O." = Non-Commissioned Officer.

## Pages 4–9 — Diary text (1–31 December 1917)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". Sheets No. 181–186.

### Page 4 — 1–6 December 1917 (Sheet No. 181)

> **1917 Dec. 1.** — Bright sunny day with a cold wind blowing. Visited Railhead and all Companies this morning, finding everything going along smoothly. Advised D.H.Q. that no Officer of this Unit had expressed a willingness to exchange with Captain Gunter for 3 months and asked for further instructions in the matter.
>
> **2.** — Bright and cold again. Called upon D.H.Q. but nothing to comment upon there. Took the votes of No.2 Company this p.m., 89 men recording, of which the Deputy Presiding Officer was advised. Mileage covered for week ending today, was 3,749 miles.
>
> **3.** — Bright morning, clear and cold. Met G.O.C. at 9-30 a.m. and proceeded with him on his inspection of No. 3 Company, passing on to No.1 Company afterwards. He expressed great pleasure at the condition of our Horses, harness and wagons, and complimented both Officers and Men. Polled votes of No. 3 Company this afternoon, 101 votes being recorded.
>
> **4.** — Bright, cold day again. Orders out to clear Railhead by H.T., so called on Town Major to try and locate No. 3 Company nearer Railhead. Told us we would be likely to get into BOURECQ but very little accommodation there, so matter standing over for time being. Arranged for D.A.C. wagons to haul from refilling points for Artillery, and No.1 Company could clear Railhead. Polled votes of No.4 Company this p.m. 82 men recording. Proposed inspection of Transport by Corps Commander cancelled.
>
> **5.** — Still bright and cold. Attended a G.C.M. at D.H.Q. at 10 a.m., which was over by 11-30 a.m. Called at D.H.Q. with the S.S.O. but nothing new there. Visited No.1 Company this p.m. and took the votes of the men of the Company, totalling 145.
>
> **6.** — Another clear cold day. Inspected transport of D.H.Q. and Signallers, finding them in splendid shape, particularly D.H.Q. Nothing else of any importance happening, all our work proceeding well and no complaints. Ordered No.2 Company to haul by H.T. from railhead beginning tomorrow. Took votes of Salvage Co. and Train HQ. this afternoon.

### Page 5 — 7–13 December 1917 (Sheet No. 182)

> **1917 Dec. 7.** — Much milder today and very dull. Nothing out of the ordinary happening, and all our work progressing favorably.
>
> **8.** — Raining today. Went around all the Companies with the S.S.O. this morning, but found nothing out of the ordinary and all work progressing favorably. Called at DHQ. but nothing new there. Dirty night.
>
> **9.** — Raining all day long. Went out to No.4 Company this morning, and with the O.C. made an inspection of 1st Line Transport of 43rd and 116th Battalions. Wagons and horses in good shape of both Battalions, but harness of 116th not at all in good condition. Captain V.H. Graham reported back from No.10 Squadron, R.F.C., and was posted as S.Q. [uncertain] to 8th Brigade temporarily.
>
> **10.** — Fairly decent day, though dull. Nothing new around here, visited all Companies, finding everything going well. Took a few of the remaining votes and advised D.H.Q. and Presiding Officer at Base of number taken up to date. Clear night. Mileage covered for week ending 9th, 4,540 miles.
>
> **11.** — Chilly today, but no rain. Called at D.H.Q. regarding Corps letter re Thaw conditions during coming winter, and gave location of all our refilling points and fuel dumps. Nothing else happening of any interest.
>
> **12.** — Mild again but no rain. Inspected 1st Line Transport of R.C.R's and 42nd Battalion finding both in splendid condition. Nothing else of any importance happening around here.
>
> **13.** — Mild day again. Went around all the Companies together with S.S.O. but found all work progressing favorably and no complaints. Called at D.H.Q. but nothing new there.

### Page 6 — 14–18 December 1917 (Sheet No. 183)

> **1917 Dec. 14.** — Nice bright day again. Operation Orders came in first thing regarding impending move. Went around all the Companies with the S.S.O. to see how well prepared they were to move and found everything apparently ready. Asked for recommendation for M.S.M., so passed same on to Company Commanders for their recommendations.
>
> **15.** — Fine bright day again. Called at D.H.Q. and advised them of our proposed arrangements for moving Train Companies. Railhead moves to ECQUE on the 19th and will necessitate the use of more M.T. S.S.O. and Adjutant visited 2nd Train to see how their arrangements fit in with ours.
>
> **16.** — Dull cold day, but no rain. Sent in recommendations for M.S.M. to D.H.Q. Arranged for No.2 Company to move early in the morning and to go straight through to CHATEAUD'ACQ [uncertain] All other arrangements progressing and move should be fairly simple. Nothing else happening around here, slight snow-fall tonight.
>
> **17.** — Word came in late last night cancelling all orders for the move. Went around companies this a.m. cancelling all previous arrangements for moving. Received word about 5 p.m. that 8th Brigade would move in morning to BUSNES, advised No.3 Company immediately to get into touch with Brigade H.Q. and prepare to move with Brigade. Supply arrangements altered accordingly.
>
> **18.** — Went with S.S.O. to see 11th Divisional Train this morning and learned that as yet they had no definite orders to move. Went over all their lines to see locations and everything is in good shape there. 8th Brigade moved into BUSNES area and 3 Company located for night at L'ECLEME [uncertain] Operation Orders came in tonight governing the whole move and 9th Brigade moves in the morning.

### Page 7 — 19–23 December 1917 (Sheet No. 184)

> **1917 Dec. 19.** — Bright cold day again, with heavy frost. No.4 Company left NEDONCHELLE for the L'ECLEME [uncertain] area this morning, and No.3 Company left latter area for HESDIGNEUL. Capt: Oliphant went down to new area and made arrangements with Area Commandant to locate Companies in FOSSE 2 until 11th Division Companies pull clear. Received Artillery Operation Orders this evening; three batteries move in the morning, and the balance on the 22nd.
>
> **20.** — Movement order for three batteries cancelled about mid-night last night and put off until 21st, remainder as before. No.4 Company left L'ECLEME [uncertain] this morning and will remain one night at HESDIGNEUL. About 4 p.m. orders came in for moving Artillery which is spread over three days and necessitates our drawing for them from LILLERS until 22nd. Everything going fairly well with our moves.
>
> **21.** — Railhead for Infantry Brigades moved this morning to SAINS-BOUVIGNY and Capt. Oliphant moved down to new area as most of the Division are there now. No.2 Company left about 6 a.m. for NOEUX-LE-MINES going right through. About 5 p.m. Divisional H.Q. advised that owing to a hitch in bussing arrangements, part of 7th Brigade remained in the area which necessitated sending to NOEUX-LE-MINES for rations. This was carried out and everything arranged with little difficulty.
>
> **22.** — Fine bright day and not quite as cold. Moved our Headquarters this morning into BRACQUEMONT locating near the Companies. Nos. 2, 3, and 4 Companies have now taken over the lines of corresponding Companies of 11th Divisional Train. Artillery draw from LILLERS for last time this morning and whole Division and 5th Divisional Artillery will draw from SAINS-BOUVIGNY to-morrow 23rd.
>
> **23.** — Fine clear cold day again. Went with S.S.O. to see Officer i/c Coal Supply and made arrangements for fuel. Called at D.H.Q. but found nothing new there. Visited Nos.2 and 3 Companies this afternoon and went over their lines and billets finding everything in very good shape. No.1 Company moved in this evening. Wire received extending Lt-Col. Findlay's leave to January 5th, 1918.

### Page 8 — 23–28 December 1917 (Sheet No. 185)

> **1917 Dec. 23 Ctd** — leave to January 5th, 1918.
>
> **24.** — Dull misty day and decidedly warmer. Visited No.1 Company this morning together with the O.C. Company made an inspection of the lines etc finding everything in good shape and men fairly comfortably housed. Called at D.H.Q. but nothing very new there. Everything running well; re-filled twice today to give men more time off to-morrow. All quiet round here.
>
> **25.** — Much milder today, with snow towards late afternoon. Paid a visit to all the Companies during the day to wish them the Compliments of the Season and found the men all well provided for. Nothing out of the ordinary happening except the Christmas festivities.
>
> **26.** — Mild again, with several snow falls during the day. Was advised first thing this morning that S.SGT-Major H.J. Bayliffe [uncertain] had met with an accident and had been taken to Hospital. Went to see him and found that his knee was badly injured and that he will be sent down to the Base. Inspection by D.A.D.V.S. revealed some suspected mange cases in No.4 Company, which were ordered to be isolated. Thaw precautions were put in force this morning but taken off again about noon, though we cleared everything from Railhead by H.T. Fine clear night.
>
> **27.** — Fine clear cold day again. Called at D.H.Q. but nothing new there, so passed on to No. 2 Company where I made an inspection of lines etc. finding everything in pretty good shape. Held a meeting of Company Commanders this afternoon and took up with them the question of outstanding promotions and the demanding of N.C.O's as reinforcements from the Base.
>
> **28.** — Clear cold day again and very windy towards evening. Visited No.4 Company again this morning to see how mange cases were progressing and found very little improvement though horses have been clipped and washed. Went to D.D.S.& T's office this afternoon and took up various matters there, particularly regarding supplies of Burning Oil, which they promised to increase. All quiet around here and our work progressing favorably. Clear cold night.

### Page 9 — 28–31 December 1917 (Sheet No. 186)

> **1917 Dec. 28 Ctd** — to increase. All quiet around here and our work progressing favorably. Clear cold night.
>
> **29.** — Dull misty day though still cold. Called at D.H.Q. where I learned that there will be a meeting of D.A.Q.M.G: S.S.O's and Q.M's of the Division to discuss economy in food stuffs etc. on December 31st. Visited Nos. 1 and 2 Companies and found everything in good shape at each place. Nothing else of any interest happening and all quiet around here. Misty night.
>
> **30.** — Dull and mild today. Visited Nos.2, 3, and 4 Companies this morning in company with the V.O. and inspected the horses. One new case of mange reported though it is a slight one, and the others show great signs of improvement. Nothing else of importance happening here and all work progressing favorably.
>
> **31.** — Dull day but much colder again. Attended meeting, with S.S.O. at D.H.Q. this morning when economy in rations, equipment etc was discussed and various suggestions were adopted. Visited No.1 Company this p.m. and inspected 20 Remounts which arrived up from the Base. Nothing much to boast about, but may improve with keeping. Aeroplanes dropped bombs near No. 1 Company about 5 p.m. but no damage done.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *[signature — [uncertain]] Major, / a/O.C., 3rd Canadian Divisional Train.*

## Open questions / uncertain items (December 1917)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 23" and the title page is marked "23.", but the contents page reads "Volume 17.— 10." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number.
- The Place column on every diary sheet is blank; no place of the unit is recorded in the column, only in the entry text.
- 23 December appears twice, once at the foot of Sheet No. 184 (page 7) and once at the head of Sheet No. 185 (page 8) as "23 Ctd", completing the entry; 28 December is likewise split across Sheet No. 185 (page 8) and Sheet No. 186 (page 9) as "28 Ctd". Both are transcribed as written.
- The place name written "L'ECLEME" on 18, 19 and 20 December is not read with confidence; it may be L'Écluse. It is recorded here as L'ECLEME [uncertain] (3 flags).
- The destination on 16 December is read as "CHATEAUD'ACQ" [uncertain]; the spelling is unclear in the typescript.
- On 9 December Captain V.H. Graham "was posted as S.Q. [uncertain] to 8th Brigade temporarily"; the abbreviation is not read with confidence.
- On 26 December the name is read as "S.SGT-Major H.J. Bayliffe" [uncertain]; the surname spelling is not certain.
- The signature at the foot of the December sheets is not read with confidence; the diarist signs as Major, a/O.C., 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, and the signature itself is recorded as [uncertain] and not used as a name.
- The repeated handwritten initial mark at the end of most entries is not read with confidence and is not transcribed against every entry.
- The "23rd" at the end of the 22 December entry ("will draw from SAINS-BOUVIGNY to-morrow 23rd") is as written; it appears to be the diarist noting the date of "to-morrow".
- Names read as: Captain Gunter; Captain V.H. Graham; Capt. Oliphant; Lt-Col. Findlay; S.SGT-Major H.J. Bayliffe [uncertain].
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given. No casualties by enemy action are recorded in this volume; the only injury is the accidental knee injury to S.SGT-Major Bayliffe on 26 December, and aeroplane bombs dropped near No. 1 Company on 31 December caused no damage.
