## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The "Place" column of the diary sheets is blank throughout this month; no place is entered against any entry. The "Remarks and references to Appendices" column is also blank throughout; no appendices are listed. The printed footer on Sheets 1–6 reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."; on Sheets 7–8 it reads "(Azo30). Wt. W12523/M1229. 75,000. 1/17. D. D. & L., Ltd. Forms/C.2118/14." A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. ## Pages 4–11 — Diary text (1–31 July 1918)

### Page 4 — 1–5 July 1918 (Sheet No. 1)

Army Form C. 2118. The printed heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through. A stamp at the top right reads "H.Q. DIVISIONAL TRAIN / No. Original / Date 4 AUG 1918 / 3rd CANADIAN DIVISION".

> **1918 July 1.** — Fine, hot. Corps Sports held at TINQUES today were largely attended. Arranged transportation of as many Train Personnel as could be spared. 9th C.I.B. finished move today, going into Reserve. Inspected all convoys at Refilling Points today, and found all O.K. Companies busy rebuilding Camps, etc. Hostile planes over all night.
>
> **2.** — Fine and hot and dusty. Lieut: RASON proceeded to Base Depot, LE HAVRE, for HT Course. During his absence, Capt: PRIKLER will act as S.O., 3rd M.T.Company in addition to his duties as S.O., 9th C.I.B. until return of Lieut: UNDERWOOD from Leave. Major McLEOD went to D.A.D.O.S. 1st Army to close up accounts there as we are on 3rd Army in this Area. 9th Bde. C.F.A., which has been with 5th Imperial Division, reports back tomorrow. We ration them for consumption 3rd inst: first time. Lieut: CARSON and 9th Bde.C.F.A. Section report back to No.1 Company tomorrow.
>
> **3.** — Fine and hot. Inspected all Companies as as arranged for Eliminations for VIth Corps Horse Show. Companies all re-building and arranging Lines.
>
> **4.** — Still hot and dusty. At request of Division 'A', sent Lieuts: FERG, DAY, and GUNTER, to judge turns-out of Field Ambulances, to find out which Ambulance would show. 10th Field Ambulance was chosen. Train arriving regularly at Railhead and everything going smoothly. Arranged for green fodder with Purchase Board, and also arranged with D.A.D.S. for 2 tons Bran, as requested by D.A.D.V.S.
>
> **5.** — Fine and hot. Companies paraded turns-out this morning, 1 Officer, 1 N.C.O., 10 GS turns-out, and 1 Water Cart from each Company. Everything very satisfactory. Picked 12 GS turns-out, 1 Water Cart and Lieut: GEORGE, and Sgt: MACMILLAN. The two extra turns-out are to be in reserve. Arranged to place above detail in separate Camp, and give Lieut: GEORGE a free hand and all assistance required to put them in shape for finals. Capt: de GRAVES and Lieut: HESSEY gone to buy paint and other necessary materials as same cannot be obtained from Ordnance in time.

### Page 5 — 6–9 July 1918 (Sheet No. 2)

> **1918 July 6.** — Inspected all Companies and Re-filling Points, also visited Railhead. Show teams out twice for wagon drill today. All available wheelers painting wagons a uniform colour. All Train work proceeding very satisfactorily. V.O. reported 'sick'. All Companies more or less affected with "Spanish Influenza", but no cases very serious. No.4 Company had 29 men down in one day, but still able to carry on.
>
> **7.** — Hot and dusty. Around Re-filling Points with Capt: De GRAVES. Train late, but all deliveries and re-filling completed before 2.00 p.m. Train inspected by Brigadier-General SECOMBE, D.D.S. & T., Third Army. Special attention paid to horses, harness, and wagons, and General SECOMBE expressed his satisfaction at "such creditable showing". He also attended wagon drill of teams entered in the Competition, and was again congratulatory. Appeared to be considerably surprised at the smallness of our horses taken as a Unit, and said that the majority were Artillery Wheelers, and should not have been issued to the Train as Heavy Draft horses. All the other Trains we would meet had much heavier horses, but he said ours were in very good condition, and that our showing was exceptionally good. Was also highly pleased with the wagons throughout the Train.
>
> **8.** — Still hot. Inspected teams for Competition and found them progressing satisfactorily. Visited DOULLENS with S.S.O. to get material for final touches for harness and wagons. Saw A.A. & Q.M.G., and visited 8th C.I.B. Headquarters. Everything satisfactory.
>
> **9.** — Rained last night. Cooler with occasional showers during the day. Show teams moving off today and will bivouac near Ground at SAULTY, as they have to show at 9.00 a.m. Had final inspection and found everything in good shape. All turns-out very creditable. Transport return for week ending July 7th shows averages :- No.1 Company, 12.3 miles. Total mileage 2019 miles. No.2 " 8.2 " " " 801 " No.3 " 7.1 " " " 602 " No.4 " 8.3 " " " 790 " Longest daily haul, 15 miles, shortest daily haul, 2 miles. Inspected Re-filling Points with S.S.O.

### Page 6 — 10–15 July 1918 (Sheet No. 3)

> **1918 July 10.** — Fine. Rode to SAULTY to see VIth Corps Horse Show. Transport of 3rd Canadian Division very good, the Division carrying off 3 Firsts in the 4 Transport Events. Our own turns-out were not in the running, although they were very creditable considering the disadvantages laboured under. Sick list of the Train still very high, No.1 Company specially suffering, although Sick Parades of all Companies are abnormal.
>
> **11.** — Wet and showery. Went to Officers' Clothing Store and E.F.C. with Dripping Chits. Went round Re-filling Points and Railhead with S.S.O. Inspected Company Convoys at Re-filling Points. Around with Capt: LEVI of Purchase Board, looking over fields for grazing and green forage.
>
> **12.** — Showery. At Divisional Headquarters with S.S.O. Arranged to send N.C.O. and men to cut two fields of green forage. Inspected horses of Train at Watering Parade at NOON. All appeared to be in good condition, although one or two showing signs of hard work. With a chance to rest up they could easy be got up into first class condition.
>
> **13.** — [No entry for 13 July 1918; the date column runs from 12 to 14 July.]
>
> **14.** — Fine and cool, occasional showers in afternoon. New car to replace one evacuated from LE CAUROY arrived. Is not in good condition, and is obviously a built up car of the same make as the one evacuated, (WOLSELEY). Informed by M.T. Company that it has been adversely reported on, as they have no spare parts for this make of Car, and do not think it will stand up under heavy work on roads in this Area. Lieut:Col: McALPINE, Officer i/c CASC Personnel arrived to inspect Train Transport, but on learning O.C. would return tomorrow, postponed Inspection until his arrival. All Companies in good shape and doing their work satisfactorily. Sent car to BOULOGNE to meet Col: FINDLAY and Major BLACKSTOCK, D.A.A.G. S.S.O. went to HESDIN to see D.A.D.S., Third Army, also VIth Corps M.T.Company to arrange re wood, etc.
>
> **15.** — Wet. Inspected Companies and Re-filling Points, and then left on Reconnaisance with D.A.Q.M.G., (Major HARRISON), to pick Train Company locations and Re-filling Points in A SOMERIN, SUB ST. LEGER, and REBREUVIETTE, in the event of a move being found necessary. Lieut:Col: FINDLAY returned from Leave and took over Command, Capt/ de GRAVES resuming Command [Entry continued on Sheet 4.]

## Open questions / uncertain items (July 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 30" but the contents page reads "Volume 18 – 7." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number.
- The continuation of the 15 July entry appears at the top of Sheet 4 under the date label "14 Ctd." — the label appears to be a typist's error, as the sentence ("of No.1 Company, and Major McLEOD taking over as S.S.O.") completes the 15 July entry; both are transcribed as written.
- 13 July 1918 has NO entry: the date column on Sheet 3 runs from 12 to 14 July. No entry has been invented for it.
- 3 July: "Inspected all Companies as as arranged" — "as as" is as written (repeated word in the typewritten text).
- 5 July: several words are overtyped in the typewritten text ("Picked 12 GS turns-out", "The two extra turns-out", "and give Lieut: GEORGE"); the reading given is the clean reading of the final overtype.
- 15 July: the reconnaissance places are written "A SOMERIN, SUB ST. LEGER, and REBREUVIETTE"; on 30 July the same place is written "SOMBRIN" — both spellings as written.
- 16 July: the Field Ambulance efficiency table lists No. 13 twice (83.00% and 71.90%) and No. 4 twice (72.05% and 55.70%) — as written; the diary does not explain the duplication.
- 27 July: "Lieut: Rayon reported back from his [uncertain] Course of Instruction in H.T." — the word before "Course" is overtyped and cannot be read; it is recorded as [uncertain]. The name is written "Rayon" here but "RASON" on 2 July; both spellings are as written and appear to be the same officer.
- The signature at the foot of the July sheets is not read with confidence and is recorded here as [uncertain]; the O.C. at the time was Lieut-Colonel Findlay (returned from leave and took over command on 15 July), but the signature itself is not asserted to be his.
- Names read as: Lieut. Rason / Rayon; Capt. Prikler; Lieut. Underwood; Major McLeod; Lieut. Carson; Lieuts. Ferg, Day and Gunter; Lieut. George; Sgt. MacMillan; Capt. de Graves; Lieut. Hessey; Brigadier-General Secombe (D.D.S. & T., Third Army); Capt. Levi; Lieut-Col. McAlpine; Col. Findlay; Major Blackstock; Major Harrison (D.A.Q.M.G.); Capt. V. H. Graham; Col. W. D. Greer; Staff-Sgt. Crabtree; Major Thackery; Col. Corrigan; Major MacGillivray; Col. Scott; Capt. Hudson; Dvr. Kidney; Dvr. Sproston; Dvr. Clarke; Capt. Higgin; Capt. Fisher.
