# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — June 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps, 3rd Canadian Division (Headquarters, 3rd Canadian Divisional Train)
- **Month:** June 1918 (Volume 29) — this volume covers 1 to 30 June 1918
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** June 1918, p. 1 – p. 9 (image files page-0285 – page-0293 of the diary)
- **June 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–9): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–30 June, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The "Place" column of the diary sheets is blank throughout this month; no place is entered against any entry. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets is "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" with "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through; the printed footer reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S." A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General; "C.A.P.C." = Canadian Army Pay Corps; "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade; "M.T." = Mechanical Transport; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "F.P. No. 1" = Field Punishment No. 1; "G.S." = General Service (wagon); "S.A.A." = small arms ammunition.

## Pages 4–9 — Diary text (1–30 June 1918)

### Page 4 — 1–7 June 1918 (Sheet No. 1)

Army Form C. 2118. The printed heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through. A stamp at the top right reads "H.Q. DIVISIONAL TRAIN / No. Original / Date 2 JUL 1918 / 3rd CANADIAN DIVISION", and "29" is written in at the top right corner.

> **1918 June 1.** — Bombing from planes pretty heavy during the night. S.S.O. and Adjutant to railhead. Took adjourned case at No.1 Co. and dismissed it. Paymaster prior to leaving for England, paid all Companies, went round with him. Fine.
>
> **2.** — Fine. No.2 moves from RELY to LIETTRES. Claim for fire at RELY came in for 29,000 Francs, the whole place is not worth that. Started official enquiry.
>
> **3.** — Mileage for week ending June 2nd, total distance for Train 7,469 miles, average per wagon per day, No.1 Co. 10.8, No.2, 8.00, No.3, 10.4, and No.4, 9.00. Longest distance any one wagon, No.3 Co., 20 miles, other Companies 10; shortest, water cart, 1 mile. Fine.
>
> **4.** — Started off early to meet the G.O.C. and help him inspect transport of the new Engineer Bde. A.D.V.S. did the horses, and I, the wagons and harness. Wagons generally were in fair shape but the harness all round bad, not properly fitted and badly cared for. Lunched with the General, home about 6.00 p.m.
>
> **5.** — Went to railhead and inspected convoys, called at No.1 Company, said "Good-bye" to Major Adams, who leaves tomorrow for England on his way to Canada. Lieut: Higgins reported case of Stealing. Fine.
>
> **6.** — Adjutant to Railhead and No.2 Company. Called at No.3 Company and inspected new way of carrying drag ropes on wagons. Went on and heard a case at No.3 Company, adjourned it. Hot and fine.
>
> **7.** — S.S.O. gone to conference at Corps H.Q. Last month's salvage for the Train included among other things the following :- Dripping 700 lbs., waste paper 141 lbs., old horse shoes 500 lbs., new 110., oil drums 8, brake shoes 26, scrap iron 28 lbs., lead 63 lbs., 1 chaff cutter, 1 pump, 4 bags empty tins for solder extraction, etc. etc. Lieut: Green of Special Platoon called and dined. Fine.

### Page 5 — 8–14 June 1918 (Sheet No. 2)

> **1918 June 8.** — Court of Enquiry on Fire at No.2 Company. Letter from 3rd Cdn.Division 'Q' saying I will inspect transport of 3rd Cdn.Engineer Bde. periodically and from time to time report. Fine.
>
> **9.** — Fine. Visited Railhead with Adjutant; all running smoothly and the convoys looking extremely well. Called on Colonel McAlpine at PERNES. He arranged to take Adjutant to Base tomorrow to look over N.C.O's. Went on and tried case of Corporal at No.1 Company, Reprimanded him.
>
> **10.** — Adjutant left at 10.00 to pick up Col: MacAlpine and go to Pool. Mileage for week ending 9th, average mileage per day per Company, No.1, 17, No.2, 8.4, No.3, 8.6, No.4, 7.6, total distance 5,872 miles. 18 wagons of No.1 Company travelled 18 miles per day longest distance. Not so bright, but fine.
>
> **11.** — Inspected turns-out from Machine Gun Battn: handed over to Lieut: Hessey. Teams only fair, wagons fair but short of equipment, harness well repaired but not clean. Adjutant returned. Fine.
>
> **12.** — Visited Railhead and inspected Train Transport. No.1 already shows improvement since Capt: Church took over command. S.S.O. and Adjutant gone to Purchase Board. St/Sgt: Crabtree left for School of Instruction. Received wire to attend Board of Officers at GROSVILLE 14th inst: Acting as Transport Judge tomorrow Machine Gun Battn: competition and Sports. Called in at Divisional H.Q. Fine.
>
> **13.** — All day judging at 3rd Can.Machine Gun Battn: Hot and fine.
>
> **14.** — Started 7:45 a.m., picked up Staff Captain at H.Q. 4th Division and went to GROSVILLE as Members of Board of Enquiry on the sufficiency or otherwise of a Canadian Subaltern's pay. General Bell, President, A.A.Q.M.G., 1st, and Field Cashier, 1st Cdn.Div. were the other Members of the Board. Evidence from Subalterns was rather painful hearing and the Board were absolutely unanimous in their Findings and recommendations for increase. Fine.

### Page 6 — 15–22 June 1918 (Sheet No. 3)

> **1918 June 15.** — Wire received all Supply Trains would be 12 or 14 hours late, advised Companies accordingly. No sooner this done than Train arrived at Railhead, it had evidently got through before accident, re-advised Companies and got it cleared before mid-day. Major McLeod gone off to see about flour for paste. Called on Col: MacAlpine re Capt: Church going to No.1 Train. Called at Divisional H.Q. Fine.
>
> **16.** — Orders for Capt: Church to report at once to 1st Train.
>
> **17.** — All day at 1st Divisional Sports. Lieut: Gunter called re his dating. Fine.
>
> **18.** — Transport return for week ending June 16th :- No.1 Company 2,797 miles, average per wagon, 9.7 miles. No.2 " 1,034 " " " " 10.1 " No.3 " 1,087 " " " " 6.8 " No.4 " 939 " " " " 7.6 " Total distance for the Train, 5,857 miles, longest haul any one wagon 9 miles, shortest 1. Fine.
>
> **19.** — 52nd and 58th Battns: moving up North tomorrow. Dined at No.2 Company. Col: Greer called. Fine.

> **20.** — Fine. Found out which Hospital Webb was in. Wiped out a lot of correspondence and destroyed surplus papers. Rain towards evening.

### Page 8 — 27 (continued) – 30 June 1918 (Sheet No. 5)

> **1918 June 27 Ctd.** — head at GOUY EN ARTOIS. S.S.O. has to use my horse tomorrow, cars being out of commission. No.2 Company moved to GOUY EN ARTOIS relieving No.2 Company, 2nd C.D.T.
>
> **28.** — Fine. S.S.O. gone his rounds on horse-back. No.3 Company moves to GOUY EN ARTOIS TODAY, taking over from No.4 Company 2nd C.D.T. Normal system of Supply comes into force today and M.T. clears railhead and delivers to Re-filling Points, where Train Companies pick up and deliver to First Line Transport. This system will remain in force in this area and Train wagons will be kept loaded overnight ready for emergencies. In order to do this wagons will have to deliver daily, and then return to Refilling Points and load up with supplies for delivery following morning.
> Location of Re-filling Points today is as follows :- No.1 Company, ETREE WAMIN, (Divisional Troops). No.2 " GOUY EN ARTOIS.(7th C.I.Bde.) No.3 " GOUY EN ARTOIS.(8th C.I.Bde.) No.4 " LIENCOURT. (9th C.I.Bde.).
>
> **29.** — Fine. No.1 Company moved to GOUY EN ARTOIS this morning, inspected them on the march, all in very creditable shape and horses quite fresh. March discipline decidedly good. I leave for England this evening for a fortnight's rest. S.S.O. will take over Command of Train during my absence. Capt: de Graves act as S.S.O., and Lieut: Ferg as Adjutant. Capt: Prikler posted as S.O. 9th Bde. vice Mr. Ferg. No.4 Company moved to GOUY EN ARTOIS this afternoon. All Train Companies now located there, although Nos.1 and 4 will not be able to move into their lines until tomorrow when Nos. 1 and 3 Companies, 2nd C.D.Train move out.

## Open questions / uncertain items (June 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 29" but the contents page reads "Volume 18 – 6." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number.
- The cover's date line writes the year split across the two dates (apparently "19" after "1st" and "18" after "30th"); rendered here as 1–30 June 1918.
- The "Place" column of the diary sheets is blank throughout this month, so no place is recorded against any entry; the Train's headquarters location in early June is not stated in the diary (No. 2 Company was at Rely until 2 June, and the O.C. worked the Rely–Pernes–Liettres area).
- 26 June: "after handing over to O.C., 74th" is as written; what "74th" refers to is not clear from the diary.
- 26 June: "Lieut: Ferg reported down with the new eopidemic." — "eopidemic" is as written (presumably "epidemic", i.e. the Spanish influenza noted again in July).
- 29 June: "Capt: Prikler posted as S.O. 9th Bde. vice Mr. Ferg." — "S.O." is as written; the July diary uses "S.O." the same way (see 2 July 1918).
- The signature at the foot of the June sheets is not read with confidence and is recorded here as [uncertain]; it is signed as "Major, a/O.C. for Lieut-Colonel", i.e. the Major was acting O.C. while the Lieut-Colonel (the O.C.) was away on leave from 29 June.
- The spelling of Colonel McAlpine's name varies in the diary: "McAlpine" on 9 June and "MacAlpine" on 10 and 15 June — both spellings as written.
- Names read as: Major Adams; Lieut. Higgins; Lieut. Green (Special Platoon); Colonel McAlpine / MacAlpine; Lieut. Hessey; Capt. Church; St/Sgt. Crabtree; Major McLeod; Lieut. Gunter; Col. Greer; Major Healey; Col. Webb; Webb; Paymaster Blake; Capt. Burke (C.A.P.C.); Capt. Bawlf; Lieut. Ferg; Capt. de Graves; Capt. Prikler; General Bell.
- No casualties are recorded in this volume. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given.
