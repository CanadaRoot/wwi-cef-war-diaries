# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — January 1917

- **Unit:** Headquarters, 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps, 3rd Canadian Division
- **Month:** January 1917 (Volume 12) — this volume covers 1 to 31 January 1917
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** January 1917, p. 1 – p. 8 (image files page-0120 – page-0127 of the 370-page diary)
- **January 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–8): cover, contents, diary text 1–31 January, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. From 18 January the sheets change to a lined form (footer "T.124. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."). A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "D.A.A. & Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "A.D.M.S." = Assistant Director of Medical Services; "F.P.I" = Field Punishment No. 1; "D.S.O." = Distinguished Service Order; "I. & L." = imprisonment and hard labour (as used here, "2 years I. & L."); "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer; "C.A.V.C." = Canadian Army Veterinary Corps. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets is the unit name printed at the top left.

### Page 3 — 1–9 January 1917 (Sheet No. 96)

> **January 1.** — Quiet day. At Railhead, bread and candles short on pack train.
>
> **January 2.** — C.S.M. Hillsdon up on charge of drunkenness, 1.1.17. Referred to F.G.C.M. Lieut Franklin reported for duty. No fresh meat on pack train. Dull heavy day. Not cold. Capt. McCallum called re special leave. Major Beasley returned from leave.
>
> **January 3.** — Raining, cold and windy. F.G.C.M. Moran, Lieut Grant prosecuting. Forwarded application for F.G.C.M. in Hillsdon case. Honours list published announcing award of D.S.O. to Lieut. Colonel. Findlay.
>
> **January 4.** — Cold, windy and wet.
>
> **January 5.** — Fine day, bright and clear. Lieut Richards returned from leave. At railhead, train late. Saw D.A.Q.M.G. re special leave for Capt. McCallum. Advised to withhold for signature of Lt. Col. Findlay. Informed McCallum. M.O. returned from leave.
>
> **January 6.** — Fine and bright. Train on time
>
> **January 7.** — Fine, bright day. Communication from Division requesting report in reference to carrying out of sentence of 2 years I. & L. in Moran case. Reported in favour of sentence being carried out. A/Adjutant at Railhead. Church Parade at 2.30, Major Woods officiating. Train very late. V.O. requested letter of recommendation of his work in Unit. Did not comply. Informed he was obtaining same from O.C. all companies. Immediately instructed Company Commanders against this — vide K.R. & O. para. 444
>

>

### Page 4 — 9–14 January 1917 (Sheet No. 97)

> **January 9 (continued).** — "Neglect of Duty", left railhead yesterday to get lunch. Train arrived during his absence. Reprimanded him. Received notice of F.G.C.M. on C.S.M. Hillsdon would be held here on 11th inst.
>
> **January 10.** — Dull, raw weather. Train late again. No advice up till noon. Visited D.H.Q. and advised D.A.Q.M.G. of action regarding convoys. He concurred. V.O. requested to be paraded before A.D.V.S. Train reported about 5.00 p.m. First Coy. reached station in good time for offloading. Wagons all back by about 8.00 p.m.
>
> **January 11.** — H.Q. Office occupied during a.m. by F.G.C.M. on Hillsdon case. Train reported in about 1.00 p.m. A.D.V.S. visited H.Q. Coy. to inspect cases of suspected mange. He ordered a number of horses isolated. Refused to have our V.O. paraded and stated if he insisted to have him parade before G.O.C. Division. Application received from V.O. to be paraded before G.O.C. Division advised one Coy. would have to move out of present location. Visited No 2 Coy. with view of putting in second Coy. in their billets. Found no spare accommodation.
>
> **January 12.** — Train late again. Arrived about 1.00 p.m. Paraded V.O. before before G.O.C. at 6.00 p.m. A.D.V.S. was present and a charge was laid of neglect of duty, and inefficiency. Matter was referred to D.D.V.S. Capt. Phillips' quarters burned out. No damage to property but he lost most of his kit. H.Q. Coy. Fire Brigade responded very promptly and blaze was quickly extinguished. Orders received for No 4 Coy. to move to Mingoval. Capt. Church proceeded to Mingoval and could not find accommodation. Later reported he had secured quarters in Acq. C.O. of 9th Divisional Train called. They are relieving No 4 Coy. Showed him billets now occupied by No 4.
>
> **January 13.** — Train reported 8 hours late leaving Boulogne. Arrived about 4.00 p.m. F.G.C.M. reduced C.S.M. Hillsdon to rank of Corporal. Lieut Fisher returned from leave. No 4 Coy moved to Acq. Capt. Church reports very poor accommodation.
>
> **January 14.** — Train late, very late, wagons returned at 1.30 a.m. 15-1-17. Court of Inquiry on Fire in Capt. Phillips' hut.

### Page 5 — 15–19 January 1917 (Sheet No. 98)

> **January 15.** — Capt. Church and Lieut George proceeded on leave. Capt McCallum paraded by M.O. to A.D.MS 3rd Canadian Division and granted 3 weeks furlough at Menton. Proceeds tomorrow. Train late, arrived at about 12.30 noon.
>
> **January 16.** — Dry and cold. Train not reported at 9.00 a.m. Corpl. Wells, No 2 Coy up on charge of neglecting to obey orders. Reprimanded him, Cancelled his leave. Col. Findlay reported at 5.00 p.m.
>
> **January 17.** — Took over command of Train. During my absence Major Freeman carried on very well, am glad of this. He had a good test as Pack train was more often very late than on time. Coys had to be moved to other areas and he had some nasty cases to deal with, all of which he handled to my liking. Move tomorrow to new area and dont know yet where Train Hd. Qrs. are to be. Tried case of Drunk from No 3 Coy and gave 21 days F.P. No 1. Major Denniston came to lunch. He was on way to Court Martial, when his car broke down. Snowed all day but mild.
>
> **January 18.** — Moved to BETHONSART, Hd. Qrs and Hd. Qrs. Coy. No 4 Coy moved but had to return to ACQ. No 3 to MINGOVAL, No 2 GUESTREVILLE, All moves well carried out in spite of bad roads and snow storm.
>
> **January 19.** — Visited all refilling points with S.S.O. all new sites but they are making the most and best of things. Called in at Divi. Hd. Qrs. HERMAVILLE and took up question of sending baggage wagons to units when a move is on, and hauling rations from railhead at same time The Pack Train did not come in until 9.30 p.m. and No 3 Coy did not get to bed until 4.00 a.m. It has been more or less like this for some time and it puts a considerable strain on my Officers and Men. Great credit is due to them for the cheerful way they are carrying on. Cold, frosty and thick fog, late evening and night.

### Page 6 — 20–25 January 1917 (Sheet No. 99)

> **January 20.** — Train in today by 12.00 noon. S.S.O. AND Capt. Hennessy gone to CAMBLIGNEUL to fix up billets for Train H.Q. and Hq. Coy. S.S.O. saw Gen. Hill re complaint we made to Gen. re difficulty in getting coal, he admitted he had been misinformed and expressed regret. A.A. & Q.M.G. will explain to Gen. We are in no way to blame. D.A.Q.M.G. jumped on S.S.O. and told him we had fallen down, which much upset him and he certainly does not deserve this In fact thanks to the S.S.O's energy and ability the D.A.Q.M.G. has not fallen down himself. Frosty, cold and rough all day.
>
> **January 21.** — Train only came in at 11.00 a.m. and some of the men did not turn in until 4.30 a.m. Lieut Franklin reported back from leave. Town Major to mess with us. S.S.O. is trying to get his SUPPLY Officers billeted at ACQ, this being most central place. 44 reinforcements arrived for 43rd Battn. and no word from D.A.Q.M.G. S.O. found out late last night and S.S.O. could only get B. B. & B. for them at that late hour. H.Q. Coy came in in good time in spite of slippery roads. Men can be accommodated but little or nothing for horses and no wagon parks. I am afraid to put them in fields in case frost breaks and we get tired, so parking on roads. Cold and dull.
>
> **January 22.** — Inspected quarters of H.Q. Coy and refilling points. Frequent slight falls of snow. Cold and dull. Train in 4.00 p.m.
>
> **January 23.** — All batmen before me because of a row last night, ordered Paymaster's batman back to duty. H.Q. Coy busy erecting sleeping bunks in barns. 15 degrees of frost last night. Pack train reported to arrive at 6.00 p.m. There does not seem to be any improvement here and it is rough on men and horses. Still cold and frosty but sunny and bright. 'Planes busy.
>
> **January 24.** — Wrote to Col. Ruttan and Coats at Pay Office. Bright and cold.
>

### Page 7 — 25–29 January 1917 (Sheet No. 100)

> **January 25 (continued).** — inspection. Pack train in at noon. O.C. 4th Train called and we had a long chat over things at home and here. Bright and cold.
>
> **January 26.** — Called at Divi. H.Q. with S.S.O. Went into question of Transport if M.T. is taken off. Train will be late. Hung up at Railway Crossing 20 minutes one way and 30 minutes on return journey. The passing of trains only took a couple of minutes at most and delay caused much holding up of transport with its necessary evils. A.D.V.S. inspected suspected horses and declared one at any rate had mange. This is the one the V.O. said had straddled a rope or rubbed himself against a tree, thus showing the extent of his veterinary knowledge. Clear and frosty. Charge Sheet against S.S.M. Courtney dated Decr. 31.1916 arrived from Shorncliffe last night for me to deal with. Train in at 8.00 p.m. 27th. Clear and cold.
>
> **January 27.** — Yesterday's train did not come in until 8.00 a.m. and at 8.00 p.m. this evening word came through that the train would be in any minute. At 12.00 p.m., 4 hours later, we were phoned twice that it was in, but at 10. the following morning it was not yet in, which means horses and men have had an unnecessary wait in the cold of nine hours. This is hard on men and horses and shows some bad management somewhere. Inspected 1st Line Transport of 9th Bde, Clear but bitterly cold. The lateness of train makes inspection useless until late afternoon and then it is dark before inspection is over.
>
> **January 28.** — At 10.00 a.m. yesterday's Pack Train came in. Train of today at 3.30 p.m. So cleared two in one day. Men all pretty tired. Draft of 19 horses arrived today, inspected same. They are a good bunch with the exception of one slightly lame. Inspected 1st Line Transport of 9th Field Ambulance. Capt Church and Lieut George returned from leave They were 3 hours on rail and horribly cold. Many of the carriages had broken windows, some even without doors. Here again something is rotten. Cold inclined to be raw.
>
> **January 29.** — Pack train advised as in at 1.00 p.m. R.T.O. advises Capt Hanmore or no one else can proceed to England by train before the 31st. Drew attention of Town Major to untidy state of yard round Signallers and 4th Supply Column quarters, in the yard of this billet Fine but very cold.

### Page 8 — 30–31 January 1917 (Sheet No. 101)

> **January 30.** — At 10.00 a.m. no word of Pack Train, but arrived at 11.30 a.m. Remanded C.A.V.C man up on charge of drunkenness for further evidence. Had Lieut Franklin up to correct and add to his application for transfer to Signallers. Resumed case adjourned in morning, heard ten witnesses for and against and gave 7 days F.P. No 1. Enough considering this hard weather. Cold, raw and some snow. H.Q. Coy horses and wagons removed off road as required.
>
> **January 31.** — V.O., Capt Hanmore, drew cheque from Paymaster to enable him to return home and left today Pack Train in at 5.00 a.m., delivery is now at the scheduled hours, during the day. Snowing hard.
>
> [Signed:] *[signature — appears to read "Findlay" [uncertain] as to first name] Lt. Col. / O.C. 3rd Canadian Div. Train*

## Open questions / uncertain items (January 1917)
- The cover reads "Volume 12" and the contents page reads "Volume 17 — 1." — both as written.
- The contents page says "Diary Text 6 Pages" but the diary text occupies pages 3–8 (6 sheets, Sheets 96–101); the count is consistent this month.
- The O.C. is identified in the diary text as Lieut. Colonel Findlay: the honours list of 3 January 1917 announces the award of the D.S.O. to Lieut. Colonel Findlay, and 5 January notes special leave "for signature of Lt. Col. Findlay". The signature at the foot of the January sheets appears to read "Findlay" [uncertain] as to the first name. This identifies the O.C. whose signature in the October–December volumes was recorded as [uncertain].
- 9 January and 25 January each appear twice, once at the foot of one sheet and once at the head of the next, with the entry continuing across the break; both are transcribed as written.
- "C.S.M. Hillsdon" (2 January) is reduced to Corporal by F.G.C.M. on 13 January.
- Driver Moran's F.G.C.M. (3 January) and the sentence of 2 years I. & L. (7 January) follow his desertion charge of 27 December 1916.
- The V.O., Captain Hanmore, is in repeated conflict with the O.C. over mange cases and a letter of recommendation; he leaves to return home on 31 January.
- 17 January: the O.C. (Lt. Col. Findlay) resumes command after leave; Major Freeman had acted as O.C. Train during his absence.
- 18 January: the Train moves — Headquarters and Headquarters Company to Bethonsart, No. 3 Company to Mingoval, No. 2 Company to Guestrevillle [as written, "GUESTREVILLE"], No. 4 Company ordered to Mingoval but returns to Acq.
- Names read as: Lieut. Colonel Findlay (O.C., D.S.O.); Major Freeman (acting O.C.); C.S.M. Hillsdon; Lieut. Franklin; Major Beasley; Driver Moran; Lieut. Grant; Lieut. Richards; Capt. McCallum; M.O.; Major Woods; V.O. (Capt. Hanmore); Capt. Strathy; Staff Sergt. O'Malley (No. 3 Coy); Capt. Phillips; H.Q. Coy Fire Brigade; Lieut. Fisher; Capt. Church; Lieut. George; Corpl. Wells (No. 2 Coy); Major Denniston; S.S.M. Courtney; Col. Ruttan; Coats (Pay Office); Gen. Hill.
