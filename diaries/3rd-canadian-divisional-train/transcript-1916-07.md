- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** July 1916, p. 1 – p. 9 (image files page-0065 – page-0073)
- **July 1916 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–9): cover, contents page, diary text 1–31 July, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). The diary is written in the first person by the Officer Commanding. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. The Place column is blank throughout — no place is recorded in the diary's Place column, so places named in the index are taken from the entry text where a place is named. Words struck through in the original are noted in the open questions, not silently dropped. Entries that run across a page break are given whole under the page where they begin, with the continuation noted. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "D.D.S & T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "G.C.M." = General Court Martial; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "D.D.R." = Deputy Director of Remounts; "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "H.D." = heavy draught; "L.D." = light draught; "F.P. No. I" = Field Punishment No. 1; "C.C." = confinement to camp/barracks. The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12."

## Pages 3–9 — Diary text (1–31 July 1916)

### Page 3 — 1–7 July 1916 (Sheet No. 54)

> **1916 July 1** — Off early to serve as Member of G.C.M. and Adjutant as Judge Advocate. Back at 6-30, tried two cases. S.S.O. and O.C. Headquarters Company answered for me. Ground Sports at 1st & 3rd Trains, 3rd Train took 2 Firsts and 4 Seconds. Aeroplanes very active and more numerous than they have been lately. Very fine and very warm.
>
> **2** — Adjutant and I visited Divisional Headquarters, had talk about visit of 2nd Staff to our Billet, and prospect of our having to move from here. Church Service, with Band from 2nd Brigade to accompany Hymns. Late wire to switch 500 C.M.R's rations to party in YPRES to-morrow morning. Very fine all day.
>
> **3** — With S.S.O. to Divisional Headquarters, am likely to be turned out of Billets. Paid visit to Dentist. S.S.O. on phone arranged to be allowed to pay 62.50 for straw. Inspected No 2 Coy. of Train. Beastly stopping time out of town. Very fine and warm. Lively.
>
> **4** — S.S.O. [uncertain] R.S.O. to HAZEBROUCK. Put O.C. Headquarters Company in orders as attached to S.S.O. for instruction, and Capt. Oliphant as acting O.C. Headquarters Company. Wet, fine, wet later accompanied by thunder storm. Fine night. Artillery busy.
>
> **5** — S.S.O. and I visited Divisional Headquarters. I also had an interview with Colonel McBrian. Webb all day round with S.S.O. Headquarters Company and 3rd Field Ambulance played off for final in cup tie, result draw, they will play again to morrow and to a finish. O.C. Salvage saw me re a case. Captain Craig saw me re special leave, and we talked the matter over. Rain early, fine later.
>
> **6** — Warned Captain Webb that letter had gone through ordering him to England to take command of 4th Train, and to be ready at a moment's notice. 7th Brigade Sports. I judged horse events along with Colonel McBrian and Major Robbins. Train took Headquarters Company 1st with 91 points, No. 2 Company 2nd with 90½ points, No. 4 Company 3rd with 86½ points. Major McMillan badly injured at the jumps, and after that all jumps cut out. I must say turnouts were allround excellent. 1st prize in Officers Chargers won with 100% marks. No aeroplanes observed. Things generally quiet. Fine all day.
>

### Page 4 — 7–12 July 1916 (Sheet No. 55)

> **1916 July 7** — Got special leave fro Captain Craig to go to Base to consult Colonel Birkett re his nose, if he is detained send car back and wire Division. Called at Divisional Headquarters. Webb gone to England to take command of 4th Train. Craig with him to see Dr Birkett. Generally fine.
>
> **8** — Inspected Candidate from 49th Battalion for his Commission, could not recommend him. Visited Divisional Headquarters. S.S.O. at meeting at D.D. of S.&T. Colonel McBrian and I judge horse events at 4th Brigade Sports, they are along way from perfect especially the harness. D.D.S.&T. 4th Army sent Major McGillivray here but no instructions as to purpose, If it is to take the place of Webb as O.C. Headquarters Company, I will strongly object as he is a M.T. man and S.S.O. has not got his majority yet, so why in creation send any more Majors. I cannot spare time to train Majors, and it is unfair to those who were put here from the first. Fine all day.
>
> **9** — Put S.S.M. Curran through a practical examination on transport and he proved to know uncommonly little for a S.S.M. The G.O.C. and A.A.&Q.M.G. inspected Train transport and the General asked me to express his appreciation of what he had seen, to Officers and men from him. Had a talk to him about Major McGillivray being sent to us, he appreciates the Train point of view, and will think over what I said and see what can be done. Dined with General. Beautiful all day.
>
> **10** — Interviewed V.O. A.D.V.S. came and inspected train horses suffering from skin disease, and gave his instructions re cure. Interviewed Major McGillivray and saw A.A.&Q.M.G. re same Officer, then went on and waited to give evidence on Court Martial but it was adjourned until to morrow. Capt. Hennessy there from 10 a.m. to 6-30 p.m. Fine all day.
>
> **11** — At Court Martial. Called in at Divisional Headquarters, back at Court Martial after lunch and never called, two days wasted. Major McGillivray before Board for examination as to efficiency Board composed of President Lt.Colonel W.O. Simson, Members Major H. Greer, D.A.Q.M.G., and Major Corrigan S.S.O. all 1st Division. S.S.O. took Bayliffe, Brand and Hutchins to see D.D. of S.&T re their Commissions. POPERINGHE shelled in late afternoon about 20 shells. Dull but fine. Captains Craig and Fortin gone to Hospital sick, probably evacuated.
>
> **12** — Had one of my Officers up and gave him a good telling off, he was getting a little slack, and this cannot be tollerated. Poor old POPERINGHE getting shelled again this morning, and very [Entry continues on next page.]

### Page 5 — 12–15 July 1916 (Sheet No. 56)

> **1916 July 12** — [Continued from previous page.] nearly got one of my S.O's, had it not been a blind one I could have said good bye. Examined every horse in Headquarters Company Nos. 2 & 3 Coys. for mange, isolated four suspicious cases. During my absence Adjutant examined man from 2nd C.M.R. for Commission. All are busy fixing details for move of Lahore 9th Artillery Brigade, and the 8th and 9th, the day seemed all too short for what had to be done. Fine but dull.
>
> **13** — No. 3 Company back. O.C. reported and we talked over matters, he is most anxious to get Bayliffe back into his Company when he gets his Commission, and I think he should have him, he has done very good work there, and has not made himself cheap with N.C.O's and men. Called at Divisional Headquarters. Delivered letter from Major McGillivray re Staff course. Called & got certain maps from Colonel Lamb. Saw more of my horses, harness and wagons go away to Lahore to day, it hurt like hell to part with them after so much pains to get them into such good shape. Just as soon as they left me, and they started out loaded, after all my schooling hang me if there were not men riding on loaded wagons, I should like to see them in a couple of months much improved, I don't think, well! well! the sooner I get on to the new lot the better for me, but will they like it? Dull, rain later.
>
> **14** — Called at Headquarters, spoke to D.A.Q.M.G. re the necessity of sending early information re the moves of Units. A talk to A.A.&Q.M.G. re Major McGillivray and Captain Strathey. Captain Clarey called, first time I have seen him since Salisbury days. Ordered ten wagons out to help [uncertain] Engineers stores to front. Fleet of aeroplanes came over about 7 p.m. Warned a/Sgt. Wallace to be ready to go to D.D. of S.&T. with me to morrow. Sent a note re the return of Drivers we sent with Lahore. Dull morning, some rain, but fine afternoon and evening.
>
> **15** — Took a/Sgt. Wallace to keep appointment at D.D. of S.&T. Captain Oliphant came with me. For all the questions candidates are asked we might be spared the necessity of going there, it is an utter waste of time that can be better spent. Adjutant and I visited Headquarters. I handed into A.A.&Q.M.G., Major McGillivray's protest re examination. A.D.V.S. round to see horses again. S.S.O. finds difficulty in getting freshly burnt lime for disinfecting purposes, air slacked is no use. POPERINGHE is out of bounds for all wagons, so wire came late in evening, but no information where those drawing water from there are to get it now. "Q" notified that Captains Craig & Fortin have been evacuated from No. 9 Field Ambulance. [Entry continues on next page.]

### Page 6 — 15–18 July 1916 (Sheet No. 57)

> **1916 July 15** — [Continued from previous page.] 3.45 aeroplanes dropping bombs over us, one in No. 3 Company might have done much harm but by the Grace of God did not. Our planes were asleep, he had done all the damage before they were after him. 10 wagons detailed for Engineers' Dump to front line. Otherwise quiet all day and fine.
>
> **16** — Morning started very dull. A.A.&Q.M.G. rang up re S.S.M. Curran. I called him in and told him how matters stood and that he would be transferred to one of the other Companies, and would have to work to make good. If he tried his utmost then I would be prepared to help him, not otherwise. S.S.O. and D.A.Q.M.G. gone to look up artillery and ascertain some information re their feeding etc. No service to day, Pardre never came to make arrangements. Want Capt. Church to explain why he turned over teams and drivers to No. 2 Company without [uncertain] 24 hours forage and rations, also why change teams before turning over, and why send one with sore shoulder. Smart tricks must not be indulged in if it was such. Headquarters Company to move so as to feed the 3 Brigades of Artillery and the 1 Howitzer from STEENVOORDE end. V.O. to go also. 12 wagons out taking Engineers Stores to front line. Turned to rain.
>
> **17** — Tried case from No. 3 Company. D.D. of S.&T called had long talk to him about the hanging up of promotions. Went with him on his inspection of Headquarters Company and No. 2 Company, expressed his satisfaction at what he saw. Afterwards I went to STEENVOORDE, some trouble about the handing over of wagons to Train by 3rd Artillery Division. I reckon we should have four more wagons since there is a Howitzer Brigade. Did not see horses or wagons. Artillery seem reluctant to turn them over to Train, but all this will adjust itself in time. Major McGillivray called seems very averse to go before Board, I recommended him to see A.A.&Q.M.G. and ask if Board could be adjourned until answer came re an application he put in as he is not anxious to stay with the Train. 12 wagons detailed to take Engineers Stores to front line. Dull early, fine later.
>
> **18** — Called at Divisional Headquarters. S.S.O. and Adjutant to GODAERSVELDT. Got letter from Webb, he thinks I have these artillery horses and wagons for him when he comes out, what a disappointment S/Sgt. Brand to School of Instruction on 20th. Wagons to be again detailed for Engineers, only 6 this time all from No. 3 Company. Inspected this Company to day, everything extremely neat & tidy, horses all in good shape. Fine rain early but cleared up later on and very fine afternoon.

### Page 7 — 19–23 July 1916 (Sheet No. 58)

> **1916 July 19** — Adjutant and I to STEENVOORDE where we saw O.C. Headquarters Company re Train Wagons and Horses for Artillery Brigade, called on Staff re same. Left Paymaster to pay and went on to No 4 Coy. Inspected them, picked up Paymaster then home. 7 wagons out this evening at 9 p.m. Yesterday wagons did not get back until 3 a.m. Have to get rid of 35 H.D. Horses in exchange for equal No. of L.D. Fine all day. A few planes up at 5 a.m. and 7 p.m.
>
> **20** — Inspected First Line Transport of 8th 9th & 10th Field Ambulance 9th & 10th show some improvement but 8th has gone back, I hope I will not find any other Units so bad, if so there will be some Wigs on the green. Saw Colonel Simson and had a long talk about promotions and sending out Majors who really should be junior to some of the Captains who came out with the 1st. It seems to have paid to wait for the 2nd 3rd and 4th Contingents, but give me boys who came out on the first call, they are the right stuff, the worst of it is the right stuff is being pushed aside by something very like rubbish. Called in at Divisional Headquarters. Brand gone to School of Instruction. Planes busy in evening. Fine and hot all day.
>
> **21** — Three German planes very busy about 7/30 right over us, they seemed to be chased off by ours, but these I could not see only hear their machine guns. Inspecting First Line Transport of 9th Brigade, the 60th are best of the bunch. Planes again busy in evening. Inspected horses and wagons of 3rd Artillery Brigade and am rather pleased than otherwise with them, some splendid teams, but I do not like the Canadian Wagon. Fine & bright all day.
>
> **22** — General clean up. In afternoon with Adjutant to 1st C.D.T. where they roped me in to do some judging. Simson seems to have lost much of his keenness and no wonder when they will send out senior Officers from home who should be junior to many out here who have taken all rough and tumble from the first. I can see signs of it in my own Train. If Colonel Simson had been put at Shorncliffe I would bet my life there would not have been all this bother. Dull part of the day a little rain but fine afternoon and evening, things quiet.
>
> **23** — Went with Colonel McBrien to see General Farmer about Major McGillivray being attached to me and about the promotion of Stirrett and Oliphant. All this bother started with the 2nd Train coming out with surplus Majors, they will not revert or go home, the W.O. have referred it to [Entry continues on next page.]

### Page 8 — 23–27 July 1916 (Sheet No. 59)

> **1916 July 23** — [Continued from previous page.] Canada and they will do nothing, hence the injustice to good proved men and the beginning of discontent which I fear will get worse instead of better and I cannot see how to remedy it, the Train will and must suffer. S.S.O. seedy, but all moves going on smoothly.
>
> **24** — Last night Capt. Stirrett S.S.O. sent to Hospital after M.O. had consultation, some kind of a stroke, he did not seem to mind about the stroke, but was worried at the idea of leaving this Train. Inspected Signallers First Line Transport also Divisional Headquarters, called on A.A. & Q.M.G. talked over McGillivray, decided to post him as O.C. Headquarters Company. Asked for Captain Clarey as S.S.O. Oliphant back to his old Company, and Phillips to Headquarters Coy. Saw McGillivray and told him he would be in orders. I hope he will make good, for it will save trouble if he does. All are more than sore at his being pitched forked in and I cannot wonder at it. Scott would have been a far better man, and I am sure he would have got the support which I am afraid McGillivray will only get very gingerly, he is most anxious to do well, and I can only give him a fair trial, and if he is letting the Company down, will have to get him removed. No. 2 moved out and No. 4 in, changed Billets. I must look into complaints from Church & Phillip about leaving dirty camps, all is not right between these two Officers. Fine.
>
> **25** — Had talk with Major Scott re vacancy in Train for S.S.O. Called at Divisional Headquarters and spoke to Major Cameron on the subject who advised my applying for him. Complaint from 9th Bde. Field Ambulance re team of horses sent them. Rev. Wood came to tea and arranged for Sunday Service. Dull morning, fine later.
>
> **26** — Inspected First Line Transport of 8th Brigade, took Major Scott with me so that he would know and see my methods of doing it. At Railhead early, no O.C. Headquarters Company there, and his convoy poor. Paymaster complained about O.C. Headquarters Company butting into his job. Fine rain early but cleared up later.
>
> **27** — Refilling Point, again O.C. Headquarters Company absent and convoy if anything worse than yesterday Saw Captains Church & Phillips and gave them a jawing about petty little grievances. Col. Neil D.D.R.—A.D.V.S. D.D.R. inspected the horses of Train up here, the latter cast jibbers etc. Had V.O., Captain Phillips and Major McGillivray before me re case of suspected mange and Capt. [Entry continues on next page.]

### Page 9 — 27–31 July 1916 (Sheet No. 60)

> **1916 July 27** — [Continued from previous page.] Phillips' interference with V.O., settled this once and for all. O.C. Headquarters Company making inventories, lists and what not instead of getting down to business and learning to know his Company. Captain Oliphant up from No. 2 Company. Misty in morning but fine later though dull.
>
> **28** — Started in mist to go down and inspect First Line Transport of 9th Brigade, inspected same and got back about 4-30 p.m. Fine.
>
> **29** — Inspected Pioneer Battalion and found things decidedly better. No noticeable improvement in Headquarters Company, and O.C. instead of going himself to inspect First Line Transport sent Captain Phillips. I am afraid this man is no good as a C.O. for Headquarters Company. Still calling on us for wagons at night for Engineers Stores. Quiet. Very warm after mist cleared.
>
> **30** — Saw Major McGillivray, had a talk to him, and got him to read & sign report I am sending in and request that he be removed from his Command. Had interview with Captains Heasley and Morrison re filling posts of O.C. Headquarters Company and S.S.O. respectively, explained to them that if they accept and are appointed, it does not necessarily follow that they get their majorities, they are willing to come on these understandings. C. of E. Service. Boiling hot.
>
> **31** — Acting S.S.O. and I called at Divisional Headquarters. A.A.&Q.M.G. rang me up re McGillivray, S.S.O. and O.C. Headquarters Company, he is sending on my letter re the former. Visited Corps Headquarters. D.A.Q.M.G. Corps called with a representation from Army re promotions and discussed same. Very hot all day.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *[signature — [uncertain]] Lieut.-Colonel. / O.C., 3rd Canadian Divl. Train.*

## Open questions / uncertain items (July 1916)
- The cover reads "Volume 6" but the contents page reads "Volume 16 — 7." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number.
- Sheet numbering: the July sheets are numbered 54–60, but June's last sheet was also numbered 54 — Sheet 54 is used twice, once for 29–30 June and once for 1–7 July. Both as written.
- 7 July, 12 July, 15 July, 23 July and 27 July each run across a page break (Sheets 54–55, 55–56, 56–57, 58–59 and 59–60); each entry is given whole under the page where it begins.
- 4 July: the verb between "S.S.O." and "R.S.O." is heavily overtyped and is not read with confidence; it appears to read "took" and is recorded as [uncertain].
- 9 July: the rank abbreviation before "Curran" is partly obscured in the original; it is read as "S.S.M." because the same man is named "S.S.M. Curran" on 16 July.
- 10 July: "A.D.V.S." is overtyped at the first letter; read as A.D.V.S.
- 14 July: the word "take" in "Ordered ten wagons out to help [uncertain] Engineers stores to front" is struck through in the original; it is legible as "take" and is recorded as [uncertain].
- 16 July: a small illegible mark stands before "24 hours" in "without [uncertain] 24 hours forage and rations"; recorded as [uncertain]. "Pardre" is typed as written (presumably "Padre").
- 21 July: a stray "x" is typed between "they" and "seemed" ("they x seemed to be chased off by ours"); the "x" is not carried into the transcription.
- 27 July: at the foot of Sheet 59 the words "H" and a struck-through "Phillips" are a false start, picked up again at the head of Sheet 60 as "Phillips' interference with V.O."; the entry is given as continuous text.
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the July sheets is not read with confidence; it is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name. The signature is identical in form to the one at the foot of the June sheets.
- The Place column is blank on every July sheet; no headquarters place is stated for the month.
- Casualties in the Train itself: Major McMillan badly injured at the jumps at the 7th Brigade Sports (6 July, not a battle casualty); Capt. Stirrett, the S.S.O., sent to hospital after a stroke (24 July); Captains Craig and Fortin evacuated sick from No. 9 Field Ambulance (15 July). No deaths in the Train are recorded in this volume. Poperinghe was shelled on 11 and 12 July and bombs fell on the Train on 15 July, with no casualties recorded.
