# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — June 1917

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps (C.A.S.C.), 3rd Canadian Division
- **Month:** June 1917 (Volume 17) — this volume covers 1 to 30 June 1917
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** June 1917, p. 1 – p. 13 (image files page-0161 – page-0173 of the 370-page diary)
- **June 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 161–173 of 370): cover, contents, diary text 1–30 June, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). The Place and Hour columns are blank throughout; no place is named anywhere in the June 1917 diary text. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Words struck through by the writer are noted as such. A repeated handwritten initial mark reading "J.A.F." [uncertain] appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. The diary is written in the first person by the Officer Commanding, Lt.-Col. Findlay (see the signature at the foot of the 30 June sheet and the letter quoted on 16 June). "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "S.S.O." = as written (held by Major Heasley); "F.P. No. 1" = Field Punishment No. 1; "S.M." = Sergeant-Major; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "A.S.C." = Army Service Corps; "R.C.R's" = Royal Canadian Regiment; "P.P.C.L.I." = Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry. The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."

## Pages 3–13 — Diary text (1–30 June 1917)

### Page 3 — 1–4 June 1917 (Sheet 126)

> **1 June 1917.** — Have a small vein in thigh and tore muscle, which is keeping me in bed for a day or two by Doctors orders. Capt Phillips reported back from leave. 2 beat 13 at football, 2 to nil, a very one sided game. Very fine.
>
> **2 June 1917.** — Bed all day. Nothing to report.
>
> **3 June 1917.** — Feeling easier this morning but that's being taped up. All very quiet. Fine, sunny & warm.
>
> **4 June 1917.** — Still in bed. Doctor will not permit me to go and judge for Genl. MacBrien tomorrow. Says it is taking too much risk. Adjutant Genl to represent me at conference re Divisional Sports. Planted lettuce and radish seeds in tent boxes. Just heard three damned aeroplanes dropped bombs last night on 1st Division and killed three wounding badly several others. Poor Corrigan (D.S.O. M.C.) among them, three fingers badly smashed, wound in groin and leg but he may recover, hope so he was a good sort, straight and clean white all through. Sunny and shot all day. Heard also Capts Oliphant and Graham, S.M. Bayliffe and J. L. Read all mentioned.

### Page 4 — 5–7 June 1917 (Sheet 127)

> **5 June 1917.** — Blister on leg broke and very painful. Still have to keep it up. Oliphant gone to meeting of Sports Committee, Rear Q. Col. Webb O.C. 4th Train called and discussed transport conditions, agreed with letter re what should and what should not be furnished. Lt. Fisher reported back from Paris leave. Genl. Hill called re a man from Train recommended for his commission. Very hot.
>
> **6 June 1917.** — Up and dressed. Leg much better. Lt. Spalding reported here for duty on orders from London. Why he was sent here when we have no vacancies in the Train ticks me. Lt. Higgins reported back from leave. Told off Sergts MacGregor & Wills of No. 1 Company for overstaying their passes. O.C. and Lt. Bell from 4th Reserve Park called about an order to replace the 16 Park wagons doing Engineers work by Train wagons.
>
> **7 June 1917.** — Dust storm yesterday afternoon followed by rain in evening and at night by heavy rain storm, thunder and lightning. Authority came through for Capt. [uncertain] to be posted as Staff Captain to 5th Bde and Capt. Oliphant as (continued on next page)

### Page 5 — 7–9 June 1917 (Sheet 128)

> **7 June 1917 (continued).** — Adjutant. Dvr. Day before me for creating a disturbance and striking another driver in his tent. 14 days F.P. No. 1 and forfeits 28 days pay. He has the dirtiest sheet of any man I have seen yet. Train played the 1st Train at football and we got beat two to nil, the better team deservedly won. Some rain and thunder during day. Adjutant gone to see Lt. Bell 4th Reserve Park re entries for show.
>
> **8 June 1917.** — Quiet night. Met G.O.C. Corps at Div. H.Q. who bid us farewell to take a higher command. Everyone without exception rejoices at his promotion but regrets his leaving the Canadians. His tenure of office has been as successful as it was popular. Beginning to walk easier. Adjutant judging pack animals for show entries. Fine.
>
> **9 June 1917.** — Heavy Arty. work going on last night, and we must have given the Hun a hot time. Last night the little doctor was evacuated with a severe attack of hay fever, and replaced by Doctor Bell. Fine.

### Page 6 — 10–13 June 1917 (Sheet 129)

> **10 June 1917.** — Very quiet all night. Inspecting horses to be entered in coming show. Considering they are carrying on their work they do the Companies great credit.
>
> **11 June 1917.** — Pouring hard accompanied by thunder and lightning, cleared towards mid-day. Made an inspection of 123rd Pioneers. Especially the teams and wagons they are entering for show. Asked specially to inspect a certain limber (note not for entry) Limber ungreased, filthy dirty, tailboards broken, tailboard chain and pin missing, bottom broken. Train played Supply Column at football and beat them 3 to 1. Capt. Graham reported back from leave.
>
> **12 June 1917.** — Meeting of Company Officers for general discussion. Further inspection of teams and wagons for Sports. All busy making further improvements. Fine.
>
> **13 June 1917.** — Final inspection of complete turnout on parade, and a most excellent display. It would have to be something exceptionally good to beat any of the four wagons and teams, the only thing that will do it will be exceeding eye-wash and a judge who appreciates that sort of thing. I don't! I believe on active service to stick to active service conditions and force of circumstances is the only thing that has made me deviate, as I have done. Called at Divl. H.Q. [uncertain] (continued on next page)

### Page 7 — 13–15 June 1917 (Sheet 130)

> **13 June 1917 (continued).** — Many men in each Company busy getting on spit and polish. Fine.
>
> **14 June 1917.** — Good day of tournament. Major Freeman gone on leave, Sports were exceptionally good. Train Coy's came out:— Skipped horse No. 1 1st, No. 3 2nd, No. 4 3rd, A.S.C. wagon and team No. 1 1st, No. 2 2nd, 3rd bracketed for 3rd place. In open event No. 1 Company 1st, No. 2 Coy. 2nd. Capt. Church acting for O.C. No. 1 Coy. during absence of Major on leave. Fine.
>
> **15 June 1917.** — S.S.O. gone on leave. Capt. Phillips acting S.S.O. Meeting of Officers to discuss entries for Corps Sports. Settled several important points called in the Train team to replace 1st prize team injured, cannot understand why the Major [uncertain] knowing he had such a splendid team [uncertain] did not call them in, trim them up and enter them at Divl. Sports, they would win my opinion (continued on next page)

### Page 8 — 15–17 June 1917 (Sheet 131)

> **15 June 1917 (continued).** — have beat the prize team.
>
> **16 June 1917.** — All busy getting ready for Corps Sports. The following letter has been received with much appreciation from the General.
>
> "Divisional Train" — Dear Findlay — I want to thank you very much for the assistance which the Train gave to the Divisional Tournament yesterday in supplying help in various ways which I think in no small measure contributed to the day's success. I wish you would convey my thanks to S.M. Proctor who worked hard for days before the show in preparing the grounds and jumps. His work undoubtedly was largely responsible for the success of the tournament. He deserves great credit. Will you please also thank S.M. Bayliffe and his assistants for the excellent way in which the events in that arena were run off. — I am sure that the first-rate showing made by the Train entries will have pleased you as much as it did myself. — Yours truly (signed) J. Lipsett.
>
> **17 June 1917.** — Officers and N.C.O's from other units inspecting equipped A.S.C. horse to get pointers for show. Had to evacuate our best horse, kicked at Divl. Show. Col. Webb called in evening. Fine and very hot.

### Page 9 — 18–19 June 1917 (Sheet 132)

> **18 June 1917.** — Went with Col. Webb to 1st Corps sports. They were somewhat badly arranged, waste of time during and between events. Flat racing on wet ground a farce. Team jumping splendid. Jumps too crowded. Turnout very good. Thunder, lightning and heavy showers. All my entries to Corps Sports marched off. Looked well.
>
> **19 June 1917.** — Corps Sports. Morning broke with thunder, lightning and rain. Heard case against S.M. Lawrence No. 3 Company and reduced him to his permanent rank. Cannot keep his vile temper under control, a fatal fault in a N.C.O. Train took 2nd and 3rd in A.S.C. Event Corps Sports, 2nd and 3rd open event and 2nd skipped horse. 1st line Transport, R.C.R's took 1st and P.P.C.L.I. 2nd. The Division as a whole were equally successful in the other entries and (continued on next page)

### Page 10 — 19–22 June 1917 (Sheet 133)

> **19 June 1917 (continued).** — will be more than well represented in Army. Lt. Richards returned from leave, his father died when he was home. Thunder storm, unsettled day.
>
> **20 June 1917.** — Went with Adjt. to try and see Train of Col. Marks but the event came on so late could not stop. Ambulance horses very good. All the Imperial Trains I have seen seem to get a better class of horse than we do. Wheeler Yates of No. 1 Company injured by explosion of an old [uncertain] detonator. Three fingers of right hand badly lacerated. V.O. Chambers of 8th Bde called to do anything he could for us during absence on leave of our own V.O. Rain storm early morning and evening.
>
> **21 June 1917.** — O.C. 1st Div. Train came to lunch, who discussed what should and what should not be worn at Army Sports, and how worn, trimming up and comparing authorities.
>
> **22 June 1917.** — Nothing of importance. Cold and wet.

### Page 11 — 23–25 June 1917 (Sheet 134)

> **23 June 1917.** — Inspected all Companies lines. Trying out best team from No. 4 Company to go with team from No. 1 Company if I enter in Army Show. Tried case of Dvrs. Martin and Bishop of No. 1 Company for taking a horse & cart out of the lines without leave, and without any pass going away for 7 hours to take laundry to the laundress. They took my punishment of 28 days F.P. No. 1 and lucky they should think themselves. I gave them the chance. Adjt. gone to represent me at meeting at H.Q. Divn re wearing the dumbbell on shoulder. Final inspection of the show entries.
>
> **24 June 1917.** — Saw all competitors off to show. 6 remounts came in, all but 6 very poor lot. Rainy night, some shelling and quite a number of bombs dropped, but no damage reported to Train. Fine all day.
>
> **25 June 1917.** — Horse show. Was much struck with the superiority of the horse flesh of all Imperial Trains etc., etc., even the Police are mounted on better horses than I am myself. I cannot help thinking in this respect Canadians are getting (continued on next page)

### Page 12 — 25–27 June 1917 (Sheet 135)

> **25 June 1917 (continued).** — a raw deal. Judging from what I saw my officers inform me there are splendid horses at the Base, surely they are more necessary here. Recent remounts are of a shockingly poor class. I believe, also, the Canadians in England can out-class us in horse flesh, there must be selfishness somewhere to account for this. I am convinced our horses were as clean if not cleaner than any in the show. Harness I consider in better condition and they had nothing over us in care of wagons. Lieut. Graham reported back for duty from Hospital. Temperature moderate. Fine all day. Rain evening.
>
> **26 June 1917.** — Major Marshall S.A.Q.M.S. picking out refilling points for us when the Brigades go back into rest. No easy job as most of the land is cropped. Called on A.Q.M.S. Corps re Railhead and M. Transport. O.C. No. 1 Coy. reported back from leave. Cool but sunny fine.
>
> **27 June 1917.** — S.S.O. Major Heasley returned from leave 2 am this morning. Making arrangements for leaving this area. Parts of the day very hot and fine.

### Page 13 — 28–30 June 1917 (Sheet 136)

> **28 June 1917.** — Our Artillery very lively during night and early morning. Heavy rain storms early morning. Fine during day and rain again in evening, particularly heavy at night.
>
> **29 June 1917.** — Col. Simpson came and inspected lines he will take over when the move is completed. Inspected No. 2, 3 and 4 Convoys. Just got all arrangements made for move when wire came in cancelling move. Capt. McCallum reported back from Hospital. Fine.
>
> **30 June 1917.** — Day opened cool and raw with continuous rain all day. Meeting of C.O. Coys to arrange for medical inspection to decide fit and unfit for Regimental Duty.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *[signature — J. [uncertain] Findlay] Lt.-Colonel, O.C. 3rd Canadian Divisional Train.*

## Open questions / uncertain items (June 1917)
- The Place and Hour columns are blank on every sheet; no location is given for the Train at any point in June 1917, so the unit's whereabouts this month cannot be fixed from this volume.
- 1 June: the football result is written "2 beat 13 at football, 2 to nil" — the teams are given as numbers only (presumably Companies or other Trains); read as written.
- 3 June: "taped up" is read as written (the O.C.'s injured leg being taped).
- 4 June: the wounded officer's surname is read as "Corrigan" (D.S.O., M.C.); the hand is not entirely clear. "Sunny and shot all day" is as written — the phrasing is the diarist's own. "S.M. Bayliffe" is confirmed by the same name in the 16 June letter; the 4 June entry also names "J. L. Read".
- 5 June: the word "letter" in "agreed with letter re what should and what should not be furnished" appears struck through in the original but remains legible; transcribed as written.
- 7 June: the captain posted as Staff Captain to 5th Bde is [uncertain] — the name appears to read "Keanessy" (possibly "Kennessy" or "Kennedy") and is not used as a name in this transcript. The entry continues onto the next sheet, ending "Capt. Oliphant as Adjutant."
- 13 June: one word between "Called at Divl. H.Q." and "Many men in each Company" could not be read with confidence; it appears to read "in". The entry continues onto the next sheet.
- 15 June: two words in the sentence about the Major and the splendid team appear struck through by the writer and could not be read; both are marked [uncertain]. The entry's closing sentence ("they would win my opinion have beat the prize team") is as written across the page break.
- 16 June: the letter from Maj.-Gen. J. Lipsett names the O.C. as "Findlay" and praises S.M. Proctor and S.M. Bayliffe; it is quoted in full as written in the diary.
- 19 June: "1st line Transport, R.C.R's took 1st and P.P.C.L.I. 2nd" is as written.
- 20 June: one word before "detonator" is heavily struck through (it appears to read "two") and is marked [uncertain]; "detonator" itself is written in darker ink above the line. The following sentence is read as "Three fingers of right hand badly lacerated."
- 24 June: the remount figures as read are contradictory — "6 remounts came in, all but 6 very poor lot" — the first figure may be 16; transcribed as read.
- The repeated initial mark at the end of most entries is read as "J.A.F." [uncertain]; the signature at the foot of the 30 June sheet reads "J. [uncertain] Findlay, Lt.-Colonel, O.C. 3rd Canadian Divisional Train."
- Sheet numbers 128–133 and 135 are inferred from the consecutive run (126 and 127 seen on pages 3–4, 134 on page 11, 136 on page 13); diary text page numbers 2–12 are printed at the top right of each sheet. The date column on each sheet is headed "June 1917".
- Five entries run across a page break: 7 June (pages 4–5), 13 June (pages 6–7), 15 June (pages 7–8), 19 June (pages 9–10), 25 June (pages 11–12). No dates are missing and no date is duplicated; all 30 days of June 1917 have entries.
- No deaths are recorded in the Train itself this month. The only Train casualty is Wheeler Yates, No. 1 Company, injured 20 June (three fingers of his right hand badly lacerated by an exploding detonator). The 4 June bombing casualties (3 killed, several badly wounded, including Corrigan) were in the 1st Division, not the Train.
- A planned move of the Train was arranged on 27–29 June and then cancelled by wire on 29 June; no move took place in June. Col. Simpson inspected the lines he was to take over.
