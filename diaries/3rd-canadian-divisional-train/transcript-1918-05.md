# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — May 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps (CASC), 3rd Canadian Division (Headquarters, 3rd Canadian Divisional Train)
- **Month:** May 1918 (Volume 28) — this volume covers 1 to 31 May 1918
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** May 1918, p. 1 – p. 8 (image files page-0277 – page-0284 of the 370-page diary)
- **May 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 277–284 of 370): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–31 May, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading; the cover is partly handwritten, and the title page is typewritten with "ORIGINAL" and "CONFIDENTIAL" headings and a handwritten "28" at the top right. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. The Place column is blank throughout the diary sheets; places named in the text are given as written. Abbreviations as written: "D.H.Q." = Divisional Headquarters; "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "S.A.A." = small arms ammunition; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "F.P." = Field Punishment; "D.D.S.& T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "D.D.M.S." = Deputy Director of Medical Services; "A.P.M." = Assistant Provost Marshal; "R.S.O." = Railhead Supply Officer; "H.T." = horse transport; "inst:" = instant (of this month). The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."

## Pages 4–8 — Diary text (1–31 May 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The diary sheets are numbered "Sheet 1." to "Sheet 5."

### Page 4 — 1–5 May 1918 (Sheet No. 1)

> **1 May 1918.** — S.S.O's of 2th and 3rd Divisions called and met my S.S.O. arranging for taking over of certain units and stores. Adjutant with Capt: Fisher and Lieut: Hessey gone to new area to look out horse standings. 6 Shells came in at 4-30, half of them duds. Fine.
>
> **2 May 1918.** — Left BARLIN at 2.00 p.m. Ten minutes before leaving got a wire to say Railhead would be DIEVAL not HOUDAIN as arranged for. No telephonic communication at BEUGIN or BAJUS, and on a move there are no places for Train Headquarters, so arranged that Adjutant and S.S.O. get into Stables at Chateau BRYAS with Divisional Headquarters and I remained with No.3 Company at BEUGIN. Visiting BRYAS daily. Fine.
>
> **3 May 1918.** — Called at Divisional Headquarters and found out about next move. Capt: Oliphant and Major McClellan called. Sale of the Train Salvage is as under for the month, 3 Boyer Boilers, 30 Oil Drums, 350 oat sacks, 117 petrol tins, 45 sandbags, 225 lbs. lead paper, 70 bread sacks, 1200 horse shoes, 2 tar barrels, 1 pump and 12' of hose, 3 old tents, 1120 lbs. dripping, 4 sniper's plates, 20 lbs. paper, 500 rounds S.A.A., used, 200 unused, 1 L.E.Rifle, 10 sacks tins for solder extraction. Fine.
>
> **4 May 1918.** — Fine. No.3 received sudden orders to move to FAUCQUENHEM at 11.30 when I called at Divisional H.Q. with Capt: Fisher. Hurried on to dump at DIEVAL and saw convoy then back to BEUGIN. No.3 got lunch, packed up and had started off at 2.00 p.m. Signed papers recommending St/Sgt. Crabtree for his Commission. - Col: McAlpine called late and we had a great talk over matters. It looks as if he was going to be a good help to us.
>
> **5 May 1918.** — Wet. L.P. Scott, a reinforcement who only came to us yesterday, I had to send to the Ambulance today (swollen knees), there being no M.O. within miles. A reinforcement like that is worse than useless. An Artillery Officer came in to billet. S.S.O. and Adjutant came in for a couple of hours.

### Page 5 — 6–12 May 1918 (Sheet No. 2)

> **6 May 1918.** — Went to Divisional H.Q. and took Paymaster to meeting there of staff from home, getting back 5.15. Col: Hanson of 5th Artillery came in at 7.00 a.m., took over Mess and Office. Arranged for lorry to call and move Train H.Q. in morning.
>
> **7 May 1918.** — S.S.O. and Adjutant moved from BRYAS and I from BEUGIN to LA GOULEE to the old quarters we had in December last year. New Paymaster came with us. & Very wet in morning.
>
> **8 May 1918.** — Train mileage for week ending 5th May, average miles per Company per day, 10.7, total distance 7669 miles, on agricultural farm, 9 miles. Longest distance any one wagon 26, No.1 Company, shortest water cart 1. Fine. Major Adams called.
>
> **9 May 1918.** — Fine. St/Sgt. Crabtree to be paraded in front of General Hill, for interview re his Commission.
>
> **10 May 1918.** — Colder. Paymaster finished paying all Companies, he now can square up his accounts and hand over to his successor. Received a somewhat peculiar letter from Major Adams marked 'Personal', but in spite of this I had to hand it to the Adjutant to make certain enquiries.
>
> **11 May 1918.** — Again dull and cold. From SAINS BOUVIGNY salved and turned into railhead BARLIN to R.S.O. among other things 6700 empty Petrol tins, 1600 empty petrol cases, 7 loads empty oil casks, 2 loads oil drums, 2 loads drum jars and casks, 2 loads sacks. The General passed in his car, got out and had a talk on things in general. Met Capt: Routh the new A.P.M., last time I met him was over a year ago on the Somme.
>
> **12 May 1918.** — S.S.O. and Adjutant and Lieut: Green gone to AIRE to try and get boots. Sent reply to Major Adams, I would come up in the morning and deal with the cases he wrongly remanded for F.G.C.M. Dull.

### Page 6 — 13–19 May 1918 (Sheet No. 3)

> **13 May 1918.** — Showery and cold. All day at No.1 Company trying three cases, gave one man 28 days F.P. No.1, and remanded two for F.G.C.M., detailing Major Adams to take the Summary.
>
> **14 May 1918.** — Between 12 and 1.00 a.m. a Company of Chinese came in without an Officer, soaked to the skin and banked in anywhere, chicken houses, rabbit hutches, and any place affording shelter from the rain. As far as could be made out their Officers left them, went to their own billets, leaving the Chinese to find for themselves and frighten the wits out of the inhabitants. Train mileage for week ending 12th inst:— No.1 Company average 10.6 miles per wagon, total distance 2,229 miles. No.2 " " 11.0 " " " " 1,526 " No.3 " " 10.8 " " " " 1,325 " No.4 " " 8.6 " " " " 1,383 " Total distance covered by Train 6,463 miles, longest distance for any one wagon, 20 miles No.1 Company, several of 18 miles No.2 Company. S.S.O. and Adjutant back to former billet to find key.
>
> **15 May 1918.** — Very fine and really hot. Called with S.S.O. at Division. Harris of Supply Column called, agreed to judge for 1st Brigade in competition.
>
> **16 May 1918.** — Very fine, sunny and warm. Major McClellan left today for duty in England. Adjutant Paymaster and I went to hear the 'Dumb-bell' entertainment at FAUCQUENHEM by special invitation; from there I went on and tried a case at No.1 Company at AUCHEL and inspected the Company, and found it in splendid shape, particularly the wagons.
>
> **17 May 1918.** — Again very fine. Bombing pretty lively round about midnight last night.
>
> **18 May 1918.** — Again All day doing judging for 1st Canadian Division. Loaned complete Supply Section to 14th Divisional Train, Imperials, and can ourselves still carry on. Very hot and fine.
>
> **19 May 1918.** — Again fine and hot. Usual bombing last night. [Entry continues on Sheet No. 4.]

### Page 7 — 19–26 May 1918 (Sheet No. 4)

> **19 May 1918 (continued).** — Mr. Green of the Special Platoon giving Supply Sections special instruction in musketry.
>
> **20 May 1918.** — Mr. Green again instructing. Adjutant on Court Martial. S.S.O. out all day making complete rounds. Hot and fine.
>
> **21 May 1918.** — Inspecting 1, 2, and 3 Field Ambulances for Shield Competition at VILLERS-SUR-SIMON DENIER and [word struck through] MAROEUIL. On way back called in at 4th Divisional H.Q., where D.D.M.S. had to see Colonel [uncertain], suffering from gas knee. Found a Garden Party going on. Stayed three quarters of an hour, and only got back at 7-15, a long and hard day. Very hot indeed.
>
> **22 May 1918.** — Again very hot. Transport mileage for week ending 19th, average for the four Companies, 11.2, 11.5, 10.3, and 7.8 miles. Total mileage, 5,940; longest haul per wagon, 15 miles; shortest, 1. Average per Company, 1485 miles.
>
> **23 May 1918.** — Capt: Fisher called, reported a man to come up before me for stealing.
>
> **24 May 1918.** — Called at Div.H.Q. re move. S.S.O. out making Supply arrangements. Heard case of theft of beer, and remanded for F.G.C.M. 24 Reinforcements arrived, accommodated them for the night in loft of this billet. Wet.
>
> **25 May 1918.** — Went with S.S.O. to Div.H.Q. and to Refilling Point at WITTERNESSE. No.4 Company moved into FONTES. Called with S.S.O. on Col: Draper at 8th Brigade re potatoes. Fine.
>
> **26 May 1918.** — Fine. Case of S.M. from No.4 Company today before me, and re-primanded him. At 4.00 p.m. received word Railhead changes tomorrow to WITTERNESSE. Companies ordered to move as under:— No.1 to BLESSY with refilling point at WITTERNESSE. No.2 to RELY with refilling point at FONTES. No.3 to FONTES and their refilling point there also. [Entry continues on Sheet No. 5.]

### Page 8 — 26–31 May 1918 (Sheet No. 5)

> **26 May 1918 (continued).** — No.4 to ESTREE BLANCHE with their refilling point at same place. Train Headquarters remains at LA GOULEE. Could not go to dine with Colonel Draper. S.S.O. and Adjutant out now to put above into effect.
>
> **27 May 1918.** — Railhead changes today to WITTERNESSE. Lieut: Green of Special Platoon left this morning, and we shall all miss him. Visited Railhead in morning with S.S.O. ordered some spade work to be done so as to permit of another road being used, and lessen congestion of wagons at Railhead. Visited all Companies with Adjutant in their new locations. All seem to be settling down nicely. S.S.O. and Adjutant again to railhead in evening. Fine.
>
> **28 May 1918.** — A little duller, but fine. Bombs were lively last night. S.S.O. and Adjutant gone round Companies and Railhead. Mileage for week ending 26th, 6,658 miles, or an average of 10.7 miles per day per wagon. Longest distance any one wagon 20 miles, shortest 1 mile.
>
> **29 May 1918.** — Not quite so bright. Adjutant and Capt: Robinson gone on Court Martial. Case dismissed.
>
> **30 May 1918.** — 8th and 9th Brigades change over today, but Companies that feed them do not change. Adjutant and I called on Colonel McAlpine at PERNES, then on to No.2 Company and saw Capt: Church and his Company. From there to No.1 and tried a case of stealing. Adjourned the case for further consideration. Called in at Div.H.Q. Fine.
>
> **31 May 1918.** — Fine. Went to HAM-EN-ARTOIS to inspect No.9 Field Ambulance; on from there to BELVAL to inspect No.13. Wire came in for No.2 Company to be prepared to move on receipt of order from RELY to LIETTRES. Total ration strength for month, 654,165 men; 137,413 horses.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] d'Auvergne Findlay, Lieut-Colonel, O.C., 3rd Canadian Divl. Train.

## Open questions / uncertain items (May 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 28" but the contents page reads "Volume 18 — 5." — both as written.
- The contents page says "Diary Text 5 Pages", which matches Sheets 1–5 on pages 4–8; there are no appendices. May has no separate handwritten title page like April; the title page on page 3 is typewritten.
- 1 May: "S.S.O's of 2th and 3rd Divisions" — the ordinal is typed "2th", presumably a slip for 2nd; it is transcribed as written.
- 2 May: "BEUGIN or BAJUS" and "Chateau BRYAS" as written.
- 3 May: the salvage sale figures are transcribed as read, including "3 Boyer Boilers", "225 lbs. lead paper" and "4 sniper's plates".
- 4 and 16 May: "FAUCQUENHEM" as written.
- 5 May: "L.P. Scott" as written.
- 7 May: "LA GOULEE" as written (also 26 May); "& Very wet in morning" — the "&" is as typed.
- 11 May: "2 loads drum jars and casks" and "SAINS BOUVIGNY" as written.
- 14 May: the mileage table figures are No.1, 2,229 miles; No.2, 1,526 miles; No.3, 1,325 miles; No.4, 1,383 miles; the four company figures add to the stated Train total of 6,463 miles exactly.
- 16 May: "Adjutant Paymaster and I" as typed, with no comma.
- 19 May: the entry begins at the foot of Sheet No. 3 and continues at the head of Sheet No. 4; both parts are transcribed as one entry. The same applies to 26 May (Sheets 4–5).
- 21 May: a word before "MAROEUIL" is struck through in the original (typed over with "XXXX"); the Colonel's name after "D.D.M.S. had to see Colonel" is not read with confidence and is recorded as [uncertain]. "Suffering from gas knee" as written.
- 31 May: "to inspect No.13" — the unit is not spelled out in the entry; it follows the inspection of No.9 Field Ambulance.
- No entry is missing: every day 1–31 May 1918 has an entry. The Place column is blank on every diary sheet.
- Names read as: Lieut-Col. d'Auvergne Findlay (O.C.); Majors Adams and McClellan; Colonel McAlpine; Captains Oliphant, Fisher, Church, Robinson and Routh (new A.P.M.); Lieuts. Hessey and Green; St/Sgt. Crabtree; L.P. Scott; Colonel Hanson (5th Artillery); General Hill; Harris (Supply Column); Colonel Draper (8th Brigade); "The General" of 11 May is not named.
- No Train casualties are recorded in May. A Company of Chinese labourers arrived without an officer on 14 May and took shelter in chicken houses and rabbit hutches; 24 reinforcements arrived on 24 May.
