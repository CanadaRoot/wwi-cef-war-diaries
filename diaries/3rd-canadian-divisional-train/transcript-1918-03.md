# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — March 1918

- **Unit:** Headquarters, 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, 3rd Canadian Division (Canadian Army Service Corps)
- **Month:** March 1918 (Volume 26) — this volume covers 1 to 31 March 1918
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** March 1918, p. 1 – p. 11 (image files page-0256 – page-0266 of the diary)
- **March 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–11): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–31 March, Appendix A (diagram of Divisional Train lines and farm plots). Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S." The Place and Hour columns are blank throughout; the Train was at La Targette for the whole month. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. Abbreviations as written: "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "A.A.Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "F.P. No. 1" = Field Punishment No. 1; "C.C.S." = Casualty Clearing Station; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "H.T." = Horse Transport; "M.T." = Mechanical Transport; "A.F.W." = Army Form W.; "S.S.M." = Squadron Sergeant Major (here, senior warrant rank in the Train); "Wblr." = Wheeler; "Sdlr." = Saddler; "Farr." = Farrier; "Dvr." = Driver; "Y.M.C.A." = Young Men's Christian Association. Appendix A is a hand-drawn diagram; its labels and the plot table are transcribed as read, not redrawn.

## Pages 4–10 — Diary text (1–31 March 1918)

### Page 4 — 1–7 March 1918 (Sheet No. 14)

> **1/3/18.** — Inspected transport of 8th Brigade. They have improved all round, gave them 30 minutes lecture. Division rang up and wired re Oliphant going to 2nd Train. Replied by wire. Tried case of Supply Man for malingering, and remanded him for F.G.C.M.
>
> **2/3/18.** — Called at Division H.Q., saw Colonel Gibson and had talk on the amount of land Train could cultivate, Major Brook also joined in. Making out recommendations for honours. Saw Capt. Oliphant re his going to 2nd Train and told him what I said to Colonel Gibson on the subject. Bitterly cold, and snow later.
>
> **3/3/18.** — Will take recommendations for honours myself to Division H.Q. Applied for Capt. McLeod's promotion on A.F.W. 3023, and delivered it personally to Division.
>
> **4/3/18.** — Cold, fog and wet. Making up Diary for month. Called in at Division and altered list for Honours, having had another Honour added for Other Ranks. Owing to wet very little ploughing could be done.
>
> **5/3/18.** — For week ending 3/3/18, 114 teams were used on Divisional Farm doing an average mileage 11.5 miles per day; 35 teams on Train Farm covering an average distance of 10.7 miles per day; the average distance travelled for all purposes, teams and train wagons, works out at 9.5 miles per day per team.
>
> **6/3/18.** — The case from No.3 Company I remanded for F.G.C.M. returned by Division for me to deal with myself. I had the man up before me, and pointed out how serious the charge was. I gave him the limit I am allowed to impose, viz: 28 days F.P. No.1. Wrote to A.A.Q.M.G. giving account of Train Transport work for week ending 3rd inst: Amount of time on the Divisional and Train Farms, and amount of land broken.
>
> **7/3/18.** — Beautiful Spring morning. The following names submitted by Company Officers have been forwarded to me for recognition for good services rendered. No.1 Company, Cpl Ward and Dvr. Foster, No.2 Company S.S.M. Wicks, and S/Sgt/Sdlr. Burrell, No.3 Company S/Sgt. Farr, McKenzie and Dvr. Elver, No.4 Company Farr/Cpl. Turner and Dvr. Fisk. Previous names received from Companies by the Acting O.C., but not recorded are as follows:— S.S.Major (W.O.) H.J. Bayliffe, Wblr/S/Sgt. Wakelin A.J., Sdlr/S/Sgt. Burrell P., Cpl. (a/Sgt). McMillan R., S/Sgt. Crabtree, T.B.

### Page 5 — 7–11 March 1918 (Sheet No. 15)

> **7/3/18. Ctd.** — McKenzie and Dvr. Elver, No.4 Company Farr/Cpl. Turner and Dvr. Fisk. Previous names received from Companies by the Acting O.C., but not recorded are as follows:— S.S.Major (W.O.) H.J. Bayliffe, Wblr/S/Sgt. Wakelin A.J., Sdlr/S/Sgt. Burrell P., Cpl. (a/Sgt). McMillan R., S/Sgt. Crabtree, T.B.
>
> **8/3/18.** — Called in at Divisional H.Q., nothing startling. Visited all 4 Train Companies, and spent a considerable time on Train Farm. Bright and fine.
>
> **9/3/18.** — Called meeting of Company Commanders and discussed men's leave, and other Train matters. Capt. Richards reported his knee was troubling him again and he was ordered to report to C.C.S. tomorrow. Adjutant going round all Companies. I am not satisfied that No.3 Company [uncertain] is progressing; it certainly is not doing so at same rate as the others. Gardening and farming. Fine and bright, but cold.
>
> **10/3/18.** — Inspected Nos. 4 and 1 Companies. Watched Major Adams inspecting Baggage wagons and teams. Pointed out as they passed defects in the fitting of harness, more especially breechings. They have done splendid work in Nos. 1, 2, and 4 Companies re-arranging and improving their camps in an intelligent way, and this in addition to the farm work they are doing. No.3 Company does not show the same good results. Called in at Division H.Q. in afternoon, all out. Went on and saw Major Scott at 2nd Train. M.O. gone on leave. Capt. Richards evacuated to C.C.S. Very fine day. Capt. Aldous called and had tea.
>
> **11/3/18.** — Made another inspection of all companies and farming done by them. Had to jerk up Capt. Phillips for not calling his men to attention when I appeared in his lines. Ordered one of his Corporals to be paraded before him for same reason. Major Adams gone on week's leave to Paris. Frost at night, but beautiful day. ### Page 6 — 11–17 March 1918 (Sheet No. 16)

> **11/3/18. Ctd.** — cleaner. Stables, clean. Major McLelland reported back from leave, and also Subaltern from No.1. Mileage for week ending this date, 641 teams employed, 478 in Train work, 103 on Divisional Farm, 59 on Train Farm, covering 6,400 miles in week, or 9.7 per team. Longest hauls 30, 19, 14 and 12 miles, shortest 1, 1, and 2.
>
> **12/3/18.** — Fine morning. Inspected 8th Field Ambulance. Horses well groomed and in good condition. Hoofs and docks require more attention. Shoeing good. Harness clean but requires much more dubbin to make it more pliable, soft, and keep it in good condition. Wagons in good running order; a few minor repairs necessary and more attention to small details. Stables clean and sweet. Late last night orders came through to haul from railhead by H.T., this necessitated taking teams off Divisional Farm. Inspected 9th Field Ambulance this afternoon. Horses good shape, clean well shod and cared for. Harness clean and soft but requires a little more dubbin to keep leather right. Wagons require some repairs and should be cleaner. They had only moved in to new camp which accounts for a good deal. Called at Division H.Q., saw A.A.Q.M.G.
>
> **13/3/18.** — Hauling from Pack Train and delivering too. Inspected First Line Transport of 7th Bde and reported on same.
>
> **14/3/18.** — Wet morning and colder, turning to fine later. All Companies carrying on improvements. Y.M.C.A. gave concert to Train owing to [uncertain] could not attend.
>
> **15/3/18.** — Wrote to Y.M.C.A. expressing regret at unavoidable absence from Complimentary Concert and expressing at same time our thanks and gratitude.
>
> **16/3/18.** — G.O.C. made thorough inspection of the whole Train, and had tea at No.1 Coy. Going away he said to me "I should feel proud of my Command," therefore I gather he was satisfied with his inspection. Very fine all day.
>
> **17/3/18.** — Arrived at 1st Train to help in their inspection. I took harness, Col Webb, Wagons and Equipment. Major Critten[uncertain] Horses, and an Imperial Col, the Lines, Billets, Kitchens etc.

### Page 7 — 17–20 March 1918 (Sheet No. 17)

> **17/3/18. Ctd.** — It was a long tiring job and I only got back here at 7.00 P.M. Very fine day.
>
> **18/3/18.** — Called in at Div. H.Q. saw A.A.Q.M.G. re a man from No.2 Company, absent 7 days without leave, and was told it would be quite right for me to deal with him myself. I had a talk on Generals Inspection of Train, he was pleased at what he saw, and on my judging at 1st Train yesterday. Captain Oliphant reported back from leave, and left in evening to report as Adjutant to 2nd Train. Dealt with the man who overstayed his leave and gave him 28 days F.P. No.1. Went to the Dumb Bell Concert in evening with Adjutant, Paymaster and Interpreter, and a splendid show it was. Fine all day cloudy towards night.
>
> **19/3/18.** — Rain all day. Called at Divisional H.Q. Asked Blackstock to post Capt. de Graves as Adjutant vice Oliphant to 2nd Train. Farm work on Company plots for week ending 17th 29 teams with 14 wagons, time 139 hours plus 10 hours repairing implements; 139 hours. Work on Train Farm for same period 6 teams for 30 hours. Acres under cultivation, No.1 Company 9680 square yards, No.2 6214 sq. yds., No.3 4960 sq. yds., No.4 4840 sq. yds., total 25,694 sq. yds. Add for Train Farm, 21760, and it gives for the Train, 9.82 acres. Routine work for Train for week 672 wagons and teams, covering miles per wagon per day for No.1 Company 14.2 miles, No.2 Company 7., No.3 Company 9.3., No.4 Company 9.3., or an average for the Train of 9.9 miles.
>
> **20/3/18.** — Working out acreage for Train Farm land and plotting same. Sent copy to A.A.Q.M.G. Land cultivated exclusive of private plots 9.7 acres. Tried Dvr. Webster of No.4 Company for coming on parade with dirty bits. Ordered him to parade for next 10 evenings and show clean bits to Orderly Officer of the Day and to do 2 hours a day for the period doing harness cleaning.

### Page 8 — 21–26 March 1918 (Sheet No. 18)

> **21/3/18.** — Fine all day. Away judging transport of 5th, 10th, 4th, and 12th Cdn. Field Ambulances for Shield.
>
> **22/3/18.** — Called at Div. H.Q. Went with S.S.O. to 62nd Division S.S.O., re taking over units from them. Inspected Remounts and distributed them among companies. Fine.
>
> **23/3/18.** — Capt: Graham and Lieut: Ferg gone to Forward area to take over Reserve Rations in forward positions, consequent on side slip. S.S.O. called on S.S.O. 62nd and arranged for taking over some of his stores. Fine all day.
>
> **24/3/18.** — Consequent on heavy operations all leave stopped until further notice. 2nd are moving in and we are feeding some of their units. S.S.O. gone to make final arrangements. He called on Div. H.Q. for me. No mails for four days. Fine and bright.
>
> **25/3/18.** — Dull morning, looks like rain. S.S.O. sprained his ankle at Railhead this morning getting out of Car. S.O. Div. Troops got a shocking cold. At midnight, wire came through to stand to in case we had to retire, but in morning, stand to was taken off. Frost during night.
>
> **26/3/18.** — M.O. reported back from leave 1.00 a.m. S.S.O. and I called at Div. H.Q. I took up the question with A.A.Q.M.G. re the recall of all my wagons detailed to Corps and PERPAY; also on stone carting, only during this uncertain time. Went on, with S.S.O. fixed up about a store for reserve rations etc. Called in at Corps H.Q. and saw Col: Simpson, and had long talk with him re posting of Officers etc. Col: Scott was there also. Later sent a letter as requested showing vacancies, and submitted names to fill same. Mileage for week ending 24th, average per wagon per day, No.1 Company 9 miles, No.2, 9.3., No.3, 7.4., No.4, 9.4., longest haul for [uncertain] 2, 3, 3, and 4 Companies, 18, 20, 20 and 16 respectively. Agricultural work done by Train, 36 hours, or about 72 miles. Total mileage covered, 7,868.

### Page 9 — 27–31 March 1918 (Sheet No. 19)

> **27/3/18.** — Capt: de Graves called on Col: Simpson and arranged that he come and dine. He did so with me and I had a long talk to Major Adams. Simpson told him to hang on where he was left about 11 p.m. At 3.00 a.m. Gas ready signal came through and stand to. Hot work going on over Vimy Ridge.
>
> **28/3/18.** — Shells dropping fast and furiously all round, in dumps and in Companies' lines. Stand to off, but shelling continues, and we are in a warm place. Railhead changed to ACQ and little M.T. available. S.S.O. out looking for a new refilling point. Ordnance moving and if things don't get any quieter we shall be compelled to follow. Rain has come on, and that should help us.
>
> **29/3/18.** — Rained all night and still raining this morning, with a fair wind. This is one of the few times the Boche has had the elements against him. One Engineer Officer killed near the Dump yesterday and two of the Train hit, but not enough to keep them from duty after being dressed. Called on D.A.Q.M.G. re possibility of having to move the Train opposed to it, so concluded to remain in present position and wait developments. Cleared up by afternoon and was fine night.
>
> **30/3/18.** — Fair, quiet night. All news received is reassuring. General Currie's stimulating letter to Corps came in. The following striking passage occurs, "Under your devoted Officers in the coming battle you will advance or fall where you stand facing the enemy. To those who will fall I say — "You will not die but step into immortality"." That is the sort of stuff to get from one of the best; is it any wonder he is loved by his Corps. Stormy night, wind and rain.
>
> **31/3/18.** — Easter Sunday. Rain stopped, sun out, and guns comparatively quiet. Yesterday I remanded a man in No.2 Company for F.G.C.M. charged with striking his superior officer and detailed Major Adams to take Summary of Evidence.

### Page 10 — 31 March 1918 continued (Sheet No. 20)

> **31/3/18. Ctd.** — Capt: Hudson of Divisional Farm asked for teams, found it impossible under existing circumstances to detail any, and was more than surprised at his asking. Still quiet and very little shelling. Mr. F.W. Carson as reinforcement from Base, reported to Corps and Corps sent him to Division, they rang me up and I sent car for him. He got here at 7-30 p.m. and I gave him the same old warning re drink, cards, etc. Told Adjutant to post him to No.4 Company, and he will take him down and introduce him. Showers during day. Daily average rationed during month:— Men 23,128, Horses 5,268. Highest numbers any one day,— Men 24,896, Horses 6,115.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *[signature — [uncertain]] Lieut.-Colonel, / O.C. 3rd Canadian Div. Train*

## Open questions / uncertain items (March 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 26" but the contents page reads "Volume 18 - 3." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number. The cover also carries the handwritten note "with appendix 'A'".
- 7 March is split across Sheets No. 14 and No. 15 ("7 Ctd."), 11 March across Sheets No. 15 and No. 16 ("11 Ctd."), 17 March across Sheets No. 16 and No. 17 ("17 Ctd."), and 31 March across Sheets No. 19 and No. 20 ("31 Ctd."); all continuations are transcribed as written.
- 9 March: one word in "I am not satisfied that No.3 Company [uncertain] is progressing" is not read with confidence.
- 11 March: "641 teams employed" is as typed, but the three subtotals given (478 in Train work, 103 on Divisional Farm, 59 on Train Farm) add to 640, not 641.
- 14 March: the phrase in "Y.M.C.A. gave concert to Train owing to [uncertain] could not attend" is not read with confidence.
- 17 March: the name of the officer who took the horses at 1st Train reads "Critten[uncertain]" — the remainder of the name is not read with confidence.
- 19 March: "time 139 hours plus 10 hours repairing implements; 139 hours" is as typed; the second "139 hours" appears to repeat the first. The acres figure here (9.82 acres) differs from 20 March (9.7 acres, exclusive of private plots) and from Appendix A (9.80 acres).
- 26 March: "longest haul for [uncertain] 2, 3, 3, and 4 Companies" is as typed; the company numbers look duplicated (probably Nos. 1–4) and are not read with confidence.
- 27 March: the sentence "Simpson told him to hang on where he was left about 11 p.m." is garbled as typed; transcribed as written.
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the March sheets is not read with confidence; it appears to read "Lawrence Lindsay" [uncertain] but is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name.
- The repeated handwritten initial mark at the end of most entries is not read with confidence and is not transcribed against every entry.
- Appendix A: Plot 4 is labelled "Train" in the diagram while the table lists a Plot 4 of 12,100 sq. yds.; the table totals 47,474 sq. yds., given as 9.80 acres. The map reference is read as "42a 0.6., Sheet 51 B." — as written.
- Names read as: Capt. Oliphant; Col. Gibson; Maj. Brook; Capt. McLeod; Cpl. Ward; Dvr. Foster; S.S.M. Wicks; S/Sgt./Sdlr. Burrell; S/Sgt. Farr; McKenzie; Dvr. Elver; Farr/Cpl. Turner; Dvr. Fisk; S.S.Maj. (W.O.) H. J. Bayliffe; Wblr/S/Sgt. Wakelin A. J.; Sdlr/S/Sgt. Burrell P.; Cpl. (a/Sgt.) McMillan R.; S/Sgt. Crabtree, T. B.; Capt. Richards; Capt. Aldous; Capt. Phillips; Maj. Adams; Maj. McLelland; Col. Webb; Capt. de Graves; Blackstock; Dvr. Webster; Capt. Graham; Lieut. Ferg; Col. Simpson; Col. Scott; Maj. Adams; Capt. Hudson; Mr. F. W. Carson; Gen. Currie.
- Casualties in this volume: one Engineer officer killed near the dump on 28 March (reported 29 March) and two Train men hit (wounded) but able to remain on duty after being dressed. Capt. Richards was evacuated to the C.C.S. on 10 March with a knee injury; the S.S.O. sprained his ankle on 25 March.
