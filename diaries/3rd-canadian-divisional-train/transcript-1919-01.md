# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — January 1919

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, Canadian Army Service Corps (C.A.S.C.), 3rd Canadian Division (styled "H.Q. 3rd Canadian Divisional Train" on the cover)
- **Month:** January 1919 (Volume 36) — this volume covers 1 to 31 January 1919
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** January 1919, p. 1 – p. 8 (image files page-0354 – page-0361 of the diary)
- **January 1919 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–8): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–31 January, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" not struck through on these sheets). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Handwritten insertions and corrections in the original are noted where they occur. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. The signature at the foot of the January sheets is read as [uncertain] (see open questions). "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "S.O." = Supply Officer; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "F.P." = Field Punishment; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "M.Ps." = as written; "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column; "R.T." = as written; "O.B.E." = Order of the British Empire. The place column reads "In the Field" for every entry this month; the diary text names railheads and places as written.

### Page 4 — 1–5 January 1919

> **In the Field, 1-1-19.** — Capt Wilks gone on leave. S.S.O. has trouble getting Pack Trains cleared at Enghien and at Tournai same time. Wet.
>
> **In the Field, 2-1-19.** — Sent wagon to Mons [inserted above the line, replacing a struck word] to collect reserve stores up left behind. Getting ready to receive orders to entrain. Times of January 1st came in and see by it that Major McLeod has the O.B.E. Gold and [uncertain].
>
> **In the Field, 3-1-19.** — All Companies coming into their final areas. Demobilization (advance) scheme in. We can make arrangements now to facilitate matters arranging to clear railhead on the 5th by R.T. Capts Fisher and Higgins called. Some rain.
>
> **In the Field, 4-1-19.** — Arranged that Coys come in [uncertain]. O.C. No.1 came to dinner, he tells me all his men are tired out, and I dont wonder at it.
>
> **In the Field, 5-1-19.** — Capt Hudson called in on his way back to Canada to say good-bye, all M.Ps. are recalled. Transport return for week ending Dec. 29th

### Page 5 — 6–13 January 1919

> **In the Field, 6-1-19.** — Major deGraves gone on leave. M.O. joined Mess and will begin demobilization Medical examinations of Train. Lieut Hills reported to relieve Lieut Underwood as S.O. Coln., Interpreter reported back from leave at 3.30. Left for Jemappes to settle up claim for coal got there G.D. car had to get repaired and put up for night. Dry but cold.
>
> **In the Field, 7-1-19.** — Had greatest difficulty getting Claimant to accept payment and get receipt. Only able to start back at noon.
>
> **In the Field, 8-1-19.** — Wet and cold nothing to report.
>
> **In the Field, 9-1-19.** — Again wet. Coal situation serious S.S.O. gone to back area to see if he can get any. preparing recommendations for Foreign decorations.
>
> **In the Field, 10-1-19.** — Fine. Submitted recommendations. Tried case of driver in No 3. Coy who refused my punishment and elected to be tried by F.G.C.M. he has not a chance and is a sour stubborn individual Deputed Adjt. to take evidence.
>
> **In the Field, 11-1-19.** — Fine but cold. Arrangements are being made to give a Train dance this evening.
>
> **In the Field, 12-1-19.** — Dance came off and was a great success. All running smoothly.
>
> **In the Field, 13-1-19.** — Two Men up for drunkenness on F.P. and returned to duty. Inspected No 1 Coy there not improving, this talk of demobilization has demoralised every one and no one seems to have the old interest or desire to give of their best.

## Open questions / uncertain items (January 1919)
- 2 January: the sentence about Major McLeod ends "has the O.B.E. Gold and [uncertain]" — the final word is a faint fragment (looks like "—et") and cannot be read.
- 4 January: "Arranged that Coys come in [uncertain]" — the word after "in" is faint and cannot be read.
- 6 January: "as S.O. Coln." is read as written (presumably "S.O. Column"); "G.D. car" is read as written.
- 14 January: the No. 3 Company total for the week ending 5 January reads "3735 [uncertain]" — the middle digit is faint and may be a 5. The four company totals as read (4473 + 2190 + 3735 + 2872 = 13270) do not add to the diary's Train total of 13070 — a discrepancy of 200, as written. The other weekly totals in this month check out (week ending 29 December 1918: 8948; week ending 12 January: 7758; week ending 19 January: 7015; week ending 26 January: 6691).
- 19 January: "two and a half Hard [uncertain] to be painted" — the word after "half" is read as "Hard" but the sense is unclear (possibly "hours").
- 20 January: "He took [uncertain] a letter from General to G.H.Q." — a word between "took" and "a letter" is struck through/overtyped and cannot be read.
- 30 January: the visiting colonel reads "Col Simson" — read as written; the name is not fully certain.
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the January sheets is a flourish and is not read with confidence; it is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name.
- Every date 1–31 January has an entry; no dates are missing and no date is duplicated. The place column reads "In the Field" throughout, so the Train's exact location is not stated in January — the text places it around Tournai (railhead), Enghien and Jemappes.
- Names read as: Capt Wilks; Major McLeod; Capts Fisher and Higgins; Capt Hudson; Lieut Hills; Lieut Underwood; Major deGraves; Capt Bonnie; Lieut George; Col McAlpine; Major Balfour; Capt Keay; DesBrisay (temporary M.O.); Col Simson.
- No casualties are recorded in this volume. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only.
