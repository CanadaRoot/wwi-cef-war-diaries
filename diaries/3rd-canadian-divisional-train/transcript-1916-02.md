## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The diarist's initials "J.A.S." appear at the end of most entries and are transcribed each time. The Place column is blank on Sheets 1–2 (28–31 January); "Fletre" is entered as the place from 1 February. "Coy." = Company; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "Adjt." = Adjutant; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant & Quartermaster General; "D.D. of S. & T." = Deputy Director of Supplies & Transport; "D.A.P.M." = Deputy Assistant Provost Marshal; "A.P.M." = Assistant Provost Marshal; "A.D.M.S." = Assistant Director of Medical Services; "D.T." = Divisional Train; "D.H.Q." = Divisional Headquarters; "C.R.A." = Commander Royal Artillery; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "H.D." = heavy draught (horses); "L.D." = light draught; "Dft." = draught; "G.S." = General Service; "O.R." = other ranks; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "R.C.R." = Royal Canadian Regiment; "Bde." = Brigade; "Battn." = Battalion; "R.S.P." = as written (not expanded). The unit stamp on each sheet reads "H.Q., DIVISIONAL TRAIN / 3rd CANADIAN DIVISION". The "Remarks and references to Appendices" column is blank throughout.

## Pages 3–15 — Diary text (28 January–29 February 1916)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed footer reads "T.2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."

### Page 3 — 28–29 January 1916 (Sheet No. 1)

> **28 Jan 1916.** — Received orders from Canadian Corps to meet and take command of the 3rd Canadian Divisional Train at BAILLEUL Railway Station at 6.49 pm this evening.
>
> Met train as ordered and took over from Col. McMillan with parade state.
>
> Ordered Major Webb to take command and march the Train to Fletre where I would meet them and direct them to their Billets. On reaching Billets observed they were all pretty well blown & several limping.
>
> Ordered foot parade for the morning. J.A.S.
>
> **29 Jan 1916.** — Allowed all to to take it easy. Medical Officer reported no serious cases after foot inspection.
>
> All look a good clean lot of men. No wagons, horses or technical Equipment come, not even a cooking pot, and no riding horses or bicycles, and it is a terrible handicap to say nothing of the time wasted getting Ordnance which is at present in BAILLEUL, a long way off. No cars for S.S.O or Requisition Officers.
>
> Ordered Parade and inspection for 8.00 a.m. in morning. J.A.S.

### Page 4 — 30–31 January 1916 (Sheet No. 2)

> **30 Jan 1916.** — All three Companies (4th still in England) had fallen in punctually — made casual, not minute inspection. Addressed the parade, pointing out to all ranks what was expected of them and what I intended to have, that all appointments were temporary and I would not hesitate to change or send home anyone not giving satisfaction. Made a special point of my inability and regret at not being able to post certain of the officers sent from England, who were displaced through no fault of their own. I called for, and expect to get full loyalty and perfect support at all times from every one under my command. Located Refilling Points nearer new Billets — many Consultations with Staff.
>
> Thick fog all day. J.A.S.
>
> **31 Jan 1916.** — Some tents & Equipment to carry on with — Arranged for brick for horse stands. Asked Staff to have all horses & wagons returned to Train. Paid off my old Batman who came with my horse loaned from 1st Div Train.
>
> Drew maps for Coys.
>
> Fine but very cold — no sun. J.A.S.

### Page 5 — 1–3 February 1916 (Sheet No. 3)

Place: Fletre.

> **Fletre, Feb. 1.** — Conference with O.C. 1st Div Train re transfer of 7 N.C.O's from his train to mine. These N.C.O's and Lieut Hennessy (Adjt.) reported in evening. Fine, cold a glimpse of sun. J.A.S.
>
> **2.** — M.O. sent one man to hospital, case not diagnosed, but very high temperature and pulse. Supply Sections took over their duties — Good work but after a little time, and on [occupied — written above the line] receipt of full equipment should cut time, in issuing in half. Dull dry but raw cold. J.A.S.
>
> **3.** — Requisitioning Officers started out on their duties to-day. Very awkward that a move should be on before Train gets establishment, more cohesion and better cooperation between Staffs would make things run much more smoothly, want of transport made it none the easier — but all good practice for the new men most of whom are shaping well — Dull & cold. J.A.S.

### Page 6 — 4–6 February 1916 (Sheet No. 4)

Place: Fletre.

> **Fletre, Feb. 4.** — M.O reported case of measles (civil population) — 2 Motor Cars arrived for S.S.O. 18 Loads of brick for H.Q. Coy. Cold & Dull — rain later. J.A.S.
>
> **5.** — Fixing new Refilling Point for H.Q. Coy. Present road cutting up badly. Train getting quickly into shape, a few mix-ups in moves, but nothing serious and all straightened out easily. The 70 men from Train dropped at ABBEVILLE to bring wagons & horses along arrived this evening 77 Draft horses 46 Riding with them. M. Mathieu joined H.Q. Coy as Interpreter. One man left at ABBEVILLE to bring on balance of Stores. J.A.S.
>
> **6.** — Got O.C. Coys to meet me at H.Q. Coy. and distributed without any picking, all horses, wagons & stores that came from Abbeville. Sorry to see all harness is emergency harness. Taking them all round the horses draft are good and saddle very fair. The drivers are a bit raw & want schooling. Officers went back with Capt. Hargraves to his Coy. Bicycles minus lamps & pumps

### Page 7 — 6 February (continued)–7 February 1916 (Sheet No. 5)

Place: Fletre.

> **Fletre, Feb. 6 (continued).** — or tool bags. M.O sent one A.S.C. driver to hospital to-day bad eye sight, so bad that he should never have been sent out here. He also reported case of an officer with Diphtheria. Caution in orders about drinking no water except from water cart. Dull all day — fine rain late afternoon. J.A.S.
>
> **7.** — Got permission for H.Q. Coy. to shift billets into adjoining farm, all will be moved by evening. Capt Laver gone to join 1st Div Train. Meeting of S.S.O's at Hdqrts (Corps). A.D.V.S. sent over two farriers from C.M.R's to be interviewed — have applied for their transfer to 3rd D.T. M.O. reported Driver badly kicked by horse on knee, from H.Q. Coy. V.O. inspected horses No. 3 Coy. Convoy went to Hazebrouck for brick & for third time in succession were turned back although the acting D.A.Q.M.G. assured me all arrangements had been made for bricks to be handed over, and there would be no mistake. Something rotten with the State of Denmark. Sunshine Train intermittant. Dined new Billet. Capt. Turnbull as guest. J.A.S.

### Page 8 — 8–10 February 1916 (Sheet No. 6)

Place: Fletre.

> **Fletre, Feb. 8.** — Arranged with A.A. & Q.M.G. to change all the H.Q. Coy harness (emergency) for breast harness. All 3 Coys have now got interpreters, they may be good interpreters, but socially are impossible. Remanded a case from H.Q. Coy to D.A.P.M. Studebaker car broken down after two days running. These cars are absolutely useless on these roads and for this kind of work. Authority granted and farriers referred to yesterday, reported for duty and posted to H.Q. Coy & No. 2. Cold rain — sleet all day with few bright intervals. J.A.S.
>
> **9.** — Most of the day busy, general routine in office. Went with Adjt to No. 3 Coy. met them starting out on a ride for map reading instruction. S.S.O notified that the 10 lbs. hay ration would be resumed on and after 13th inst. Fine all day, frost evening. J.A.S.
>
> **10.** — No. 3 Coy moved to Billets near their Refilling Point. Units have been returning to this Company poor horses & keeping their good ones. M.O. disinfected room occupied by Major Powers (diphtheria case). Reported case of suspicion to A.P.M. to-night

### Page 10 — 13 February (continued)–15 February 1916 (Sheet No. 8)

Place: Fletre.

> **Fletre, Feb. 13 (continued).** — made arrangements re feeding with S.S.O, O.C. H.Q. Coy arranged for Extinguishers for Billets, Dumps &c, 24 in all. Early, fine rain — Later, Dry and fair amount of sun. J.A.S.

J.A.S.
>

### Page 11 — 15 February (continued)–18 February 1916 (Sheet No. 9)

Place: Fletre.

> **Fletre, Feb. 15 (continued).** — to-day: — only fine part of morning, remainder of day fine rain. J.A.S.
>
> **16.** — O.C. H.Q. Coy returned. Col. S. called — had a long chat re Train affairs; has handed us a letter from C. which much amused us. Sent instructions to No. 2 Coy to turn over wagons to 29th Battn 6 Bde. for their move. Two wagons sent for by H.Q. Coy other side St Omer, arrived. Very high wind night & all day, early rain but later fine — wind still high. J.A.S.
>
> **17.** — Capt. Graham gone back to major portion of the Train with Arty. Looking for refilling point for No. 3 Coy, decided best place is where No. 3 Coy is now, so Company will not move. Fine all day, wind cold & drying. J.A.S.
>
> **18.** — Visited No. 3 Coy & disposed of case there, visited H.Q. Coy to see how they were getting on, all running smoothly & feeding being done O.K. Capt Craig flew in with new Sunbeam car from Shorncliffe. Drs. inoculating at No. 3 Coy. Frost during night, turned to cold fine rain all day. J.A.S.

## Open questions / uncertain items (February 1916)
- The cover reads "From 28th January to 29th February 1916" and "Volume 1", but the contents page reads "Volume 16 - 2." — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number. The contents page's "Diary Text 13 Pages" matches the 13 diary sheets (Sheets 1–13).
- The word "Headquarters" on the cover is struck through in ink.
- The Place column is blank on Sheets 1–2 (28–31 January); the diary text puts the Train at Bailleul Railway Station on 28 January and marching to Fletre that evening, so Fletre is the place for 29–31 January.
- 28 January: "Allowed all to to take it easy" on 29 January is transcribed with the double "to" as written.
- 2 February: the word "occupied" is written above the line over "on" in "and on receipt of full equipment"; it does not fit the sentence as written and is transcribed as an interlineation.
- 6 February: the entry runs from the foot of Sheet 4 onto the head of Sheet 5 ("Bicycles minus lamps & pumps or tool bags"); both parts are transcribed as written.
- 7 February: "Sunshine Train intermittant." is transcribed as written; its meaning is unclear.
- 10 February: "Reported case of suspicion to A.P.M. to-night" — the recipient is written "A.P.M."; a suspected diphtheria case is the likely subject, but the diary does not say so.
- 14 February: "D.D. of Remounts" is written as shown; it may be the Deputy Director of Remounts.
- 16 February: "Col. S." is given only as an initial, and "a letter from C." likewise; the same "Col. S." may be Col. Simpson, who is named on 27 February and 1 March.
- 22 February: "S.S.O gone to R.J. [uncertain]" — the abbreviation "R.J." is read as written; its meaning (a place or an office) is not clear.
- 22 February (continued): "1600, brick to-morrow, ½ each to Nos. 2 + 3 Coy." — "1600" is read as written; it is probably a quantity of bricks (1,600) but could be a time.
- 27 February: "Wire & Thaw came in this afternoon" is transcribed as written.
- 28 February: the diarist spells the name "Franklin" in "Paid Roy, Grant, Franklin & Laver" and "Franklyn's" in "Oliphant & Stirret for Franklyn's wreath" — both spellings as written. The second name is read as "Stirret". A wreath for Franklyn implies a death, but the diary gives no further detail.
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the February sheets is not read with confidence; it appears to read "J. A. S. Findlay" but is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name. The initials "J.A.S." at the end of most entries are the same hand.
- Names read as: Col. McMillan; Maj. Webb; Lieut. Hennessy (Adjutant); M. Mathieu (interpreter); Capt. Hargraves; Capt. Laver; Capt. Turnbull; Maj. Powers (diphtheria case); Lt. Church; Lt. McCallum; C.S.M. Bayliffe; Capt. Roy; Capt. Craig; Capt. Graham; Fortin; Col. Simpson; Maj. Green; Maj. Morgan; Oliphant; Stirret; Roy; Grant; Franklin (Franklyn).
- No human casualties are recorded in this volume: one man sent to hospital undiagnosed (2 February), one A.S.C. driver sent to hospital with bad eyesight and one officer with diphtheria (6 February), a driver kicked by a horse on the knee (7 February), the M.O. taken to hospital with internal pain (11 February), and a suspected diphtheria case (15 February). No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; the artillery refilling point is given only as "G.28 A."
