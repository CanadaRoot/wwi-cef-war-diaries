## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Ditto marks in the original are written out as the repeated text, with "(ditto)" noted. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only. "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "D.D. of S. & T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "D.D.R." = Deputy Director of Remounts (as used here); "M.O." = Medical Officer; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "G.C.M." = General Court Martial; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "R.S.O." = Railhead Supply Officer (as used here); "M.T." = Mechanical Transport; "S. & T." = Supply and Transport. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets is the unit name printed at the top left. The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the October sheets is read as [uncertain] (see open questions).

### Page 3 — 1–4 October 1916 (Sheet No. 76)

> **October 1.** — Time put back one hour at 1 a.m. this morning, all O.C.'s notified and instructed to do the same Received A. 52-98 from Canadian Corps to send Major McGillivray to England and Captain McLennan to 2nd Train, and that I would get Major Freeman of 2nd Train and Captain Beasley from 1st Train for posting in mine. Saw Captain Graham and spoke to him about Lieut. Aldous, had this Officer up and showed him correspondence, and talked to him about his promotion. Wire came late in evening to change time of refilling tomorrow morning for one day from 10 a.m. to 7 a.m.; orders issued accordingly. Captain Hennessy came in at night to make sure the above wire had reached us. He also informed me he was offered a Staff Captain job, but while grateful for offer he declined as there are instances where D.A.Q.M.G. posts have been given without first being a Staff Captain. I did not advise him one way or the other as he knows his own mind best, and in all honesty I do think if given a D.A.Q.M.G. post direct, would fill it splendidly. He is an Officer of wide knowledge and great possibilities. Had a bad touch of fever and a cold even in the sun and under blankets. Lovely day.
>
> **October 2.** — M.O. was away all day yesterday helping at Dressing Station, and only returned for breakfast. Gave him letter re his complaint this morning and asked him to furnish information desired. Refilling at Railhead although early went off without a hitch, and all Companies there on time. Feeling much better to day but groggy. M.O. gone to Ambulance again this evening. Major McGillivray called and had a talk, but he cannot leave until relieved by Major from 2nd Train as I am short of Officers. Wet, cold and rain all day. Feeling ever so much better this evening.
>
> **October 3.** — Day opened wet but cleared up later. Captain Beasley reported for duty and posted to Head Quarters Company vice Captain McLennan whom I sent to 2nd Train as ordered, and O.C. sent him back as he wanted to see Corps about it first, however that is his kettle of fish. Railhead changed for tomorrow, and refilling time 9 a.m. S.S.O. and Adjutant to Rail and Headquarters for me.
>
> **October 4.** — S.S.O. early to Railhead, refilling delayed some three hours owing it is understood to slight breakdown on line, hard on the poor horses. Gave evidence at G.C.M. trial of Lieut. Hessey. At the time of trial and because of it and other reasons, twelve of my Officers were unavailable for duty, but things ran on O.K. O.C., 2nd Train called on me re keeping Major Freeman

### Page 4 — 4–8 October 1916 (Sheet No. 77)

> **October 4 (continued).** — instead of changing with Captain McLennan, and I gave him a letter to say I had no objection, but that I had carried out my orders and would make no move in the matter. O.C. 4th Train called and I was delighted to see him, and gave him any hints in my power.
>
> **October 5.** — At Railhead with S.S.O. and what a muddle there. R.S.O. seems afraid to take any initiative, this specially refers to a rum issue sanctioned by wire. Went on and saw D.D. of S. & T. and put my case before him re transfer of Captain McLennan and Major Freeman. Reported also same information to Division. Cannot wait any longer to send Major McGillivray, and will send him tomorrow. The seven remounts arrived and were a very fair stamp. This D.D.R. seems ahead of our last. Some showers early but fine most of the day. 11 p.m. wire came in notifying us Horse Transport would not go to Railhead and that Supply Column had been notified.
>
> **October 6.** — Lorries were not at Railhead this morning, but train was late and this enabled us to have them arrive in time. Fixed new refilling points for Nos. 2, 3, & 4 Companies at BOUZINCOURT, Divisional Troops remain at SENLIS. This will give my horses the much needed rest they require, and enable me to get busy on Horses, Wagons, and Harness, also it enables me to return wagon to Salvage and Sausage Valley recalled yesterday. Captain McLennan (lent by 2nd Train) left at 2-15 to relieve Major McGillivray who goes to England. Turnout from Salvage returned in filthy state, and O.C. Salvage notified to give facilities for Drivers to look properly after their teams etc. Called at Divisional Headquarters at 5 p.m., no Supply Lorries had arrived, and sent S.S.O. off to find out cause of delay.
>
> **October 7.** — Lorries did not come in last night in time for refilling to be done before 10 p.m. Visited Divisional Headquarters. Lieut. George reported here for duty, posted him to Head Quarters Company. Captain Hennessy came to lunch and tea. Called in at Nos. 3 & 4 Coys. Submitted my list for honours, two Officers and two other ranks. Wrote to Division on receipt of a personal letter from D.D.S. & T. re getting Major Freeman to report from 2nd Train. S.S.O. to Railhead to see if all was going alright and Pack Train in on time. Submitted names for honours list, Captain Hennessy delivered them personally. Fine all day, rain began at 5 p.m. but not heavy.
>
> **October 8.** — S.S.O. to Railhead, and Adjutant for me to Divisional Headquarters. Colonel Massey came up with and introduced Major Freeman, will post him to Headquarters Company.

### Page 5 — 8–12 October 1916 (Sheet No. 78)

> **October 8 (continued).** — Captain Beasley as S.S.O. and Captain McCallum as O.C. No. 2 Company. The 3rd Divisional Artillery are here, but not attached to us or the 4th. I shall not be sorry when I get them back, and get a chance of looking after the Transport. All the Transport in the Division is in fairly good shape considering conditions under which they are working, especially the horses. Major Freeman, Captains Beasley, McLennan and McCallum have all taken up their new duties. Spoke to Captain Phillips of Headquarters Company and told him I expected every assistance to be given to Major Freeman, as he was a new Officer coming among strangers, this was in the presence of the Major, and I warned him if he did not make good I would make no bones about saying so. M.O. all day at 10th Field Ambulance. Showery and rain.
>
> **October 9.** — S.S.O. gone to Railhead. M.O. again to Ambulance. Acting Adjutant and my Batman gone to Dentist at WARLOY. Stayed in and made notes for another lecture. S.S.O. 7th called and O.C. No. 2 Company. Orders have come in that 7th move to WARLOY and 9th to Brickfields tomorrow. Notified Nos. 2 & 4 Companies. Cold but fine.
>
> **October 10.** — Train again very late at Railhead. Tried and dismissed a case from No. 4 Company and wrote to O.C. not to waste my time remanding cases like that with such poor evidence. If the man is no good return him to his unit. Inspected No. 2 Company before they moved to WARLOY. Called at Divisional Headquarters, could get no word of my artillery coming back to me. Captain Graham, O.C. No. 3 Company called asking for Lieut. Fisher back as soon as possible. Fine all day.
>
> **October 11.** — Location of No. 2 Company given to V.O. for him to visit them. S.S.O. gone to see Staff Captain of Lahore who are moving to 4th Army area, and no word if we are to feed them there. I feel sure we have not, but have nothing to this effect. 2nd Divisional Train pulled out and made no arrangements to feed 15th Reserve Park, with a little trouble we could do it. Could get no further news if we feed Lahore; they are getting so far away they cannot come for it. I am glad we have not their horses and wagons. Captain Dick Turpin called this afternoon, have not seen him for over a year since he was in 1st Supply Column. A few showers but generally fine.
>
> **October 12.** — S.S.O. away all day getting in touch with all units moved and on the move. Lieut. Grant

### Page 6 — 12–16 October 1916 (Sheet No. 79)

> **October 12 (continued).** — reported back from leave, and Captain Church left this evening. Visited Nos. 2 & 4 Coys. at WARLOY. Had a long talk to General Mairne [uncertain] re horses turned over to him when we were at BUSSEBOOM. Called in at Divisional Headquarters. Met the General who kindly asked how everything in the Train was going, and was glad to be able to say well. Shall be glad when these moves are all over, there seems to be no complete chain of information from top downwards. For instance 3rd Pioneers moved to day and when I called at the Division they did not know anything about it. Division were to move, and all feeding arrangements made when wire came cancelling same. This sort of thing makes it very hard on the Train and Supply Column. Fine all day.
>
> **October 13.** — S.S.O. out very early and will have to be until we can get re assembled again. 8 Limbers returned from Sausage Valley, and bar one with gnawed tail board, all in good condition though smothered with mud. Horses also in good condition. All the limbers cleaned and repaired before evening. Divisional Headquarters not moving yet. The move of the 3rd Field Company Engineers fixed. Colonel Simson back from leave, called on him but he had gone to AVELOY, railhead for 1st and 4th. O.C. Salvage called and arranged with him to send transport to him when he moves same time as 8th Brigade, but date and time uncertain yet. Fine some very slight showers.
>
> **October 14.** — Visited Divisional Headquarters, they move tomorrow 8 a.m. to CANAPLES. 3rd Entrenching Battalion from CONTAY to LAVICOGNE. 7th and 8th Field Company from HERRISART to LAVICOGNE, and 8th Canadian Infantry Brigade from ALBERT to WARLOY. Mostly fine.
>
> **October 15.** — Left SENLIS 8-20 after seeing Headquarters Company move off and camp cleaned. Returned Town Majors three tents he lent us on arrival. CANAPLES with Adjutant and Paymaster 10-15 a.m. New Town Major here and had great difficulty in arranging billets, only got fixed up at 12-15 p.m. 7th and 9th draw from Canaples Railhead with train, 8th by lorries. Great number of men allowed to ride on loaded wagons, the Divisional Headquarters, Signals, and 8th Field Ambulance the greatest offenders. Asked it to be reported in orders that this is strictly forbidden. Some rain.
>
> **October 16.** — Headquarters Company pulled out for BERNAVILLE at 8 a.m. arriving there 10-15 a.m. Adjutant, Paymaster and self left in car at 8-40 and arrived 9-15 a.m. Billets all arranged by Town

### Page 7 — 16–23 October 1916 (Sheet No. 80)

> **October 16 (continued).** — Major. Had a walk round to get the lie of the ground place before Headquarters Company comes in. Short haul for horses, and they made good time over very fair roads. Called on Divisional Headquarters. Beautifully fine all day.
>
> **October 17.** — The only drinking water fit for use here at BERNAVILLE is from a well at the Brewery, and the mean devil makes the troops pay for it @ ½ a franc for a water cart = 120 gals. Inspected Headquarters Company and the remounts, they are not so good as the last lot. Called at Divisional Headquarters. The D.A.Q.M.G. came back to tea. Some rain and cold.
>
> **October 18.** — Court-Martial all day. Two nasty and long cases. Dull and wet.
>
> **October 19.** — Captain McCallum called, arranged to hear a case for him to day at 5 p.m. Adjutant saw the A.D.M.S. re a case returned from the 8th Field Ambulance as unfit for duty; how on earth do they expect me to use such a man, if he cannot do their transport is it likely he can do mine, one would have thought they would have evacuated him. M.O. called to case at No. 3 Company. Ordered V.O. to turn in his second horse with saddle etc. for Paymaster's use. Weather rainy.
>
> **October 20.** — Train Headquarters and Headquarters Company pulled out of BOURNAVILLE at 9 a.m. arriving in good time and condition at FROHEN LE GRAND. Before leaving saw lines etc. all left neat and tidy. All refilling points fixed, and orders issued to move again 9 a.m. D.A.Q.M.G. and Captain Hennessy came, got scratch lunch. Fine.
>
> **October 21.** — All left FROHEN LE GRAND at exact time. Self, S.S.O. and D.A.Q.M.G. went ahead in car to visit 50th Division and see over horse standings for all Companies, Billets, etc., at HAUTE AVESNES saw halting place at for night at COUROY. Fine but bitterly cold and nearly frozen to death.
>
> **October 22.** — Left LE COUROY at 9-30 a.m. for VILLERS CHATELLE arriving at 10-45 a.m. Headquarters Company left at 8-45 a.m. arriving at 3 p.m. Very good roads and they travelled light, about 15 miles; they are all in the open, Billets are scarce. Weather fine and dry, but bitterly cold, not the crisp Canadian cold, this sort gets into and through you.
>
> **October 23.** — Adjutant, S.S.O. and O.C. Headquarters Company gone to 60th Division to make final arrangements

### Page 8 — 23–27 October 1916 (Sheet No. 81)

> **October 23 (continued).** — for taking over from them by early morning of 26th. Saw D.A.Q.M.G. re calling back my S.O. and Supply Details with Lahore. He will wire authority. 34 wagons from 21st Reserve Park to be attached to us for duty, assemble at Railhead tomorrow when M.T. comes off. Heard case from No. 2 Company — reprimanded the Corporal, this well met the case. Terrible uncertainty as to when Pack Train arrives, and a little difficulty in getting full quota of wagons from 21st Reserve Park. Turned over two wagons to be attached to Baths for collecting dirty clothes. Raw all day, rain in evening and night.
>
> **October 24.** — S.S.O. to Railhead, no word yet when pack train will be in — it came in at 1-30 p.m. Captain Church reported back from leave. Lieut. Richards and his Details back from the Lahores. Called at Divisional Headquarters. No. 4 Company to HAUTE AVESNES. No. 3 Coy. tomorrow. Wet all day.
>
> **October 25.** — Operation Amendment Order No. 67 changes as follows :— Headquarters (G) close at VILLERS CHATEL and opens at HERMAVILLE 10 a.m. 26th. Remainder of Divisional Headquarters remain at VILLERS CHATEL. Divisional Headquarters will close at HERMAVILLE and VILLERS CHATEL and open at AUBIGNY on 29th. Adjutant and S.S.O. gone to Railhead. Called at Divisional Headquarters and had a conversation re the 60th Divisional Train who do not propose to pull out till their Headquarters Company when their Division move from this area. Replied to further query from Corps re Captain Strathy. Dull some showers.
>
> **October 26.** — F.G.C.M. before we started, and I tried case of a man from Salvage Company. Left about 11 am for HAUTE AVESNES in car with Paymaster and Adjutant, arriving at 12 noon. Had to walk part of the way as the blooming old car 1457 in spite of all its puffing and blowing could not make the grades. All the new billet evacuated except men washing out passages; as they could not dry it passages soon got worse. Got settled in very quickly. Sent off letter to Captain Hennessy to deliver to Colonel McRae in London and also an introduction. A good deal of rain all day.
>
> **October 27.** — Starting to meet Duke of Devonshire when a further report was called for re Captain Strathy so remained to write it out, and had to send car for S.S.O. at Railhead so missed reception. Very sorry but report more important. Called at Divisional Headquarters. Saw Billeting

### Page 9 — 27–31 October 1916 (Sheet No. 82)

> **October 27 (continued).** — Officer about moving Headquarters Company. Interviewed Y.M.C.A. Officer re same, and detailed Captain Phillips of Headquarters Company to make final arrangements tomorrow morning. Called at Railhead. Showery.
>
> **October 28.** — Morning broke very fine. S.S.O. and Adjutant to Railhead. Saw Captain Graham re promotion of Lieut. Aldous and wrote recommending letters promotion. Called at Divisional Headquarters Picked up D.A.A.Q.M.G., he, I, and S.S.O. motored to LILLERS and consulted D.D. of S. & T. re several important matters. Very cold and rain came down in sheets. Called in on return at D.A. & Q.M.G. who warned me for a Court-Martial, and gave me a message for S.S.O. who dropped off at Railhead re bombing school. One of our own aeroplanes crashed down close to Billet, the Pilot killed instantaneously, and machine badly smashed. The heavy wind seems to have caught his machine, and he lost control. Heavy rain and strong wind.
>
> **October 29.** — S.S.O. to Railhead. Dealt with a case from No. 3 Company of Corporal Wheeler sleeping in his shop instead of billet allotted to him. Reprimanded. Visited No. 2 Company at ACQ, they are getting their standings for horses, and Billets for men well fixed up. Wet and cold all day.
>
> **October 30.** — Clear star lit night, but morning broke wet. Adjutant and S.S.O. to Railhead. Saw Officer from 60th Reserve Park, and he is to let me have full return of Personnel, Horses etc., so that I can arrange now to detail them. Paymaster gone to pay Headquarters Company at AURIGNEY. O.C. Headquarters Company, 60th Divisional Train came to discuss wagons he had available for hauling supplies. Adjutant called at Divisional Headquarters. V.O. came to me complaining of his Billet and I referred him to Town Major. He told Paymaster he had already subscribed to Kitchener's Memorial Fund, but I have very grave doubts if this is so. He is a poor specimen compared with Captain Roy. I was very amused the other day when he asked me to recommend him for the post of A.D.V.S., and I hope he liked his answer. Wet all day and cold.
>
> **October 31.** — Adjutant and S.S.O. to Railhead and Divisional Headquarters. Captain Church and Interpreter came and I told them to get Billeting settled promptly so that there would be no grumbling by Landlord. From what I gather the 60th have never settled anything for all the time they were here. Captain Oliphant came to lunch, and went after with Paymaster to Field Cashier.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *[signature — [uncertain]] Lieut-Colonel, / O.C. 3rd Canadian Divl. Train*

## Open questions / uncertain items (October 1916)
- The cover reads "Volume 9" and the contents page reads "Volume 16 — 10." — both as written.
- 4 October and 8 October each appear twice, once at the foot of one sheet and once at the head of the next, with the entry continuing across the break; both are transcribed as written.
- The place name is spelled "SENLIS" on 6 October and "SENLIS" [uncertain] on 15 October (the O.C. left "SENLIS" for Canaples); the spelling as written is SENLIS throughout. "BOURNAVILLE" on 20 October appears to be the same place as "BERNAVILLE" on 16–17 October — both spellings as written.
- "General Mairne" on 12 October is read as written but is not certain — recorded as [uncertain].
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the October sheets is not read with confidence; it appears to begin with "S" and end in "Dudley"-like letters [uncertain] and is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name.
- "Lahore" (11, 23 and 24 October) is as written — the Indian troops the Train was feeding/arranging for.
- Names read as: Major McGillivray; Captain McLennan; Major Freeman (2nd Train); Captain Beasley (1st Train); Captain Graham; Lieut. Aldous; Captain Hennessy; Lieut. Hessey; Captain McCallum; Lieut. George; Captain Phillips; Major [uncertain] (Colonel Massey introduced Major Freeman); Lieut. Grant; Captain Church; General [uncertain] (12 October); Colonel Simson; Lieut. Richards; Captain Strathy; Captain Oliphant; Captain McKinnon; Corporal Wheeler (No. 3 Company); Captain Roy; Duke of Devonshire.
- One aeroplane crash is recorded on 28 October: a British aeroplane crashed close to the billet, the pilot killed instantaneously, machine badly smashed.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; places are named only. No Train casualties are recorded in this volume.
