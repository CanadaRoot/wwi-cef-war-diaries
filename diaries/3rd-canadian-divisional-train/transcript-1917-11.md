# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Train — November 1917

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Train, 3rd Canadian Division, Canadian Army Service Corps (C.A.S.C.)
- **Month:** November 1917 (Volume 22) — this volume covers 1 to 30 November 1917
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005004 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5020, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** November 1917, p. 1 – p. 9 (image files page-0218 – page-0226 of the 370-page diary)
- **November 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 218–226 of 370): cover, title page, contents, diary text 1–30 November, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout on Army Form C.2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. There is heavy overtyping throughout: where a word is struck through and replaced, the replacement is transcribed, and the struck word is noted only where it affects the reading (see open questions). The Place and Hour columns are blank throughout; the Date column gives "Nov. 1917" with the day number. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not transcribed against every entry here. The printed footer on each diary sheet reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S." Map references are given as written (G.9.C. centre); no latitude/longitude coordinates are given. Place names are as written: "WATAU", "VLAMERTINGHE", "RENINGHELST", "ABEELE", "ST. VENANT", "LILLERS", "MEDONCHELLE" / "NEDONCHELLE", "NORRENT-Fontes", "LA GOULEE", "BOORECQ", "ESTREE-BLANCHE", "FLECHIN", "BAILLEUL-LEZ-PERNES", "BETHUNE", "Poperinghe". "S.S.O." = Senior Supply Officer; "A.A. & Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "D.A.Q.M.G." = Deputy Assistant Quartermaster General; "D.D. of S. & T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport; "A.D.M.S." = Assistant Director of Medical Services; "A.P.M." = Assistant Provost Marshal; "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial; "F.P. No. 1" = Field Punishment No. 1; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "S.M." = Sergeant Major; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "Q.M." = Quartermaster; "a/S.S.O." = acting Senior Supply Officer.

## Pages 4–9 — Diary text (1–30 November 1917)

Army Form C.2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed instruction at the top left reads "Instructions regarding War Diaries and Intelligence Summaries are contained in F. S. Regs., Part II. and the Staff Manual respectively. Title pages will be prepared in manuscript."

### Page 4 — 1–5 November 1917 (Sheet No. 175)

> **1 November 1917.** — From 10 p.m. to 3.30 a.m. German aeroplanes came over and deluged us with bombs, but little damage in the close vicinity of camp, no targets hit. Capt. de Graves went off with Col. Simson to dine. At tea time yesterday Col. Simson, Major Morrison, Major McLelland, Capt. Hennesy, Capt. McDougall and myself sat down, also Capt. de Graves and Major Hennessy. No one in the hut but who were at one time or are still of 1st Division. Wire came in last night F.G.C.M. postponed.
>
> **2 November 1917.** — Nothing of importance.
>
> **3 November 1917.** — Camp Commandant called and complained that we were dumping manure on old dump and not where he had told us. This is being rectified, but he is making a mistake, position we were dumping absorbed a lot of filthy water which we ordered to be drained into brook from which animals drink. Give some people a little authority and they ride it to death, at least the brainless ones do. However he has the say in this case and takes the responsibility. Major Morrison, S.S.O. of 1st called. Wet.
>
> **4 November 1917.** — Moved to WATAU. As far as H.Q. are concerned, we are very well housed and comfortable. S.S.O. gone on leave. He takes wire to Capt. Oliphant to inform him his leave is extended for a month. Dull but dry.
>
> **5 November 1917.** — V.O. [uncertain] case of suspected glanders in No. 1 Company. Capt. Bisnop's movement order to join Balloon Section arrived. Lieut. Gwynne reported for duty and is attached to No. 1 Company. Went back to No. 1 Company, suspected case was glanders. All the necessary precautions taken to prevent infection. Inspected No. 4 Company. Lieut. Hessey could not get into the location allotted, got mired and wisely parked as near as he could get and got fixed up most creditably. Called on O.C. 1st, re case of glanders and informed him of same, as he takes over the lines or part of those we occupied. Told Lieut. Gwynne if I find his drinking propensities in any way interfere with his work I will have no compunction but to break him. Handed O.C. No. 4 Company Capt. Bisnop's movement orders.

### Page 5 — 6–12 November 1917 (Sheet No. 176)

> **6 November 1917.** — Major Scott phoned up from Poperinghe his arrival. Told him to join No. 1 Company giving him location, and getting in touch with Major Freeman through 1st Train phone and that I would like first opportunity of looking him up. Rain.
>
> **7 November 1917.** — Col. Dean called with Capt. Gobey. Adjutant and I went out to No. 1 Company. I saw and talked to Major Scott on things in general. Major Freeman is initiating him into Company affairs before he leaves. Wet.
>
> **8 November 1917.** — Visited D.D. of S. & T. with a/S.S.O. There were no complaints of any kind. Majors Scott and Freeman came and dined, this was a farewell dinner to Major Freeman. Showery.
>
> **9 November 1917.** — Interviewed the reinforcements from Base, whose ranks were not confirmed in England, they were willing to revert and stay. Paymaster gone on leave. First part of move back to first line relieving 1st Division takes place today. Capt. Cartwright called about position of new dump when we move, informed him it would be in same old place. Arranged with him to settle all train billeting. Adjutant gone to 1st Train to try and find out if and when they are moving, so that we can make our arrangements to move in. Paraded S.M. Murray before the General who interviewed him and recommended him for his Commission. Fine, colder.
>
> **10 November 1917.** — Doctor attending the old man at my billet. Movement orders from A.D.M.S. came in, arranged to move out of here on the 12th. Very wet.
>
> **11 November 1917.** — S.S.O. arranged for change of railhead to VLAMERTINGHE tomorrow. Huns dropped bombs on the dumps we take over from tomorrow, six wounded, two likely to prove fatal. Adjutant gone to 1st Train to make final arrangements for our taking over from them. Later reports state the two Supply men are dead. Very wet.
>
> **12 November 1917.** — Artillery opened up pretty hard last night and continued this morning and afternoon. Planes very busy. Capt. de Graves got hit on hand with splinter from anti-aircraft gun, but not very dangerous. Moved from WATAU back to G.9.C. centre relieving H.Q. 1st Train. Fair.

### Page 6 — 13–18 November 1917 (Sheet No. 177)

> **13 November 1917.** — During week ending the 11th, the Train covered, adding the journeys of all teams for all purposes, 5,595 miles. Besides supplies, it carried salvage, building material, Q.M. stores, Officers' kits and mails. S.S.O. and Adjutant gone to A.A. & Q.M.G.'s to talk over move pending, and in afternoon gone to new location to fix dump positions. Warrant for my leave came in today 29/11 to 29/12. Dull but fine.
>
> **14 November 1917.** — Arranged for No.4 Company to move out to ST. VENANT early tomorrow morning and make it in one day, 25 miles. Very heavy bombardment all last night, no casualties in Train. Fair.
>
> **15 November 1917.** — Intercepted transport of 9th Brigade on the move between ABEELE and RENINGHELST, and inspected them on line of march. All were very muddy but considering the want of facilities lately for keeping things clean, they did not show up badly at all. With very few exceptions the horses were in very good shape. The exceptions were a few rather thin, but all were hard. Camp Commandant came fussing around again about the manure dumps. He informed me [uncertain] General was coming in who was particularly fussy and he was most anxious not to [uncertain] [uncertain] back up. I told him I would do what I could to help him but the amount would depend on the men I could spare from more important work. Slight showers and ground mist.
>
> **16 November 1917.** — Adjutant and S.S.O. gone to new area to fix up locations of Dumps and Train H.Q. to move into on the 18th. O.C. of 8th Train called here, taking over from me when I move. All handing over of remaining Units completed and everything left square. Heavy ground fog during night. No planes over and very little shelling of camp.
>
> **17 November 1917.** — With Adjutant visited new area and billets, away from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. FLECHIN furthest East [uncertain] point, NEDONCHELLE furthest South. With our backwards and forward movements often retracing our course, we must have traveled about 150 miles. Luckily it was not wet, but cold and a ground mist.
>
> **18 November 1917.** — H.Q. moved from VLAMERTINGHE district to LILLERS district. No.4 Company at MEDONCHELLE, No.3 ESTREE-BLANCHE, No2 at BOORECQ, Train H.Q. at LA GOULEE, Divisional H.Q. at NORRENT-Fontes, railhead LILLERS. No standings. All the Companys that are in stood the journey well, others on the way looking very good. Fine.

### Page 7 — 19–23 November 1917 (Sheet No. 178)

> **19 November 1917.** — Adjutant gone round to see if all Companies have come in. S.S.O. returned from leave. Fine.
>
> **20 November 1917.** — O.C. No2 Company reported in early this morning, and all well. Visited No.2 Camp and found them rapidly settling in horses. The majority of them have no cover, but all the men are well housed. Tried two cases from Salvage, one drunk and the other absent without leave. In the former case he admitted he was soused, had no excuse, it was his second offense, but as his first was over a year ago, I gave him 28 days F.P.No.1. The other case should not have been brought up at all, the man had a good and proper reason. Dull and some showers.
>
> **21 November 1917.** — Wire from the Base to return car sent us in error. Drew attention of Salvage Officer to the amount of bread his Unit is wasting. Adjutant gone to try and locate No.1 Company on the move to arrange for their men to go on leave, could not connect up. Fine but dull.
>
> **22 November 1917.** — Majors Scott and Lieut. Grant reported in. No.1 Company now on their way to this area. Visited No.3 Re-filling Point and inspected transport. Horses in good condition, but harness and wagons show that there has not been much time to devote to care of them while in forward area. Inspected Signals, horses very good. Parked in open on side road; wagons and harness very fair. Tried one of No.4 Company's men, gave him benefit of the doubt and let him off. Also two men from No2 Company, they elected to go before a F.G.C.M. On referring case to Headquarters was instructed to deal with them myself. Dull but fine.
>
> **23 November 1917.** — M.O. reported all the Salvage men were dirty, and told him to order Lieut: Breckenridge in writing to have them bathed within a specified time; also to see he had a proper latrine dug. Lieut: Breckenridge out of camp and cannot be found, was away all night. The two men from No.2 Company changed their mind and took my punishment, gave them each 21 days F.P.No.1, and [uncertain] returned to Company duty. Fine but dull.

### Page 8 — 24–29 November 1917 (Sheet No. 179)

> **24 November 1917.** — Lieut: Breckenridge, O.C. Salvage Company paraded before me for being absent and neglecting to notify me of his intention to leave the district. He certainly erred because of his crass ignorance. Cold and sleet falling. Tried case from No.3 Company, one of four that would not take their C.O.'s punishment.
>
> **25 November 1917.** — Had Salvage Company on parade and inspected all their transport. All things considered it was in very fair shape. Some of the worst musty hay I have ever seen came up today. French stuff, and why it was ever taken over beats me. It would not be safe to [uncertain] it to pigs let alone give for fodder. S.S.O. convened Board and condemned it.
>
> **26 November 1917.** — Watched Salvage Company at drill. Went with M.O. to AD.M.S. Called on A.A. & Q.M.G. and had a long talk on things in general. Got M.O.'s leave warrant arranged for same date as my own. Fine generally. Tried 6 charges by A.P.M., Salvage men in Estaminet and 2. M.C.O.'s out of Bounds.
>
> **27 November 1917.** — S.S.O. gone round all Re-filling points. Lieut: F. Hills reported for duty from the Base. Capt. J. Oliphant wired he was at BETHUNE returned from leave, sent car to fetch him. M.O. lecture to Salvage. For week ending November 25th, the Train covered a distance of 4,808 miles, made up as follows, :-
>
> No.1 Company, 2,560 miles. No.3 Company, 557 miles.
>
> " 2 " 1,033 " " 4 " 658 "
>
> Major Scott and Lieut. Gwynne came to tea. Had a long talk with the former re posting Capt. Graham, should he come back to this Train.
>
> **28 November 1917.** — Wire came in for No.1 Company to move into BAILLEUL-LEZ-PERNES tomorrow. O.C. and M.O. going on leave this afternoon. Major Heasley took over Command during absence of O.C.
>
> **29 November 1917.** — Visited No.1 Company this morning in new location, found them fairly well fixed, though a little crowded. Visited No.2 Company this p.m. and allotted Remounts to Companies requiring same. Wire received for S.S.O. to accompany D.A.Q.M.G. to Corps Headquarters in the morning.

### Page 9 — 30 November 1917 (Sheet No. 180)

> **30 November 1917.** — Fairly decent day. Went to Corps Headquarters with D.A.Q. M.G. this morning and talked over coal situation with A.Q.M.G., eventually agreeing upon future course of action. Nothing else of any interest happening, and everything going very well.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *[signature — J. Heasley [uncertain]] Major, / A/O.C. 3rd Canadian Divisional Train.*

## Open questions / uncertain items (November 1917)
- The cover reads "Volume 22", but the contents page reads "Volume 17 - 11" — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary, as in other months of this diary.
- The title page is p. 2 and the contents page is p. 3; in the September 1917 volume of this diary the order is reversed (contents p. 2, title p. 3).
- The diarist for 1–28 November is the O.C., Lt. Col. Findlay, as established in earlier volumes of this diary; he is not named in this month's text. The entries for 29 and 30 November are in the acting O.C.'s voice, Major Heasley having taken over command on 28 November.
- 1 November: "Capt. Hennesy" and "Major Hennessy" both appear in the same entry — both as written. Earlier volumes of this diary spell the name "Hennessy" throughout, so "Hennesy" may be a typing error, or a different officer.
- 1 November: "Wire came in last night F.G.C.M. postponed." — the abbreviation is written "F.G.C. M." with a space before the final letter.
- 5 November: "V.O. [uncertain] case of suspected glanders" — the word between "V.O." and "case" is overtyped and illegible.
- 5 November: "Capt. Bisnop's" — the name is typed "Bisnop" in both occurrences. A "Staff Captain Bishop" appears in the August 1916 volume of this diary, so this may be the same officer with the name mistyped. Transcribed as written.
- 7 November: "Capt. Gobey" has a small superscript mark after the name that could not be read.
- 12 November: the opening word "Planes" is struck through and "Artillery" written over it; transcribed as "Artillery opened up pretty hard last night...".
- 15 November: "9th Brigade" — "9th" appears typed over "5th" (or a similar numeral); the top reading is "9th".
- 15 November: three [uncertain] words — "He informed me [uncertain] General was coming in" (the overtyped word appears to read "some"); "he was most anxious not to [uncertain] [uncertain] back up" (both words overtyped and illegible).
- 17 November: "FLECHIN furthest East [uncertain] point" — the word between "East" and "point" is overtyped and illegible.
- 17 November gives the place as "NEDONCHELLE" and 18 November as "MEDONCHELLE" — both as written; the same place (Nédonchel).
- 21 November: "Salvage" is typed twice before "Officer" ("Drew attention of Salvage Salvage Officer") owing to overtyping; transcribed once.
- 23 November: "and [uncertain] returned to Company duty" — the overtyped word appears to read "then".
- 25 November: "It would not be safe to [uncertain] it to pigs" — the overtyped word appears to read "feed".
- 30 November: the signature appears to read "J. Heasley" — consistent with Major Heasley taking over command on 28 November — but the handwriting is a flourish and is not read with confidence; recorded as [uncertain].
- The O.C.'s leave warrant (13 November) gives leave 29/11 to 29/12, but the 28 November entry says the O.C. and M.O. went on leave that afternoon — both as written.
- Mileage: week ending 11 November, 5,595 miles; week ending 25 November, 4,808 miles, made up of No.1 Company 2,560, No.2 Company 1,033, No.3 Company 557 and No.4 Company 658 miles — the four figures sum to 4,808, as written.
- Every day 1–30 November 1917 has an entry; no dates are missing and no date is duplicated.
- Names read as: Lt. Col. Findlay (O.C., diarist to 28 November); Major Heasley (acting O.C. from 28 November); Col. Simson; Major Morrison (S.S.O., 1st Division); Major McLelland; Capt. Hennesy; Capt. McDougall; Major Hennessy; Capt. de Graves; Major Scott; Major Freeman; Capt. Bisnop; Lieut. Gwynne; Lieut. Hessey; Col. Dean; Capt. Gobey; Capt. Cartwright; S.M. Murray; Capt. J. Oliphant; Capt. Graham; Lieut. Breckenridge (O.C. Salvage Company); Lieut. F. Hills; Lieut. Grant; the S.S.O., Adjutant, M.O. and Paymaster (unnamed in this month); the Camp Commandant (unnamed); the General of 9 November (unnamed).
- Casualties: 11 November — German bombing of the dumps the Train was about to take over: six wounded, two of them (Supply men) later reported dead. 12 November — Capt. de Graves hit on the hand by a splinter from an anti-aircraft gun, "but not very dangerous". 14 November — very heavy bombardment, no casualties in the Train. 5 November — one horse with suspected glanders, confirmed as glanders; an animal casualty, not a personnel casualty. No other casualties are recorded this month.
