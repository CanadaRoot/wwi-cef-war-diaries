## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D. D. & L. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Divl" = Divisional, "Bgde" = Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "O.R." = other ranks.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished initial on every row that is not legible — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row.

## Page 3 — Diary text, 1–5 March 1916

[Handwritten "March 1916" at top, "18th Canadian Battalion" across the top, and "Original" and "Confidential" written across the top of the form. The Place column is blank on this page; locations are given in the text. Form C. 2118.]

### 1 March 1916 — S.P. trenches

> Battn in S.P trenches. Operation orders for cooperation in attack on INTERNATIONAL trench, on our left, (attached) Bombardment commenced 5 p.m. until 5.30 p.m. This was kept up during the night of March 1/2, becoming very intense about 2.30 A.M. 20th Can Bn was on our right and East Yorks (Imp) on our left. 5 o.r. admitted to hospital.

[Transcriber's note: the diary gives the bombardment as 5 p.m. to 5.30 p.m.; the Time Table on page 11 has the artillery ceasing fire at 5.20 p.m. and re-opening at 5.21 p.m.]

### 2 March 1916 — S.P. trenches, 5 A.M. and 8 p.m.

> 5 A.M. Our position as yesterday. Bombardment intense until 5.15 A.M. 18th Bn did not take part in attack. Artillery on both sides very active all night. 8 p.m. B. Coy relieved D Coy. At dusk enemy proceeded counter attack by trench bombardment. This attack was not made on our front; casualties. 5 o.r. killed 15 o.r. wounded. LIEUT BURTON admitted to hospital (shell shock.)

### 3 March 1916

> Our position as yesterday. 2 o.r. admitted to hospital. 24 o.r. arrived as reinforcements.

### 4 March 1916

## Page 9 — Appendix I, Operation Order No. 1 (page 1)

[Typed. Handwritten "Appendix 1" and "Copy No. 9" at top left.]

> OPERATION ORDER No. 1.
>
> By Lieut. Col. E. S. Wigle,
>
> Comdg. 18th Canadian Battalion.
>
> 1st March, 1916.
>
> INTENTION
>
> 1. The enemy is entrenched opposite our frontage, Left Sector, 4th C. I. B. The 20th Bn. is on our Right, holding the Right Sector, and on our left are the EAST YORKS. It is the intention that the 17th Division will assault the BLUFFS on our left on the 2nd March at an hour to be named later.
>
> On the 1st March, certain operations are to be carried on in which the 2nd Canadian Division will take part.
>
> DISPOSITION.
>
> 2. The 18th Battalion is distributed as follows: 'C' and 'D' Companies are holding the front line: 'C' Company on the Right, 'D' on the Left; the dividing line being trench P 3, Bay 4. 'B' Company, (less 1 platoon) holding Strong Point 8, at entrance to BOIS CONFLUENT; 1 Platoon 'B' Co. Bn. Hdqrs. 'A' Company is in Battalion Reserve, North East corner of RIDGEWOOD. Machine Gun Section, front line; Grenadiers in front line and P. & O. C. T. and S. P. 8. Scout Platoon, front line. Headquarters details — DEAD DOG FARM.
>
> (b) In case of retaliation and any of the positions occupied by units of the 18th Bn. are bombarded, Officers Commanding will take all necessary precautions to avoid casualties: 50% of the front line should be put in support trenches. 'B' Co., less 1 Platoon, will be distributed in S. P. 8, P 5 and 6, and 'A' Company will take [uncertain — a word is struck out here] the G. H. Q. line, 2nd line North of BRASSERIE-RIDGEWOOD road. One platoon of 'B' Company at Headquarters, if billet is bombarded, will vacate and occupy trenches in the locality.
>
> (c) The 2nd in Command will be in the front line during the operations.
>
> GRENADES:
>
> 3. For operations in the front line there are 250 Smoke Candles and 250 'P' Grenades. These will be detonated and distributed in the front line and used according to the attached schedule, but, before being used, another order will be issued.
>
> (b) Mills Grenades are distributed in Support Trenches at Junction of C. T's, 300 Mills Grenades being placed on the left side of traverse in each place. Grenadiers will be organised and have parties distributed along support trench and a reliable man placed in charge of each section, who will hold these in readiness to stop an attack and, if the enemy should get into the front line, be prepared at once to deliver a counter attack. The forces occupying the front line and 'B' Co. will organise for a counter attack.
>
> (c) When the attack is delivered by the 17th Divn., Trench Mortars will open an intense fire on the enemy's position.
>
> (d) During the night of March 1/2, BANGALORE torpedoes will be laid under Brigade arrangements and these will be exploded at an hour to be named later.
>
> COMMUNICATIONS:
>
> 4. Officers in Command of Units will detail runners in case of emergency. No communications will be sent by buzzer.
>
> (b) Signalling Officer will be ready at all times to repair any lines which are broken.
>
> (c) All [uncertain — a word is struck out here] movements of other troops and individuals in Brigade areas must be controlled by units in different localities.
>
> (d) If retaliation is asked for in front line, it may be difficult to get it at once as the artillery in support is engaged on other frontages.
>

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: 15 other ranks killed in action — 5 on 2 March, 2 on 5 March, 1 on 13 March, 1 on 14 March, 2 on 27 March and 4 on 30 March — and 1 other rank died of wounds on 15 March; no names of the dead are given. Wounded: 15 on 2 March, 1 on 13 March, 1 on 14 March, 6 on 27 March and 3 of the 13 men admitted to hospital on 30 March, plus single wounded admissions on 17, 20, 21 and 29 March. Lieut A. H. Burton was admitted to hospital with shell shock on 2 March and invalided to England on 16 March. The rest of the month's losses are sickness and hospital admissions.
- Movements: the battalion rotates between the S.P. trenches (1–5 March), Divisional Reserve at La Clytte (5–10 March), the S.P.T. trenches (11–16 March, with Battalion Reserve at Dead Dog Farm and Ridgewood), Brigade Reserve at Ridgewood with A Coy at Hallebast Corner (17–21 March), trenches M. & N. with Battalion Reserve at Vierstraat (22–27 March), and Divisional Reserve at La Clytte again (28–31 March), with A Coy holding trenches P.1, 2+3 from 28 March and B Coy relieving A Coy in trench P.1 on 30 March. It is relieved by the 19th Battalion on 5, 17 and 28 March, relieves the 19th Battalion on 11 March, and relieves the 58th Battalion on 22 March.
- Officers named: Lieut.-Col. E. S. Wigle, commanding (Operation Order No. 1, Appendix I); Lieut W. J. Baxter, Acting Adjutant (signed Operation Order No. 1; proceeded on leave 25 March); Capt G. H. Emery (admitted to hospital 5 March); Lieut Rispin (returned from temporary attachment to 1st Brigade Staff 5 March); Lieut A. H. Burton (shell shock 2 March; invalided to England 16 March); Lieut R. H. Sheffield (arrived as reinforcement 10 March); Maj G. J. Ingram and Maj A. C. Prince (both transferred to C.T.D. Shorncliffe, 10 March); Capt W. D. Perry (dismissed from His Majesty's service, 10 March); Maj R. Emmerton (sick, transferred to England, 10 March); Capt A. B. Laing (transferred to England, 10 March); Lieut J. Beeson (admitted to hospital 11 March); Lieut K. D. McKay (returned from hospital 14 March, admitted again 15 March); Lieut Kerr (reported to 4th Canadian Brigade C.F.A., 15 March); Lieuts J. C. Cullen and R. C. Mitchell (arrived as reinforcements 21 March); Lieut V. N. Smallpiece (attached for instruction, posted to A Coy, 24 March); Capt T. C. Lamb and Capt McKeough (proceeded on leave 25 March); Lieuts J. A. Cane, A. S. Allen and D. M. Northcombe (arrived as reinforcements 29 March); Maj L. E. Jones (returned to duty from command of the Trench Warfare School, 29 March). Appendix II is signed by Brig.-Gen. C. H. Harrington (Canadian Corps) and Lt.-Col. C. A. Ker (2nd Canadian Division).
- Notable: on 1–2 March the battalion co-operated in the 17th Division's assault on the Bluffs, supporting the attack on International Trench with an intense bombardment, smoke candles and 'P' grenades, a simulated attack by the 20th Battalion, and Bangalore torpedoes laid overnight (Appendix I and the Time Table, page 11); the battalion itself did not take part in the attack but took 5 killed and 15 wounded on 2 March. On 27 March several mines were fired under the Mound at St. Eloi by the division on the battalion's left, and the Northumberland Fusiliers and Royal Fusiliers attacked under cover of a heavy bombardment. On 10 March five officers were struck off strength in a single day. La Clytte was shelled lightly on 7 March, the billets untouched. Reinforcements during the month totalled 149 other ranks (24 on 3 March, 35 on 11 March, 40 on 17 March, 49 on 20 March, 1 on 26 March) plus 7 officers. A laudatory letter from the Canadian Corps (Appendix II, dated 22 March, received 23 March) praised the 2nd Division's trench work, wiring, enfilade machine-gun arrangements and buried cable scheme.
