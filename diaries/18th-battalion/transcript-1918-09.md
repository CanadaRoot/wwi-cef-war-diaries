## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "(A973) Wt W2215/P55 6,000 11/17 D. D. & L. Sch. 31a. Form C.2118/13." (variant spellings of the imprint occur across pages).
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed. `[struck]` marks text struck through in the original.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Cdn" = Canadian, "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade, "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles, "O.R." = other ranks, "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer, "S.B.R." = Small Box Respirator, "H.Q." = Headquarters, "N.C.O." = non-commissioned officer, "M.G." = Machine Gun, "R.A.P." = Regimental Aid Post, "C.C.R.C." = Canadian Corps Reinforcement Camp, "M.M." = Military Medal, "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial, "T.M." = Trench Mortar, "L.G." = Lewis Gun, "O.P." = Observation Post, "Bde." = Brigade, "I.R." = Infantry Regiment (German).
- Place names as written: Simencourt, Beaurains, Berneville, Dainville, Achicourt, Cherisy, Wancourt, Cagnicourt, Buissy Switch, Drocourt-Queant Support Line, Hindecourt, Arras-Cambrai Road, Canal du Nord, Oisy-le-Verger, Baralle, Morchain, Potte, Etalon, Nesle, Rouy, Rancourt, Longuevoisin, Voyennes, Buny, Ham, St. Quentin.
- Map references are trench-map sheet references as written, e.g. W.9.b.40.30.

## Page 5 — Diary text, 1–9 September 1918

[Typed. "Ref. Map 51.B. 1/40,000." at top left. Form C. 2118. "18TH CANADIAN BATTALION (WESTERN ONTARIO REGIMENT). SEPTEMBER 1918." at top.]

### 1 September 1918 — H.Q. Reserve, Simencourt

> Battalion carried on with training as per syllabus attached. 2 O.Rs. reinforcements. 4 O.Rs. awarded Bar to M.M. 33 O.Rs awarded M.M. 1 O.R. from Hospital. Lieut. M. H. Wilson and O.R. returned from C.C.R.C.

### 2 September 1918

> Battalion carried on with training as per syllabus attached. 1 O.R. returned from leave. 2 O.Rs. returned from hospital. Lieut. M. R. Sloan [uncertain] and 1 O.R. admitted to hospital. In accordance with 4th C.I.B. Operation Order No. 15, Battalion moved to BEAURAINS area. Battalion paraded at 4.00 p.m. and proceeded by BERNEVILLE - DAINVILLE STN - ACHICOURT to BEAURAINS, where Coys. were bivouaced in old trenches. 18th Cdn. Bn. Operation Order No. 236 attached.

### 3 September 1918 — Beaurains, M.11.e.

> In accordance with 4th C.I.B. Operation Order No. 3, Battalion moved to area between CHERISY and WANCOURT. 18th Cdn. Bn. Operation Order No. 237 attached. 3 O.Rs proceeded on leave. 3 O.Rs returned from leave. 2 O.Rs returned from hospital. 3 O.Rs reinforcements.

### 4 September 1918 — M.25.d. (Bde. Support)

> Battalion rested throughout the day. Lieut. G. Cook proceeded on leave. 1 O.R. returned from leave. 1 O.R. admitted to hospital. 3 O.Rs to 1st Army Rest Camp. 1 O.R. to 1st Army School of Cookery.

### 5 September 1918

> Battalion rested throughout the day. 5 O.Rs returned from hospital. 3 O.Rs from Escort duty. 3 O.Rs returned from leave.

### 6 September 1918

> Battalion rested during morning, and moved to CAGNICOURT area in the afternoon. Battalion Headquarters located at CHATEAU and Companies in Sunken Road (V.8.). 3 O.Rs proceeded on leave. 2 O.Rs returned from leave. 3 O.Rs returned from hospital. Lieut. A. J. Ferguson [uncertain] & 1 O.R. proceeded on [uncertain] Course to LA TOUQUET. 1 O.R. wounded.

### 7 September 1918 — Cagnicourt, V.8. & 9.

> Battalion rested during the day. Pay parades were held. 1 O.R. reinforcement. 1 Casual O.R. rejoined. Lieut. F. J. Goodman proceeded on leave. 1 O.R. attached 2nd Cdn. Div. [uncertain]

### 8 September 1918

> Divine Service was held near Battalion Headquarters at 10.30 a.m. Battalion rested for remainder of the day. 1 O.R. returned from leave. 1 O.R. Reinforcement.

### 9 September 1918

> Bath parades were held during the morning. Battalion rested during remainder of the day. Capt. J. Levitt (C.M.G.) proceeded on leave. 2 O.R. from [uncertain] Course. 1 O.R. reinforcement.

## Page 6 — Diary text, 10–13 September 1918

[Typed. Form C. 2118. "- 2 -" at top.]

### 10 September 1918 — Cagnicourt, V.8 & 9. (Bde. Support)

> Battalion rested throughout the day. Lieut. J. Morgan & 1 O.R. returned from leave. 1 O.R. killed in action. Lieut. J. E. Wylde & 45 O.Rs Reinforcements. 1 O.R. returned from hospital.

### 11 September 1918

> Battalion rested throughout the day. 1 O.R. returned from leave. 6 O.Rs awarded M.M. 7 O.Rs Reinforcements.

### 12 September 1918

> Battalion rested during the morning. The 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade relieved the 6th Canadian Infantry Brigade on the night 12th/13th. The 18th Canadian Battalion relieved the 21st Canadian Battalion in the left front line, as per disposition 18th Cdn. Bn. Order No. 238 attached. Coys. & Details moved forward independently by overland route to ARRAS-CAMBRAI Road at W.6.d.80.70. where guides met & guided to their respective positions in the line. Relief complete at 1.35 a.m. Pass Word for the night - "Trout". 3 O.Rs proceeded on leave. Lieut. T. V. Milford & 2 O.Rs returned from hospital. Lieut. J. Boyd & 2 O.Rs arrived as reinforcements.

### 13 September 1918 — Left Front Line, from W.3.b.75.65 to W.8.a.30.15.

> At 5.00 a.m. this morning, the enemy attempted a stealth raid against one of our posts this morning at W.9.b.40.30. This was repulsed. Pte. Jolie of "A" Coy. shot and killed the foremost German and wounded another, breaking up the party. Body of the dead German was recovered. He was an N.C.O. from the 63rd I.R. 13th Div. and apparently leader of the raiding party. None of our men were missing. Protective patrols covered the Battalion frontage during the night, but had nothing to report. Our artillery active during the day on enemy's forward and rear areas. Hostile artillery active at intervals. Our snipers covered enemy's forward area during the day. Many [uncertain] were observed. Enemy snipers very active in W.9.b. and W.15.b. Visibility poor throughout the day, and little movement could be seen except on ARRAS-CAMBRAI Road and vicinity. About 5.00 p.m. our artillery fired on OISY-LE-VERGER. Pass word for the night - "Knife". [uncertain] 3 O.Rs wounded.

## Page 7 — Diary text, 14–15 September 1918

[Typed. Form C. 2118. "- 3 -" at top.]

### 14 September 1918 — Left Front Line, from W.3.b.75.65 to W.8.a.30.15.

> At 12.30 a.m. this morning, two Coys. of 1st Cdn. Bn. carried out a successful minor operation and established positions in brick ovens (C.34.b.1.9. and C.23.a.5.2. to C.34.c.45.20) The following patrols covered Battalion frontage during the night 13th/14th: (1) Lieut. Stokes with 2 Bn. Scouts & covering party of 13 O.Rs. left our Outpost line at 10.20 p.m. at W.9.c.7.5., proceeded across Railway tracks through old camp to Railway at W.9.c.45.40, then left covering party at this point. The officer and 2 Scouts worked forward to Canal at W.9.d.10.15. The lock and Canal as far as ARRAS-CAMBRAI Road has a good depth of water but no crossings could be found. Machine guns very active in this area. Patrol then returned with covering party to our lines at point of exit at 12.35 a.m. (2) patrol of 1 Officer and 12 O.Rs detailed to cover left Battalion frontage were unable to do so on account of constant machine gun fire sweeping flat in W.3.b. & 9. At 1.30 a.m. from W.2.d.20.0., during our raid on left, enemy artillery appeared to be dropping short. A white flare went up followed by swift [uncertain] enemy artillery then lengthening out. Our artillery active during the day on enemy forward and rear areas. Much artillery [uncertain] and considerable number of gas shells were used. At 10.00 a.m. this morning, an enemy battery was observed firing from X.10.c.7.60. Our artillery was notified and action taken. Direct hits were observed and men were seen running from gun positions. Sniping activity on both sides below normal during the day. Visibility good at intervals during day. Movement below normal. Pass word for the night - "Fork". Major J. S. Bell & Capt. R. C. Elliott proceeded on leave. 1 O.R. to hospital. 1 O.R. returned from hospital.

### 15 September 1918

> The following working parties were employed during night 14th/15th. "A" Coy. worked during early part of night on improvements in their own area. 1 Officer and 17 O.Rs from "D" Coy. worked for five hours digging a 4-ft. trench connecting rifle pits from W.1.d.20.00 to W.2.c.90.00. Support Coys. carried rations for front line Coys. 2 Patrols covered the Battalion front during the night. (1) Lieut. [uncertain] and 4 Bn. Scouts & covering party of 11 O.Rs left our line at 7.30 p.m. worked through Canal North of ARRAS-CAMBRAI Road to approx. W.9.d.00.65., thence North-West to approx. W.2.d.80.85, thence South-West to W.9.b.00.85., thence South through woods and came to point of exit to our lines at 12.15 a.m. Enemy used very few flares but machine guns were very active in W.9.b. and d. (2) In accordance with 4th C.I.B. letter of 14th, patrol of 1 Officer & 10 O.Rs left our line at W.3.b.90.70., proceeding North-East. A similar patrol of 1st C.M.R. Bn. was met approx. W.4.d.20.90. where a joint post was established. No enemy encountered. Our artillery normal during the day. Enemy artillery very active.

### 15 September 1918 (continued) — Left Front Line, from W.3.b.75.65 to W.8.a.30.15.

> especially in the afternoon, and considerable individual movement was observed. Hostile battery positions were seen firing from X.10.c.7.60. and X.2.b.75.70. At 5.30 p.m. four of our planes forced down an enemy two-seater plane, which landed in W.23. About 9.30 p.m. enemy artillery put over a number of gas shells on forward area and in vicinity of Baralle [uncertain]. No casualties were caused. Pass word for the night - "Spoon". 2 O.Rs proceeded on leave. 3 O.Rs arrived as reinforcements. 3 O.Rs returned from hospital. 1 O.R. Killed in Action. and 3 O.Rs wounded.

### 16 September 1918

> Following patrols covered Battalion front on night 15th/16th. Lieut. Stokes & 4 Bn. Scouts with covering party of 12 O.Rs left our lines at W.9.c.55.60. at 9.20 p.m., crossed the ARRAS-CAMBRAI Road to the creek and left covering party at W.9.c.90.80. The Officer & Scouts went along creek to woods in W.19.b.55.60, then along road through woods to W.9.b.10.80. They remained at this point until 11.15 p.m. About 11.35 p.m. 11 rockets were sent up from W.9.b.7.75 which described a [uncertain] in W.9.b.50.98. These were followed by 14 or 15 [uncertain] which landed about 30 yards from point W.9.b.65.80. No enemy were seen but after a burst from our heavy M.Gs. Shouts were heard at East side of Canal. Patrols returned by same route and entered our lines at point of exit at 12.15 p.m. Hostile artillery on both sides during day. At 4.10 p.m. this afternoon enemy laid down a 10-minute bombardment in W.9.c. and d. and in W.23. about 9.30 p.m. Enemy movement above normal. Considerable movement was observed across country in R.31.b. and X.16.b. In accordance with 4th C.I.B. Order No. 16, 18th Canadian Battalion was relieved in the front line by 21st Canadian Battalion. Relief complete at 11.15 p.m. [uncertain] Battalion moved back to positions vacated by 21st Cdn. Bn. in BUISSY SWITCH, as per Disposition and 18th Canadian Battalion Operation Order No. D.239, attached. Protective patrols covered Battalion frontage until relief was completed. 2 O.Rs on leave. 2 O.Rs returned from leave. 4 O.Rs returned from hospital. 5 O.Rs wounded. 1 O.R. admitted to hospital.

### 17 September 1918 — Buissy Switch, V.10.a. & V.11.

> Battalion rested during day in old German trenches. In accordance with 4th C.I.B. Order No. 16, the 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade moved back to trenches in the DROCOURT-QUEANT Support line in the vicinity of CAGNICOURT during evening 17th, as per 18th Canadian Battalion Operation Order No. 240 attached. Lieuts. A. J. Craig, S. C. Berrie, A. F. Jones, A. R. Babcock and W. A. Cash arrived as Reifs. 2 O.Rs to leave. 4 O.Rs retd. from leave. Lieut. A. J. Hermansson [uncertain] & 1 O.R. retd. from 1st Army Rest Camp. 1 O.R. admitted to hospital.

## Page 9 — Diary text, 18–21 September 1918

[Typed. Form C. 2118. "- 5 -" at top.]

### 18 September 1918 — Cagnicourt, V.8.b and d. & V.9.

> "C" & "D" Coys. in DROCOURT-QUEANT SUPPORT LINE and trench immediately forward in V.9.b. Battalion H.Q. moved back to CHATEAU in the morning. "A" & "B" Coys. & H.Q. Details bathed at HENDECOURT in afternoon. On night 17th/18th 1st party consisting of 6 Officers & 175 O.Rs worked 3 hours digging approx. 170 yds of trench in V.7.e. 1 O.R. returned from leave. 1 O.R. proceeded on course.

### 19 September 1918

> Battalion rested during the day. On night 18th/19th "C" & "D" Coys relieved "A" & "B" Coys. in BUISSY SWITCH, relief commencing at dusk, and completed at 11.00 p.m. Disposition of "A" & "B" Coys. same as "C" & "D" Coys on 18th. 18th Canadian Battalion Operation Order D.241 attached. Capt. T. R. Heyward proceeded on leave. 1 O.R. returned from course. 1 O.R. wounded. 3 O.Rs from 1st Army Rest Camp.

### 20 September 1918

> "A" & "B" Coys. bathed at HENDECOURT during morning. Battalion rested during remainder of day. On night 19th/20th a gas projection was successfully carried out by 2nd Special Coy. R.E. on targets in W.5.b. and d., W.10.c. and d. and W.15.b. 1000 drum pure Phosgene gas were used. 18th Canadian Battalion relieved 21st Canadian Battalion in the LEFT FRONT LINE on night 20th/21st, as per disposition and 18th Canadian Battalion Operation Order D.242, attached. Relief complete at 9.50 p.m. Protective patrols covered Battalion frontage until completion of relief. Pass word for the night "Midge". 1 O.R. reinforcement. 3 Officers to Course. 1 O.R. returned from leave. 9 O.Rs proceeded on leave. 1 O.R. returned under escort. 1 O.R. wounded.

### 21 September 1918 — Left Front Line, from W.3.d.00.80. to W.14.b.65.15.

## Page 22 — Appendix 7, Information re ground and obstacles in the vicinity of Morchain [uncertain] and the Somme Valley

[Typed. "7" at top. "INFORMATION RE ground and obstacles in the vicinity of MORCHAIN, [uncertain], and SOMME VALLEY, given by Cpls. BELL and PATTERSON, I.P., formerly stationed in the area."]

> MORCHAIN. MORCHAIN is all in ruins - w - as burnt and destroyed by the enemy in 1917 retreat. According to inhabitants interviewed during February and March, the enemy fortified all the cellars, and joined many of them by underground passages. Many of these cellars dug-outs may be hastily prepared by enemy now. Village is on high ground and commands wide views in all directions especially bridges from the west from PETRAIN and BRESLINCOURT.
>
> POTTE. POTTE is also in ruins. In the Cha[uncertain] and immediately on the roadside and following same side by side for 500 metres south, are trenches facing West, and hidden by hedges and trees. Observation from these trenches would command movement on the road BRESLINCOURT - PETRAIN. On the high ground between POTTE and LICOURT trenches exist on both sides of the road and run parallel to same for some 1500 metres. On the day in March, 1916, above-named inspected an underground passage under the road and connecting these trenches. The approximate position of this passage is described by reference- AMIENS Sheet 17, 3.K.47.78.
>
> Obstacles. On the high ground comprised between EPEHY [uncertain] - FARGNY-MORCHAIN-BETHENCOURT and running about N.E. and S.W. a wide belt (6 to 7 yds.) of closely built barbed wire entanglement constructed by the enemy and left by him intact. Its length would run into a few kilometres, and it would present, if still in existence, serious barrier for infantry from the west over this open country. As far as can be remembered the improvised bridges existing before March 1918 were all for traffic not heavier than some 7 tons on one axle. At all these places at least three bridges are necessary, one about 10-12 metres over the Canal one slightly nearer over the river, and one or more small ones over the rivulets in the marshes. The Somme valley at these points can be estimated to be a serious obstacle to all traffics without bridges or boats.

> INFORMATION regarding CANAL du NORD between RANCOURT and REUY, given by I.P. Cpl. Hallen, formerly stationed in the locality.
>
> Bridges. The only places in known to this [uncertain] in the district are the approaches to BREUIL to BUVERCHY and RAMECOURT - these are wooden bridges. In his opinion [struck: "will-be-easy-"] the crossing of the canal by infantry will be easy, as the water is only three or four feet deep, but will be difficult for vehicles, as the banks are made of brick and are very steep.

>
> River Ingon. From ROUY-le-Petit to the Somme, the river Ingon runs in a very marshy valley, side by side with incomplete CANAL du NORD. West of ROUY-le-PETIT it is easily passable on the road from NESLE to former place.
>
> Bridges. Between ROUY-le-PETIT and ROUY-le-GRAND the bridges existing in March 1918 were only improvised and of heavy wooden structure (logs). There was also a 'passerelle' of wood about 2 ft. wide joining the two places about 700 to 1,000 metres in length over the marsh.
>
> Ground. The side of the valley on the northern side of the river at ROUY-le-GRAND is honeycombed with blown-up German dug-outs which could no doubt be put [uncertain] into use again by retreating enemy. In March the River Ingon and the Canal were fairly deep, especially the latter and would be an impediment to troops without bridges.
>
> Roads. Heavy transport proceeding East from NESLE would do well to avoid this valley. The best way to cross the Somme is via MESNIL-St-Nicaise, BETHENCOURT, or via VOYENNES. These roads were at that time in good condition.

## Notes

- Diary date range covered: 1–30 September 1918, complete; no date gaps in the diary text. The diary text is 8 pages (pages 5–12 of the file set), Volume 37.
- Commanding Officer: the diary is signed on page 12 by a Major "for Lieut-Colonel, Comdg. 18th Canadian Battalion, Western Ontario Regiment" — the signature reads "V. Belle" [uncertain]. Adjutant/assistant adjutant signatures across the appendices are mostly Lieut. R. R. Hartry (Asst. Adjt.), with Lieut. R. C. Elliott [uncertain] signing Orders D.241 and D.242, Lieut. C. Cook signing Order D.243, and J. H. MacRae [uncertain] signing the 24 September disposition.
- No pages are missing from the file set: all 54 images (pages 1–54) are present and in order. The contents page lists appendices 1–23, and all 23 are present; pages 52–54 (envelope and First Army letter) are extra material not listed in the contents.
- The 16 September disposition (Appendix 10, pages 25–28) has a numbering oddity: post numbers 26, 27 and 28 do not appear anywhere on the four sheets, and post number 36 is used twice — once for a "D" Coy. support post (W.2.d.80.85, Bombing & Rifle) and once for the "C" Coy. reserve Lewis Gun positions.
- Discrepancy in the relief of 25/26 September: the note to "A" and "D" Companies (Appendix 21, page 47) and the contents page both say "D" Coy. is to be relieved by the 13th Cdn. Bn. and "A" Coy. by the 14th Cdn. Bn., while Order No. D.244 itself (Appendix 22, page 48) reverses this — "D" Coy. relieved by the 14th Canadian Battalion and "A" Coy. by the 13th Canadian Battalion. The guide details in D.244 match D.244's own version.
- Page 42 is the reverse of the 21 September disposition state sheet 2; it carries only show-through of the table and was not transcribed separately.
- The disposition-state tables (pages 19–20 and 40–41) are handwritten and faint; individual specialist counts in the body of the tables are partly illegible, so the tables are transcribed with locations, companies and platoons in full and the sheet/grand totals as written, with [uncertain] against column totals that could not be read with confidence.
- The diary text is typed and generally legible; most [uncertain] flags fall on handwritten appendix pages (orders D.239–D.244, disposition states) and on a few typed words obscured by overtyping or faint print.
- Awards noted in the month: 33 O.Rs. awarded the Military Medal and 4 O.Rs. awarded the Bar to the M.M. (1 September); a further 6 O.Rs. awarded the M.M. (11 September).
- Casualties recorded in the diary text (killed and wounded only, as written): 1 O.R. killed in action (10 September); 1 O.R. killed in action and 3 O.Rs. wounded (15 September); 3 O.Rs. wounded (13 September); 5 O.Rs. wounded (16 September); 1 O.R. wounded (6 September, 19 September, 20 September); 3 O.Rs. wounded (21 September); 1 O.R. wounded (24 September).
