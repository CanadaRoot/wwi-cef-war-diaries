## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text for April is TYPED on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The printed heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through and "18TH (WESTERN ONTARIO) CANADIAN BATTALION" typed in. The form footer reads "2353 Wt. W2541/1454 700,000 5/15 D. D. & L. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Bgde" = Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "O.R." = other ranks, "O.P." = observation post, "M.G." = machine gun, "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding, "R.W. Kents" = Royal West Kents, "Boche" = the enemy (as written).
- The Remarks column carries a repeated initialled mark on every diary page, reading "C. H. [uncertain]" — it is not transcribed row by row.
- Place names as written: Bois-des-Alleux, Mont-St-Eloy, Estree Cauchie, Zivy Cave, Thelus, Vimy Ridge, Neuville-St-Vaast, Les Tilleuls, Cramer Haus, Paynesley Tunnel, Bois de la Ville, Goulot Wood.

## Page 3 — Diary text, 1–4 April 1917

[Typed. "Confidential." at top left, "Apr 1-3" handwritten at top right, "20" at top right corner. Form C. 2118.]

### 1 April 1917 — Bois-des-Alleux

> Battalion billeted in huts in Bois-des-Alleux near Mont-St-Eloy.

### 2 April 1917, 9 am — do

> Battalion marched to training ground near Estree Cauchie, where exact taped replica of enemy frontage to be attacked, was laid out. Four other ranks admitted to Hospital (Sick).

### 2 April 1917 (continued) — do

> A complete rehearsal of the contemplated attack was carried out by the 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade over the taped area representing the German frontage. Fighting equipment worn by all ranks. Reorganisation and consolidation practised when objectives reached. 16 O.R's attached to 4th Canadian Trench Mortar Battery as carrying party for the offensive.

### 3 April 1917 — do

> Company and Platoon inspections of ammunition and equipment. Extra water-bottles were issued to each man for reserve supply of water.

### 4 April 1917 — do

> Battalion Pay-parades. Two Platoons "D" Company relieved 2 Platoons of 31st Canadian Bn., for the purpose of digging "jumping-off" trenches in front of our own front line and cutting lanes in our wire, ready for the Battalion's advance. These Platoons were under the Command of Lieut's. W. K. Rooney and J. M. Fisher and the fact that these two difficult operations were carried out successfully without even the slightest casualty reflects the highest credit upon all concerned. Major L. E. JONES rejoined Battalion today from Senior Officers' Course in England.

[Typed. Form C. 2118.]

### 5 April 1917 — Bois-des-Alleux

> Battalion training and inspections. Completion of Pay-parade. Lieut. W. T. Bartlett admitted to hospital (Sick). 2 O.R's returned from hospital.

### 6 April 1917 — do

> Final training of Battalion in attack over taped area. During the night 6th/7th Lieut's. W. K. Rooney and J. M. Fisher with small party made careful reconnaissance of "jumping-off" trenches and wire, the preparation of which, for the attack, was almost complete. This party reconnoitred under heavy rifle and M.G. fire as they were observed owing to the bright Moonlight, but no casualties occurred. This inspection was necessary as before Companies could be assembled in their respective formations an absolute assurance that the work was complete, had to be rendered. The Commanding Officer, Lt-Col. G. F. Morrison, and Battalion Scout Officer Lieut V. M. Eastwood, made personal reconnaissance of enemy front from O.P's, and reported that both lines of enemy wire were sufficiently cut, so as to offer little obstacle.

### 7 April 1917 — do

> Battalion resting. 6 O.R's admitted to hospital (Sick). 5 O.R's, Casuals rejoined.

## Page 5 — Diary text, 8 April (continued) – 9 April 1917

[Typed. "(3)" at top centre. Form C. 2118.]

### 8 April 1917, 7.45 p.m. — (Cont'd)

> Battalion commenced to move to its battle position on frontage A.10.b.2.7. to A.10.b.6.1. with Battalion H.Q. located at ZIVY CAVE. The Battalion, comprising 12 Platoons, approximately 600 strong, moved into the line by Platoons at 100 yds interval. Bombs and shovels were picked up en route, and despite the fact that the enemy heavily shelled all routes and O.P's. all the night, the assembly was completed without a casualty. All Companies reported by messenger to Battalion H.Q., their being in position. "A" Coy. 2.20 a.m. "B" Coy. 2.25 a.m. "C" Coy. 3.45 a.m. & "D" Coy. 3.40 a.m.

### 9 April 1917 — (no place given)

> At Zero hour, viz 5.30 a.m., the advance was made. Simultaneously with the opening up of the Artillery Barrage the Battalion left the "jumping-off" trenches and attacked the German front line. Very little opposition was met with whilst capturing the first line system of trenches. The enemy barraged "No-man's-land" for about 15 minutes, after which his Artillery fire became very indiscriminate. The support line was captured without any difficulty and the Battalion Objective (Black), a line of trench from A.11.a.79.36. to A.11.a.45.75., finally reached. At 6.05 a.m. a message was received at Bn. H.Q. saying that the Black Objective had been captured and was in the act of being consolidated. The casualties up to this point had been very slight, considering the magnitude of the operations. Major C. C. ### 9 April 1917 (continued)

> 100 yards from the objective, and Lieut. W. J. McLean was also killed while leading his men across "No-man's-land". After the death of Major Gwyn, Lieut. P. ## Page 12 — Appendix I, Instructions for the Offensive, p. 1

[Typed. Handwritten "Appendix 1" at top left, "10" at top centre, "SECRET. Copy No. 4" at top right.]

> 18th CANADIAN INFANTRY BATTALION INSTRUCTION FOR THE OFFENSIVE.
>
> Ref: Roclincourt 1/10000.
>
> Thelus N.W. and Thelus N.E. 1/5000.
>
> Tables "A" [uncertain].
>
> Barrage Map.
>
> S.S. 135.
>
> 1. INFORMATION. The Canadian Corps will take the VIMY RIDGE in conjunction with operations on other portions of the front. The 19th Canadian Battalion is attacking on our Left and the 3rd Canadian Infantry Brigade on our Right. A portion of the 6th Canadian Infantry Brigade is to attack through the 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade when the latter's objectives have been captured.
>
> 2. INTENTION. To capture and consolidate portions of the German Support Line, the BLACK OBJECTIVE and the RED OBJECTIVE. The task of the 18th Canadian Infantry Battalion will be to capture and consolidate that part of the BLACK OBJECTIVE lying between A.11.a.70.35. and A.11.a.45.75.
>
> 3. BOUNDARIES. The 18th Canadian Infantry Battalion Battle-boundaries, forward of British front line, will run as follows: Northern: The NEUVILLE-ST-VAAST — LES TILLEULS road. The road will be inclusive to the Battalion. Southern: From A.10.a.65.0. on British front line, to A.10.b.8.1. on German front line, to A.11.a.0.1. [uncertain] to A.11.a.7.3. on LILLE ROAD.
>
> 4. PLAN. The 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade has for its objective the German Front and Support trenches (First and Second INTERMEDIATE OBJECTIVES respectively) and the BLACK and RED OBJECTIVES. Frontage: About 700 yards. The Brigade will attack with the 18th and 19th Battalions in the front line, the 21st Battalion in Reserve and the 20th Battalion broken up for mopping-up and carrying parties. The leading Waves of the 18th and 19th Battalions will go straight through to the BLACK OBJECTIVE, which they are timed to reach at 0.32 minutes, leaving the mopping-up parties to deal with the intermediate trenches. The 21st Battalion will follow the 18th and 19th Battalions to the BLACK OBJECTIVE, where it will re-form and commence the advance at 0.75. The 5th Canadian Infantry Brigade on the left of the 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade will attack according to a similar plan. The 6th Canadian Infantry Brigade will follow the 4th and 5th Brigades and, re-forming in the RED OBJECTIVE, commence the advance at 0.245 to the BLUE OBJECTIVE. It will also have the BROWN OBJECTIVE — the enemy guns in BOIS DE LA VILLE — GOULOT WOOD — AND BOIS DU GOULOT — as its objective.
>
> 5. DISPOSITION. The 18th Canadian Battalion will assault on a 3 Platoon frontage, and in 4 Waves, with two intermediate lines of moppers-up from the 20th Canadian Infantry Battalion. Companies will be disposed as under:—
>
> Right — Centre — Left — Reserve (4th Wave)
>
> "B" Coy. "C" Coy. "A" Coy. "D" Coy.
>
> Waves will be composed as under:—
>
> First Wave will consist of 1 Platoon from each of "B" "C" and "A" Companies.
>
> Moppers-up will consist of 2 lines, each of 6 Sections of the 20th Battalion.
>
> Second Wave will consist of the respective 2nd Platoons of "B", "C" and "A" Companies.

## Page 13 — Appendix I, Instructions for the Offensive, p. 2

[Typed. "(Page 2)" at top centre, "11" at top right, "SECRET. Copy No. 4" at top right.]

> 5. DISPOSITION. (Continued) 3rd WAVE will consist of the respective 3rd Platoons of "B", "C" and "A" Companies. The "C" Coy. Platoon in this Wave will form the Battalion carrying party and the other 2 Platoons will form the Battalion consolidating party. 4th WAVE (Battalion Reserve) will consist of the 3 Platoons of "D" Company. Sufficient distance to preserve formation, to be maintained between Waves. See Plate "A", which indicates distances to be maintained. These however are subject to change as they must conform with the distances maintained between Waves of Flank Battalions. The 4th Platoon of each Company will remain at the Transport lines.
>
> 6. JUMPING-OFF TRENCHES. Jumping-off trenches will consist of present Observation line on Battalion frontage and will be prepared for the purpose. All attacking troops to be in position two hours before 0.
>
> 7. HEADQUARTERS. Battalion and Brigade Headquarters and Report Centres will, prior to 0., be located at ZIVY CAVE. During the advance the 18th Canadian Infantry Battalion will establish Report Centre near the German dugouts at A.10.b.85.48.
>
> 8. ARTILLERY. Artillery arrangements have been completed by the 2nd Canadian Divisional Artillery. Barrage Map may now be seen at Battalion H.Q.
>
> 9. MACHINE GUNS. Brigade Machine Guns have been grouped under Corps control for Barrage work. In addition to these the G.O.C., 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade will have Mobile Reserve of eight Machine guns of the 4th Machine Gun Company.
>
> 10. STOKES GUNS. Stokes Guns of the 4th Canadian Trench Mortar Battery will Barrage German trenches till 0.6 at which time 4 Guns will come under command of Officer Commanding 18th Canadian Battalion.
>
> 11. ADVANCE. At 0. the Artillery, Stokes, and Machine Gun Barrages will open and the Infantry will advance over the open to the assault. (A) At 0.3 the Barrage will lift from, and the Leading Wave will capture, the German front line. The first line of moppers-up will remain in, and deal with this trench. The First and Second Waves will continue to follow the Barrage. (B) At 0.8 the Barrage will lift from, and the Leading Wave will capture, the German Support line. The 2nd line of moppers-up will remain in, and deal with this trench. The 1st and 2nd Waves will continue to follow the Barrage, which will advance according to the Barrage Table. At 0.32 the Barrage will lift from, and the Leading Wave will capture, the BLACK OBJECTIVE. The 2nd and 3rd Waves will continue to the BLACK OBJECTIVE. The 4th Wave will enter, or halt short of the BLACK OBJECTIVE, as circumstances may require.
>
> 12. CONSOLIDATION. (A) After mopping-up the German front line the 1st Line of moppers-up will join the 2nd Line of moppers-up in the German Support Line and consolidate this line. (B) After capturing the BLACK OBJECTIVE, the 18th Canadian Battalion will consolidate this line.

## Notes on this month

- Casualties (Vimy Ridge, 9 April): 2 officers killed — Major C. C. Gwyn (killed by a machine-gun bullet about 100 yards from the Black Objective) and Lieut. W. J. McLean (killed leading his men across No-man's-land); 4 officers wounded — Lieuts. W. G. Worth, S. C. Kirkland, C. E. Tuck and W. K. Rooney; 40 other ranks killed and 120 wounded. Sergt. E. W. Sifton of "C" Coy single-handedly attacked a machine-gun crew and bayoneted every man, but was shot by a dying German. After Major Gwyn's death Lieut. P. Jerdan took command of "B" Company. Later in the month: 2 other ranks wounded 25 April; otherwise sickness and hospital admissions only.
- Movements: the battalion trains at Bois-des-Alleux and rehearses the attack over a taped replica of the German frontage near Estrée-Cauchée (2 April, full 4th Brigade rehearsal); moves to its battle position on the night of 8/9 April (Battalion H.Q. at Zivy Cave); attacks at Zero hour 5.30 a.m. on 9 April (Easter Monday) and captures the Black Objective by 6.05 a.m.; relieves the 1st R.W. Kents in the captured line at dusk on 10 April (Battalion H.Q. at Cramer Haus, then A.6.c.8.8); relieved by the 24th Battalion on the night of 13/14 April and rests at Bois-des-Alleux; moves into Divisional Reserve at Paynesley Tunnel 16 April (relieving the 25th Battalion); relieves the 31st Battalion in Brigade Reserve at Cramer Haus 21 April; relieved by the 24th Battalion 26 April to rest camp at 8.c.3.2.
- Officers named: Lt-Col. G. F. Morrison (Commanding Officer; made a personal reconnaissance of the enemy front 6 April; admitted to hospital sick 19 April); Major K. H. McCrimmon (Adjutant; established Battalion H.Q. at A.11.d.1.8 on 9 April); Major C. C. Gwyn (killed 9 April); Major W. J. Gander (senior officer in the line 9 April, reported consolidation complete at 10.50 a.m.); Major L. E. Jones (rejoined from Senior Officers' Course, 4 April); Lieuts. W. K. Rooney and J. M. Fisher (dug the jumping-off trenches and cut wire lanes 4 April; reconnoitred them under fire on the night of 6/7 April; Rooney wounded 9 April, rejoined from hospital 22 April); Lieut V. M. Eastwood (Battalion Scout Officer; reconnaissance 6 April; patrols 12–13 April); Lieut W. H. Lewis (advanced Battalion H.Q. 9 April); Lieut P. Jerdan (took command of "B" Company 9 April; to hospital sick 27 April); Lieut D. A. G. Parsons (commanded "A" Company 9 April); Lieut W. T. Bartlett (to hospital sick 5 April); Lieut D. R. MacDonald (reinforcement 16 April); Lieut C. J. Jackson (to hospital sick 24 April); Lieuts. A. R. MacKedie, L. C. Jarvis, H. E. P. Ralph and W. R. Wright (reinforcements 30 April); Lieuts. H. W. Smith and A. E. Cook (reinforcements 14 April); Lieut E. E. Beskel, Lieut Lloyd and Lieut W. J. McLean (messages of 9 April; McLean killed).
- Notable: the month is dominated by the Battle of Vimy Ridge. The battalion, about 600 strong in 12 platoons, attacked at 5.30 a.m. on Easter Monday 9 April 1917, took the German front and support lines with little opposition, and reached the Black Objective (A.11.a.79.36 to A.11.a.45.75) by 6.05 a.m. Straw stacks just ahead of the objective turned out to hide concrete machine-gun emplacements, and the straw was used to make the men's funk-holes comfortable on the night of 9/10 April. On 13 April daylight patrols found the Germans retiring along the roads between Vimy and Willerval, digging in before Acheville, and the battalion pushed outposts 400 yards beyond the Lens–Arras railway. The appendix preserves the full offensive instructions (Appendix I), the Zivy subway sketch, Operation Orders D 21 and D 23, the re-organisation memorandum, the attack formation diagram, the brigade's relief message, Move Order M.25, and a series of handwritten field messages from the morning of 9 April reporting the Black Objective gained — including a company commander's blunt note that two of his platoons were almost exhausted after five days digging jumping-off trenches and cutting wire, that his telephone wire was down, and a postscript that the 6th Brigade's Vickers gunners had departed without being relieved.

The contents list only four appendices (I, 2, 3, 4) but the set contains many more appendix items. Duplicate filmings also occur at pages 29 (of 28), 31 (of 30), 37 (of 36) and 42 (of 41).
