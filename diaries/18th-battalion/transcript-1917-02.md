## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The printed heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through and "18th Canadian Battalion" written in by hand. The form footer reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/16. Sir J. C. & S."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Bgde" = Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "O.R." = other ranks, "or" = other ranks, "O.P." = observation post, "Y.M.C.A." = Young Men's Christian Association.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated initialled mark on every row, reading "C.P.L." [uncertain], except on the final page (27–28 February), where the Remarks column is blank; it is not transcribed row by row.
- Place names as written: Auchel, Lozinghem, Burbure, Raimbert, Haillicourt, Ruitz, Estree Cauchie, Ecoivres, Thelus, Vistula Shelters, Rhine Shelters, Maison Blanche.

## Page 3 — Diary text, 1–6 February 1917

[Handwritten "Original" at top left, with "Confidential" beneath it. "February 1917" at top right. Form C. 2118.]

### 1 February 1917 — Auchel

> Battalion in Rest Billets. Physical Exercise, Bayonet fighting, Company and Platoon drill. 19 or sick admitted to hospital. 2 or returned from Hospital.

### 2 February 1917 — do

> Battalion Training. Physical Exercise, Platoon training in attack and withdrawal of specialists. Company inspections.

### 3 February 1917 — do

> Battalion parade in full marching order for Commanding Officer's inspection. 7 or sick admitted to hospital.

### 4 February 1917, 9 am — do

> Battalion Church parade. Afternoon, recreational Training. Football final. [uncertain — a line is illegible] 1 or returned from Hospital.

### 5 February 1917, 9 am — do

> Battalion paraded in full marching order for inspection by the Canadian Corps Commander. Sir Julian Byng. High praise was won by the Battalion for their cleanliness and smartness in all departments. 4 or returned from Hospital.

### 6 February 1917 — do

> Training. Physical drill, Bayonet fighting, communication drill. Company in attack and Respirator drill. 3 or arrived as reinforcements. 7 or admitted to hospital. 6 or returned from hospital.

## Page 4 — Diary text, 7–13 February 1917

[Handwritten "February 1917" at top right. Form C. 2118.]

### 7 February 1917, 9.30 — Auchel

> Battalion route march in full marching order. Route viz. Auchel, Lozinghem, Burburt [uncertain] and Raimbert back to Auchel. 2 or admitted to hospital.

### 8 February 1917 — do

> Battalion Training. Morning & noon Battalion in attack. Afternoon. Section drill. 3 or admitted to hospital 1 or returned from hospital.

### 9 February 1917 — do

> Battalion in training. Training by specialists, in Bombing, Machine Gun, Bombing and reconnaissance, and Communications. 3 or wounded, accidentally by premature explosion of a Grenade.

### 10 February 1917 — do

> Company inspection by Company Commanders. Physical drill and Company training. 2 or admitted to hospital 2 or returned.

### 11 February 1917 — do

> Church parade at 10 am in Y.M.C.A Building. Afternoon recreational training. Football. Major T. A. McIntosh and Lieut. Rowe admitted to hospital sick.

### 12 February 1917 — March

> Battalion marched from Auchel to Haillicourt in conjunction with the 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade

### 13 February 1917 — (no place given)

> 18th Canadian Bn marched to Billets at Ecoivres via Ruitz, les [uncertain] and Estree Cauchie, at which place we rested for lunch.

## Page 5 — Diary text, 14–20 February 1917

[Handwritten "February 1917" at top right. Form C. 2118.]

### 14 February 1917 — Ecoivres

> Battalion in Divisional reserve in the front of Thelus sector. Lieut T. M. Fisher arrived as reinforcement. Sgt T Dougall awarded the "Medaille Militaire" for bravery in the field. 3 or returned from hospital.

### 15 February 1917 — do

> Battalion Training. Company and Platoon drills. Company inspection

### 16 February 1917 — do

> do — Lieut E. E. Beskel admitted to hospital sick. 2 or returned from hospital.

### 17 February 1917 — do

> Battalion training. Specialist Instruction. Sgt J Dougall [uncertain] granted commission in 18th Battalion from 16.2.17.

### 18 February 1917 — do

> Routine Training. 7 or admitted to hospital, 8 or returned from hospital.

### 19 February 1917 — Thelus Sector

> 18th Battalion relieved 20th Canadian Battalion in front line of Thelus Sector. Map ref 57c N.11. [uncertain] trench map. A, B and C coys in front line D coy in support.

### 20 February 1917 — do

> Trenches in poor condition in several parts, our artillery active during morning. This sector in very muddy condition, necessitating continuous work by the Battalion to keep in repair.

## Page 6 — Diary text, 21–26 February 1917

[Handwritten "February 1917" at top right. Form C. 2118.]

### 21 February 1917 — Thelus sector

> Our Trench Mortars very active making several direct hits on Enemy O.P. at [uncertain] M 8.11. Corp Wackett claims one Hun. 2 or admitted to hospital.

### 22 February 1917 — do

> Our artillery active during day. 2 or Killed in action, 3 or wounded while on patrol. Enemy working parties seen at intervals. 70 or arrived from 2nd Canadian Entrenching Bn as reinforcements.

### 23 February 1917 — do

> Working party in rear of German lines dispersed by our Artillery fire. Enemy "Whiz bang" battery active during afternoon. 1 or Wounded. 2 or admitted to hospital sick. Heavy bombardment on our left during night.

### 24 February 1917 — do

> Nothing unusual to report. Our artillery and Stokes mortars active. 4 or admitted to Hospital sick.

### 25 February 1917 — Relief

> 18th Canadian Bn was relieved in front line by 20th Canadian Bn. 18th Battalion moved into Brigade support. at Vistula Shelters, Rhine Shelters and Maison Blanche. Completion of relief reported by code word "BLUE."

### 26 February 1917 — Bgde Support

> Battalion in Brigade support, nothing unusual to report. 2 or admitted to Hospital.

## Page 7 — Diary text, 27–28 February 1917

[Handwritten "February 1917" at top right. Form C. 2118. Signed at the foot: "G. F. Morrison Lt Col. Commanding 18th Canadian Battalion."]

### 27 February 1917 — Bgde Support

> Battalion in Brigade support. During night of 27/28th severe artillery bombardment on both sides. 2 or wounded. 2 or returned from hospital.

### 28 February 1917 — do

> Position as yesterday comparatively quiet. working parties cleaning up trenches.

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: 3 other ranks wounded accidentally by the premature explosion of a grenade 9 February; 2 other ranks killed in action and 3 wounded while on patrol 22 February; 1 other rank wounded 23 February; 2 other ranks wounded in the night bombardment of 27/28 February. No other battle casualties are recorded; the rest of the month's losses are sickness and hospital admissions.
- Movements: the battalion rests and trains at Auchel (1–11 February), marches to Haillicourt 12 February, then to billets at Ecoivres 13 February via Ruitz and Estree Cauchie; in Divisional Reserve in front of the Thelus sector 14–18 February; relieves the 20th Battalion in the Thelus sector front line 19 February; relieved by the 20th Battalion 25 February and moves into Brigade Support at Vistula Shelters, Rhine Shelters and Maison Blanche.
- Officers named: Sir Julian Byng, Canadian Corps Commander (inspected the battalion 5 February and praised its cleanliness and smartness); Major T. A. McIntosh and Lieut. Rowe (admitted to hospital sick, 11 February); Lieut T. M. Fisher (reinforcement, 14 February); Lieut E. E. Beskel (admitted to hospital sick, 16 February).
- Notable: Sgt T. Dougall was awarded the Médaille Militaire for bravery in the field (14 February), and Sgt J. Dougall was granted a commission in the 18th Battalion from 16 February 1917 (17 February) — the diary spells the surname Dougall in both entries, with different initials. The Thelus sector trenches were in poor, very muddy condition and needed continuous repair work. Trench mortars scored several direct hits on an enemy observation post 21 February, and Corporal Wackett claimed a German shot. 70 other ranks arrived as reinforcements from the 2nd Canadian Entrenching Battalion 22 February.
- No date gaps: entries run 1–28 February without a break.
