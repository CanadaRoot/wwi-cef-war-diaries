## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Divn" = Division, "o.r." = other ranks, "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding, "C.T.D." = Canadian Training Division, "N.C.O." = non-commissioned officer, "R.S.M." = Regimental Sergeant-Major, "D.R.O." = Divisional Routine Order, "D.S.O." = Distinguished Service Order, "D.C.M." = Distinguished Conduct Medal.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished initial ("CPL:") on every row that is not legible — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row. Two rows carry additional remarks, noted on their pages.
- Place names as written: Somme, Courcellette, Sausage Valley, Warloy, Lavicogne, Bonneville, Remaisnil, Sibiville, Houvelin, Bruay, Barlin, Hersin, Bully Grenay, Maroc Sector.

## Page 3 — Diary text, 1–4 October 1916

[Handwritten "Original Confidential" at top left, "18th Canadian Battn" under the printed "WAR DIARY" heading, and "October 1916" at top right. Army Form C. 2118. The Summary column is headed with the diary-text page number "2". A "14" appears at the top right of the sheet.]

### 1 October 1916 — Somme

> the night of 1/2nd 18th Can Battalion relieves 21st Canadian Battalion in line in front of Courcellette. 22nd Canadian Battalion on our left.

### 2 October 1916 — Somme (ditto), 3.15pm

> Battalion advanced 500 yds and dug new trench and held position taken. Left flank was left exposed owing to the Battn on our left being unable to advance but this was covered by our Lewis Guns.
>
> Battalion still holding position gained

### 3 October 1916 — [place column blank]

> Battalion moved into reserve at Sausage Valley. Lieut. L. A. Bissell taken on as reinforcement from 28-9-16. Lieut R.E. Cock and 3 o.r. wounded 9 o.r. sick admitted to hospital. 66 o.r. arrived as reinforcements

### 4 October 1916 — [place column blank]

> Position as yesterday. Lieut H. W. McBRIDE arrived as reinforcement Major. L. E. Jones and Major J. A. McINTOSH (Adjut.) reported wounded. 24 o.r. reported "Killed in action". 78 o.r. reported wounded. 11 o.r. reported missing. Lieut. L. A. Bissell wounded.

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: the month's heavy losses come from the attack of 2 October in front of Courcellette (Somme): 24 other ranks reported killed in action, 78 wounded and 11 missing on 4 October, with Maj. L. E. Jones and Maj. J. A. McIntosh (Adjutant) reported wounded, and Lieut. L. A. Bissell wounded the same day (he had only joined as a reinforcement on 3 October). Lieut. R. E. Cook and 3 other ranks were wounded on 3 October. Later in the month, 3 other ranks were reported "Died of wounds" on 24 October, and on 27 October a further 2 other ranks were reported killed in action on 2.10.16. No other battle casualties are recorded; the rest of the month's losses are sickness and hospital admissions.
- Movements: 1–3 October the battalion is in the line in front of Courcellette on the Somme, advancing 500 yards and digging a new trench on 2 October, then moving into reserve at Sausage Valley on 3 October. 6–17 October it marches back through billets at Warloy, Lavicogne, Bonneville, Remaisnil, Sibiville, Houvelin, Bruay, Barlin and Hersin, reaching Bully Grenay on 17 October. On 23 October it relieves the 20th Canadian Battalion in the front line in the Maroc Sector, holding the line until relieved in turn by the 20th Battalion on 29 October, then back in Brigade Reserve at Bully for 30–31 October.
- Officers named: Lieut.-Col. H. L. Milligan (proceeded on leave 6 October); Maj. G. F. Morrison (assumed command of the battalion 6 October, and signs the diary as commanding officer); Maj. L. E. Jones (wounded 4 October, returned from hospital 22 October); Maj. J. A. McIntosh, Adjutant (wounded 4 October, proceeded on leave 18 October); Maj. C. O. Fairbank (ordered to report to G.O.C. C.T.D. Shorncliffe, 9 October); Lieut. L. A. Bissell (joined 3 October, wounded 4 October); Lieut. R. E. Cook (wounded 3 October); Lieut. H. W. McBride (joined 4 October, admitted to hospital 20 October); Lieut. M. Dunsford (joined 8 October); Lieut. W. J. Baxter (admitted to hospital 8 October, returned 22 October; signs Appendix II as Adjutant); Lieut. W. K. Fraser (admitted to hospital 11 October; also awarded the Military Cross, Appendix III); Lieuts. V. M. Eastwood, J. Mc. Watt and P. G. Might (joined 20 October); Lieuts. S. C. Kirkland and W. G. Worth (joined 22 October); Sgt. W. J. Morris (appointed Lieutenant in the 17th Canadian Battalion, 8 October).
- Decorations: on 7 October decorations awarded by the General Officer Commanding-in-Chief were announced, and on 15 October a parade was held by the G.O.C. 2nd Canadian Division to present decorations for bravery in the field. Appendix III (Extract D.R.O. 2864) lists them: D.S.O. — Lieut. J. J. Richardson; Military Cross — Lieuts. R. G. Elliott, E. R. V. Lloyd, W. K. Fraser, W. S. McClinton and H. H. Sykes (wounded); D.C.M. — W/Sgt. C. E. Routley, Pte. R. H. Burgess and Pte. J. Nelson; Military Medal — 13 men; and a Bar to the Military Medal for Pte. R. C. Sheridan.
- Cover/contents disagreements: the contents page lists Appendix II as "A. & B. Cos. to supply 1 Lewis Gun each", but the appendix itself (page 9) says "'A' and 'C' coys will supply one Lewis Gun each". Also, Appendix I (Operation Order, 22 October) says the 18th Battalion "will be relieved by 19th Canadian Bn at 12 noon" on the 23rd, while the diary entry for 23 October says the battalion "relieved 20th Canadian Battalion in front line" — the order goes on to give the distribution for taking over the front line from the 20th Battalion, so the 19th Battalion relief appears to concern the battalion's position in reserve/support rather than the front line.
- Reinforcements: 66 other ranks on 3 October, 128 on 10 October, 38 on 17 October, 92 on 22 October and 10 on 26 October, plus the officers named above.
- No date gaps: entries run 1–31 October without a break.
