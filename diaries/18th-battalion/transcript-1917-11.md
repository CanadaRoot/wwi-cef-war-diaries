## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D. D. & L. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- " " (ditto marks) in the Place column repeat the place above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Cdn" = Canadian, "Div" = Division, "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "O.R." = other ranks, "R.A.P." = Regimental Aid Post, "P.B." = permanently base [uncertain — the abbreviation is as written], "P.T. & B.F." = Physical Training and Bayonet Fighting, "R.C's" = Roman Catholics, "Y.M.C.A." = Young Men's Christian Association.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished mark reading "C.P.L." [uncertain] on every row and is not transcribed row by row.
- Place names as written: CAESTRE, YPRES, POTIJZE, Abraham Heights, BOATHOUSE, Hillside Farm, Tyne Copse, Toronto Camp, Brandhoek, Ouderdom, Robecq, Renninghelst, LA CLYTTE, Bailleul, Hazebrouck, St Venant, Auchel, Lillers, Villers Au Bois, Villers Camp, Cellars Camp, Neuville St Vast, Vancouver Rd.
- Many appendix sheets are filmed front and back; the reverses are mostly show-through of the text on the front, with "Paymaster" handwritten on them, and are described rather than transcribed.

### 1 November 1917 — CAESTRE

> Battalion in Reserve Camp. Coy parades, inspections of arms and equipment. Extra water bottles (1 per man) issued. 2 O.R. admitted to hospital.

### 2 November 1917 — CAESTRE, 10.30 a.m.

> Battalion entrained at CAESTRE and detrained at YPRES about 3 p.m. from there marching to POTIJZE where supper was served on the roadside. Transport having left at 6 A.M. came by road. (See O.O. D.164.)
>
> After supper the Battalion moved into support at Abraham Heights, relieving the 75th Battalion whose guides met the Bn. at the junction of Jill and K. tracks (D.21.b.45.90) these tracks being trench mat walks. Relief completed at 11.40 p.m. Men went in funk holes & shelters around their respective Coy H.Q. viz. A Coy H.Q. D.15.b.9.5. B Coy. D.15.c.6.5. C Coy rear right. D.15.b.3.5 and D Coy. D.15.b.8.6. Battalion Hdqrs being in a Pillbox, BOATHOUSE D.15.a.3.3. and R.A.P. D.15.a.3.3.

## Page 7 — Diary text, 6 November (continued)–9 November 1917

[Form C. 2118. "WAR DIARY 18th Canadian Battalion INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. (Erase heading not required.)"]

### 6 November 1917 (continued) — POTIJZE

> The weather conditions making these fatigues very hard, mud being waist deep in places. 1 O.R. Killed in Action. 6 O.R. wounded.

### 7 November 1917 — POTIJZE, from 8.30 a.m. to 11.45 a.m.

> 4 Officers and 180 O.R. of A & B Coys dug 150 yds of ditch on both sides of road running from D.14.d.4.3 to D.14.d.6.1. ditches being 6' wide and 3' deep.

### 8 November 1917 — Relief

> During night 8th/9th. 18th Battalion took over front line between D.12.b.45.90. and D.6.b.65.38. relieving portions of the 22nd Canadian Bn and 25th Canadian Bn. For disposition and location see Operation Order #167 (attached). Capt W.J. GANDER and Lieut. H B JOHNSON, M.C. being wounded. Lieut. W S COLDWELL admitted to hospital Gassed.

### 9 November 1917

> Position as shown on Operation Order #167. Owing to bad weather and the continual shelling by the enemy, the front line and supports were in poor condition, the mud & water in many places being waist deep.

## Page 8 — Diary text, 9–12 November 1917

[Form C. 2118. "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. 18th Canadian Battalion" — the heading is crossed out and re-written. Place column: "D.12.b.45.90 to D.6.b.65.38".]

### 9, 10, 11 and 12 November 1917

> During the whole of this tour, the officers and men held this part of the line under the most severe conditions possible. Great difficulty was experienced in the evacuating of casualties from front line to R.A.Ps and dressing stations. Front line trenches were subjected to frequent barrages and the rear country was also heavily shelled and bombed. The supports on this front were reached by a series of tracks, being trench mat walks, and rations had to be carried by mules up these tracks, each track being subjected to continual shellfire, the transport and ration parties were fortunate in escaping with the loss of 3 men killed and 1 mule which fell off the duckboard track and owing to the depth of the mud, had to be shot. ### 13 November 1917 — MOVE, 11.30 a.m.

> Battalion entrained at YPRES and detrained at BRANDHOEK siding about 12 noon, from there marching to TORONTO CAMP. Transport moving by road at 10 a.m.

### 14 November 1917 — Toronto Camp

> Battalion in huts at Toronto Camp. Muster parade held during morning. Battalion resting and cleaning up during afternoon. 8 O.R. admitted to hospital.

### 15 November 1917 — 7 a.m. and 10 a.m.

> Battalion marched to embarking point near Ouderdom and proceeded by bus to Robecq via, Ouderdom, Renninghelst, LA CLYTTE, Bailleul, Hazebrouck St Venant arriving at Robecq about 3 p.m. Battalion billeted in buildings on outskirts of village

### 16 November 1917 — 9.30

> Battalion moved from Robecq to Auchel via Lillers in motor lorries arriving at destination about noon.

### 17 November 1917 — 10 a.m.

> Battalion again proceeded by motor lorries to Villers Au Bois arriving about 2.30 p.m. & being billeted in Villers Camp. 4 O.R. admitted to hospital.

>

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: the 8–12 November tour of the front line (Passchendaele sector) cost 45 other ranks killed in action, 6 officers and 60 other ranks wounded, and 1 officer and 25 other ranks gassed — the battalion's own summary on Page 9. Officers named as casualties: Capt. W. J. Gander, Capt. V. M. Eastwood M.C., Lieut J. N. Mowbray, Lieut E. C. [uncertain], Lieut J. F. Perkins, Lieut W. E. Mitchell, and Lieut W. S. Caldwell (gassed). Earlier in the month, 1 other rank was killed and 2 wounded on 3 November (evacuation work), 7 other ranks wounded on 4 November, and 1 other rank killed and 6 wounded on 6 November (fatigue parties); the 9–12 November tour also lost 3 more men killed on ration/mule tracks. No other battle casualties are recorded; the rest of the month's losses are sickness and hospital admissions.
- Movements: 1 November in reserve camp at Caestre; 2 November by train to Ypres, marching to Potijze, then into support at Abraham Heights (Passchendaele); 5 November back to reserve camp at Potijze on the Ypres–Menin Road; 8–9 November into the front line between D.12.b.45.90 and D.6.b.65.38, relieving the 22nd and 25th Battalions; relieved by the 87th Battalion on the night of 12/13 November; 13 November by train to Brandhoek siding and Toronto Camp; 15–17 November by bus and motor lorry south through Robecq and Auchel to Villers-au-Bois; 28 November by light railway to Cellars Camp, Neuville St Vast; 29 November relieved the 21st Battalion in support at Vancouver Road, where the month ends.
- Officers named: Lt.-Col. L. E. Jones [uncertain] (signed the diary as commanding officer); Capt. J. E. Bell (Adjutant, signed most appendices); Capt. W. J. McNeill (evacuation party, Order No. D.164); Major Mackintosh (5th Brigade, Order No. 166); Capt. W. J. Gander and Lieut. H. B. Johnson M.C. (wounded 8 November); Lieut. W. S. Caldwell (gassed); Lieut. J. N. MacRae and Lieut. E. L. Addy (reinforcements, 20 November); Lieuts C. J. Jackson, T. V. Millford, M. R. Sloan and W. H. J. Kreitzer (reinforcements, 24 November); Lieut. C. E. Oglesby (rejoined from hospital, 25 November); Lieuts R. E. Lawrance and L. E. Boulton (reinforcements, 27 November); Major T. J. Richardson D.S.O. (rejoined from England, 30 November); Sgt Wilson [uncertain] (Lewis Gunners, 20 November syllabus).
- Notable: the front-line tour of 9–12 November was held in waist-deep mud and water under frequent barrages, with rations carried by mules along duckboard tracks — one mule fell off a track and had to be shot in the mud. On 3 November the battalion sent platoons to help evacuate wounded from the front line at Hillside Farm and Tyne Copse. Enemy aircraft bombed the rear area on the night of 4 November. Order No. 168 (9 November) concerns a 20th Battalion attack north of the 18th's line, with the 18th holding its line and "B" Company at the 20th's disposal; it also describes contact-aircraft markings. The battalion received 108 other-rank reinforcements during its rest at Villers-au-Bois (56 on 20 November, 22 on 21 November, 30 on 24 November) plus 26 men rejoined from base hospitals and 18 rejoined casualties.
