## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The printed heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through and "18th Canadian Battalion" written in by hand. The form footer reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/16. Sir J. C. & S."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Bgde" = Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "O.R." = other ranks, "or" = other ranks, "D.R.O." = Divisional Routine Order, "M.O." = Medical Officer, "Hun" = the enemy (as written).
- The Remarks column carries a repeated initialled mark on every row, reading "C.P.L." [uncertain], and is not transcribed row by row.
- Place names as written: Thelus, Mont St Eloy, Roclincourt, Zivy Cave, Bois-des-Alleux, Estré-Cauchee.

## Page 5 — Diary text, 12 March (continued) – 13 March 1917

[Form C. 2118. The page is numbered "4" in the Remarks column.]

### 12 March 1917 (continued)

> busy cleaning and repairing his trenches which appear to be in bad condition. 5 or returned from hospital.

### 13 March 1917 — (no place given)

> Position as yesterday. 4.15 am Enemy attempted a raid on our outpost on the right flank of frontage due North of Vises group of craters. At 4 am red & green flares fired from German front line appeared to be the signal for the heavy bombardment which followed, directed to our front and support lines. Our outpost consisting of one bomber and 2 Lewis gunners [uncertain] were in the act of withdrawing to front line. The raiders approached in two parties, about 30 men in all. Two bombers Pte R. Wackett and W. H. Webster, who were covering the withdrawal of outpost No 16, opened fire on the Huns and R. Wackett claims three. One Hun managed to creep up to one of the bombers and said in English "Come with me" Pte Webster managed to break away and continued to throw bombs with good effect. One Hun was afterwards found by Lieut Tuck who secured identification. Our casualties were 3 Killed and 14 wounded by enemy shell fire.

## Page 6 — Diary text, 14–19 March 1917

[Form C. 2118. The page is numbered "5" in the Remarks column.]

### 14 March 1917 — (no place given)

> Position same, everything quiet. Patrols covered Battalion frontage. Sgt W. R. [uncertain] granted a commission in the 18th Battalion. 4 or sick admitted to hospital.

### 15 March 1917 — do

> Our artillery active doing considerable damage to enemy wire. Enemy very nervous at night, using many flares. Lt Col G F Morrison, D.S.O and Major T. A. McIntosh returned from leave. 6 or admitted to hospital.

### 16 March 1917 — do

> Considerable activity in air and several battles took place. Pte Johnson shot one of a party of Huns who on seeing their comrade fall, [uncertain] trench. Hun still remains where he fell. Nothing unusual to report.

### 17 March 1917 — do

> 18th Battalion relieved in front line by 19th Canadian Bn. 18th Battalion moved into Brigade reserve at Bois-des-Alleux. Completion of relief notified by Code words, A & B coys "Balmy". C & D coys "Dizzy". 8 or returned from hospital.

### 18 March 1917 — Bgde Reserve

> 30 [uncertain] arrived as reinforcements. Company parades and inspections. 2 or admitted to hospital.

### 19 March 1917 — do

> Position same. Lieut. P. G. Might received more orders to the Base. Battalion pay parade. 2 or admitted to hospital. 2 or returned from hospital.

## Page 7 — Diary text, 20–23 March 1917

[Form C. 2118. The page is numbered "6" in the Remarks column.]

### 20 March 1917 — Brigade Reserve

> Lt Cols. J. E. de Hartel, W. C. Green and Major McCowley returned to England to rejoin their units. Lieut. C. E. Tuck admitted to hospital. Routine.

### 21 March 1917 — Map Ref Roclincourt 1/1000

> 18th Canadian Bn. relieved 19th Battalion in front line at C2. (left subsector) 3 companies in front line and 1 company in support at ZIVY Cave. The completion of relief notified to Battalion H.Q. by code word "Evie". 7 or to hospital sick.

### 22 March 1917 — Map Roclincourt E 5th Sheet 51 [uncertain]

> Battalion in front line, position as stated yesterday. Our outposts were placed at A9 C 67-56 — A 10 92-57. Sniper de Manby fired one shot at enemy sniping plate one shot through loophole, silencing sniper. Enemy appears very nervous. 1 or returned from hospital.

### 23 March 1917 — do

> Position as yesterday. During night of 23/24th "A" we raided the enemy trenches situated at A.10. b 4.1 to A10. D 4.6. The object being to destroy known dugouts and trench mortar emplacements, this we accomplished and caused casualties among the enemy who held his position strongly manned. Our casualties were, Lieut J. R. Parke and 3 or wounded and missing, 7 or wounded.

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: Major G. V. Nelson, D Company commander, killed by shell during the relief of 5 March (funeral at Mont St Eloy 7 March); 2 other ranks wounded 5 March, later reported died of wounds; 3 other ranks killed and 14 wounded by enemy shell fire in the German raid of 13 March; Lieut J. R. Parke and 3 other ranks wounded and missing and 7 other ranks wounded in the battalion's own raid of 23/24 March; 1 man slightly wounded when the billets area was shelled 25 March; 2 other ranks reported wounded 1 March. Snipers claim 7 Germans killed 4 March. No other battle casualties are recorded; the rest of the month's losses are sickness and hospital admissions.
- Movements: the battalion is in Brigade Support in the Thelus sector, then the front line (relieving the 20th Battalion 3 March), relieved by the 8th/16th Battalions 5/6 March to Divisional Reserve at Mont St Eloy; back to the front line (C2 left subsector, relieving the 19th Battalion) about 10/11 March; relieved by the 19th Battalion 17 March to Brigade Reserve at Bois-des-Alleux; back to the front line at C2 on 21 March; relieved by the 31st Battalion 24 March to billets near Bois des Alleux; marches to Estré-Cauchee 30 March for special training over a replica of the front to be taken.
- Officers named: Lieut Col G. F. Morrison (on leave 2 March, returned 15 March); Capt H. H. McCrimmon (assumed command 2 March); Capt K. H. McCrimmon (authorized to wear the badges of rank of Major 2 March; to be Adjutant and promoted Major, D.R.O. 3588, 30 March); Lieut P. G. Might (to hospital sick 2 March; ordered to the Base 19 March); Lieut Cols. J. E. de Hartel and W. J. Green (attached for instruction 12 March; returned to England 20 March); Major McCowley (returned to England 20 March); Lieut C. E. Tuck (admitted to hospital 20 March; found a dead German and secured identification after the 13 March raid); Lieut R. D. McKay (rejoined from the Base 27 March); Capt. H. R. MacIntyre, Medical Officer (returned from leave 24 March); Lieut J. R. Parke (wounded and missing in the 23/24 March raid).
- Notable: on 13 March about 30 Germans raided the battalion's outpost north of the Vises group of craters at 4.15 am, preceded by red and green flares and a heavy bombardment; Ptes R. Wackett and W. H. Webster covered the outpost's withdrawal with bombs — one German crept up and said in English "Come with me" before Webster broke away — and Wackett claimed three Germans. Sharples, Walker and Webster were awarded the Military Medal for this action. On the night of 23/24 March the battalion raided the enemy trenches at A.10 b 4.1 to A.10 D 4.6 to destroy dugouts and trench-mortar emplacements. The battalion spent 8–10 March on fatigue work moving artillery ammunition "in enormous quantities". From 30 March it trained over a replica of the front to be taken at Estré-Cauchee.
- Date anomaly: there is no separate 11 March entry — the diary has a combined 9th/10th entry, then an entry dated "10th" (the relief of the 19th Battalion at C2), then 12 March. The relief entry is transcribed as dated but the sequence suggests it is the 11 March entry.
- Cover/contents disagreement: the cover is marked "With Appendices" but the contents page lists only "Diary Text 7 Pages" and no appendix pages are present in this month's set (9 pages total: cover, contents, 7 diary pages).
