## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D. D. & L. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118."
- The diary text for 1–23 January is typewritten; the text for 24–31 January is handwritten.
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Divnl" = Divisional, "Bgde" = Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "O.R." = other ranks, "B.R.O." = Brigade Routine Order.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished initial on every row that is not legible — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row.
- Place names as written: La Clytte, Vierstraat, Ridgewood, Westoutre, Mt. Kokerele, Spanbroekmolen, Farm Parrain.

## Page 3 — Diary text, 1–13 January 1916

> Battalion in LA CLYTTE in Divisional Reserve — "B" and D Companies were paid other Companies conducted training in specialists. Officers met together for dinners for the first time since coming over.

> La CLYTTE — Company training and inspection of feet and equipment — 2 other ranks sent to hospital

## Page 7 — Appendix I, Orders by Lt. Col. E. S. Wigle

[Typed. Handwritten "Appendix 1" at top right, and "11" at the foot. The date reads "January 25, 1916", with the "25" inserted over a correction.]

> ORDERS.
>
> by
>
> Lt. Col. E. S. Wigle.
>
> Comdg. 18th Canadian Battalion.
>
> January 25, 1916.
>
> Ref: 2nd Division Map.
>
> SHOWING SPANBROEKMOLEN;
>
> 1" -- 50 yards.
>
> I.
>
> The enemy are holding line of trenches on the frontage from SPANBROEKMOLEN to point 700 yards South, with about 1 Brigade of Infantry supported by one Bde. of Artillery.
>
> The 4th Bde. is attacking the position at 11.00 a.m. the 26th instant. The attack will be preceded by Heavy Bombardment and wire cutting which will lift at 11.00 a.m to enemy's 3rd line.
>
> The 18th Battalion on the right of the Brigade is to attack on a one company frontage and two companies in depth. The 5th Bde is on our right and the 20th Battalion on our left. The frontage will be as indicated by red line on the attached sketch — about 150 yards.
>
> II.
>
> 'A' Company will lead the attack at 11.00 a.m. with enemy 2nd line as objective and, upon reaching their objective, they will fortify the position and gain control of communicating trenches leading to enemy 3rd line.
>
> 'C' Company will follow 'A' in the attack with their objective the enemy 3rd line. In the event of 'A' Company failing to reach its objective, but reaching enemy 1st line, 'C' Company's 1st objective will then be enemy 2nd line.
>
> Each attacking Company will have at least 6 Artillery Flags and the Artillery will lift as these flags go forward.
>
> Lateral communication with flank units to be strictly maintained.
>
> 'B' and 'D' Companies will remain in Bn. Reserve at H.28.d.5.0. under orders of Battalion Commander.
>
> III.
>
> Two Machine Guns with 'A' Co. and two with 'C' Company. These will be under orders of Company Commander.

> IV.
>
> Signalling Officer will establish communication with Battle H. Q. as the attack proceeds.

>
> Bn. Grenade Platoon will be under orders of the Bn. Gde. Officer who will act in conjunction with the Co. Commanders.

> MARCH ORDERS.
>
> 18th Canadian Inf. Bn.
>
> January 25, 1916.
>
> Ref: HAZEBROUCK Sheet 1/100,000.
>
> 1. The Battalion will march to-morrow, the 26th instant, leaving Parade Ground just in rear of Battalion Headquarters at 8.45 a.m. to Farm just North of the last 'E' in MT. KOKERELE, via WESTOUTRE.
>
> 2. Dress: Marching Order without packs; Capes en Banderole.
>
> 3. Lunch in Haversacks.
>
> 4. Each Company will take 12 shovels with them.
>
> 5. Transport will remain at Transport Lines.
>
> 6. The Machine Gun Section may march in advance of the Battalion, arranging for Transport for Guns; also for Bombs and one Bale of Sandbags.
>
> 7. Each man will carry two sandbags, if available.

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: Major Sale badly hit by an enemy rifle grenade about 9.30 p.m. on 17 January and died about midnight; he was buried on 19 January with the G.O.C. Army Corps and the G.O.C. 4th Brigade attending. 2 men killed in action on 21 January (names not given). Pte Henson killed and Pte McMenemy [uncertain] wounded on 29 January; [uncertain] Innes wounded on 30 January; Pte Atherton wounded on fatigue on 31 January; Scouts Brodh... [uncertain] and Reeves reported missing on 31 January. The rest of the month's losses are sickness and hospital admissions, plus 2 other ranks taken off strength as munition workers (18 January) and 5 other ranks struck off strength under Part II Orders #2 (12 January).
- Movements: the battalion rotates between Divisional Reserve at La Clytte (1–2 and 21–26 January), the trenches at Vierstraat (3–8, 15–20 and 27–31 January), and Brigade Reserve at Ridgewood (9–14 January), relieved in turn by the 19th Battalion. On 25 January it marched to Westoutre for a tactical scheme.
- Officers named: Lt. Col. E. S. Wigle, commanding (Appendix I); Major Sale (killed 17 January); Major Emmerton (admitted to hospital 11 January, returned 26 January); Major Milligan (went on leave 17 January, returned from leave 26 January); Major Nelson (left for leave 30 January); Lt. Ambery [uncertain] (promoted to Captain, to date 18 October 1915); Pte Tepper (special leave, 3 January).
- Notable: Appendix I orders an attack on the enemy trenches at Spanbroekmolen at 11 a.m. on 26 January, with 'A' Company leading and 'C' Company following, and Appendix II gives march orders for the 26th to Mt. Kokerele via Westoutre — but the diary entries for 25–26 January record only a tactical scheme at Westoutre on the 25th and the battalion in Divisional Reserve on the 26th, with no attack recorded. The diary text changes from typewritten (1–23 January) to handwritten (24–31 January), and the handwritten entries for 24–26 January are partly illegible.
- Contents-page disagreements: the contents page gives the division as "1st Cdn. Division" (the cover says "2nd Canadian Division") and spells the commanding officer's name "E.S. Wigley", while Appendix I spells it "E. S. Wigle"; it also dates the Appendix II march to "the 16th", while Appendix II itself is dated 25 January and orders the march for the 26th.
- No date gaps: entries run 1–31 January without a break.
