## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "o.rs" = other ranks, "Bn" = Battalion, "Bde" = Brigade, "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles, "C.R.E." = Commander Royal Engineers, "R.Es" = Royal Engineers, "M.G.Coy" = Machine Gun Company, "N.C.O." = non-commissioned officer, "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding, "R.S.M." = Regimental Sergeant-Major, "D.S.O." = Distinguished Service Order, "M.C." = Military Cross, "D.C.M." = Distinguished Conduct Medal, "m.n." = midnight.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished initial on every row that appears to read "C.H." — it is not transcribed row by row.
- Place names as written: Villers au Bois, Villers Camp, Vimy, Avion, La Targette, La Folie Farm, Bois de Chauliere, Goupigny, Mericourt, Sallaumines, Noyelles sous Lens.

## Page 4 — Diary text, 1–11 September 1917

[Typed. "ORIGINAL" and "CONFIDENTIAL" at top left; "WAR DIARY — 18th (WESTERN ONTARIO) CANADIAN BATTALION. INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY — SEPTEMBER 1917." Army Form C. 2118. "Instructions regarding War Diaries and Intelligence Summaries are contained in F. S. Regs., Part II. and the Staff Manual respectively. Title Pages will be prepared in manuscript. (Erase heading not required.)" Place column reads "VILLERS AU BOIS" throughout.]

### 1 September 1917 — Villers au Bois

> Battalion in billets at VILLERS AU BOIS (village area). Company parades and inspections. Platoon and specialist training carried out during the morning. Afternoon, recreational training such as Football, Baseball etc. 2 o.rs admitted to hospital.

### 2 September 1917 — Villers au Bois, 7.45 a.m. and 9.00

> 7.45 a.m. Communion service. 9.00 Battalion parade for Divine Service. 6 o.rs to hospital (sick).

### 3 September 1917 — Villers au Bois, 9 a.m. and 11.00

> Specialist training of Bombers, Grenadiers, Scouts and Bayonet fighting. 11.00 Battalion parade, the afternoon being devoted to recreational exercise. The Battalion Football team played a drawn game with the 20th, Bn in the Championship series.

### 4 September 1917 — Villers au Bois

> Training as yesterday. 10 casuals rejoined Bn from base hospitals.

### 5 September 1917 — Villers au Bois

> Battalion Pay parades. Lieut. C.E.Oglesby arrived as reinforcement.

### 6 September 1917 — Villers au Bois

> Company parades and inspection of Rifles and Equipment. Lieut. C.E.Oglesby and 85 o.rs attached to R.Es as carrying party. 4 o.rs to hospital.

### 7 September 1917 — Villers au Bois, 7.00 p.m.

> Training of all Lewis gunners at Ranges. Company parades and training. 7.00 p.m. Military Tattoo arranged by 4th, Brigade. Massed bands and Pipers being the chief item. Program attached

[The words "Program attached" are handwritten in the Summary column.]

### 8 September 1917 — Villers au Bois

> Battalion sports day. Football, Baseball, Running and Jumping contests, winners to represent Battalion in Divisional sports. 2 o.rs to hospital.

### 9 September 1917 — Villers au Bois

> Brigade parade for Divine Service conducted by the Army Chaplain. General, Sir, H.S.Horne, K.C.B. 1st, Army commander attended. Lieuts. J.Irons and J.McHardy arrived as reinforcements. Battalion Football team defeated the 20thBn in the Brigade final.

### 10 September 1917 — Villers au Bois

> Company parades and training in Bayonet fighting, platoon in attack and physical training. Afternoon, recreational training.

### 11 September 1917 — Villers au Bois

> Parades and training as yesterday. 10 o.rs returned from hospital.

## Page 5 — Diary text, 12–17 September 1917

[Typed. Same form heading. Place column reads "Villers Camp" for 12th–13th, then "Maps for reference. VIMY and ROUVROY. 1/10,000." for 14th, and "Night 17/18th" for the overnight entry.]

### 12 September 1917 — Villers Camp, 9.30 a.m.

> Brigade sports day. 18thBattalion winning both Football and Baseball finals, entitling them to compete in Divisional finals.

### 13 September 1917 — Villers Camp

> Company parades in fighting order, training in bayonet fighting and Physical exercise. Inspection of Box respirators. Capt. J.J.Bell returned from leave. 10 o.rs attached to Divisional M.G.Coy as carrying party.

### 14 September 1917 — Villers Camp / Avion, 1.00 p.m. and 7.30 p.m.

[Handwritten at the head of the entry: "Maps for reference. VIMY and ROUVROY. 1/10,000. See map attached marked blue."]

> 1.00 p.m. Battalion moved off from VILLERS CAMP on route to trenches in front of AVION. A halt was made at LA TARGETTE corner where supper was served and Battalion rested preparatory to passing over the top of VIMY ridge in the following order "A", "B", "C", "D" and H.Q, guides were picked at LA FOLIE FARM about 8.30 p.m. from the 1st C.M.R. regt, whom we were relieving.
>
> 7.30 p.m. Moving off from the bivouac in the following order "A", "B", "C", "D" and H.Q, guides were picked at LA FOLIE FARM about 8.30 p.m. from the 1st C.M.R. regt, whom we were relieving. The enemy artillery was extremely quiet and the relief was completed without casualty at 11.45 p.m. Disposition being "A" coy in front line, "B" and "C" coys in close support, and "D" coy in reserve. Battalion H.Q being in gun pits and dugouts in the BOIS DE CHAULIERE which was a former German big gun position at E.7.a.95.80 [uncertain — grid reference partly legible].

[Transcriber's note: the 14 September entry is overtyped in places, and the phrase about the order of march and the guides at La Folie Farm appears twice, once under each hour.]

### 15 September 1917

> During the day the enemy shelled the rear area but was very quiet on the trenches occupied by us. During the night large working parties were putting out wire entanglements in front of our line, every available man of "D" coy was engaged in carrying up wire and stakes. [uncertain] patrols from the Scout section patrolled "No Mans Land" while this work was being carried out.

### 16 September 1917

> Nothing unusual occurred. Working parties under the supervision of the C.R.E constructed Dugouts in DORIS trench and worked on DARMOUTH trench.

### 17 September 1917

> Preparations were made for taking over a company frontage on our left flank, running through the south part of AVION, and at present occupied by the 78th Canadian Bn. "B" coy was selected for this operation. During the day Capt. Reyward [uncertain], O.C. "B" coy, Lt. McHardy and 5 N.C.Os reconnoitred the new area.

### Night 17/18 September 1917

> The relief of the Company on our left was successfully completed and "B" coy moved into the new position vacated by "B" coy.
>
> [Handwritten:] See map attached position marked in green

## Page 6 — Diary text, 18–28 September 1917

[Typed. Same form heading. No place is entered in the Place column on this page.]

### 18 September 1917

> Nothing unusual to report. During the evening the usual working parties were provided, and repaired BILLIE BURKE (front line) trench, also dugouts in GLADYS trench, and extending [uncertain] to [uncertain], a distance of 250 yds of trench being dug.

### 19 September 1917

> Unusual Aerial activity on both sides. During the night a working party of 1 Officer and 32 o.rs put out 600 yds of wire in front of TORONTO ROAD from 100 yds right of Betty Trench to a point 600 yds left, thence over and along SOURIS ROAD. Parties of 40 o.rs were repairing dugouts in BETTY TRENCH and building a Dressing station in GLADYS TRENCH.

### 20 September 1917

> Battalion relieved in front line by the 26th Canadian Bn. This relief was completed by 12 m.n. and 18th Battalion moved into billets at VILLERS CAMP.

### 21 September 1917

> Battalion resting and settling down in billets. Lieut C.D.Smith arrived as reinforcement.

### 22 September 1917

> Bath and clothing parades. 2 o.rs to hospital (sick).

### 23 September 1917

> "A" "B" and "D" coys proceeded to Training area where taped replica of German frontage had been laid out. 2 o.rs admitted to hospital.

### 24 September 1917

> Battalion Pay parades all day. 4 o.rs returned from hospital.

### 25 September 1917

> Company parades and inspections. Physical training and Bayonet fighting. Lieut. C.E.Oglesby and 3 o.rs admitted to hospital.

### 26 September 1917

> Semi-finals of Canadian Corps Baseball and Football championships were played at GOUPIGNY, 18th Battalion Baseball team being defeated by the 1st, D. [uncertain]. No parades being held to-day.

### 27 September 1917

> Company parades. Physical training and platoon drill. 2 o.rs admitted to hospital.

### 28 September 1917

> Training as yesterday. Lieut T.L.Hankinson arrived as reinforcement.

## Page 7 — Diary text, 29–30 September 1917, with Honors and Awards

### 29 September 1917 — Villers Camp

> Canadian Corps Sports day. Sports held at VILLERS AU BOIS. Final events for Corps Championships being decided at this meet. Pte. D.Laird, 18th, Battalion winning "High jump". and 2nd, Division winning Football. 3 o.rs admitted to hospital.

### 30 September 1917 — Villers Camp, 7.45 a.m. and 10 a.m.

> 7.45 a.m. Voluntary Communion service. 10 a.m. Battalion paraded for Divine service after which the men were dismissed for the day. 2 o.rs returned from hospital.

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: no battle casualties are recorded in September 1917. The relief in the Avion trenches on the night of 14/15 September was completed without casualty, and the battalion's losses for the month are hospital admissions and sickness only. No men are named as killed or wounded.
- Movements: 1–13 September the battalion is in billets at Villers au Bois / Villers Camp, training and taking part in sports and a military tattoo. On 14 September it marches to the trenches in front of Avion, relieving the 1st C.M.R. Battalion; Battalion Headquarters is in gun pits and dugouts in the Bois de Chaulière, a former German big-gun position. On the night of 17/18 September "B" Company takes over a company frontage on the left flank in the south part of Avion from the 78th Canadian Battalion. On 20 September the battalion is relieved in the front line by the 26th Canadian Battalion and returns to billets at Villers Camp for the rest of the month.
- Officers named: Lt-Col. L. E. Jones (Commanding Officer; D.S.O. notification received during the month); Capt. J. J. Bell (returned from leave 13 September; M.C. notification); Lieut. C. E. Oglesby (arrived as reinforcement 5 September, attached to the Royal Engineers as a carrying party 6 September, admitted to hospital 25 September); Lieuts. J. Irons and J. McHardy (arrived as reinforcements 9 September); Capt. Rayward [uncertain spelling — Appendix II gives "Capt. T.H.J. RAYWARD"], Officer Commanding "B" Company; Lieut. C. D. Smith (arrived as reinforcement 21 September); Lieut. T. L. Hankinson (arrived as reinforcement 28 September); Lieut. W. H. Lewis (Assistant Adjutant; signed the church-parade instructions, Appendix III); Capt. J. S. Bell, Lieut. W. H. Fenton and Lieut. H. B. Johnson (M.C. notifications).
- Honours: notifications received during the month — D.S.O. (Lt-Col. L. E. Jones), M.C. (Capt. J. S. Bell, Lieut. W. H. Fenton, Lieut. H. B. Johnson), 1 D.C.M., 1 Bar to D.C.M., 27 Military Medals.
- Notable: September 1917 is dominated by sport and ceremony rather than fighting. The 4th Brigade military tattoo is given on 7 September; the battalion wins both the football and baseball finals at the brigade sports day on 12 September (and the officers' tennis, per the handwritten note on the programme); the 18th's baseball team loses the Canadian Corps semi-final at Goupigny on 26 September; and at the Canadian Corps sports day on 29 September Pte. D. Laird wins the high jump and 2nd Division wins the football. The brigade Divine Service on 9 September is attended by General Sir H. S. Horne, commander of the First Army. In the line the battalion spends its time wiring (600 yards of wire in front of Toronto Road on 19 September), repairing trenches and dugouts (Billie Burke, Gladys and Betty trenches), and patrolling No Man's Land.
- Anomalies and disagreements: the contents page lists Appendix II as a "6th Cdn. Inf. Bde." field day, and the programme's own heading reads "6TH CANADIAN INFANTRY BRIGADE", but the programme body ("4TH CANADIAN INFANTRY BRIGADE SPORTS") and all its officials are 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade — the 18th Battalion's own brigade. The contents page spells the tattoo "Tatoo" where the programme spells it "TATTOO". The 14 September diary entry is overtyped, with the order of march and the guides at La Folie Farm appearing twice. The relief order on page 17 is very faint on the film. The disposition map (Appendix IV) is filmed twice, two copies of each of its two sheets, matching the diary's references to a blue-marked map (14 September) and a green-marked map (night 17/18 September).
- No date gaps: entries run 1–30 September without a break, with an additional entry for the night of 17/18 September.
