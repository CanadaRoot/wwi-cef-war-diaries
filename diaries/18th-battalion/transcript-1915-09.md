## Source

- Record: https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=1883227
- Page images for September 1915: `page-0026-September_1915_p._1.jpg` through `page-0034-September_1915_p._9.jpg`.

## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed faithfully from the LAC page images only; spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- This month contains two separate diaries: the battalion diary (pages 2–4) and the Medical Officer's diary (pages 5–9, headed "M.O. 18th Infantry Battalion", covering 14–30 September only). Both are handwritten on Army Form C. 2118, with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information. The printed heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through and "WAR DIARY" used instead.
- Ditto marks in the original are kept as "— do —".
- Where an entry continues across the page, it is kept under the date it belongs to.

## Notes on this month

- The battalion sailed for France on the night of 14/15 September: embarked at Folkestone, the transport was rammed by a British destroyer about 10.55 p.m., was towed to Boulogne, and arrived at 5.6 a.m. on 15 September. Five men were left in hospital at Boulogne. The battalion then moved by train to St Omer and marched via Renescure and Eecke to Dranoutre, taking over front-line trenches from the 19th Battalion on 28 September.
- First front-line casualties: Capt. Hallam killed 29 September while examining a trench damaged by enemy artillery; Lt. Col. [uncertain] killed 30 September in the support trenches; Pte W. W. [uncertain] (No 53227) killed 30 September by a stray bullet while placing sandbags; Pte H. J. [uncertain] (No 53476) shot through the abdomen on 28 September; a prisoner reported killed on a listening post on 29 September.
- People named: King George V; Major General Alderson; H.R.H. Prince Arthur of Connaught; Capt. G. C. Hale (Medical Officer); Capt. Hallam; Lt. Col. [uncertain].
- The battalion diary and the Medical Officer's diary agree on the sailing, the ramming, and the march to Dranoutre, but differ in detail: the battalion diary says the battalion marched from Eecke on 21 September and bivouacked, while the Medical Officer's diary records the march to Dranoutre via Bailleul the same day, with D Company sent to R.E. Farm as a working party.
