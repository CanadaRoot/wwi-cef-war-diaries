## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished signature on every row that is not legible — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Bde" = Brigade, "Bgde" = Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "O.R." = other ranks, "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade, "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding, "R.A.P." = Regimental Aid Post, "M.G." = Machine Gun, "C.B." = Canadian Base [as written].

## Page 3 — Diary text, 1–8 May 1917

[Typed. "Confidential" at top left, "WAR DIARY", "18th. (WESTERN ONTARIO) CANADIAN BATTALION.", "MAY 1917", Army Form C. 2118. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is struck through. Reference Map: WILLERVAL 1/20,000.]

### 1 May 1917

> Battalion in Reserve camp near NEUVILLE ST VAST. Battalion parade and company training. During the afternoon recreational exercise such as Football, Baseball etc. was the order. 3 o.r.s admitted to hospital sick.

### 2 May 1917 — 9.30 a.m.

> Inspection of Battalion by Bdr.-Gen. Rennie, G.O.C. 4th. Canadian Infantry Brigade. The Officers and men being highly complimented on their smartness and cleanliness.

### 2 May 1917 — 5 p.m.

> Battalion relieved the 29th. Bn. in Divisional reserve in PAYNESLEY TUNNEL. 40. o.rs arrived as reinforcements.

### 3rd, 4th, 5th May 1917

> Position as yesterday. During this tour much valuable work was done in improving defences, wiring, and deepening of trenches, also in the collection of salvage which was spread over the whole area. Each company established a dump and rivalled each other in the collection of S.S.A [uncertain] Bombs, and Equipment etc. 23, o.rs arrived as reinforcements. 4 o.rs admitted to hospital.

### Night 6/6th May 1917

> Battalion relieved the 24th. Bn in front line with H.-Qrs, MT FORET QUARRIES, at T.22.c.9.3. disposition of companies being "D" and "C" in front line, "A" in close support, and "B" in Bn reserve. Previous to this relief, Lt. Eastwood accompanied by 1 Officer from each company made a reconaisance in daylight over area to be taken over. This was made under very heavy shell-fire. Lieut. G.V. Irwin wounded. Capt. B.R.V. Lloyd and Lieut. J.P. Chrystal admitted to hospital (sick).

### 7 May 1917

> During the day enemy heavily shelled our front and support lines, 5 O.R's being Killed and 13 Wounded. During the night the enemy maintained a continuous bombardment of gas-shells on the Railway Line so that our ration parties were unable to get through until early in the morning and owing to daylight coming on the rations could not be taken past the Reserve Line.

### 8 May 1917

> Early in the morning enemy made a counter-attack against FRESNOY which had been taken by 1st Canadian Division on the 3rd instant. The Gloucester Regiment, the left Battalion of Division on our right, on whom the brunt of the attack fell were driven back to a line just east of ARLEUX, running through T.20.c. and T.20.a.7.4. [uncertain — the second reference is partly illegible] Hence our right Battalion, the 19th Canadians, were forced to withdraw their position.

## Page 4 — Diary text, 8th (continued)–12 May 1917

[Typed. "Page 2." at top, "WAR DIARY", "18th. (WESTERN ONTARIO) CANADIAN BATTALION.", Army Form C. 2118.]

### 8 May 1917 (continued)

> from the advanced position in front of FRESNOY at noon to a position running through T.24.c. and T.30.a., connecting with the Gloucesters at T.30.a.7.4. [uncertain — the reference is partly illegible] During this attack our three lines were subjected to heavy Artillery fire. During the morning the 4th C.I.B. had lost all touch with the 19th Battalion and asked us to send a Runner to 19th Headquarters in the ARLEUX LOOP for situation report. Cpl. Randall of the Battalion Scouts succeeded in reaching 19th Headquarters and returned with the necessary information which was at once forwarded to Brigade. To accomplish this task Cpl. Randall had to pass through a very heavily shelled area and for this was awarded the Military Medal. A second trip was made for the same purpose by Lieut. Eastwood.

### Night 8th/9th May 1917

> An Inter-Company relief was completed. "A" and "B" Companies in the front line. "C" in Supports and "D" in Reserve.

### 9 May 1917

> Before day-break Lieut. J. McAmmond, who was in Command of our right Platoon, under great difficulties established communication with the 19th Battalion, both through advance Posts in T.24.c. and along Winnipeg Road (T.23.d.). During a break in communication a Pigeon Message was sent to Brigade Headquarters. Pigeons were released and flew a distance of approximately 6 miles, to ECOIVRES, where they were trapped and message wired to Brigade, the whole proceeding occupying 25 minutes.

### 10 May 1917

> Nothing unusual occurred, the usual daily strafe of Quarries and barrages to front and support lines.

### Night 10th/11th May 1917

> Battalion relieved by the 21st Battalion and moved into Brigade Reserve at T.27.d.3.5. Two Companies in C.P.R. Trench at T.27. and two Companies in Vancouver Road at T.28.a.

### 11 May 1917

> During the whole of this tour considerable difficulty was experienced in the bringing up of rations owing to the enemy use of Poison Gas and Tear Shells. Daily at dusk enemy would indiscriminately deluge the whole area between FARBUS WOOD and VIMY RAILWAY with these shells, which made it necessary for Gas Respirators to be worn. Despite this Captain G.W.P. Hodgins never failed to deliver rations at Battalion H.Q. in Mt. FOREST QUARRIES. Lieut. A.A. McLean rendered valuable assistance by personally acting as guide from Railway, forward. 6 O.R.'s admitted to hospital (sick).

### 12 May 1917

> 3 O.R.'s "Wounded". 8 O.R.'s admitted to hospital (sick), 22 O.R.'s arrived as reinforcements.

## Page 6 — Diary text, Night 12th/13th–22 May 1917

[Typed. "Page 3." at top, "WAR DIARY", "18th. (WESTERN ONTARIO) CANADIAN BATTALION.", Army Form C. 2118.]

### Night 12th/13th May 1917

> During the night Brigade called for two parties to carry wire to the front line. Lieut's. C.E. Lucas and W.R. Wright being detailed to take charge of these parties. Lieut. C.E. Lucas completed his task with the loss of 4 O.R.'s "Wounded". Lieut. W.R. Wright and party on returning were heavily shelled in MT. FORET ROAD, scattering the party. Lieut. W.R. Wright and Sgt. T. Clark being "Killed" and 6 O.R.'s "Wounded".

### 13 May 1917

> Battalion was relieved by 24th Battalion and moved back into Reserve Camp near NEUVILLE-ST.-VAAST. Move completed successfully and without casualties.

### 14 May 1917

> Bath and Pay Parades. 3 O.R.'s admitted to hospital (sick).

### 15 May 1917

> Morning Company and Platoon Training. Afternoon Recreational Training.

### 16 May 1917

> A.M. Battalion Parade and Company and Platoon Drill. P.M. Recreational Training.

### 17 May 1917

> A.M. Physical Training. P.M. Recreational Training. 48 O.R.'s rejoined the Battalion from C.B. Fatigue.

### 18 May 1917

> Preparation for G.O.C.'s Inspection. 4 O.R.'s admitted to hospital (sick). 5 O.R.'s returned from hospital.

### 19 May 1917

> Inspection of 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade by General Sir Julian Byng, G.O.C. Canadian Corps. The G.O.C. expressed great surprise that, considering the heavy fighting and hardships passed through during the last month, it was possible for men to turn out so clean and in such fine condition. 10 O.R.'s of the 18th Battalion received decoration of Military Medal on this Parade. 5.00 p.m. The 18th Battalion relieved the 27th Battalion on the RIDGE (observation) LINE, with Battalion H.Q. at THELUS CAVE. For disposition see Map attached.

### 20th (21st) May 1917

> Battalion working on completion of RIDGE LINE trench. Nothing unusual occurred.

### 22 May 1917

> Received Order from Brigade to stop all work until further orders.

## Page 7 — Diary text, 23rd–31 May 1917

[Typed. "WAR DIARY", "18th. (WESTERN ONTARIO) CANADIAN BATTALION.", Army Form C. 2118.]

### 23rd, 24th, 25th, 26th May 1917

> Nothing unusual occurred.

### Night 26th/27th May 1917

> Battalion relieved 28th Battalion in left sub-sector, Battalion H.Q. in MT. FORET QUARRIES. Relief completed at 2.30 a.m. 27th.

### 27 May 1917

> Enemy Artillery active all day on our line.

### 28 May 1917

> 1.00 a.m. Enemy opened heavy barrage on Battalion H.Q. MT. FORET QUARRIES, our Support Line at T.23.c.6. and our Front Line. The barrage on the Front Line lifted at 1.15 a.m. then two raiding parties, each estimated at 25 or 30 men, one on each side of the MT. FORET – Acheville ROAD, were seen approaching our wire. Fire was opened by M.G.'s and rifles. Unable to get through the wire and checked by our fire the enemy retired. Of the party on the left frontage two of the enemy succeeded in getting through the wire, one of whom was severely wounded and taken, the other gave himself up and was immediately sent back to Brigade H.Q. In the evening a dead German was brought in. On the right frontage wiring was in progress and a N.C.O. and 6 men were well out in front of our wire, when the barrage opened the N.C.O., Cpl. L. Skilton gave orders for the party to retire. Immediately after the raiding party were seen and dispersed by our fire. As soon as all quietened down a search party went out but failed to find the three men of the protective patrol who were found not to have returned. Two rifles were found in a shell-hole but no other trace of them. The right raiding party came on the road and immediately to the right of it. The left party about 350 yards from the road. At daybreak Huns were seen carrying in casualties and stretchers were seen during the day on the ROUVROY ROAD also our observers report seeing 10 or 12 dead lying in NO-MAN'S-LAND today at T.18.c.6.5. Our casualties during the raid were 3 men Missing, 1 man Killed and 1 man Wounded.

### Night 29th/30th May 1917

> Battalion relieved by 21st Canadian Battalion and moved into Brigade Support with H.Q. at T.27.d.3.5.

### 30th, 31st May 1917

> Working parties during nights carrying wire to front line and work on Canada trench under supervision of Canadian Engineers.

## Page 9 — Battalion Order No. D.26, 2 May 1917

[Typed. "SECRET." and "Copy No. 9" at top right, "Wednesday 2.5.17."]

> 18TH (WESTERN ONTARIO) CANADIAN BATTALION ORDER NUMBER D.26.
>
> 1. A further attack on the German trenches will be carried out by the Canadian Corps within the next few days. All preparations for the attack will be completed by dawn May 3rd.
>
> 2. The general line of the objective for the Canadian Corps will run approximately as follows:—
>
> From T.17.d.5.5. – T.22.b.7.6. – U.19.a.0.4. to U.25 Central – C.1.b.6.5. (This point inclusive to 18th Corps.)
>
> 3. The 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade will be in Divisional Reserve and will move into Support Area on night of May 2nd/3rd.
>
> 4. In accordance with para. 3, the 18th (Western Ontario) Canadian Battalion will move into PAYNESLEY TUNNEL and vicinity, relieving the 29th Canadian Battalion. Companies taking over dispositions as existed on 22nd inst., when they were in this sector.
>
> 5. Companies will move off in the following order, commencing at 6.00 p.m.
>
> "A" Coy. "B" Coy. "C" Coy. "D" Coy.
>
> An interval of 100 yards will be maintained between Platoons.
>
> 6. Companies will detail advance parties composed of 1 Officer per Company and 1 N.C.O. per Platoon, who will take over and arrange details as to Billets. These Advance Parties will move off at 4.00 P.M. Lieut. Eastwood and Scout Section will move off at same time and take over for Battalion Headquarters.
>
> 7. Battalion will move into the line in Fighting Order, without greatcoats. Men's packs will be piled by Companies in vicinity of cook-kitchens by 4.00 p.m. Officer's baggage for Transport Lines must be ready for removal to Transport at 5.30 P.M.
>
> 8. Battalion Headquarters will be at PAYNESLEY TUNNEL.
>
> 9. Completion of relief will be notified by runner to Battalion H.Q.

## Page 17 — Report on enemy raid on No. 11 Platoon, night 27th–28th May 1917

[Handwritten.]

> Report on Enemy Raid on No. 11 Platoon
>
> 18th Batt. frontage.
>
> Night of May 27th – 28th 1917
>
> Time: 12.45 a.m.
>
> Received orders from C.S.M. Williams to post covering party of six men to protect a B. Co. wiring party on left flank. Sgt. F.C. - Casualties: 7 May — 5 other ranks killed, 13 wounded by enemy shelling of the front and support lines. Night 12th/13th — Lieut. W. R. Wright and Sgt. T. Clark killed and 6 other ranks wounded while carrying wire to the front line; a further 4 other ranks wounded under Lieut. C. E. Lucas the same night. 28 May raid — 3 men missing, 1 man killed, 1 man wounded (the three missing men were Cpl. L. Skilton's protective patrol; only two rifles were recovered). Lieut. G. V. Irwin wounded 6 May. No other battle casualties are recorded; the rest of the month's losses are sickness and hospital admissions.
- Movements: the battalion rotates from Reserve camp near Neuville St Vaast to Divisional Reserve in Paynesley Tunnel (2 May), to the front line at Mt Foret Quarries (night 6/7 May), to Brigade Reserve at T.27.d.3.5 (night 10/11 May), back to Reserve Camp near Neuville-St.-Vaast (13 May), to the Ridge Line with Battalion H.Q. at Thelus Cave (19 May), to the left sub-sector at Mt Foret Quarries (night 26/27 May), and into Brigade Support at T.27.d.3.5 (night 29/30 May).

- Notable: on 8 May the enemy counter-attacked Fresnoy, taken by the 1st Canadian Division on 3 May, driving the Gloucester Regiment back east of Arleux and forcing the 19th Canadians to withdraw; the 18th lost touch with the 19th Battalion and Cpl. Randall carried messages through heavy shelling. On 9 May communication with the 19th Battalion was re-established in part by carrier pigeon — the birds flew about 6 miles to Ecoivres and the message was wired to Brigade in 25 minutes. On 19 May General Sir Julian Byng inspected the brigade and 10 other ranks of the 18th Battalion received the Military Medal on parade. On the night of 27th/28th May the Germans raided the battalion's front at Mt Foret Quarries: two raiding parties of 25–30 men each were beaten off, one German was taken prisoner and one severely wounded, and three men of a protective patrol went missing.
- No date gaps: entries run 1–31 May without a break (23rd–26th are grouped as "Nothing unusual occurred").
