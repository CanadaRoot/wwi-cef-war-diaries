## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through on each diary page; "Original Confidential" is handwritten at the top left and "June 1916" at the top right. The form footer reads "2449 Wt. W14957/M950 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Bgde" = Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "O.R." = other ranks, "(accd)" = accidental, "M.O." = Medical Officer, "R.S.M." = Regimental Sergeant Major, "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding, "C-in-C" = Commander-in-Chief, "D.C.M." = Distinguished Conduct Medal, "D.S.O." = Distinguished Service Order.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished initial on every row that is not legible — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row.
- Place names as written: Bedford House, Norfolk Lodge, Strong Point 7, Trench 31R, Ecluse 8, Bluff Tunnel, Bern, Pollock, the Loop, Dickebusch, Vijverhoek, front line trenches 28–32.

### 6 June 1916

> Position as yesterday. Heavy evening bombardment of Bern and Pollock and reserve trenches from 6.15 5 p.m. [uncertain] Small party of Enemy penetrated right Junction of Bern and Pollock consequent upon the destruction of the M.G. emplacement at Bern [uncertain] Junction. Enemy were enabled to do so by means of an old communication trench but were driven out by remainder of the Garrison at the Bern under Cpl Routley. They evidently hoped to find trench unoccupied. Garrison of the Loop were called upon to "Stand to" by the sentry Pte Montgomery, and rapid fire was opened as the Boches retired. A wounded Boche was secured in front of Pollock. 10 O.R. Killed in action. 30 O.R. wounded. 24 O.R. arrived as reinforcements.

> 12 to 14 — WAR DIARY

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: 13 other ranks killed in action (1 on 3 June, 10 on 6 June, 2 on 23 June); 5 other ranks died of wounds (9, 10 and 19 June, and 2 on 22 June); about 94 other ranks recorded as wounded across the month, plus Lieut. T. S. Aikman wounded on 20 June. The heaviest day was 6 June, when an enemy raid on the Bern–Pollock trench junction cost 10 killed and 30 wounded. A wounded German prisoner was taken the same day. The rest of the month's losses are sickness and accidental injuries, with many hospital admissions and returns recorded daily.
- Movements: the battalion rotates between Brigade Reserve at Bedford House, the front line (trenches 28–32), and Brigade Reserve at Dickebusch, relieved in turn by the 20th Battalion — into the line 5 June, out to Dickebusch on the night of 11/12 June, back into the line 17 June, out to Bedford House 24 June, and back into the line 29 June. Companies are disposed at Bedford House, Norfolk Lodge, Strong Point 7, Trench 31R, Ecluse 8, Bluff Tunnel and the Tunnell while in reserve.
- Officers named: Major G. W. Nelson (invalided and struck off strength, 4 June); Lieut. W. T. Baxter (appointed Adjutant, 4 June; on leave 10–19 June); Capt. T. C. Lamb, Paymaster (acting Adjutant, 10–19 June); Capt. G. C. Hale, Medical Officer (invalided to England, 4 June); Capt. S. M. Loghrin (to hospital 2 June, returned 13 June); Lieut. H. A. Colter (to hospital 3 June, returned 13 June); Major R. J. Brook, 3rd Canadian Battalion (Distinguished Service Order, King's Birthday Honours, 5 June); Sgt W. R. Cole (appointed Temporary Lieutenant, 7 June); Lieut. J. F. Perkins (reinforcement, 13 June; on leave 22 June); Major L. E. Jones and Lieut. J. J. Richardson (returned from leave, 14 June); Lieuts. J. A. McIntosh and D. E. A. Rispin (approved to wear captain's badges, 18 June); Capt. F. H. Shuttleworth (proceeded to England, 16 June); Lieut. R. O. Mitchell (returned from hospital, 17 June); Lieuts. H. H. Sykes, J. C. Mewburn, F. C. Maund and H. D. Dunnett (reinforcements, 20 June); Lieut. T. S. Aikman (wounded, 20 June); Lieuts. W. P. Cole (on leave from 22 June, returned 30 June); Capt. R. F. Flegg, Medical Officer (to hospital 24 June, returned 28 June); Major A. Campbell, Canadian Army Medical Corps (acting Medical Officer, 24–28 June); Lieuts. E. R. V. Lloyd and N. H. Russell (reinforcements, 26 June); Lieut. St. Gunn [uncertain] (struck off strength, sick in England, 27 June); Lieut. R. H. Sheffield (returned from hospital, 29 June); Major H. L. Milligan (commanding, signed Operation Order No. 18, Appendix II).
- Notable: on 6 June a German party penetrated the junction of the Bern and Pollock trenches after a heavy evening bombardment, exploiting a destroyed machine-gun emplacement and an old communication trench; the Bern garrison under Cpl Routley drove them out, the Loop garrison stood to on Pte Montgomery's warning, and a wounded German was captured — this fell within the Battle of Mount Sorrel (2–13 June), though the diary never names the battle. On 14 June the diary records the Commander-in-Chief's congratulations to the Canadian Corps on their "gallant and successful attack" (the 13 June counter-attack), and that all watches and clocks were advanced 60 minutes at 11 p.m. Honours: the Distinguished Service Order to Major R. J. Brook; the Distinguished Conduct Medal to Sgt R. Cunningham and the Military Medal to the late Pte T. Jones (12 June); Military Medals to Cpl C. E. Tinch, A/Cpl J. Borland, L/Cpl A. H. Jones, A/Cpl E. Lawson and Pte E. Hancock (5 June). Reinforcements arrived in drafts of 174 [uncertain], 24, 20 and 40 other ranks, plus six lieutenants. A large fatigue effort on 12 June put 1,280 [uncertain] other ranks on wiring and carrying in the front line.
- No date gaps: entries run 1–30 June without a break.
