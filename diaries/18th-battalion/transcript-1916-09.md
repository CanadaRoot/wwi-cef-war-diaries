## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The printed heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck out on the diary pages. The form footer reads "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "o.r." = other ranks, "s.o.s." = struck off strength, "D.R.O." = Divisional Routine Order, "Bde" = Brigade, "Bn" = Battalion, "C.T.D." = as written.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished signature on every row, reading like "C.P.L." — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row.
- Place names as written: Tournehem, Ardruick, Aux-les-Chateau, Cramont, Bonneville, Herissart, Vadincourt (also written Vadencourt), Albert, Brickfields, Tara Valley, Lavicogne, St Leger les Domarts, Chalk Pits, Sausage Valley, Sunken Road.

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: the 15 September attack cost the battalion 7 officers killed in action, 4 wounded and 1 missing (named in the list on page 7), and 49 other ranks killed, 203 wounded and 69 missing (20 September entry) — 56 killed, 207 wounded and 70 missing in total. No other battle casualties are recorded this month; the rest of the month's losses are sickness and hospital admissions.
- The attack, 15 September 1916: at 6.20 a.m. the battalion attacked the German front and second lines to a depth of 1,200 yards and held the ground gained, both objectives gained. The battalion had moved up from the Brickfields bivouac at Albert on 14 September to take the right of the 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade frontage (18th Battalion right, 20th centre, 21st left), and was relieved by Gordon Highlanders on the morning of 16 September. The 15 September date was the opening of the Battle of Flers-Courcelette on the Somme.
- Movements: the month opens at Tournehem with musketry practice and drill, then a march and train move south on 4 September (entrained at Ardruick, arrived Aux-les-Chateau 10.30 p.m., marched to billets at Cramont), marching through Bonneville, Herissart and Vadincourt to bivouac in the Brickfields at Albert on 9 September. After the attack the battalion rested at Tara Valley and the Brickfields, then marched back through Vadencourt, Lavicogne and St Leger les Domarts, returning to Albert on 25 September and moving forward again via Chalk Pits, Sausage Valley and the Sunken Road, relieving the 21st Battalion in the line on 30 September.
- Officers named: Lieut-Col. H. L. Milligan (Major H. L. Milligan promoted to Lieut-Col. from 8 July 1916, D.R.O. 2728; signs the diary as Commanding Officer); Lieut. J. A. McIntosh (to be Temporary Major from 1 July 1916, D.R.O. 2793); Lieut. W. F. Cole (invalided to England, sick, 10 September); Lieut. G. Brooks (arrived as reinforcement, 23 September); Lieut. W. F. Huycke (proceeded to C.T.D. Shorncliffe, England, 25 September). Officers killed 15 September: Capt. S. M. Loghtrin [uncertain], Capt. W. S. McKeough, Lieut. W. J. Morris [uncertain], Lieut. F. C. Maund, Lieut. J. C. Newburn [uncertain], Lieut. A. B. Barnes, Lieut. W. H. Willard. Officers wounded: Lieut. H. H. Sykes, Lieut. J. M. Chisholm, Lieut. H. W. Smith, Lieut. N. H. Russell. Missing: Lieut. B. J. Bates.
- Anomalies: page 2 is not a contents page but a later handwritten note, "NOT MUCH HELP To A HISTORIAN / 18 Bn / Sep 17/16", apparently commenting on the diary's brevity; the contents page is page 3. The contents lists "Diary Text 4 Pages" only — there are no appendices this month. The 14–15 September dates were entered a day ahead and corrected by circling (see the transcriber's note on page 5). Place-name spellings vary within the month: VADINCOURT (8 September) against VADENCOURT (18 and 24 September); "ARDRUICK" (4 September) is as written. Two words on page 5 are struck out (the destination of the 1st and 4th Platoons on 13 September, and the unit named before "Gordon Highlanders" on 16 September).
- No date gaps: entries run 1–30 September without a break. 16 September has two entries — the relief by the Gordon Highlanders in the morning (page 5) and the battalion in reserve at Tara Valley with reinforcements arriving (page 6).
