## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "(Apogn. Wt. W2544/M299 75,000 1/17. D. D. & L., Ltd. Forms/C.2118/14." (variant spellings of the imprint occur across pages).
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed. `[struck]` marks text struck through in the original.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Cdn" = Canadian, "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade, "O.R." = other ranks, "R.A.P." = Regimental Aid Post, "S.B.R." = Small Box Respirator, "H.Q." = Headquarters, "N.C.O." = non-commissioned officer, "M.G." = Machine Gun, "C.T." = communication trench, "S.O.S." = Save Our Souls (call for artillery support), "S.A.A." = small arms ammunition, "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer, "P.P.C.L.I." = Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry, "D.C.M." = Distinguished Conduct Medal, "D.S.O." = Distinguished Service Order, "M.C." = Military Cross, "G.S." = General Service, "O.P." = observation post, "C.E." = Canadian Engineers, "T.M." = Trench Mortar, "R.S.M." = Regimental Sergeant Major, "Q.M." = Quartermaster, "M.O." = Medical Officer, "T.O." = Transport Officer, "S.B." = stretcher-bearer, "A.A." = anti-aircraft, "R.C." = Roman Catholic.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished initial on every row, resembling "b.b." — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row except where it carries an appendix reference or other note.
- Place names as written: Brethancourt [spelled BRETENCOURT in the original], Neuville Vitasse, Mercatel Section, Cojeul River, Wailly Woods, Lattre St. Quentin, Avesnes-le-Comte, Habarcq, Hauteville, Fosseux, Hendecourt, Brickfields, Chat Maigre, Telegraph Hill, Sugar Factory, Boisleux-au-Mont, Bellacourt. "MERCATE" and "MERCATEL" are as written in the original (the Mercatel/Mericourt sector).
- Map references are trench-map sheet references as written, e.g. S.12.a.29.62.
- In the handwritten Disposition State tables, blank cells are shown as —. "H.Q. 2." in the Platoon column is as written. Row and column totals are transcribed as written; where the original's arithmetic does not reconcile, this is noted in the transcriber's notes for that page.

## Page 4 — Diary text, 1–9 June 1918

[Typed "(1) WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. 18th CANADIAN BATTALION, WESTERN ONTARIO REGT." at top, "Confidential" at top left. Form C. 2118. "Maps for reference. 51 C. S.E. 51b.N.W. 1/20000 51 C 1/40000."]

### 1 June 1918 — Support line (M.17.a.36.40. to M.18.c.60.30.)

> Battalion in Brigade Support, left sub-sector, NEUVILLE VITASSE frontage. Situation quiet, with nothing unusual to report. Battalion furnished working parties improving and cleaning trenches in this area. 2 O.Rs. admitted to hospital "wounded".

### 2 June 1918 — Support line

> Position as yesterday. Enemy artillery active on battery positions in vicinity of Bn.H.Q. with all calibres. Working parties again furnished as yesterday. 2 O.Rs admitted to hospital.

### 3 June 1918 — Relief

> Battalion relieved in support by the 28th Canadian Battalion. Relief completed at 12.30 a.m. 4th 1 O.R wounded. On relief Battalion moved into Divisional reserve, being billeted at BRETENCOURT (R.26.d.) Lieuts. J.H.Barkley and M.M.Wilson [uncertain] arrived as reinforcements.

### 4 June 1918 — Brethancourt (R.26.d, Map 51.C)

> The second series of four letter code calls were taken in to use throughout at m/n 3/4th. Company parades and inspections of Kit, Clothing and Equipment. Pay parades occupied the remaining part of the day.

### 5–9 June 1918 — Brethancourt

> Battalion training as per syllabus attached. Recreational training, games such as Baseball, Football etc, indulged in each afternoon. Lieut. C.S.Woodrow and 12 other ranks arrived as reinforcements 5th inst. This officer was hit in the head by a fragment of enemy shell which burst near Battalion Orderly Room and was evacuated to hospital same day. 4 other ranks admitted to hospital, sick, on 6th inst. Lieut. T.V.Milford proceeded on leave to U/K on 6th, and 2 O.R's returned from hospital same day.

### 9/10 June 1918 — Relief (Ref. Map 51.b. N.W.)

> The Battalion relieved the 27th Canadian Battalion in front line, Right Sub Section, Divnl. Section. Battalion moved off from BRETENCOURT at 8.30 p.m. in fighting order, taking up positions as follows: "C" & "D" Coys. in Front Line from S.6.a.05.60 to S.12.c.75.96., "B" Coy. being in close Support from S.11.b.30.55. to S.5.d.05.60. and "A" Coy. in Reserve from S.5.c.22.30. to S.11.a.50.70. Battalion H.Q. located at S.4.a.20.60. in deep dugout recently constructed under Cdn. Engineers supervision. Relief complete at 1.35 a.m. 10th inst. Two patrols covered the Battalion frontage during the night, No. 1 Patrol consisting of Sergt. Manby and 13 Bn. Scouts were out from 1.00 a.m. till 3.30 a.m. No. 2 Patrol consisting of 1 N.C.O. and 12 Scouts from "C" & "D" Coys. were out from 1.00 a.m. till 4.00 a.m. No enemy were seen or encountered by either patrol.

## Page 5 — Diary text, 10–12 June 1918

[Typed "(2) WAR DIARY 18th CANADIAN BATTALION, WESTERN ONTARIO REGIMENT. INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY." Form C. 2118.]

### 10 June 1918 — Front line (S.6.a.05.60 to S.12.c.75.96)

> "C" Coy. Lewis Gun at S.12.a.55.17. moved to S.12.a.25.55. so as to cover dead ground and approaches up the COJEUL RIVER. Conditions & Defences. Trenches on the whole in good condition as regards depth, width and fire bays. Wire on frontage of two front line Coys. in [uncertain] condition. Accommodation. Coy. H.Q. in good shelters in sides of sunken roads, and funk holes in trenches in good shape. Our artillery was active during the day on enemy forward area and rear country. Lieut. McRae and four Battn. Scouts made a daylight reconnaissance, leaving our lines at S.12.b.29.62. at 4.30 p.m. and proceeded down the bank of the COJEUL RIVER to S.12.b.25.79., Reconnoitring wire in that vicinity, returning to our lines at point of exit at 10.45 p.m. Three of the enemy were seen leaving hedge at S.6.d.51.10., disappearing shortly. 1 O.R. killed — 1 O.R. wounded. Capt. Jackson [uncertain] and 1 O.R. to hospital.

### Night 10/11 June 1918

> During the night two protecting patrols covered our frontage. No.1 Patrol of 1 N.C.O. and 14 O.Rs. No.2 Patrol of 1 N.C.O. and 15 O.Rs. These patrols went out at 10.00 p.m. returning at 3.00 a.m., reporting situation quiet. Working party improving trenches.

### 11 June 1918 — Front line

> Visibility good. Our artillery active throughout the day in enemy forward area, and on movement in T.2 and T.3. Lieut. A.T.Fergusson and 28 O.Rs. arrived as reinforcements. 2 O.Rs. to hospital, sick.

### Night 11/12 June 1918

> Working parties furnished by "A", "B" & "D" Coys. for deepening trenches in their respective areas, "B" Coy. also supplying a carrying party bringing material up to new Coy. H.Q. dugout in course of construction under O.C. supervision at S.11.b.40.80. "C" Coy. parties improved front line trenches and funk holes and filled gaps in wire at S.12.b.75.20, also cleaning out C.T. in S.6.a. which had been blown in by enemy retaliation to a successful raid carried out by the 15th Battn. [uncertain] on our right.

### 12 June 1918 — Front line

> Situation fairly quiet. Lieut. J.J.Spencer and four O.Rs. made a daylight patrol, leaving our lines at S.6.b.10.25. at 2.00 p.m. and proceeded N.E. up old C.T. to S.6.d.25.85. where they were stopped by a strong block of concertina wire. Appearance of long grass in vicinity showed no signs of enemy patrol movement. About 100 rounds of our 4.5 Howitzer ammunition was found in old gun pits in vicinity of S.6.c.65.30. Party returned to our lines at point of exit at 4.30 p.m. and reported no enemy seen. 1 O.R. to hospital, sick.

## Page 6 — Diary text, night 12/13–13 June 1918

[Typed "(3) WAR DIARY 18th CANADIAN BATTALION, WESTERN ONTARIO REGIMENT. INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY." Form C. 2118.]

### Night 12/13 June 1918 — Front line (S.6.a.05.60 to S.12.c.75.96)

> A patrol consisting of Lieuts. McRae and Stokes and 5 Battn. Scouts. covered by a party of 1 Sergt. and 12 men, with Lewis Gun, left our lines at S.12.a.29.62 at 6.30 p.m. and proceeded down bank of COJEUL RIVER to approximately S.12.b.42.72. from which point they crawled out in the long grass towards hedge, in an endeavour to locate a post which three of enemy were seen holding after the raid of morning of 12th. Though patrol remained at this point until 10.45 p.m., none of enemy were seen. Patrol returned to our lines at point of exit at 11.30 p.m., reporting enemy machine guns in S.6.b. and two in S.12.a. very active. A protective patrol consisting of 1 officer and 14 O.Rs covered our frontage from S.6.a.15.30 to S.5.b.65.10. between 11.00 p.m. and 2.30 a.m., reporting no enemy seen. Working parties of all Coys. employed during night improving their trenches. "B" Coy. also supplied a party to carry material to new Coy. H.Q. 96th Inf. Bde. on our right carried out a raid at 12.00 midnight on enemy's posts. Gas was projected into NEUVILLE VITASSE at 2.00 a.m., targets being dugouts in sunken roads at H.20.a.20.50.

### 13 June 1918 — Front line

> Our artillery was active on enemy forward area and on movement. Considerable aerial activity. 1 O.R. to hospital, sick.

### Night 13/14 June 1918

> Following patrols covered the Battn. frontage during the night; Lieut. McRae and 3 Battn. Scouts left our line at S.12.a.29.62 at 6.30 p.m. and proceeded down the bank of the COJEUL RIVER to approx. S.12.b.46.70. After reconnoitring, they returned to our lines at point of exit at 12. M.N. reporting no enemy seen and some enemy machine gun activity. Lieut. Stokes and 4 Battn. Scouts, with covering party of 1 N.C.O. and 14 men with Lewis Gun, left our lines at S.12.a.29.62 at 9.30 p.m. and proceeded down bank of river to approx. S.12.b.05.72 where covering party was left. They reconnoitred this vicinity but returned owing to enemy machine gun activity, the whole party returning to our lines at point of exit at 12. M.N. reporting no enemy seen. A Protective patrol of 1 N.C.O. and 12 men covered our frontage from S.12.a.15.90 to S.6.b.65.10 between 10. p.m. and 1.00 a.m. No enemy seen. All Coys. worked during night improving trenches and posts. "B" Coy. also supply party to work on new Coy.H.Q. Enemy retaliated on our front to our artillery barrage covering raid by 5th C.I.B.

## Page 7 — Diary text, 14–17 June 1918

[Typed "— 4 — WAR DIARY 18th CANADIAN BATTALION, WESTERN ONTARIO REGIMENT. INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY." Form C. 2118.]

### 14 June 1918 — Front line (S.6.a.05.60. to S.12.c.75.96)

> Our Artillery was active on enemy forward area and movement. 3 O.R.'s admitted to hospital. 2 O.R.'s returned from hospital. 1 O.R. Wounded and 1 O.R. Accidentally Wounded. Captain C.W.F.Hodgins M.C., (Transport Officer) attached to 5th C.I.B. H.Q., as Staff Learner.

### Night 14th/15th June 1918

> The following patrols covered the Battalion frontage during the night. Lieut. S.S.Stokes [uncertain] and 3 Bn. Scouts left out lines at S.6.c.15.15. at 6.00 p.m. proceeding up old C.T. to S.6.b.20.90. Patrol returned to point of exit at 9.30 p.m. reporting no enemy seen and took out a Battle patrol of 15 other ranks with Lewis Gun, patrolling Battalion frontage, returning to our lines at midnight reporting no enemy seen. Lieut. J.J.McRae and 5 Bn. Scouts left our lines at S.12.a.71.98. at 10.00 p.m. proceeding up Road to Hedge at approx. S.12.b.05.20. to locate M.G. This was definitely located back of Junction of Hedge and Road at S.12.b.21.25. This M.G. is not very active. Patrol returned to our lines at 11.30 p.m. reporting no enemy seen. "A" & "B" Coys. supplied working parties to improve trenches in their areas.

### 15 June 1918 — Front line

> Our Artillery active on enemy forward area and on considerable movement in enemy rear country. Lieut. C.H.Williams proceeded on leave to England. 213203 Arm/Cpl. [uncertain] Kelley D.C.M. awarded the D.C.M. for gallantry in attacking an enemy post near NEUVILLE-VITASSE in the latter part of May.

### Night 15th/16th June 1918

> "A" & "B" Coys. supplied working parties for trench improvements in their areas. Two protective patrols covered the Battalion frontage until Bn. was relieved by the 19th Canadian Battalion at 12.07 a.m. On relief "Complete" Bn. moved back into Brigade Support, taking over positions vacated by the 19th Cdn. Bn. Companies were reported in position by 2. a.m.

### 16 June 1918 — Brigade Support (Bn.Hdqrs at S.2.b.40.10.)

> Bn. in Brigade Support. Disposition as per Disposition State & Sketch attached. Lieut. H.B.Krug arrived as a reinforcement.

### 17 June 1918 — Brigade Support

> The following Work Parties were employed last night under 2nd Cdn. Divnl. Engineers. "A" Coy. party working on trench improvements at M.25.c. "B" Coy. party working on trench improvements in C.T. at S.11.b. "C" Coy. party improving C.T. at M.26.a. "D" Coy. party digging trench at M.25.a. 1 O.R. returned from leave to U/K.

## Page 8 — Diary text, 18–21 June 1918

[Typed "— 5 — WAR DIARY 18th CANADIAN BATTALION, WESTERN ONTARIO REGIMENT. INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY." Form C. 2118.]

### 18 June 1918 — Brigade Support (Bn. H.Q. at S.2.b.40.10.)

> The following work parties were supplied last night: "A" Coy. party digging trench in M.35.c. and carrying material to M.35.c.10.40. "B" Coy. party digging trench in S.11.b. and working on new Coy. H.Q. at S.11.b.40.80. "C" Coy. party improving trenches in S.35.a. and digging [uncertain] tunnels under roads in S.4. "D" Coy. party digging C.T. in M.35.a. At 2.30 a.m. the 32nd Divn. on our Right put down a false S.O.S. barrage on their front in order to mislead the enemy. At 9.00 P.M. enemy shelling vicinity of "A" Coy. (M.25.c.), with yellow cross Gas shells. 1 O.R. admitted to hospital.

### 19 June 1918 — Brigade Support

> The following work parties were supplied last night: "A" Coy. party deepening trench in M.29.d. and carrying material [uncertain] for C.E's to Divnl. O.P. at M.35.c.10.40. "B" Coy. party digging trench in S.11.b. and working on Coy. H.Q. at S.2.a.00.55. "C" Coy. party digging new C.T. (James Ave. [uncertain] in M.35.a. "D" Coy. party digging trench in M.29.d. and tunnelling under road at M.34.1.50.40. Enemy shelling S.29.a. & d. and S.11.a. & b. with gas shells from 10.00 to 11.00 p.m. 1 O.R. admitted to hospital, 1 O.R. returned from hospital. 9 O.R.'s reinforcements.

### 20 June 1918 — Brigade Support

> The following work parties were supplied last night: "A" Coy. party carrying material to Divnl. O.P. "B" Coy. digging trench from S.11.c. to S.11.c. At 12.45 a.m. enemy aeroplane dropped six bombs in M.34.a.20.70, no damage being done. At 1.00 a.m. party of the 20th & 21st Bns. under cover of Artillery, T.M. & M.G., barrage, raided enemy positions on 20th Bn. frontage, inflicting numerous casualties and securing 1 prisoner. At the same time parties from the Div. on our right raided enemy posts inflicting casualties and taking prisoners.

### 21 June 1918 — Brigade Support

> The following work parties were supplied last night: "A" Coy. party improving trenches in M.35.a. and carrying material to Divnl. O.P. "B" Coy. party digging trench in S.11.b. "C" Coy. party deepening James Ave. C.T. "D" Coy. party improving trenches in M.35.a. and tunnels under road at M.34.d. At 1.40 a.m. the 19th Bn. raided enemy positions in S.12.b. under cover of a barrage. They found the enemy out-post line deserted. At night "A" Coy. supplied work party for work on Divnl. O.P.

>

>
> 1. The 18th Canadian Battalion will relieve the 27th Canadian Battalion in the Right Sub-Section of the 2nd Cdn. Div'l. Sector on the night 9th/10th June 1918 with Battalion H.Q. at S.4.a.2.6.
>

## Notes

- Diary date range covered: 1–30 June 1918, with no date gaps in the diary text. Every day of the month is accounted for (5–9 June and 23–26 June are grouped entries).
- Commanding Officer: the diary is signed at 30 June by Major J. L. Bell [uncertain — signature], "Comdg. 18th Canadian Battalion, Western Ontario Regiment", while Lt-Col. J. E. Jones D.S.O. was on leave to England from 25 June. The Adjutant throughout is Lieut. C. Cook (his signature on Orders D.218 and D.219, on the Defence Scheme and on the Administrative Instructions is illegible and is marked [uncertain]).
- Place-name spelling: the village the battalion was billeted in from 3–9 June and whose baths were used on 22 June is spelled "BRETENCOURT" throughout the original (map reference R.26.d.). The sector is spelled "MERCATE" in the contents and in Order D.217 and "MERCATEL" in the Defence Scheme and in Order D.218.
- The first line of the 27 June diary entry is struck through in the original but remains legible; it is transcribed with a [struck] marker.
- The "From" map references of the D Company platoon 14 and 15 rows on the 16 June Disposition State (page 26) are struck through and overwritten in the original; the corrected references are transcribed.
- Page order: the appendices are not in chronological order as filmed — the syllabus for 28–30 June (pages 28–30) comes before the syllabus for 22–26 June (page 31). No pages are missing: the contents page lists the diary text (6 pages) and all appendices, and all are present. Page 13 is the blank reverse of Order No. D.218.
- The 10 June Disposition State (pages 21–23) gives a battalion grand total of 605 other ranks (24 officers); the 16 June Disposition State (pages 25–26) gives 638 other ranks (22 officers). Several row and column totals in the originals do not reconcile with their components; each case is noted under the relevant page.
- [uncertain] flags: 27 in total, covering illegible names and words in the diary text and orders, the commanding officer's signature, four illegible adjutant/staff-captain signatures, the sketch signatures, the overwritten total on the 10 June Disposition State sheet I, and the unclear figures noted on pages 21, 23, 24 and 26.
