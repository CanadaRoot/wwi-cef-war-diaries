## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "(Apagn. Wt. W2544/M299 75,000 1/17. D. D. & L., Ltd. Forms/C.2118/14." (variant spellings of the imprint occur across pages).
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed. `[struck]` marks text struck through in the original.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Cdn" = Canadian, "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade, "O.R." = other ranks, "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer, "S.B.R." = Small Box Respirator, "H.Q." = Headquarters, "N.C.O." = non-commissioned officer, "M.G." = Machine Gun, "R.A.P." = Regimental Aid Post, "C.T." = communication trench, "G.S." = General Service, "M.O." = Medical Officer, "T.O." = Transport Officer, "Q.M." = Quartermaster, "R.S.M." = Regimental Sergeant-Major, "R.Q.M." = Regimental Quartermaster-Sergeant, "O.C." = Officer Commanding, "S.O.S." = Save Our Souls (artillery distress signal).
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished initial on every row, resembling "C.P.H." — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row except where it carries an appendix reference or other note.
- Place names as written: Bretencourt, Blairville, Boyelles, Mercatel, Wailly, Neuville Vitasse, Ficheux, Boisleux St. Marc, Agny, Beaurains, Cojeul River.
- Map references are trench-map sheet references as written, e.g. S.5.d.80.70, M.31.b.00.70, N.19.a.70.80.

## Page 4 — Diary text, 1–3 May 1918

[Handwritten "18 Canadian [uncertain] Battalion" and "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY." at top, "MAY 1918" at top right, "Confidential" at top left. Form C. 2118.]

### 1 May 1918 — Bretencourt

> MAPS for Reference. 51B Edn 2. 51C Edn 2. Battalion in reserve billets at Bretencourt. R. 26. d. Company Parades and inspections. Training of specialists by Instructors. Recreational training and games during afternoon.

### 2 May 1918 — Bretencourt

> do. Company inspections of arms and Equipment. Battalion parade for Gas lecture. Games such as football, Baseball etc during the afternoon. 17. O.R. arrived as Reinforcements, and 3 O.R. from hospitals.

### 3 May 1918 — Relief, 7.30 p.m.

> During night of 3/4th 18th Canadian Bn relieves the 29th Canadian Bn in Front line Right subsection. Battalion moved off from Bretencourt at 7.30 p.m. passing along road to Blairville branching off to a cross country track about ½ mile before reaching Blairville. From this track to sunken road at S.5.c.25.25 and on through communication trenches to their respective positions. Positions and locations being A. C. and D coys in front line, B coy in support. Each of the 3 front line companies having 2 platoons in front line and 2 in close support. from S.5.d.80.70 to S.12.c.70.25. Bn H.Q. at S.11.a.60.50 to S.11.a.65.00. [struck: Bn H.Q. at S.11.a.60.36.] Relief completed at 11.35 p.m. 2 O.Rs. wounded. During this tour rations were brought to railway track by wagons and from there to support lines by pack mules (all [uncertain] attached).

## Page 5 — Diary text, 4–5 May 1918

[Form C. 2118. Place column: "S. 5. d. / S. 6 / S. 12. c."]

### 4 May 1918

> Position as shown. During the day working parties furnished by Battalion building dug-outs and repairing trenches. Enemy artillery being very active during the whole of the day. During the night 4/5th several patrols covered Bn frontage. 1 Patrol of 11 O.R. under Lieut. MacRae & Stokes [uncertain] the main road at 11 p.m. at S.12.a.22.60. and proceeded along the river from there to S.12.a.90.10 and N.W. to S.12.a.81.79 returning S.W. entering trench at point of exit, no enemy were encountered or seen. a 2nd Patrol of 16 O.R. under Lieut. R. G. Sheridan accompanied by 7 O.R. of the 29th Bn [uncertain] went out to recover the body of one of their men. This patrol went up an old O.T. to approx S.6.d.10.80 where 16 men were left as covering party. Lieut Sheridan and the men of the 29th Bn continued up this trench to S.6.c.30.95 where the body was recovered. Nothing unusual occurred.

### 5 May 1918

> Enemy artillery active shelling the whole area and also rear country, sending a number of gas shells into Boyelles. 3 O.R. wounded. Considerable movement was observed in enemy lines during the whole day. at 5.55 p.m. an explosion was observed to take place in enemy lines at T.10.a which appeared to be either a flare or carbide dump. Working parties again supplied for work on trenches and new Bn H.Q. in Mercatel [uncertain]. During night 5/6th enemy relief was suspected and our artillery shelled roads and [uncertain] rear of enemy trenches.

## Page 6 — Diary text, 6–8 May 1918

[Form C. 2118. Place column: "Right S[uncertain]".]

### 6 May 1918 — night 5/6 May

> During night 5/6th 3 patrols covered Bn frontage and made reconnaissances of hedge and ground directly in front. One patrol under Lieut. Stokes and another under Lieut. Sheridan, no enemy were encountered and nothing unusual occurred with the exception of considerable M.G. fire by enemy.

### 6 May 1918 — day

> Intermittent shelling by enemy during the day. 50 O.R. of B coy working in 4 shifts worked 6 hours on New Bn H.Q. 100 O.R. of B coy worked 3 hours carrying wire & [uncertain] from [uncertain] dump to A coy front line. All available men of D coy were employed clearing and repairing trenches in their own area. 1 O.R. wounded.

### 7 May 1918

> Situation quiet during the day. During night 7/8th working parties were supplied as last night. 18 O.R. arrived as reinforcements.

### 8 May 1918

> Our artillery shelled at intervals enemy trenches and rear country. at 8.45 p.m. Enemy put down barrage on our front support lines lasting about 15 minutes. 1 O.R. being wounded. During night 8/9th [uncertain] covered Bn frontage. One patrol under Lieut MacRae and Sheridan with 20 O.R. and Lewis Gun went out to end of a sap at S.6.d.11.00. where Lieut. Sheridan and 18 O.R. remained as covering party to Lieut MacRae and 2 scouts who proceeded S.E. to definitely locate enemy M.G. post which was supposed to be in a shell crater. It was [uncertain] after locating this to bomb up remainder of [uncertain] with Lewis Gun and [uncertain] the post. but owing to considerable activity of artillery with gas shells and shrapnel our patrol was forced to withdraw.

## Page 7 — Diary text, 9–14 May 1918

[Form C. 2118. "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY." struck through at top. Place column: "Relief." against 11 May.]

### 9 May 1918

> artillery fairly active on both sides during the day. Considerable movement was observed in enemy lines during the day and our artillery paid attention to this. During the night fatigue parties were cleaning and repairing trenches in our area. 2 patrols under Lieut Sheridan & Stokes patrolled Bn frontage from 10 p.m. to 3 a.m. no enemy being seen or encountered.

### 10 May 1918

> Situation quiet during the day. Usual working parties at night improving trenches. Protection and reconnaissance patrols covered Bn frontage with nothing unusual to report. 1 O.R. wounded.

### 11 May 1918

> During the morning our right front was subjected to [uncertain] attention by enemy heavy trench mortar. Protective patrols covered Bn frontage, nothing unusual occurred. During night 11/12 Battalion was relieved in front line by 19th Canadian Bn. On relief 18th Bn moved into Brigade Reserve in the Purple Line system of trenches (see map attached). A. C. and D coys being in Purple firing line and B coy had 2 platoons in Intermediate line and 2 platoons at Mercatel switch. Relief being completed by 12.15 a.m.

### 12 May 1918

> Nothing unusual occurred, men employed digging funk holes & making shelters during the day.

### 13 May 1918

> During nights 12/13 fatigue parties digging funk holes in support line and cleaning trenches.

### 14 May 1918 — 9.30 a.m.

> B coy moved from position at Mercatel Switch to position in Purple support line (see map attached) being relieved by the 20th Can Bn. The position being from M 31 b.00.70 to M.31 a.00.70.

### 22 May 1918 — Relief

> Company Parades and inspection in morning. During night 22/23rd Battalion relieved 31st Canadian Bn in left Battalion frontage. Battalion moved off from WAILLY at 9.00 p.m. in fighting order passing [struck] to front line position in front of Neuville Vitasse. Bn H.Q. at M.16.a.95.55. ### 23 May 1918

> Situation quiet. Two patrols covered Battalion frontage during night and reported our shelling causing casualties in enemy [uncertain]. 5 O.R. admitted to hospital.

### 24 May 1918

> Nothing unusual occurred during the day. Working parties furnished by Battalion for work during the night on trenches in [uncertain]. Two patrols were out during night. No.1 patrol Lieut McRae and 3 scouts located an enemy post at approx N.19.a.30.50 but owing to bright moonlight were unable to get close enough, so returned to our line. Patrol 2 under Lieut Sheridan proceeded to same post but the enemy had in the meantime been alarmed and arose our party [uncertain] with rifle fire and bombs. No casualties occurred.

### 25 May 1918

> Working parties again furnished by Bn for work during the night on trenches. Out party of 1 officer & 12 other ranks constructed wire from N.19.a.25.57. to N.19.a.05.50. 2 Patrols covered Bn frontage during the night, nothing unusual to report.

## Page 10 — Diary text, 26–27 May 1918

[Form C. 2118.]

### 26 May 1918

> Desultory shelling by enemy over whole area and rear country. A courageous act was done this morning by 219203 Cpl. J. A. Kelley. At 2.15 a.m. Cpl. Kelley with a covering party left our trench at N.19.a.70.89. and proceeded up an old communication trench towards enemy lines to definitely locate positions and defences of enemy post located in this trench. Cpl Kelley was able to get close up to this post and found 3 Germans on sentry duty. He rushed the [uncertain] in front of this post and shot 2 of the occupants and dragged the third across the barrier. Several of the enemy were seen to run back along the trench. Our enemy M.G. opened up and bombs were thrown but Cpl Kelley returned safely to our line with his prisoner. Shortly after Cpl Kelley had returned our line, a party of the enemy was seen to come into and down the trench toward the raided post. Party estimated at 15 & 18. Several of them appeared on sides of the trench looking around apparently trying to locate the prisoner we had taken. Cpl Dougall and sentries in A coy immediately opened fire and one of the enemy were seen to fall and the remainder disappeared.

### 27 May 1918

> Nothing unusual happened during the day. Two patrols covered Battalion frontage during the night. No enemy being encountered or seen.

No.2 party of 8 Bn Scouts with 2 other [uncertain] each of 1 N.C.O. and 4 men (to be left at trench intersections) under Lieut J. W. MacRae left our line at N.19.a.81.99. at 11.30 p.m. proceeded up old trench to intersection of trench at N.19.a.88.79. At this point [uncertain] of the sentries were seen looking over barricade at entrance of trench. The Scout party got to within 250 ft of [uncertain] when they were seen by sentries who immediately threw bombs which went over the raiders heads. J. MacRae and Bomb Sgt J. Manly rushed the barricade, the remainder of the party bombing the post and [uncertain] up the trench. on climbing the barricade a German was seen dragging another [uncertain] up the trench and a [uncertain] running and giving alarm. Unfortunately at this moment our barrage opened up on [uncertain] cooperating in [uncertain] in front of [uncertain] and other dropping [uncertain] around, causing [uncertain] to retire. Our bombing [uncertain] at this post, No.1 party immediately bombed post at N.19.a.73.80 throwing twenty bombs into post and into [uncertain] behind, continuous [uncertain] and groaning [uncertain] from post but No.1 party were also prevented from following up by our barrage. Both raiding parties returned to our lines without casualty.

### 29 May 1918

> Nothing unusual happened during the day. Bn furnishes working parties during the night. Patrols covered Bn frontage.

## Notes

- Diary text covers 1–31 May 1918 with no date gaps: every day of the month is entered. The battalion was in the line 3/4–11/12 May (right sub-section, front line), in brigade reserve 11/12–15/16 May (Purple Line), in divisional reserve at Wailly 15/16–22/23 May, and in the line again 22/23–29/30 May (left sub-section, front line, in front of Neuville Vitasse), then in brigade reserve (Purple Line) from 30 May.
- The diary is signed at 31 May by the Commanding Officer, Lt.-Col. L. E. Jones, D.S.O. (signature read as "L. E. Jones" — [uncertain]).
- The Adjutant throughout the month is Lieut. C. Cook, who signs the battalion orders, defence scheme, reports and training syllabuses. Lieut. Stokes is named as guide officer in Orders D.211 and D.212. Lieut. Sloan is named as guide officer in Order D.214.
- Command change noted in the diary: Maj. J. A. McIntosh was attached to Canadian Corps school on 19 May.
- Casualties recorded in the diary text: 2 O.R. wounded 3 May; 3 O.R. wounded 5 May; 1 O.R. wounded 6 May; 1 O.R. wounded 8 May; 1 O.R. wounded 10 May; 2 O.R. wounded and Lieut. H. L. Scully wounded 31 May. No killed are recorded in the diary text for May 1918. (Casualty lists do not appear as a separate appendix this month.)
- Named in the diary text: Lieut. MacRae (patrols 4, 8, 24, 28 May; raid 27/28 May), Lieut. R. G. Sheridan (patrols 4, 6, 8, 9 May; raid 27/28 May), Lieut. Stokes (patrols 4, 6, 9 May), Lieut. J. W. MacRae (raid 27/28 May), Bomb Sgt. J. Manly (raid 27/28 May), Cpl. J. A. Kelley, No. 219203 (prisoner raid 26 May), Cpl. Dougall (26 May), Maj. J. A. McIntosh, Lieut. H. L. Scully (wounded 31 May), Sgt. Rowlands (training syllabus, 20 May).
- The contents page (p. 2) does not match the appendices exactly: it lists Order D.214 as "dated the 28th May" (the order itself is dated 22 May), it numbers two different items as Appendix 2 and two as Appendix 3, and it lists a "Front line system of trenches" and a "Disposition of 18th Battalion on the 9th" under Appendix 10 which are in fact pp. 32 and 58–60.
- Duplicates: p. 55 is a faint duplicate/carbon copy of p. 54 (sheet 2 of the 23 May Disposition State); p. 63 and p. 31 are two copies of the same 23 May disposition sketch map, the p. 31 copy carrying additional hand-drawn trench markings.
- Reverses: pp. 14, 17, 19, 23, 25 and 27 are the backs of typed/handwritten sheets; their text is show-through from the other side and is not separately transcribed. "Paymaster" is handwritten on the reverses of the training syllabuses (pp. 14, 23, 25, 27).
- The Provisional Defence Scheme (Appendix 7, pp. 41–42) is dated 1 June 1918, i.e. the day after the month covered.
- The diary text's Remarks column initial, resembling "C.P.H.", appears on every row and is not transcribed row by row.
- Map references in the diary and appendices use sheets 51B and 51C (1:20,000) and 51B S.W. / 51B S.E. / 51C S.E.
