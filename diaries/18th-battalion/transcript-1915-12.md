## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D. D. & L. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Divnl" = Divisional, "Bgde" = Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "O.R." = other ranks, "B.R.O." = Brigade Routine Order.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished initial on every row that is not legible — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row.
- Place names as written: La Clytte (also written LA CLYTTE / LA-CLYTE), Vierstraat, Ridgewood, Westoutre, Ypres.

## Page 5 — Diary text, 19–24 December 1915

[Handwritten "December 1915" at top and "18th Can B" at top right. Form C. 2118.]

### 19 December 1915 — Ridgewood

> RIDGEWOOD — A gas attack was attempted this morning on an Imperial division (49th) on our left flank about YPRES. The battalion "stood-to" from 4 am until 9-30 am. The gas was not thick on our front but sufficient to make the eyes irritable — Nothing [uncertain] our front. — 4 other ranks returned from hospital — 3-O.R. admitted to hospital

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: Pte Newlands and a second private (name illegible) killed in action 26 December; Pte Shoring [uncertain] killed in action 27 December; 2 men killed when La Clytte was shelled 29 December (names not given). No other battle casualties are recorded; the rest of the month's losses are sickness and hospital admissions.
- Movements: the battalion rotates between the trenches at Vierstraat, Brigade Reserve at Ridgewood, and Divisional Reserve at La Clytte, relieved in turn by the 19th Battalion. On 31 December it marched to Westoutre for a practice attack (Appendices II and III), returning at 5 pm.
- Officers named: Major R. Emmerton (to hospital 4 December, returned 21 December); Major R. L. Wigle (appointed Orderly Officer to 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade, 12 December); Major Dale (returned from hospital 5 December); Lieut J. P. Campbell and Lieut Abbott (attached for instruction, 2 December); Lieut B. A. Kean, Lieut R. A. McKay and Lieut Barton (joined the battalion, 14 December); Lieut C. E. Ankney (appointed Machine Gun Officer, 24 December); Lieut McKeough (attached to 2nd Division for instruction, 28 December); Major G. W. Nelson, Adjutant (signed the Attack Orders, Appendix III).
- Notable: a German gas attack was attempted on 19 December against an Imperial division (49th) on the battalion's left flank about Ypres; the 18th Battalion stood-to from 4 am to 9.30 am and was only lightly affected. Christmas Day 1915 passed very quietly in the trenches, and Christmas and New Year wishes were received from the King, the Duke of Connaught and Major-General Sir Sam Hughes.
- No date gaps: entries run 1–31 December without a break.
