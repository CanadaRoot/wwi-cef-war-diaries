## Page 16 — Appendix IV, Report on raiding enterprise, night 26/27 July 1916

[Typed. Handwritten "Appendix 4" at top.]

> 18th Canadian Battalion.
>
> Report on raiding enterprise by 18th Canadian Battalion on the night 26/27 July, 1916.
>
> The raiding party and flankers left our trench at 10.30 p.m., proceeding to old French trench. The Scout Officer — Lieut. Allen — with Privates Forrester and Martin made their way to enemy wire, to locate and prepare a lane for the raiding party to enter. At 1.05 a.m., [a word is struck out] much thicker than it appears from O.P's, both officers agreeing that from 40 to 60 feet is a fair estimate. The wire-cutting was done without alarming the enemy and Private Martin returned to guide the raiding party to the gap. Entrance was effected, still without alarming the enemy, and 19 of the party lined the German front line trench, the remainder being left at the gap to clear and cover the party when retiring. The Bomb Officer — Lieut. McClinton — first got over the parapet and, finding the bay unoccupied, gave the signal for the rest of the party to get over; no [uncertain] alarm, and the bombing squads were sent down the trench, the bombing officer reported having himself covered about 50 yards of the trench, finding it unoccupied, with no evidence of gas, although the trench is evidently patrolled, because whilst cutting the wire Lieut. Allen reports hearing footsteps going along the trench, however as the gap had not been made, there was no opportunity afforded [uncertain] to get at the patrol. During the search of the trench, which could not be made without a certain amount of noise, the enemy took alarm and the party were challenged by a big gun, who was promptly shot and toppled forward. The party, having been discovered, and not being able to do any further business in the front line, made a concerted rush on the 2nd line, bombers covering the charge. The party were brought to a sudden stop by heavy wire entanglements and it being impossible to make further progress retired under a heavy bombing defence. The enemy appeared to have the range from support to the front trench very accurately, because most of the bombs fell there. Our casualties were 1 severely wounded — Martin — 6 wounded of which 3 are slight and 2 missing.
>
> It is evident that much work is going on in support lines as sounds of a large working party were plainly heard. Just before our party was challenged, all work appeared to cease, the garrison and working party evidently standing to in anticipation of an attack, the enemy bombing commencing as soon as the [uncertain] sentry was shot. The wire between the front and second lines does not appear to be visible from the O. P's and is probably concealed by the parapets of the front line. It would appear that the enemy, as a precaution against raids, had abandoned his front line and has constructed a new line within bombing distance, wiring the intervening space.
>
> All the equipment noted in the detailed plan of the raid was found necessary and in every case used with good effect. In addition, puttees were removed and trousers tied below the knees with string. This was done to prevent puttees unrolling and becoming entangled in the wire.
>
> Two stretcher bearers and stretcher accompanied party to old FRENCH TRENCH where they remained, rendering valuable service. A flare was fired from this trench towards enemy line to indicate that party was clear. This was arranged for in case the artillery and machine guns were required to break up a counter raid. The artillery and Machine Guns arrangements for action in case they were required were most complete, special Liaison Officers being detailed by C. J. Artillery group to facilitate co-operation. No machine gun fire was turned on our party.
>
> The diversion party, consisting of one officer — Lieut. Lloyd — and 10 other ranks carried out their task as instructed. The H. Q. was not occupied. When the bombing commenced on the raiding party, the diversion party threw forty bombs in and about the German main trench. The zone of operation of both parties was from O.2.a.0.7 to O.2.a.5.6.
>
> The night was dark and extremely misty.

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: 9 other ranks killed in action (3 on 2 July, 1 on 3 July, 2 on 17 July, 1 on 20 July, 1 on 21 July, 1 on 23 July); 5 other ranks died of wounds (1 on 6 July, reported 9 July; 1 on 20 July and 2 more reported the same day; 1 on 20 July, reported 23 July); 3 other ranks "Died" on 3 July [uncertain — the word may read "Sick"]. Wounded most days, heaviest on 17 July (9 wounded) and 19 July (10 wounded). The raid of 26/27 July cost 1 severely wounded (Pte Martin), 6 wounded of which 3 slight, and 2 missing. 1 other rank reported "missing" on 27 July. No officer was killed; Lieuts. C. E. B. Corbould, A. R. Mackedie, R. O. Mitchell and H. H. Willard were wounded. No names of killed other ranks are given in the diary.
- Movements: in the front line at the Bluff / International Trench / Bean / Pollock 1–3 July; relieved by the 31st Canadian Battalion on 4 July and in reserve at Quebec Camp 4–15 July (training syllabuses 10–13 and 15 July); moved back to the front line on the evening of 15 July, relieving the 22nd Canadian Battalion; in the trenches for the rest of the month, to be relieved on the night of 31 July and move into reserve at Alberta Camp.

The Adjutant's signature on the appendices is illegible.
- Notable: the battalion's first trench raid of the month, on the night of 26/27 July against the German front line in front of Piccadilly Farm — a 48-man party (27 raiders, 10 covering, 11 diversion) under Lieut. Allen, with Lieut. McClinton bombing 50 yards of empty trench, no prisoner taken, the enemy's front line found abandoned and a new line built within bombing distance. A patrol had reconnoitred the enemy wire the night before (25 July). Gas-helmet drill and a gas demonstration feature in the training week at Quebec Camp. 179 other ranks arrived as reinforcements during the month (100 on 13 July, 49 on 23 July, 30 on 30 July).
- No date gaps: entries run 1–31 July without a break.
