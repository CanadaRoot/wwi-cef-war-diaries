## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "(A973) Wt W2215/P55 6,000 11/17 D. D. & L. Sch. 31a. Form C.2118/13." The diary text (pages 7–10) is typewritten; the cover and title page are partly handwritten.
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed. `[struck]` marks text struck through in the original.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Cdn" = Canadian, "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade, "C.M.G.C." = Canadian Machine Gun Corps, "O.R." = other ranks, "S.B.R." = Small Box Respirator, "H.Q." = Headquarters, "N.C.O." = non-commissioned officer, "M.G." = Machine Gun, "R.A.P." = Regimental Aid Post, "C.C.R.C." = Canadian Corps Reinforcement Camp, "M.M." = Military Medal, "L.G." = Lewis Gun, "O.P." = Observation Post, "Bde." = Brigade, "T.M." = Trench Mortar, "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding, "D.S.O." = Distinguished Service Order, "M.C." = Military Cross, "M.O." = Medical Officer, "Q.M." = Quartermaster, "R.S.M." = Regimental Sergeant Major, "O.C." = Officer Commanding, "T.O." = Transport Officer, "S.O." = Scout Officer, "Musk." = Musketry, "Maire" = Mayor, "G.S.G.S." = Geographical Section, General Staff.
- The LAC image files are filmed out of order relative to the printed pagination (image 0033 is printed p. 1, 0037 is p. 2, 0038 is p. 3, 0036 is p. 4, 0034 is p. 5, 0035 is p. 6, 0039–0042 are pp. 7–10); the transcription below follows printed page order.
- The two printed France map sheets (printed pp. 5–6) are not listed in the contents and are not the Appendix 24–26 maps; they are filed between the title page and the diary text.
- Appendix 2 does not appear in the page images: the contents lists "Appendix I & 2 Syllabus of training for the 1st and 2nd November", but both the 1 November syllabus (p. 11) and the 2 November syllabus (p. 12) are headed "Appendix No. 1".
- The Remarks column carries a repeated handwritten mark resembling "66" against most diary entries; its meaning is not clear and the reading is uncertain.
- The printed sub-heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through on each diary text page.
- The dates 28th, 29th and 30th November are written in parentheses at the start of the summary text rather than in the Date column.
- Duplicate filmings: printed pp. 25, 29, 32, 34 and 37 are second copies of the orders on pp. 24, 28, 31, 33 and 36 respectively (p. 37 filmed upside-down); pp. 53, 55, 57, 59, 62, 64, 66, 68, 70 and 73 are repeat filmings of the map sheets on pp. 52, 54, 56, 58, 61, 63, 65, 67, 69 and 72. They are noted as duplicates and not re-transcribed.
- For the dense printed 1:100,000 map sheets (pp. 40–73), individual small place names are, with the exceptions noted, too small to read reliably at the resolution of the page images and are not transcribed; only legible headings, marginal text, legends and imprints are given.
- Place names as written in the diary text: Aniche, Herin, Abscon, Denain, Valenciennes, St. Saulve, Onnaing, Quivrechain, Elouges, Wasmes, Frameries, Dour, Petit Wasmes, Paturages, Morchain, Ciply, Hyon, Ville-sur-Haine, Houdeng, Aimeries [uncertain], La Louviere, Jolimont, Chapelle-les-Herlaimont, Courcelles, Lotte [uncertain], Ransart, Fleurus, Keumies, Velaine, Jemap pe [as written, "JEMAPPE"], Moustier, Mormont, St-Servins, Namur, Jambes, Vicheroul, Kinaux, Cayet, Strud, St-Bluge, Coutisse, Chey, Evelette, Havelange-Verlee, Mein.

### 1 November 1918 — Aniche, G.6.a.7.3.

> Battalion Training as per Syllabi attached, Appendix No. 1. 9 O.R's on leave.

### 2 November 1918

> Battalion Training as per syllabi attached, Appendix No. 2. Lieut. J.N. Mowbray & 8 O.R's on leave. 2 O.R's returned from leave. Lieut. J.H. Barkley [uncertain] and 2 O.R's returned from Courses at 1st Army Musk. Camp. 1 O.R. on Course to Cdn. Corps School. 3 O.R's returned from Rest Camp. 1 O.R. arrived as reinforcement. 14 O.R's returned from hospital. 1 O.R. admitted to hospital. 1 Sgt. attached from C.C.R.C. as L.G. Instructor.

### 3 November 1918

> Battalion Church Parade at 10.00 a.m. on Battalion Parade Ground. Anticipating a move on the 4th inst, preparations were made in the afternoon re loading of Lewis Guns etc. Warning Order attached, Appendix No. 3.

### 4 November 1918

> Battalion embussed at 17.00 hours and moved to HERIN via ABSCON - DENAIN. Lieut. R.R. Hartry and 2 O.R's returned from Course. 1 O.R. admitted to hospital. Appendix No. 4 attached.

[Remarks: Order No. D. 254 att'd]

### 5 November 1918 — Herin, D.10.0.15.20. Map 51.A.

> Battalion resting in billets in HERIN awaiting orders to move forward. Lt-Col. L.E. Jones on leave to U.K. Major J.S. Bell D.S.O. M.C. assumes command of the Battalion. Lieut. W. Burns arrived as a reinforcement. 1 O.R. admitted to hospital.

### 6 November 1918

> Battalion marched in a heavy rain via VALENCIENNES to ST. SAULVE arriving at destination at 15.00 hours. The entire Battalion billeted in a Convent formerly used by the Germans as a Military Hospital. 12 O.R's on leave. 2 O.R's returned from leave. 14 O.R's arrived as reinforcements. 1 O.R. to hospital. Appendix No. 5 attached.

[Remarks: Order No. D. 255 att'd]

### 7 November 1918 — St. Saulve, E.4.d.65.95 Map 51.A.

> Battalion moved off at 12.00 hours by march route via ONNAING to QUIVRECHAIN arriving there about 15.00 hours. Civilians heartily welcomed and cheered the Battalion on its arrival especially when the Band formed up in front of Battalion Hdqrs and struck up the LA MARSEILLAISE. Every effort was made by the people to make things comfortable for the men. Lieut. C. Cook proceeded on Course to 1st Army Infantry School. Lieut. J.N. Mowbray and 1 O.R. returned from leave. Lieut. E.G. Barrie and 13 O.R's attached to 2nd Div. Burial Officer for duty.

[Remarks: Syllabus & Order D. 256 att'd]

### 8 November 1918 — Quivrechain, I.2-65.75. Valenciennes 12.

> Battalion moved off at 9.00 hours and crossed the Belgian Boundary at QUIVRECHAIN, a village 2 kilometres further on. Battalion rested here until about 12.30 when it moved on to ELOUGES which it reached about 15.00 hours. Here again the civilians, free again after 4 years of bondage, expressed great joy on the Battalion's arrival. The music of the Band when it played LA MARSEILLAISE and LA BRABANCONNE was drowned out by the cheering of the people

### 9 November 1918

> The Battalion moved off in fighting order at dawn and jumped over the 25th Canadian Battalion between ELOUGES and WASMES and met no opposition as they moved along to FRAMERIES where Battalion Hdqrs., was established. Rear Details moved off at 10.00 hours and marched via DOUR - PETIT WASMES - PATURAGES to FRAMERIES. The people along the route overjoyed to be free again, cheered the troops as they passed along, loading them down with flowers and victuals of all kinds. During the night the Battalion pushed forward and established a line of posts through MORCHAIN. 1 O.R. arrived as a reinforcement, 1 O.R. from hospital.

[Remarks: Narrative of operation attached No. 9]

### 10 November 1918 — Ciply, L.2-33.97

> At 04.00 hours the Battalion pushed forward and established posts in CIPLY and HYON meeting very little opposition. Rear Details moved up from FRAMERIES and reached CIPLY about 10.00 hours. There was considerable scattered shelling of village and vicinity till 15.00 hours, when the enemy guns were silenced by our batteries. 4 O.R's proceeded on leave. 5 O.R's returned from leave. 1 O.R. retd from hospital. 10 O.R's wounded, 1 O.R. killed in Action.

### 11 November 1918

> At 07.25 hours a wire was received from Brigade stating that hostilities would cease at 11.00 hours that day and for the Battalion to stand fast at the line reached at that hour. The Battalion forward post at the cessation of hostilities was at Q.19.d.30.40. (Sheet 45.) A Battalion parade was held at 11.30 hours. Major Bell congratulating the Battalion on its fine work during the recent operations. As an appreciation, the Maire, on behalf of the villagers of CIPLY, presented the Battalion with a Belgian Flag. Lieut. A.T. Fergusson and 2 O.R's retd from leave. 1 O.R. retd from Course. 13 O.R's retd from hospital. 1 O.R. wounded-gassed.

### 12 November 1918

> Battalion resting in billets in CIPLY. 4 O.R's proceeded on leave. 1 O.R. retd from leave. 1 O.R. retd from hospital.

Taylor and 3 O.R's arrived as reinforcements. 10 O.R's retd from hospital. 7 O.R's proceeded on leave. Appendix No. 11

## Page 19 — Appendix 9, Narrative of the advance, 8–10 November 1918

[Typed. "NARRATIVE" at top centre. "Appendix No. 9" handwritten at top. "Map Ref. Sheet 45 1/40,000" at top left. "387" at top right. Note: the middle of this page contains duplicated / overtyped lines in the original — a passage about CIPLY and NOIRCHAIN appears twice with slight variations, overlapping on the page. The transcription below follows the readable text in order, keeping the duplicated wording as written.]

> The night of 8th November 1918 the Battalion was billeted in ELOUGES (T/10) in close support to the 5th Infantry Brigade. On the 9th at dawn the 4th Infantry Brigade were ordered to jump through the 5th Brigade, and go forward as far as they could. The 18th Battalion (left) and 21st Battalion (Right) attacking, with the 19th and 20th Battalions in support. The 5th Brigade Outpost line was reported as follows; from O.32.b and d. through U.1.b and d. U.5.b. and d. to woods in U.15.a. and c. The 3rd Canadian Division were on the left and the 63rd Naval Division on the right
>
> The Battalion formed up at 0600 hours in ELOUGE and left the village in close order. The order of attack was, "A" Coy on the right "B" Coy on the left; "C" Coy right support; "D" Coy left support. On the morning the 5th Infantry Brigade Outpost line the Battalion deployed in Artillery formation and pushed forward. The impetus and dash of our men was so great that the enemy rearguard simply fled and by 1400 hours "A" Coy had established a line East and North of NOIRCHAIN (V 12) a distance in all of 14,000 yards. Here the advance was held up by an enemy machine gun nest on the high ground in the village of CIPLY. As soon as it became dusk our patrol pushed forward and by midnight the whole of CIPLY was in our hands and, "A" Company had established a line past and north of NOIRCHAIN V.18 a distance in all of 14,000 yards. Here the advance was held up by an enemy machine gun nest on the high ground in the village of CIPLY. As soon as it became dusk our patrol pushed forward and by midnight the whole of CIPLY was in our hands, and "A" Coy holding the line along the railway at Q.25. On the morning of the 10th the Supporting Battalions (19th and 20th Battalions) were due to jump through us at about 0800 hours, but as we had gone ahead so rapidly and communication becoming difficult, it was after 09.30 hours before these Battalions overtook us. meanwhile we had several patrol encounters with the enemy, who was again retiring. Our troops followed him up and our most forward post was established on the high ground on the outskirts of HYON (Q.20.c.40.60) where targets in the shape of Bosche were keenly taken advantage of by the Rifle Sections overlooking the valley. Many notable instances occurred during the advance some of which will be remembered by the troops as long as they live. The civilian population of each town that was liberated by our advancing Infantry came out of their homes and cheered and wept in turn. Flowers and victuals of all kinds were distributed to the troops and it was somewhat difficult to get through a place without offending someone by refusing their gifts. However, the Battalion had work of a more serious nature to perform, and the forward patrols had again come in touch with the enemy. A Platoon under Lieut. Smith surprised a party of German Sappers in the act of blowing a huge road mine at V.11.a.60.35. The Sapper who was about to fire the mine was shot dead, but the remainder got away in a German Red Cross Ambulance. Another Platoon under Lieut. Morgan did excellent work in clearing out German machine gun nests which might have caused serious loss to the Battalion. "B" Company, under Lieut. Goodman, completed the capture of CIPLEY (North) on the night of the 9th and was in communication with our "A" Company latterly by midnight.
>
> The operation was a very successful one and largely resulted in the capture of MONS. The spirit shown by all ranks was of the highest order, and the Battalion can look back with pride that their last attack was one of the longest on record during the war and performed with the least casualties. The total distance of the advance was approximately 16,500 yards, and the casualties amounted to twelve (two killed and ten wounded.)
