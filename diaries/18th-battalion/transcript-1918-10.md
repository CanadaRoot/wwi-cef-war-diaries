## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "D. D. & L., London, E.C. (A10266) Wt W3300/P713 750,000 1/18 Sch. 33 Forms/C2118/16."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed. `[struck]` marks text struck through in the original.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Cdn" = Canadian, "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade, "O.R." = other ranks, "S.B.R." = Small Box Respirator, "H.Q." = Headquarters, "N.C.O." = non-commissioned officer, "M.G." = Machine Gun, "R.A.P." = Regimental Aid Post, "C.C.R.C." = Canadian Corps Reinforcement Camp, "M.M." = Military Medal, "L.G." = Lewis Gun, "L.G.O." = Lewis Gun Officer, "O.P." = Observation Post, "Bde." = Brigade, "C.C.S." = Casualty Clearing Station, "R.A.F." = Royal Air Force, "R.S.M." = Regimental Sergeant Major, "Q.M." = Quartermaster, "T.O." = Transport Officer, "M.O." = Medical Officer, "P.T." = Physical Training, "R.Cs" = Roman Catholics, "Lce-Corpl." = Lance-Corporal, "Sergt." = Sergeant.
- Place names as written: Buissy Switch, Hindecourt, Haynecourt, Marquion, Ramillies, Escaudoeuvres, Thun-Leveque, Hordain, Eswars, Paillencourt, Estrun, Aniche, Wasnes, Marquette, Avesnes-le-Sec, Nouvelles, River Selle, Canal du Nord, Canal de L'Escaut, Douai-Cambrai Road, Arras-Cambrai Road.
- Map references are trench-map sheet references as written, e.g. W.9.b.40.30.
- Filming notes: the 31 image files are numbered in filming sequence 0001–0006 and 0008–0032; sequence number 0007 belongs to December 1918 p. 4, so there is no missing October page. The first pages were filmed out of printed order (printed p. 2 is sequence 0008, printed p. 3 is sequence 0006, printed p. 4 is sequence 0002). Printed pages 18 and 19 are duplicate filmings of the same disposition sketch (both carry filing number 374), and printed pages 23 and 24 are duplicate filmings of the same 24 October training syllabus (both marked appendix 8). The four map sheets on pages 4–7 are four different photographed sections of one large printed topographic map.
- No diary entry is missing: every date 1–31 October 1918 has an entry.

## Page 8 — Diary text, 1–5 October 1918

[Typed. "12" at top right. Form C. 2118. "18TH. CANADIAN BATTALION, WESTERN ONTARIO REGIMENT." at top.]

> Maps for Reference: 51.b.SE. 1/20,000 51.a.SW. 1/20,000 51.A. 1/40,000

### 1 October 1918 — Buissy Switch & Hindecourt Support Line, V.30.b & D, Map 51.b. SE.

> At 06:00 hrs. the Battalion moved across the CANAL DU NORD resting for the day in open fields south of the ARRAS ) CAMBRAI ROAD in W.23.& 24. At dusk the Battalion received orders to move to positions in Divisional support in HAYNECOURT VILLAGE (X 1b) with Bn. Hdqrs. in old German M.G. Dugout at X.9.c.10.20. In moving up enemy aircraft dropped bombs, killing one O.R. and wounding Lieut. J.E. Wylie and 9 Other Ranks. In accordance with 4th. C.I.B. O.O. No.20. these positions were put in a state of defense anticipating an enemy attack. One Other Rank admitted to Hospital.

### 2 October 1918 — Divl. Support, X.9.c.10.20

> At dawn the Battalion "stood to" anticipating an enemy attack, which however, failed to materialize. The day was spent in cleaning up by the men and the issueing of Battle Equipment. The enemy shelled the area spasmodically during the day. Major J.S. Bell and Capt. R.C. Elliott returned from leave to England. Two O.R.s from Paris Leave. Four O.R.s proceeded to First Army Rest Camp.

### 3 October 1918 — do —

> During the day having found good locations in respective Companies' Areas the cookers and water carts were brought up. The usual enemy scattered shelling throughout area during the day. Four O.R.s returned from First Army Rest Camp and four O.R.s from Hospital. Two O.R.s to hospital. Two O.R.s arrived as reinforcements.

### 4 October 1918 — do —

> During the day Battalion Hdqrs. was moved to a more suitable location in X.14. central. All Battle Stores, Bombs, Flares, etc. were formed into Company Dumps. A canteen was established to-day near one of our Company Hdqrs. enableing the men to keep well supplied with cigarettes and various eatables. Parties were sent forward to reconnoitre the MARQUION LINE in squares X.23, 17 and 12. During there was heavy enemy bombing in the area. Lieut. H.N. Benden proceeded on leave. Fourteen O.R.s arrived as reinforcements.

### 5 October 1918 — do —

> Weather Clear. Orders were received to take up a position in the MARQUION LINE in event of heavy hostile attack and all companies were held in readiness to move forward. "Wintertime" came into operation at Midnight. There was scattered shelling of area during the day with heavy bombing at night. One O.R. from Leave. One O.R. from Hospital. Two O.R.s arrived as reinforcements. One O.R. proceeded to and One O.R. returned from Corps Gas Course. One O.R. to S.O. & S. Course [uncertain] MILLS.

## Page 9 — Diary text, 6–10 October 1918

[Typed. "13" at top right. Form C. 2118.]

### 6 October 1918 — Divl. Support, X.14. Central, 51.b.SE.

> The Battalion bathed at MARQUION during the day, moving in small parties to and from the baths. There was scattered enemy shelling during the day and night, with heavy enemy bombing at night. Lieut. L. E. Hankinson was killed by shell fire while going over his platoon frontage at night. Capt. T.H.O. Rayward and 2 O.R.s returned from Leave. 1 O.R. to hospital

### 7 October 1918 — do —

> Orders were received that on an indefinite date the 2nd. Cdn. Division would attack and establish bridge-head over the CANAL L'ESCAUT. This involving a night attack reconnoitreing parties were again sent forward and "White" arm bands were issued to the Battalion. Nothing unusual occurred during the day. Considerable shelling and bombing at night. Capt. C. J. Jackson and 1 O.R. from leave. 2 O.R.s admitted to and 15 O.R.s returned from hospital. 7 O.R.s arrived as reinforcements.

### 8 October 1918 — do —

> Word was received that the proposed night attack over the canal L'ESCAUT was cancelled as far as 4th. C.I.B. was concerned. The Battalion moved during the night to trenches in X.24.a. & c. Battalion Hdqrs was located in an old German Ammunition dump on the DOUAI-CAMBRAI ROAD in X.24. Lieut. J.H. Barclay and 2 O.R.s to First Army Musketry Camp. 10 O.R.s to Canadian Corps School. Capt. C. J. Jackson attached as Instructor to 2nd. Divl. Wing. C.C.R.C. 6 O.R.s from Leave. 2 O.R.s to hospital. Two O.R.s killed in action. One O.R. wounded.

### 9 October 1918 — do —

> The Battalion rested in trenches in X.24.a. & b. in morning, moving off about noon overland to RAMILLIES being heavily shelled en route. Battalion Hdqrs. was located in a cellar of a house on the RAMILLIES — ESCAUDOEUVRES ROAD. At fourteen hrs. reconnoitring parties were sent out to locate available bridges over the L'ESCAUT CANAL. On receipt of orders at 17:00 hours the Battalion moved across the canal to positions in T.13. & 19. and prepared to pass through the 5th. C.I.B. in the following order: 18th. Battn. on LEFT, 19th. Battn. on RIGHT, 20th. Battn. in SUPPORT, and 21st. Battn. in RESERVE. Battn. Hdqrs. located Chateau ESCAUDOEUVRES. 2 O.R.s killed in action and 1 O.R. wounded.

### 10 October 1918 — Bn. "HQ" in Chateau Escaudoeuvres

> At 06:00 hours the Battalion assembled in T.20.a. & d. advancing to jumping off position in T.6.c. & d., from which position they jumped off under cover of an Artillery Barrage at 07:00 hrs. Battn. Hdqrs was located in a funk hole in the railway cutting at T.10.d.40.50. The 19th. Battalion was on the right and the 6th. Bde. on the left. The Barrage was not good, the progress of the troops being retarded half an hour on account of our own shells breaking just ahead of jumping off position. "A" and "B" Companies (Left & Right respectively) led off,

## Page 10 — Diary text, 10–11 October 1918 (continued)

[Typed. "14" at top right. Form C. 2118.]

### 10 October 1918 (continued) — Bn. "HQ" T.10.d. 40.50. Map 51.a.SW.

> "D" Co'y. in support, "C" Co'y. in Reserve. "A" Company reached old trenches in T.10.b. and T.11.a. but further progress was stopped by enemy M.G. fire from the left flank, The Brigade on the left not having gotten forward. "B" Co'y. reached the river in T.11.b. & d. where a line was formed and held. About 13:00 hrs. the 19th. Battalion with cavalry patrols were swung over from our right in an attempt to outflank the high ground in U.2.c. but were unsuccessful owing to an intense M.G. fire and the fact that our own artillery was out of range at this time. At 14:00 hrs. Lieut. L. J. Boulton with nine men went forward and established a post in railway embankment at T.11.b.60.60., east of [uncertain] RIVER which was found to be dry. No further attempt was made to advance during the day. During the days fighting the Battalion casualties were Lieut. W.A. Cash and 6 O.R.s killed in action. Lieuts. J. Spyer, H.N. Wilson, and A. E. Babcock and 70 O.R.s wounded. Lieut. J.C. Little and Bateman were reported missing. 3 O.R.s were admitted to hospital to-day. The night passed very quietly.

### 11 October 1918 — do —

> At 09:00 hrs. the Battalion in support to the 20th. and 21st. Battalions advanced from trenches in T.10.b. & T.11.a. & b. "C" and "D" Companies on right and left front respectively with "B" Co'y. in support and "A" Co'y. in Reserve. Battn. Hdqrs. was located in the cellar of a farm house at T.12.a.90.10 about 09:30 hrs. The two front line Battalions swung over too far to the left and "C" Company when line was threatened by enemy tanks took up a position in C.31.c. turning back three enemy Tanks by concentrated rifle [uncertain] Lewis Gun and Machine gun fire, turning five enemy M.G.s around for this purpose. The front line Battalions being driven back by the enemy Tanks on the left and the Imperial Brigade on the right also withdrawing "C" Company then dropped back to the support position in U.1.c., then immediately advancing in conjunction with "B" Company to sunken road in T.6.b. and U.1.a. forming a line there with the 20th. and 21st. Battalions and a few men of the W. Yorks Regt. who had swung over on our area. At 16:30 hrs. "C" and "D" Co'ys. attached to the 21st. Battn. advanced under an Artillery Barrage to N.30.d. & C.25.c. & b. but the Imperials on the right not getting forward the troops were drawn back to form a line through N.36.d. & C.31.c. & d. (in which position they were relieved by the 51st. Division). We were notified that the Battalion would be relieved by Units of the 51st. Division. Relief was complete at 23:00 hrs. "C" & "D" Companies were relieved by the Gordons and "A" & "B" Co'ys. by the Seaforth Highlanders. Upon relief the companies moved independently to billets in ESCAUDOEUVRES. During the days operations, Capt. W. J. Baxter and Lieut. T. V. Milford and 54 O.R.s were wounded. 11 O.R.s were killed in action and 18 O.R.s were wounded—gassed. Lieut. J. A. McMillan returned from leave. Capt. T.H.O. Rayward (and batman) proceeded to 12th. Instructors Course at Senior Officers School, Aldershot.

> School, Aldershot. 1 O.R. proceeded to and 1 O.R. returned from Corps Gas Course. 5 O.R.s proceeded to and 2 O.R.s returned from hospital.

### 12 October 1918 — do —

> The men rested during the day. At 17.00 hours the Battalion moved to the village of THUN-LEVEQUE the men were billeted in good billets there, the village having been recently evacuated by the enemy and only slightly damaged by shell fire. 3 O.R's admitted to hospital.

### 13 October 1918 — Bn.H.Q. Thun-Leveque, T.3.a.6.7. Map 51 A.

> The Battalion rested in billets during the day. The men were able to get a plentiful supply of a great variety of vegetables from the gardens in the village. The canals nearby were well stocked with fish and these were secured by means of exploding German cylindrical sticks in the water. Rear Details moved up from X.24. and joined the Battalion at noon. The canteen was opened up, with a good stock in the afternoon. Lieut. H.R. Sloan proceeded on leave. 6 O.R's returned from leave.

### 14 October 1918 — do —

> Upon receipt of warning order all preparations were made for going into the line. Rear Details moving back to ESWARS when the Battalion went forward at dusk Relieving the 24th. Cdn. Bn. in the Right front Line from N.5.d.20.55. to N.22.b.00.90. Battalion headquarters was located in a chateau at HORDAIN at N.16.b.70.50. This was formerly used by enemy as a hospital. Relief was complete at 20.25 hours. During the night the Battalion area was heavily shelled with H.E. and gas shells. One other rank was wounded. 2 O.R's proceeded and 1 O.R. returned from leave. 4 O.R's proceeded to and 20 O.R's returned from hospital. 3 O.R's arrived as reinforcements. Appendix No.3 (18th. Bn. Order No.D.250) and Appendix No.4 (Disposition Sketch) attached.

Stokes and 3 O.R's left Bn. H.Q. to reconnoitre Canal. No enemy were seen. There was considerable artillery activity on both sides during the day. HORDAIN was shelled with H.E. and gas shells. Visibility was fair and considerable was observed on enemy roads. 3 O.R's proceeded on and 1 O.R. returned from leave.

### 17 October 1918 — do —

> The following patrols covered our frontage last night. Lieut. J. A. McMillan and 12 O.R.s left our line at 20:00 hrs. at N.16.c.60.20. and patrolled from N.15.d.80.50. to N.10.c.40.40. thence to N.10.central returning to point of exit 05:00 hrs. reporting no enemy seen, but considerable M.G. fire from N.4. and N.5. 2 Officers and 2 O.R.s were out between 21:00 and 23:00 hrs. and located M.G. post at approximately N.5.b.50.50. and N.5.d.85.80. They reported that water was slowly flooding in N.5.c. and spread over from CANAL L'ESCAUT to within 100 yards of our post at N.5.c.99.45. Ours and hostile artillery acted during the night. Lieut. C. ### 18 October 1918 — do —

> A daylight patrol consisting of Lieut. Stokes and 3 Battalion Scouts reconnoitred our post in N.5.c.00.40. and as far as N.5.d.65.90. No enemy were seen. Hostile M.G.s were active from N.4.b.35.40. Party returned at 12:00 hrs. Very little artillery activity on either side during day. Weather fair but very misty. During the evening the Battalion heavily shelled with H.E. and Gas. One O.R. was wounded. In accordance with 4th. C.I.B. Order No. 26 and 18th. Battalion O.O. d.251. Battalion was relieved by the 20th. Cdn. Bn. Relief was complete at 21:10 hrs. Upon relief companies moved independently to THUN — LEVEQUE taking over billets formerly occupied there. 2 O.R.s proceeded on and 2 O.R.s returned from leave. 3 O.R.s returned from hospital. Appendix No.5. (OO.No.D.251) attached.

### 19 October 1918 — Bn. "HQ" T.3.a. 60.70.

> Battalion paraded in the morning to baths at ESWARS. At noon in accordance with instructions from Brigade, two Companies moved to PAILLENCOURT and two Companies moved to ESTRUN. Battn. "HQ" in Chateau at PAILLENCOURT at N.26.a.50.85. 5 O.R.s to and 1 O.R. from hospital. 2 O.R.s on leave.

## Page 13 — Diary text, 20–27 October 1918

[Typed. "17" at top right. Form C. 2118.]

### 20 & 21 October 1918 — Paillencourt, N.26.a. 50.85. Map 51A.

> The mornings were occupied with Company and Unit inspections. Parades for kit shortages were also held. The remainder of the day the Battalion rested in billets. Many of the men went fishing in the near by canals. On the 20th. Lieut. Col. Jones proceeded to PARIS on leave. Lieut. MacRae [uncertain] to England on leave. 2 O.R.s from hospital. Lieut. L. J. Boulton to Officers Rest House, PARIS — PLACE. Lieut. T. V. Milford died of wounds at No. 33. C.C.S. On the 21st. Lieut. Benden and one O.R. returned from leave. 3 O.R.s. from hospital.

### 22 October 1918 — do —

> In accordance with 4th. C.I.B. Order No. 23. and 18th. Battn. Order No. D.252 the Battalion moved in fighting order to the ANICHE area in A.50. The Battalion moved off at 08:30 hrs. and marched by WASNES—MARQUETTE— Road junction in H.14.a. arriving at destination at noon. Bn. "HQ" were located on RUE. DE BRION at C.6.d.20.70. 2 O.R.s on leave. Appendix No.6. (18th. Battn. O.O.No.D.252) attached.

### 23 October 1918 — ANICHE, C.6.d. 20.70. Map 51A.

> The billets of the entire Battalion here are without doubt the best and most comfortable that we have ever occupied in this country.
