## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The printed heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through and "18th Canadian Battalion" written in by hand. The form footer reads "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/16. Sir J. C. & S."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Infy Bgde" = Infantry Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "O.R." = other ranks, "or" = other ranks, "H.E." = high explosive, "Hun" = the enemy (as written).
- The Remarks column carries a repeated initialled mark on every row, reading "C.P.L." [uncertain], and is not transcribed row by row.
- Place names as written: Calonne, Maroc, Fosse 10, Double Crassier, Bethune, Bully-Grenay, Haillicourt, Auchel, Marles-les-Mines, Lozinghem.

## Page 3 — Diary text, 1–5 January 1917

[Handwritten "Original" at top left, with "Confidential" beneath it struck through. "January 1917" at top right. Form C. 2118.]

### 1 January 1917 — Calonne Sector

> Battalion in Brigade support in Calonne II sector. [Two lines struck through — [uncertain].] Capt. K. H. McCrimmon returned to duty with Battalion from Command duty. 5 or wounded while on working party. 2 or sick admitted to hospital.

### 2 January 1917 — do

> Position as shown yesterday Lieuts A. R. Parke, R. S. Morton, D. C. Wright arrived as reinforcements. 3 or sick admitted to hospital.

### 3 January 1917 — do

> 18th Canadian Bn will relieve 20th Canadian Bn in Maroc sector front line. B coy right front. D coy left front. A coy right support. C coy left support. Nothing unusual to report. Lieut Col. G. F. Morrison and Lieut Col. H. L. Milligan awarded D.S.O. for meritorious service in the field

### 4 January 1917 — Maroc

> Position as shown yesterday. Lieut H. N. Bowden and 2 or admitted to hospital. Our artillery did considerable damage to enemy wire. Sniper Godfrey shot Hun at M.10 d 7.9. Hun was seen to be carried away with bandage around his head.

### 5 January 1917 — Maroc

> Position as yesterday. Lieut J. J. Richardson D.S.O. proceeded on leave. Enemy endeavouring to repair wire entanglements, new wire observed at M.10 c 2.4 [uncertain]. Much work going on in Enemy front line.

## Page 4 — Diary text, 6–10 January 1917

[Handwritten "January 1917" at top right. Form C. 2118.]

### 6 January 1917 — Maroc

> Position as yesterday. Our artillery fired continuously on enemy wire and [uncertain] all day. Sniper Godfrey claimed one Hun. Major H. E. Jones proceeded to England on Senior Officers' course. 6 or arrived as reinforcements [uncertain — the casualty figure at the end of the line is illegible] killed in action

### 7 January 1917 — do

> Position as yesterday. Artillery did considerable damage to Enemy wire. Sniper Hood shot 1 Hun. Enemy aircraft active, planes patrolling their own lines. Capt. K. H. McCrimmon returned to duty with the Battalion from Command.

### 8 January 1917 — do

> Position as shown. Lieut. A. J. R. Craig arrived as reinforcement. 11 or returned from hospital. Nothing unusual. Patrols covered frontage and reported all quiet.

### 9 January 1917 — do

> A coy relieved B coy in Right front line and C coy relieved D coy in left front line. relief completed reported by code word "C.A.L.T." During the day our Artillery fired 97 shells and 37 Trench Mortars in this sector. Enemy very busy draining and repairing his trenches. [uncertain] reported "Died of Wounds"

### 10 January 1917 — do

> Position as reported yesterday. 2 or sick admitted to hospital, 1 or Died of Sickness. Enemy fired 20 H.E.s to our rear in direction of [uncertain] "11" at Bethune. Enemy Anti aircraft active against our planes which patrolled our lines all morning.

## Page 5 — Diary text, 11–14 January 1917

[Handwritten "January 1917" at top right. Form C. 2118.]

### 11 January 1917 — Maroc

> Position as yesterday. During afternoon our Artillery bombarded all enemy lines between Fosse 10 and Double Crassier. Enemy replied but did no damage. 3 or admitted to hospital sick.

### 12 January 1917 — do

> Raid was planned for operation this morning at 7.45 A.M. in conjunction with 5th Canadian Infantry Brigade. The object of which was to cause casualties to the enemy, secure booty and wreck all dugouts in the system of enemy trenches. This operation was postponed owing to the fact that the enemy wire was not sufficiently broken up and there being no favorable wind for smoke barrage.

### 13 January 1917 — do

> B coy was relieved in front line by a composite coy of the 20th Battalion. A coy 18th Battalion moved into reserve at Calonne and came under the orders of 20th Canadian Bn. D coy of this Battalion relieved C coy in left subsector of front line. 1 or wounded, 2 or sick admitted to hospital.

### 14 January 1917 — do

> Position as shown yesterday. A dummy raid will be carried out against enemy trenches at M.4. c. 7.6 & to M.4. c. 9.0 tomorrow, at Zero hours (11.30 pm [uncertain]). Rifle Grenades and smoke bombs will be used, providing wind is favorable for smoke barrage. Our artillery heavily bombarded wire and enemy trenches in this sector.

## Page 6 — Diary text, 15–18 January 1917

[Handwritten "January 1917" at top right. Form C. 2118.]

### 15 January 1917 — Maroc + Calonne

> operation as shown in yesterday's diary is postponed on account of the 4th Canadian Infy Bgde. postponing the whole operation. Major T. A. McIntosh appointed Adjutant from 1.12.16. Our artillery continuously bombarding enemy wire trenches. 2 or reported wounded 2 or sick admitted to hospital.

### 16 January 1917 — do

> Position unchanged. Our Stokes mortar made several direct hits on Enemy wire trenches doing considerable damage. Enemy replied with 20 H.E.s to our support and reserve lines. 2 or sick admitted to hospital.

### 17 January 1917 — do

> C. company relieved A company in front line. A company moved into former position occupied by C coy in Calonne. D coy moved up from Calonne (support) to Maroc.

### 17/18 January 1917, 11.30 pm — do

> The 20th Canadian Bn together with 21st Battalion carried out an operation against enemy trenches. 18th Battalion held front line trenches while this raid was carried out. A number of prisoners were taken and the third line of Enemy trenches was entered. Casualties sustained by the 18th Canadian Battalion were 14 wounded. Sgt [uncertain] although wounded remained on duty with his company.

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: 5 other ranks wounded on a working party 1 January; 1 other rank killed in action [uncertain figure] 6 January; 1 other rank died of wounds 9 January; 1 other rank died of sickness 10 January; 1 other rank wounded 13 January; 2 other ranks wounded 15 January; 14 other ranks wounded 17/18 January while holding the line during the 20th/21st Battalion raid. No other battle casualties are recorded; the rest of the month's losses are sickness and hospital admissions.
- Movements: the battalion holds the Maroc sector front line (relieving the 20th Battalion 3 January), with Brigade Support at Calonne; relieved in the front line by the 14th Battalion 18 January, moves to billets at Bully-Grenay, then marches to the Canadian Training Area at Haillicourt 19 January for training; marches to billets at Auchel 30 January via Marles-les-Mines and Lozinghem.
- Officers named: Capt. K. H. McCrimmon (returned to duty from Command, 1 and 7 January); Lieuts A. R. Parke, R. S. Morton, D. C. Wright (reinforcements, 2 January); Lieut Col. G. F. Morrison and Lieut Col. H. L. Milligan (awarded the D.S.O. for meritorious service in the field, 3 January); Lieut H. N. Bowden (to hospital, 4 January); Lieut J. J. Richardson, D.S.O. (on leave 5 January; acting Adjutant Major while commanding a company, 18 January); Major H. E. Jones (to England on Senior Officers' course, 6 January); Lieut A. J. R. Craig (reinforcement, 8 January); Major T. A. McIntosh (appointed Adjutant from 1 December 1916, recorded 15 January); Capt J. P. Chrystal (returned from leave, 24 January).
- Notable: a planned raid with the 5th Canadian Infantry Brigade on 12 January was postponed for uncut enemy wire and no favourable wind for a smoke barrage; a dummy raid was planned for 14/15 January and the operation postponed again by the 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade. On the night of 17/18 January the 20th and 21st Battalions raided the enemy trenches while the 18th held the front line — prisoners were taken and the enemy's third line entered. Snipers Godfrey and Hood each claim a German shot. The battalion spent the second half of the month training at Haillicourt (drill, bayonet fighting, physical training, route marches).
- No date gaps: entries run 1–31 January without a break.
