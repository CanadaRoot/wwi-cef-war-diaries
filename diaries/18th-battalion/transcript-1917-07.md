## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. Two form printings appear in this part: the sheets for 1–21 July carry the footer "T2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."; the sheets from 21 July on carry the footer "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D. D. & L. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118."
- The diary sheets carry their own small page numbers at the head of the Remarks column: 3, 4, 5, 6, (no number visible on the 21–22 July sheet), 8, 9.
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Bde" = Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "H.Q." / "H.Qrs" = Headquarters, "T.M." = Trench Mortar, "O.R." / "ors" = other ranks, "Q.M." = Quartermaster, "N.C.O." = Non-Commissioned Officer, "O.O." = Operation Order, "T.O." = Transport Officer.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated initialled flourish on every row, appearing to read "C.P.L." — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row.
- Place names as written: Barlin, Hersin-Coupigny, Camblain-le Abbè, Bouvigny Woods / Bouvigny Huts, Bully Grenay, Lens, Laurent, Cite St Pierre, Bovigny, Bovigny Chateau, Aix Noulette Farm.

## Page 5 — Diary text, 9–12/13 July 1917

[Handwritten "18th Canadian Battalion" at top, "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through, "(Erase heading not required.)" printed below. Army Form C. 2118. Sheet number "4" at the head of the Remarks column. The Place column reads "Bouvigny Wood" for 9 July and is blank for 10–12 July; the last entry has "Night 12/13." in the Place column.]

### 9 July 1917 — Bouvigny Wood

> Training during the morning. Postponed Church Parade held. About midday (12.40pm). Shell dropped by long range gun struck one of huts in which H.Q. details and portion of D Coy were billeted. 5 ors Killed. 4 ors Died of Wounds. 35 Wounded.

### 10 July 1917

> Inspection by Commanding Officer, preparatory to going into the line. 4th Brigade relieved 5th Brigade in LENS Sector. 18th Battalion relieved 26th Battalion in LENS Left Sector. H.Qrs at. M.23.d.3.9

### 11 July 1917

> Relief complete 1.30 am. Front line fairly quiet. Slight T.M. activity, shelling practically nil.

### 12 July 1917

> Front line still quiet, weather fine.

### Night of 12/13 July 1917

> 4th and 6th Brigades changed places. 4th Brigade taking over LAURENT Sector. 18th Battalion in Brigade support in Cite St Pierre. with H.Qrs at M.16.b.55.90. Battalion billeted in cellars formerly occupied by the Huns and fairly strongly fortified.

### 20 July 1917

> Day Quiet. Support line shelled at night. Ration party of A Coy caught in shelled area. and 2 men killed and 8 wounded. Our retaliation showed marked effect in lessening Enemy Trench Mortar activity during night 20/21st.

## Notes on this part

- Casualties recorded in these pages: 9 July — a long-range shell struck a hut at Bouvigny Wood, 5 ors killed, 4 ors died of wounds, 35 wounded. Night of 19/20 July — Lieut L. C. Jarvis and 1 O.r. killed by a trench mortar in the front line. 20 July — an A Coy ration party caught in a shelled area, 2 men killed and 8 wounded. 22 July — 3 men wounded. That is 13 dead and 46 wounded in this part of the month; no other battle casualties are recorded in these pages.
- Movements: the battalion is at Barlin on 1 July, moves into Divisional Reserve at Bouvigny Woods on 3 July, goes into the Lens Left Sector on 10 July (relieving the 26th Battalion), moves to Brigade support at Cite St Pierre on the night of 12/13 July, returns to the Lens Left Sector on the night of 16/17 July (relieving the 28th Battalion), and is relieved by the 29th Battalion on 22 July, going into Divisional Reserve at Bovigny village. The rest of the month is training for an attack; on 31 July the taking over of the battle front is postponed for 2 days.
- Officers named: Maj Gen. Simms (conducted the Brigade Church parade, 1 July); Lieut L. C. Jarvis (killed 19/20 July); Lieut T. R. Dougall (three daring reconnaissances in No Man's Land, 22 July); Lieut Parsons (named in O.O. No. D 136); W. H. Lewis, Lieutenant, Acting Adjutant (signed the training syllabus and O.O. No. D 136); H. E. Jones, Lieut. Col., Commanding (signed the diary, 31 July).
- Notable: Canadian Corps sports on 2 July — the 18th Battalion football team won the final 2–0 and Pte Laird of B Coy won the high jump. Heavy enemy trench-mortar activity 17–22 July, with the battalion's observers taking cross-bearings on enemy mortar flashes so the artillery could retaliate; the retaliation is recorded as very effective, nearly silencing the enemy mortars before daylight on 20 July. Special training for attack 25–28 July, including a brigade attack over a marked area on 27 July. The proposed Brigade Church parade on 29 July was cancelled for very heavy rain.
- Date gap: there is no entry for 23 July 1917 — the diary jumps from 22 to 24 July.
- Cover/contents disagreements (contents list vs. diary text and appendices in this part):
  - Contents describes O.O. No. D 136 as the 18th Bn. relieving the 23rd Bn. in the Bouvigny Huts; the order itself (page 13) says the 18th will relieve the 28th Canadian Infantry Battalion.
  - Contents describes Order D. 137 as the 18th relieving the 28th Bn. on 10–11 July; the diary for 10 July records the 18th relieving the 26th Battalion.
  - Contents describes Order D. 138 as the 18th being relieved by the 31st Battalion on 12–13 July; the diary for the night of 12/13 July records the 4th and 6th Brigades changing places, with the 18th going into Brigade support — no relief by the 31st Battalion is recorded.
  - Contents describes Order No. 140 as the 18th relieving the 29th Bn. on 22–23 July; the diary for 22 July records the 29th Battalion relieving the 18th (the reverse).
  - Page 2 is an "Original Copy" title sheet, not the contents list; the contents list is on page 3.
- The contents list states "Diary Text 7 Pages", which matches the seven diary sheets in this part (file pages 4–10). The diary sheets are numbered 3–9, with no number visible on the 21–22 July sheet.
- The 21 July entry runs across pages 7–8, and the paragraph about Lieut Dougall runs across pages 8–9.

## Notes on these pages

- No Form C. 2118 diary text appears in this range: all 13 pages are appendices — three training syllabi (pages 14, 16, 18), their four filmed reverse sides (pages 15, 17, 19, 22, which carry only show-through and a handwritten routing mark — "O.C." on page 15, "Paymaster" on pages 17 and 19, "Paymaster (War Diary)" on page 22), and six operation orders (pages 20, 21, 23, 24, 25, 26).
- Documents in this range span 5 July (training syllabus) to the night of 22/23 July 1917 (relief by the 29th Battalion, Order D 140). No casualties are recorded on any of these pages.
- Officers named: Lieut. W. H. Lewis, Acting Adjutant (signed/typed on all three training syllabi; the operation orders carry an illegible signature in the same hand).
- Movements: the battalion was in training 5–9 July (close order drill, box respirator drill, physical drill and bayonet fighting, platoon/company in attack, recreational training in the afternoons); on the night of 10/11 July it relieved the 25th Battalion in the Lens Section, Left Sub Section (Order D 137), with "A" right, "B" centre, "D" left and "C" in support, Battalion HQ at M.23.a.2.6.
- The Corps Commander was expected to visit the battalion on its training ground between 8.45 and 9.30 a.m. (NOTE on the 7 July syllabus, page 16).
- Anomalies:
  - Two different orders are both numbered D 139: Order No. D 139 d/16.7.17 (page 23), under which the 4th Brigade relieves the 6th Brigade and the 18th relieves the 28th Battalion on the night of 16/17 July; and Operation Order No. D 139, 19.7.17 (page 21), under which the 4th and 6th Brigades exchange sectors and the 18th is relieved by the 21st Battalion on the night of 18/19 July. The two supplementary D 139 orders (pages 24–25) order company-level reliefs in the line on the nights of 18/19 and 20/21 July, which sit uneasily with the battalion-level relief ordered for 18/19 July on page 21.
  - Order D 140 (page 26, dated 21.7.17) then has the 6th Brigade relieve the 4th and 5th Brigades on the night of 22/23 July, with the 18th relieved by the 29th Battalion and proceeding to billets in Bouvigny — i.e. the 4th Brigade's tour in the Lens Section ends after less than a fortnight, despite the exchange ordered on page 21.
  - The printed appendix pagination on the sheets runs 12–21 (pages 14, 16, 18, 20, 21, 23, 24, 25, 26 carry printed numbers 12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21; page 21 also carries "52" and pages 23–26 carry "53"–"56" at top right), while the film page numbers run 14–26 — the difference is the unnumbered reverse sheets filmed between them.
  - The 9 July syllabus (page 18) times Battalion Drill "at 11.36 a.m. sharp" — as written, not a round hour.
  - The 5 July syllabus (page 14) has a heavily struck-out line in the "Platoon in attack" detail that cannot be read.
