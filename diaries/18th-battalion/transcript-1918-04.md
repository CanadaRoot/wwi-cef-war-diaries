## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "(Apagn. Wt. W2544/M299 75,000 1/17. D. D. & L., Ltd. Forms/C.2118/14." (variant spellings of the imprint occur across pages).
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed. `[struck]` marks text struck through in the original.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Cdn" = Canadian, "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade, "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles, "O.R." = other ranks, "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer, "S.B.R." = Small Box Respirator, "H.Q." = Headquarters, "N.C.O." = non-commissioned officer, "M.G." = Machine Gun, "A.D.S." = Advanced Dressing Station, "R.A.P." = Regimental Aid Post, "C.T." = communication trench, "Q.M." = Quartermaster, "R.S.M." = Regimental Sergeant Major, "M.O." = Medical Officer, "T.O." = Transport Officer, "L.G.O." = Lewis Gun Officer, "I.O." = Intelligence Officer, "S.B." = stretcher bearer, "G.S." = General Service, "S.A.A." = small arms ammunition, "O.C." = Officer Commanding.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished initial on every diary row, resembling "C.P.H." — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row except where it carries an appendix reference or other note.
- Place names as written: Neuville Vitasse, Neuville St Vaast, Berles au Bois, Brethencourt, Wailly, Agny, Bailleuval, Beaurains, Mercatel, Telegraph Hill Switch, Purple Line, Purvis Line.
- Map references are trench-map sheet references as written, e.g. N.13.C.60.80.
- Disposition-state tables: blank cells are shown as —. Figures are transcribed as read, even where the original arithmetic does not reconcile; discrepancies are noted page by page rather than corrected.

## Page 4 — Diary text, 1–2 April 1918

[Handwritten "18th CANADIAN BATTALION" and "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY." at top, "APRIL 1918." at top right, "Confidential" at top left. Form C. 2118. Printed instruction at top left: "Instructions regarding War Diaries and Intelligence Summaries are contained in F. S. Regs., Part II. and the Staff Manual respectively. Title pages will be prepared in manuscript." Form footer: "(Apagn. Wt. W2544/M299 75,000 1/17. D. D. & L., Ltd. Forms/C.2118/14." — variant imprint spellings occur across pages.]

[Maps for Reference line, across the top of the Summary column: "MAPS for Reference 51 C. and 51 B. N.E and S.E and 51 b S.W."]

### 1 April 1918 — 51 C S.E.

> 18th Canadian Battalion in support to 21st Canadian Battalion.
>
> Approximately 670 [uncertain — word after the number illegible] Strength.

### Night 1/2 April 1918 — Front line at Neuville Vitasse; M.18. b.c. & d. [and] N.13.a & c.

> During night of 1/2nd 18th Canadian Battalion relieved 21st Canadian Bn in Front line at Neuville Vitasse. Disposition on completion of relief being, 'C' company in Right front line at N.13.C.60.80 and D coy left Front line at N.13.a.75.50 [uncertain — second figure partly unclear] B coy in left close support and A coy in right close support. This relief was completed at 4 a.m. without casualty. H.Q. being located at M.18.b 15.50.

### 2 April 1918

> Position as shown yesterday. There was no shelter in any of the trenches for the men, so funk holes were dug and improved as opportunity occurred. In answer to S.O.S on our left flank we opened up a heavy barrage on Enemy front and support lines, which drew retaliation from the enemy. We suffered several casualties. Lieut G.N. TUCKER being wounded. 3 ors killed in action 30 wounded. Owing to erratic shelling by the enemy it was impossible to keep telephonic communication with each Company but this communication was kept up with Brigade H.Q. with the exception of short intervals. Communication to companies was done by Runners working at night, but some dangerous trips were made during daylight, parts of the way being overlooked in aerial view of the enemy, and over ground continually swept by M.G. fire.

## Page 5 — Diary text, 3–5 April 1918

[Form C. 2118, "18th Canadian Battalion", "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY." Place column throughout: "M.18 b.c and d N.13 a and c."]

### 3 April 1918

> Considerable movement was observed in village (Neuville St Vaast) which is just in front of our line, and our artillery were very active, putting several barrages on the village, at one time making two direct hits on enemy parties. A patrol of 6 ors under Lieut Spencer on returning from a reconnaissance patrol ran into an enemy M.G. which opened fire wounding 2 of the Scouts. Patrol covered the whole Battalion frontage during the night. 1 or killed in action, 3 ors wounded. 6 ors arrived as reinforcements.

### 4 April 1918

> During night of 3/4th our frontage (Right Support) was heavily shelled and [uncertain — word reads like "torraged"] from 1 a.m. to 9.30 a.m. considerable number of gas shells being used in this shelling. Our Artillery retaliated with a barrage of heavies on enemy front and support lines. A patrol penetrated enemy front line and reported trenches unoccupied with the exception of one M.G. Lieut. R.E LAWRANCE and 7 ors were wounded during enemy shelling. 2 ors killed in action.

### 5 April 1918

> A patrol of 13 men under Lieut S.G. STOKES, and Sgt. Burns at about 5 p.m. to clear out the French [uncertain — word after "French" illegible] through N.19.a.60.85 in order to establish a protection on the flank of both our front and support line. They successfully bombed down about 150 yds then withdrew a short distance and established a post at N.9.a.70.90. They inflicted many casualties on the enemy all of whom belonged to the 15 R.I.R which identification was [uncertain — word reads "normal" or similar]. The party carried out this piece of work without casualty.

### 5 April 1918 (continued, at foot of page)

> At 9.30 this morning the Enemy attempted to raid the trenches of our right flank Battalion (19th Canadian Bn).

### 5 April 1918 (continued)

> coming over in 3 waves (about 300) but only succeeded in reaching our wire and suffering very heavy casualties. ### 6 April 1918 — Relief, 3.30 p.m.

> During night of 5/6th Battalion was relieved by the 26th Canadian Battalion. The relief was delayed owing to the exceptional darkness of the night. On relief being completed Battalion marched to billets at Berles au Bois.

[Form C. 2118, "18th Canadian Battalion", "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY." Place column, written diagonally: "Purple Reserve" / "Purple System".]

### 10 April 1918

> Day spent in cleaning, repairing trenches and building fire steps. At night reconnoitring parties reconnoitred assembly area, [uncertain — word illegible] support positions with a view to garrisoning in event of an attack.

### 11 April 1918

> Fatigue Parties cleaning and repairing trenches. At 6.30 a.m enemy laid heavy Barrage on our left Bn frontage till 9.30 a.m when he attacked but was repulsed with heavy casualties. 1 or wounded.

### 12 April 1918

> Working parties repairing and improving trenches. Lieut W.N. ROONEY and Lt. ADDY and 4 ors wounded during enemy shelling. 60. ors arrived as reinforcements.

### 13 April 1918

> Working parties repairing and improving trenches. Lieut W.H.T. Kroltzer [uncertain — surname spelling not certain] and 50 rs attached to new 2nd Cdn. M.G. Bn. 2 ors admitted to Hospital

### 14 April 1918

> Three working Parties repairing the trenches occupied. Lieut O.B. Brown and 15 ors. arrived as reinforcements. 5. ors returned from Hospital

### 15 April 1918

> Working parties as usual repairing trenches, work was commenced on digging new trench from S.11a.60.90 to S.11.a.50.95. Situation quiet. 3 ors admitted to hospital. 2. ors killed in action.

### 16 April 1918

> During night of 16/17th Battalion was relieved in Purple line (Brigade reserve) by the 22nd Canadian Bn. On relief 18th Battalion moved to billets in Berles au Bois. Relief was completed at 9.05 p.m. 50 ors. arrived as reinforcements.

## Page 9 — Diary text, 22 April (contd.)–27 April 1918

[Form C. 2118, "18th Canadian Battalion", "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY."]

### 22 April 1918 (continued)

> German sentry was observed in a post at N.19.b.80.20 and Sgt Manby with 4 scouts set out to secure him but were held up by wire in front of this post. Sgt Manby therefore shot the sentry. Germans were observed in the Church of Neuville Vitasse which is being used as an O.P.

### 23 April 1918

> Position same. Nothing unusual occurred. Enemy aerial activity on both sides. 1 or wounded and 2 ors admitted to hospital.

### 24 April 1918

> Working parties during night clearing and improving trenches in the Intermediate line. Patrols covered Battalion frontage with nothing unusual to report. 3 ors wounded.

### 25 April 1918 — Relief. Map. Sheet 51 B.

> During night 24/25th 18th Battalion was relieved in front line by 21st Canadian Bn. Relief being completed at 11.30 pm the 18th Battalion moved back into Support trenches at Telegraph Hill Switch. ## Notes

- **Diary date range:** 1–30 April 1918, complete — no diary days are missing. The diary text (pp. 4–10) runs continuously, 1 April to 30 April, and is signed by the Commanding Officer on p. 10. The C.O.'s signature is illegible — it appears to begin "W. E." — [uncertain], Lt.-Col., Cmdg 18th Canadian Infy. Bn.
- **Duplicates:** p. 18 is a faint carbon copy of the p. 17 syllabus (Saturday 20 April); p. 21 is a clear copy of the p. 19 syllabus (Friday 19 April); pp. 23, 24 and 26 are three copies of the Wednesday 17 April syllabus, with small differences in bath hours, Pay Parade times/company order, and a handwritten HQ note on p. 24 only; p. 41 is a fainter carbon copy of the p. 40 Defence Scheme p. 1; p. 64 is a duplicate filming of the p. 63 disposition state (24 April, Sheet No. 2); pp. 77 and 78 are the same Situation Map (UTS No. 341, 31.3.18) filmed twice.
- **Out of order:** the appendices are not in chronological order. The 20 April and 1 May syllabuses (Appendix 3, pp. 15–18) are filed before the 19 April syllabus (pp. 19–21) and the 17 April material (pp. 22–26); the 29 April syllabus (p. 27) precedes the orders of 29 March–9 April (pp. 28–36); the Condition of Defences report of 10 April (pp. 34–35) precedes Order D.204 of 9 April (p. 36); the 9 April disposition state, Sheet #1 (p. 54), is filed after the undated and 30 April company states (pp. 49–52); and the 1/2 April disposition states (pp. 66–72) are filed last, after the 9, 20, 23 and 24 April states (pp. 55–65). Appendix I (pp. 11–14) lists syllabuses for 23–27 April but no syllabus for Thursday 25 April is present. The contents page (p. 2) lists Appendices 2 and 4–16, including Orders D.201, D.203, D.204, D.205, D.206, D.207 and D.208; Order D.201 is filed with the orders (p. 28) and the others are all present in this month's pages.

- **Notable events:**
  - 1/2 April — the battalion relieves the 21st Bn in the front line at Neuville Vitasse, completed by 4 a.m. without casualty.
  - 2 April — heavy enemy shelling and S.O.S. barrage; Lieut Tucker wounded, 3 killed and 30 wounded; telephone communication to companies lost, runners used instead.
  - 5 April — a 13-man patrol under Lieut Stokes bombs 150 yards along the flank at N.19.a.60.85 against the 15th Reserve Infantry Regiment without casualty; the same morning the enemy raids the neighbouring 19th Bn in three waves of about 300 men and is repulsed with very heavy casualties.
  - 9 April — the battalion moves into the Purple Line system (Brigade reserve) on Vimy Ridge Day, relieving the 31st Bn.
  - 11 April — an enemy attack on the left battalion's frontage, after a three-hour barrage, is repulsed with heavy casualties.
  - 20/21 April — the battalion relieves the 28th Bn in the front line, halting at Wailly for a hot meal en route.
  - 22 April — Sgt Manby shoots a German sentry at N.19.b.80.20 after the patrol is held up by wire; Germans are observed using the church at Neuville Vitasse as an observation post.
  - 30 April — notification of the Italian Bronze Medal for Military Valor to Sgt. J. S. Farmer (killed in action).
