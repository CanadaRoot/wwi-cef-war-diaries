## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Bgde" = Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "O.R." = other ranks, "D.R.O." = Divisional Routine Order, "R.F.C." = Royal Flying Corps, "C.A.M.C." = Canadian Army Medical Corps, "M.O." = Medical Officer, "G.H.Q." = General Headquarters.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished initial on every row that is not legible — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row.
- Place names as written: Scottish Wood, Voormezeele, D Camp, I Camp, Corps Reserve, Brigade Reserve.

## Page 4 — Diary text, 8–13 May 1916

[Handwritten "Original Confidential" at top left. "WAR DIARY 18th Canadian Battalion / INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY / Month of May 1916". Form C. 2118.]

### 8 May 1916 — D Camp, 7 pm

> Battalion moved to Brigade Reserve at SCOTTISH WOOD and VOORMEZEELE. A and D coys garrison in VOORMEZEELE. LIEUT. R.H. SHEFFIELD temporarily appointed to command "A" coy.

### 9 May 1916 — Brigade Reserve

> Battalion position as yesterday. MAJOR. G.W. NELSON returned to Battalion. 9.30pm. Battalion relieved 29th Can Bn in centre section, trenches 15-16-17-18 and 19. 21st Can Bn relieved 18th Battn in Brigade reserve. 9 o.rs arrived as reinforcements 3 o.rs admitted to hospital. 1 o.r. returned from hospital.

[Transcriber's note: "29th" is written over "31st" in the relief sentence, and "relieved 31st Bn" also appears as a small note at the top of the Summary column — the diarist corrected the battalion number.]

> Position as yesterday. MAJOR. G.W. NELSON relieved CAPT. T.C. LAMB (paymaster) as Adjutant. 6 o.rs wounded. 3 o.rs admitted to hospital. 2 o.rs returned from hospital. 20th Can Bn on our right. 19th Can Bn on our left.

### 11 May 1916 — 9 pm

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: 1 other rank died 1 May; 1 other rank died of wounds 3 May; 2 other ranks killed 12 May; 26 other ranks wounded in total (1 on 6 May, 6 on 10 May, 11 on 11 May, 3 on 12 May, 4 on 13 May, 1 on 15 May). No officers are recorded killed or wounded. The rest of the month's losses are sickness and hospital admissions, which run heavily all month (notably 11 admitted 22 May and 10 admitted 28 May).
- Movements: the battalion begins May in camp (place name cut off at the edge of the page image), moves to D Camp 4 May, then to Brigade Reserve at Scottish Wood and Voormezeele 8 May. On 9 May it relieves the 29th Battalion in the centre section (trenches 15–19, later extended to trench 20) and holds the front line until relieved by the 21st Battalion 13 May. On 14 May it moves into Corps Reserve at I Camp, where it remains for the rest of the month on routine, baths, clothing parades and 400-man fatigue parties to the front line. On the night of 30–31 May the 4th Brigade relieves the 1st Brigade and the 18th Battalion goes into local reserve (Appendix I).

- Notable: the battalion spent most of the month (14–29 May) in Corps Reserve at I Camp, sending 400-man fatigue parties to the front line almost nightly. The front-line tour 9–13 May in trenches 15–19 (extended to 20) produced the month's only battle casualties. The month ends with the brigade-level relief of the 1st Brigade on the night of 30–31 May, the 18th Battalion going into local reserve at Bluff Tunnel, Bedford House and H 27 b.5.6. (Appendix I).
- Anomalies and disagreements: (1) The place name for 1–3 May is cut off at the left edge of the page image; only the ending "...NCHELST" is visible. (2) On 9 May the diarist corrected "31st" to "29th" Canadian Battalion in the relief sentence. (3) On 14 May a superscript after "25th Can Bn" reads like "3rd" but is not clearly legible. (4) On 25 May Lieut. R. H. Martin is "seconded from 31st April 1916" — April has only 30 days, so the date as written is impossible. (5) The Adjutant's surname is written "BARTER" in the diary entry of 17 May but "BAXTER" in the signature to the Move Orders (Appendix I). (6) The lieutenant who went on leave 21 May is written "GWINN" on that date but "GWYN" on his return 30 May. (7) The diary pages are all signed "H. Milligan Major, Comdg" at the foot, including the pages covering 19–29 May when the diary itself records Milligan on leave and Major Jones in command — the signatures were evidently added later.
- No date gaps: entries run 1–31 May without a break.
