## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished signature on every row that is not legible — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Bde" = Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "O.R." = other ranks, "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding, "M.G." = Machine Gun, "R.S.M." = Regimental Sergeant Major, "C.A.M.C." = Canadian Army Medical Corps, "Y.M.C.A." = Young Men's Christian Association.
- Reference map: WILLERVAL 1/20,000.
- Place names as written: NEUVILLE ST VAST, BARLIN, THELUS CAVE, RIDGE LINE, RUITZ, COUPIGNY.

## Page 3 — Diary text, 1–12 June 1917

[Typed. "Confidential" at top left, "WAR DIARY", "18th. (WESTERN ONTARIO) CANADIAN BATTALION.", "JUNE 1917", Army Form C. 2118. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is struck through. Map reference: WILLERVAL 1/20,000.]

### 1 June 1917

> Battalion in Brigade Support with H.Qrs at T.27.d.3.5. During the night Battalion was relieved by 15th. Canadian Battalion and the whole of the 4th, Brigade moved back to RIDGE LINE with H.Qrs at THELUS CAVE.

### 2 June 1917 — 12 noon

> Battalion relieved by the 7th. Canadian Battalion and moved back into Reserve camp near NEUVILLE ST VAST. Lieut. H.N. Bowden admitted to hospital (sick).

### 3 June 1917 — 10 a.m.

> 4th. Canadian Infantry Brigade relieved by 1st. Canadian Infantry Brigade. 18th. Battalion marched back to Billets in BARLIN. 6. o.rs rejoined from Hospital. 2 Instructors from Canadian Corps Gymnastic Staff attached to Battalion for instructional purposes.

### 4 June 1917 — Barlin

> Battalion resting and settling down in billets.

### 5 June 1917 — Barlin

> Battalion Pay and Bath parades. 9. o.rs returned from hospital.

### 6 June 1917 — Barlin

> Clothing parades and completion of Bath parades. Lieut. C.H. Biscoe and 42 o.rs arrived as reinforcements, many of these being old men rejoining.

### 7 June 1917 — Barlin

> Inspection of Companies by Company Commanders. Syllabus of training to be carried out (attached) 7 o.rs arrived as reinforcements.

### 8 June 1917 — Barlin

> Morning. Company parades and training. Specialists training under qualified instructors. Afternoon. Recreational training and sports. 2. o.rs admitted to hospital.

### 9 June 1917 — Barlin

> Morning. Physical training under special instructors. Afternoon. Recreational training.

### 10 June 1917 — Barlin, 9 a.m.

> Church parade. Remainder of the day spent in recreation. 3. o.rs admitted to hospital.

### 11 June 1917 — Barlin

> Morning. Training according to syllabus. Afternoon, recreational training. Lieut. L.A. Bissell and 18 o.rs arrived as reinforcements.

### 12 June 1917 — Barlin

> Morning. "C" company on specialist training under specialist Officers, "A. B. & D" coys training according to syllabus. Lieut. H.N. Bowden and 15. o.rs returned from hospital.

## Page 4 — Diary text, 12th (continued)–22 June 1917

[Typed. "(2)" at top, "WAR DIARY", "18th. (WESTERN ONTARIO) CANADIAN BATTALION.", "JUNE 1917.", Army Form C. 2118.]

### 12 June 1917 (continued) — Barlin

> A Horse show was held by Brigade at which we gained "1 second and 2 third prizes.

### 13 June 1917 — Barlin, Morning

> "B" company at ranges on Musketry instruction. Special training by R.S.M. Price of N.C.Os. 52 o.rs arrived as reinforcements. 3 o.rs returned from hospital.

### 14 June 1917 — Barlin

> Battalion training according to syllabus. 3 o.rs accidently wounded.

### 15 June 1917 — Barlin

> Battalion Sports held at RUITZ. Races, Tug of War, Football, and Wrestling during the day and a concert in the Evening by the Battalion Band.

### 16 June 1917 — Barlin, Morning

> Training according to syllabus. 48. o.rs arrived as reinforcements. 5. o.rs returned from hospital.

### 17 June 1917 — Barlin, 9 a.m.

> Church parade. "C" company on Musketry at Ranges. Afternoon being spent in recreational exercise. Battalion Football team played and beat the 4th M.G. coy in competion for the Divisional Championship.

### 18 June 1917 — Barlin, 9.15 a.m.

> Battalion parade to Brigade Sports. Battalion Football team making a draw with 20th Battalion for Brigade championship. Battalion Baseball team beating 21st. Battalion for Brigade championship. Prizes were presented at the close by Brig-General R. Rennie, C.M.G., M.V.O., D.S.O.

### 19 June 1917 — Barlin, Morning

> "A" and "D" companies on special training under Physical instructors. "B" and "C" on usual syllabus training. Afternoon. Battalion Pay parades.

### 20 June 1917 — Barlin

> Syllabus training. 11.15 a.m. Battalion parade.

### 21 June 1917 — Barlin

> "D" company on Musketry at Ranges. "C" company on Specialist training, remainder of Battalion carrying on with usual syllabus of training. 5. o.rs admitted to hospital.

### 22 June 1917 — Barlin

> Training according to syllabus. Lt-Col. L.E. Jones proceeded on leave. Major. J.A. McIntosh assumed command of Battalion.

## Page 5 — Diary text, 23rd–30 June 1917, with Victoria Cross citation

[Typed. "WAR DIARY", "18th. (WESTERN ONTARIO) CANADIAN BATTALION.", "JUNE 1917.", Army Form C. 2118.]

### 23 June 1917 (continued) — Barlin

> Company bath parades in morning. Voluntary parade to Divisional sports in the afternoon. Lieuts. W.S. Caldwell, J.G. Doherty, J.D. Parsons arrived as reinforcements.

### 24 June 1917 — Barlin, 9 a.m.

> Church parade. 2 o.rs returned from hospital. Lieut. J.M. Fisher returned from leave.

### 25 June 1917 — Barlin

> Parade of all casuals for Musketry training at ranges. Company training as per syllabus. 1 o.r admitted to hospital (injured).

### 26 June 1917 — Barlin

> Company inspections. Platoon training in attack, Physical training and Bayonet fighting. Major. W.J. Gander and 4 o.rs admitted to hospital (sick).

### 27 June 1917 — Barlin

> Commanding Officer's inspection of Companies and Units. 3 o.rs returned from hospital. Capt. J.S. Bell rejoined Battalion from Divisional School.

### 28 June 1917 — Barlin, 11 a.m.

> Battalion inspected by G.O.C. 2nd. Canadian Division. Battalion paraded in full marching order at 10 a.m. and was highly complimented on their smart appearance.

### 29 June 1917 — Barlin

> Platoon training in attack and Bayonet fighting. Lieuts. H.L. Mitchell and E.B. Johnson arrived as reinforcements.

### 30 June 1917 — Barlin

> Company inspections. Close order and Arm drill. Battalion parade at 11.00 a.m. 2 o.rs admitted to hospital.

[Typed at the foot of the diary page, below the 30 June entry:]

> 53730 L/Sgt. Sifton E.W. (Killed in Action 9.4.17) Awarded the VICTORIA CROSS. "For most conspicuous bravery and devotion to duty. During the attack in enemy trenches Sgt. Sifton's company was held up by machine gun fire which inflicted many casualties. Having located the gun he charged it single-handed, killing all the crew. A small enemy party advanced down the trench, but he succeeded in keeping them off till our men had gained the position. In carrying out this gallant act he was killed, but his conspicuous valour undoubtedly saved many lives and contributed largely to the success of the operation."

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: no men killed or wounded in action are recorded in June. 3 other ranks were accidentally wounded in training on 14 June. The month's other losses are sickness and hospital admissions. The Victoria Cross citation for L/Sgt. E. W. Sifton (No. 53730), killed in action 9 April 1917, is typed at the foot of the diary's last page: he charged a machine gun single-handed, killing all the crew, and held off an enemy party until his men gained the position.
- Movements: the battalion moves from Brigade Support at T.27.d.3.5 to the Ridge Line with H.Q. at Thelus Cave (night 1/2 June), to Reserve camp near Neuville St Vaast (2 June), and marches to billets at Barlin (3 June), where it remains for the rest of the month training.
- Officers named: Lieut.-Col. L. E. Jones (proceeded on leave 22 June); Major J. A. McIntosh (assumed command 22 June); Lieut. H. N. Bowden (to hospital 2 June, returned 12 June); Lieut. C. H. Biscoe and Lieut. L. A. Bissell (arrived as reinforcements); R.S.M. Price (special training of N.C.O.s); Major W. J. Gander (admitted to hospital 26 June); Capt. J. S. Bell (rejoined from Divisional School 27 June); Lieuts. W. S. Caldwell, J. G. Doherty, J. D. Parsons, H. L. Mitchell and E. B. Johnson (arrived as reinforcements); Lieut. J. M. Fisher (returned from leave 24 June); Brig-General R. Rennie (presented prizes at the Brigade Sports, 18 June); Lieut. W. H. Lewis (Acting Adjutant, signed Order No. D.135); Bandmaster G. W. Thomas and Sgt. H. Green (tug-of-war captains, Battalion Sports).
- Notable: June is a training month at Barlin after the battalion comes out of the line — a three-week syllabus (drill, musketry, gas helmet drill, bayonet fighting, platoon and company attack training), Battalion Sports at Ruitz on 15 June, a Brigade horse show on 12 June, and 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade Sports at Coupigny on 18 June, where the battalion's football team drew with the 20th Battalion for the Brigade championship and its baseball team beat the 21st Battalion. The battalion was inspected by the G.O.C. 2nd Canadian Division on 28 June and highly complimented on its smart appearance.
- No date gaps: entries run 1–30 June without a break.
- Anomalies: the 23 June entry is headed "23rd. cont'd." although the previous diary page ends with the 22 June entry; no text appears to be missing. The contents page lists only Appendices I–III, but the file also contains the Brigade Sports programme and two maps. The distribution list at the foot of Order No. D.135 is cut off in the film.
