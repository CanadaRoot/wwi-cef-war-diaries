## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer on the first diary pages reads "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D. D. & L. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118."; the last diary page (23–30 April) is on a later printing of the form, footer "2449 Wt. W14937/1830 750,000 1/16 J. B. C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12."
- `[uncertain]` marks any word, name or figure that could not be read with confidence — names and figures are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Bde" = Brigade, "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade, "Coys" = Companies, "o.r." / "O.R." = other ranks, "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding.
- The Remarks column carries a repeated flourished initial on every row that is not legible — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row, except where the column carries substantive text (noted in the entry).
- Place names as written: La Clytte (also LA CLYTTE), Voormezeele, St. Eloi, Dickebusch, Reninghelst, Scottish Wood, Convent Lane, Camp A, Camp I.

## Page 3 — Diary text, 1–5 April 1916

[Handwritten "Original Confidential" at top left, "18th Canadian Battalion WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. April 1916." at top, "Army Form C. 2118." at top right. Place column reads "LA. CLYTTE".]

### 1 April 1916 — La Clytte

> Battalion in Divisional Reserve at LA CLYTTE. "B" coy and 2 platoons of "D" coy moved to field trenches P.1.2 & 3. Enemy artillery active on this part of front.

#### 1 April 1916, 10 a.m.

> Our artillery made a continued bombardment of enemy communication trench and reserves vicinity of ST ELOI. This shelling was intensified from 2 p.m. to 3 p.m. and again from 5.30 p.m. to 6 p.m. 2 o.r. admitted to hospital, LIEUTS J. A. MACKINTOSH and S. L. GUNN proceeded on leave.

### 2 April 1916 — La Clytte

> Position as yesterday. At 1.24 [uncertain — a.m. or p.m.] night of April 1/2 enemy made bombing attack on trenches P.4. which was repulsed by our bombers. 3.50 p.m. further bombing raid was repulsed by our bombers and machine Guns.

#### 2 April 1916, 8 p.m.

> B & D Coys relieved by 27th Can Bn

### 3 April 1916 — La Clytte

> Battn in LA CLYTTE. General's inspection. Working party 50 men repairing dug outs at VOORMEZEELE. 3 o.r. killed in action, 3 o.r. admitted to hospital

### 4 April 1916 — La Clytte

> Battn Headquarters moved from LA CLYTTE to CAMP A. LIEUTS C.E.B. CORBOULD and H. R. MACKEDIE [uncertain] arrived as reinforcements. PART II orders No 14 show 18 o.r. struck off strength.

### 5 April 1916 — Camp A

> Battn in Reserve at CAMP A. Routine. 4 o.r. admitted to hospital 1 o.r. returned from hospital

## Page 11 — Appendix III, Operation Orders by Lt. Col. R. S. Wigle

[Typed. "COPY." top left, "SECRET" top right.]

> OPERATION ORDERS.
>
> by
>
> Lt. Col. R. S. Wigle,
>
> Comdg. 18th Can'ln Battn.
>
> Reference Map: ST. ELOI.
>
> 1. The 4th C.I.B. is attacking Craters 2 and 3 at midnight 8/9th April 1916. The 21st Battalion is attacking No. 2 crater, the 18th and 19th, No. 3 crater: 18th Battalion on the right and 19th Battalion on the left.
>
> 2. The strength of the 18th Battalion attacking party (fifty) is in command of Lt. KERR: Working (fifty) in command of LT. LAWSON. The strength of the attacking and working parties of the 19th Battalion is the same as the 18th Battalion parties. In addition to the bombers under LT. KERR, "B" Co. will detail two officers and sufficient other ranks to make up the parties required. When the attack succeeds, the attacking party and all other ranks available will at once proceed to consolidate the position and make the necessary precautions for the men who will garrison the crater.
>
> 3. The attack will be carried out as follows:—
>
> (a) The attacking party consisting of 22 bombers and 28 other ranks under the bomb Officer, will be organised bombers, bayonet men and carriers. Each bomber and each carrier will carry twelve bombs in the attack and each man of the working party 6 bombs in addition to shovels and sandbags. The bomb Officer will arrange reserve of 600 bombs where convenient at advance signal office at ST. ELOI.
>
> (b) The Bomb officer will, at least half an hour before the attack, meet the bomb officer of the 19th Battn., and arrange to launch the attack simultaneously, and immediately the attack succeeds, will notify Battle Headquarters and also notify officer in charge of the working party.
>
> (c) The whole force should be thrown in so as to create a surprise and should order "Hands up" and make all efforts to accomplish a surrender of the garrison. If not successful, take it at all costs at the point of the bayonet.
>
> (d) A password should be arranged by Bombing officers and made known to all parties engaged in the attack. All attacking parties should be divested of all written material giving identification or unit.
>
> 4. (a) At dawn, all working parties and men in excess of the garrison will be withdrawn. The garrison will consist of two officers, sixty other ranks as detailed by B Co. and two Lewis Guns. These will be relieved the evening of April 9th at dusk.
>
> (b) The garrison party must be alert at all times and be prepared to meet the counter attack that may come. In the event of any prisoners being captured, they will at once be passed to Bde. Hdqrs by guard of 1 N.C.O. and four men, two to precede and two to follow the party.

> Operations:
>
> The parties, under clause 4, operation order 39, were detailed, after which a message came from Bde H. Q. that Germans were seen on the forward lip of No. 3 crater and that a Machine Gun emplacement was suspected on the forward lip of the crater. Clarke, Assistant Brigade Grenade officer, went to make a reconnaissance. In the meantime, a further order from the Brigade to detail 20 bayonet men to support the details in paragraph 4, Bde. Order 39, was received by telephone, and, in addition, 1 officer and 40 men were to report from this Battalion to Capt. Lindsay, Brigade Grenade Officer, who was at BATTLE H. Q. 18th Battalion.

The bombers were to be turned over to Mr. Clarke, who was on the reconnaissance, when the parties reached a point about contour 40, O. [uncertain] 1.5.3. Progress was delayed by grenadier and carriers of the 18th Battalion. The Stokes guns commenced firing 6 minutes in advance of the time noted in Bde. Order 39. This was immediately followed by a heavy enemy bombardment, creating such a barrage that the parties could not get through. Mr. Clarke, in charge of the bombers, reported that he could not get through the barrage and [uncertain — the next words are overstruck] and I called the Brigadier by phone, who said the attack must proceed. ## Notes on this month

- Casualties: the diary's own running figures (they overlap between days and Part II orders, so they are not a clean total) — 3 o.r. killed in action 3 April; 6 wounded, 2 died of wounds and 9 invalided to England 7 April; 2 killed and 10 wounded 8 April; 1 killed and 25 wounded 9 April; 9 killed in action and 1 missing 11 April, when Lieut. F. Lawson died of wounds; 3 killed and Lieut. J. A. Clarke with 13 o.r. wounded 12 April; Part II orders 13 April show further deaths and killed in action (figures not clearly legible); 1 died of wounds 16 April; 1 killed 19 April; 1 killed 23 April. The rest of the month's losses are hospital admissions and sickness, with 18 o.r. struck off strength 4 April and reinforcements arriving 10 April (20 o.r.), 20 April (21 o.r.) and 4 April (Lieuts. Corbould and Mackedie) and 16 and 25 April (Lieuts. McDinton and Coulter).
- Movements: the battalion starts the month in Divisional Reserve at La Clytte, moves its headquarters to Camp A on 4 April, goes into reserve at Convent Lane via Voormezeele on 6 April and into support at Voormezeele on 8 April, then to Camp I on 11 April, to billets at Dickebusch on 19 April (relieving the 22nd Battalion), to Brigade Reserve at Scottish Wood on 21 April (relieving the 31st Battalion), into the front-line trenches 14–16 on 24 April (relieving the 21st Battalion), to A Camp at St. Huberts Hock on 27 April after relief by the 24th Battalion, and to I Camp, Reninghelst, on 29 April.

- Notable: the month is dominated by the St. Eloi craters fighting. At midnight 8/9 April the 4th Brigade attacked craters No. 2 and 3; the 18th and 19th Battalions attacked No. 3 crater, the 18th on the right. On 9 April the Army Commander sent a commendation — "how pleased he is with the good work done last night which has materially improved our position" — passed on with the Commanding Officer's own praise for the officers. A further operation against No. 2 crater on the night of 9/10 April failed: the parties could not get through the enemy barrage and "there was no change in the situation from the previous night's operations". A second attempt on the night of 10/11 April was also forced back by severe bombardment. Earlier, on the night of 1/2 April, enemy bombing attacks on trenches P.4 were repulsed by the battalion's bombers and machine guns. On 26 April a heavy enemy bombardment with bombing and artillery opened up during the relief by the 24th Battalion, and on 30 April an enemy gas attack on the battalion's right forced the battalion to stand to from 12.20 a.m. and remain in readiness all day.
