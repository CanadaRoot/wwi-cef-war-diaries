## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The form footer reads "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D. D. & L. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118." (later pages: "T134. Wt. W758—776. 600,000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S.")
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "O.R." = other ranks, "o.r" = other rank, "T.M.By." = Trench Mortar Battery, "C.C.S." = Casualty Clearing Station, "R.I.R." = Reserve Infantry Regiment (German), "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding.

- The Remarks column carries a repeated initial on every row that is not legible — [uncertain] — and is not transcribed row by row.
- "Radish" is the code name used in field messages for the Adjutant / Battalion Headquarters; "BLOW", "DUM", "LENS" and "Your J.B. received at..." are code words / phrases as written.
- Place names as written: BOVIGNY, BOUVIGNY HUTS, LE PENDU HUTS, VILLERS AU BOIS, VILLERS HUTS, BULLY-GRENAY, LAURENT sector, CITE ST PIERRE, MAISNIL BOUCHE, LENS, LENS-GRENAY Railway, LENS-BETHUNE Road, LENS-ST PIERRE Railway, CHICORY TRENCH, COTTON TRENCH, ALOOF TRENCH, AMULET TRENCH, CINNABAR TRENCH, COMBAT, COWDEN, CORSAIR, COWDRY, CORNWALL, COLLEGE, CYCLIST, CORKSCREW, COWLEY, CALIPER, CONDUCTOR, COMOTION, CARFAX, CONTROL, MARTYR'S ALLEY, CORAL, COMET, COSSACK, COCKLE, COLONY, COPPER, CAVALRY, COVE, COCKSCOMB, RESERVOIR HILL, BOIS DE RIAUMONT, FOSSE 11 DE LENS, HOSPICE, AIX NOULETTE, MAROC, MARQUELLES FARM, PIPE ROAD, GOUY SERVINS, BERLIN (C.C.S. cemetery).

## Page 3 — Diary text, 1–8 August 1917

[Typed. "Original Copy" and "Confidential" at top left. "WAR DIARY — 18th (WESTERN ONTARIO) CANADIAN BATTALION. INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY." — the words "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" are struck through. "AUGUST 1917". Army Form C. 2118. Map reference, Sheet 36B Edition 6 1/40,000.]

### 1 August 1917 — BOVIGNY

> 18th.Canadian Battalion at BOVIGNY village in billets, the Brigade being in Divisional Reserve. Owing to heavy rain, the Battalion did no training to-day. 5.o.rs attached to Divisional Burial party.

### 2 August 1917 — BOVIGNY

> No training carried out to-day owing to heavy rain. 2.o.rs returned from hospital.

### 3 August 1917 — BOVIGNY

> Heavy rain again prevented any training. Lieuts.M.R.Sloan, R.R.Bartry, J.N.Mowbrey, and C.J.Spencer arrived as reinforcements.

### 4 August 1917 — BOVIGNY

> Battalion Pay parades. The G.O.C. 2nd.Canadian Division paid the Battalion a visit in the morning. Battalion proceeded to the line in afternoon. The 4th.Canadian Infantry Brigade relieved the 6th.Canadian Infantry Brigade in the LAURENT sector. The 18th.Canadian Battalion relieved the 28th.Canadian Battalion in Brigade support (CITE ST PIERRE) M.16.b.55.8. No casualties going in and relief was completed at 11.05 p.m. Lieut J.D.Parsons and 2.o.rs admitted to hospital (sick).

[Place column for 4 August: "Map. Sheet 36 C [uncertain] 1/40,000. See O.O. 6 attached".]

### 5 August 1917 — As yesterday

> Quiet day for Battalion. The Battalion furnished carrying parties of 350 men for carrying for Trench Mortar Batteries to their gun positions. Casualties numbering 1 o.r killed and 3 o.rs Wounded.

### 6 August 1917 — As yesterday

> Battalion still furnishing Carrying parties for T.M.Bys. Nothing unusual to report. Major H.D.Dunnett returned from hospital.

### 7 August 1917 — As yesterday

> Battalion again furnished carrying parties for T.M.Bys.

### 8 August 1917 — LAURENT sector relief

> 18th Battalion relieved the 19th.Canadian Battalion in Right Sub-sector, the line was held by a series of Outposts from M.13.b.6.5. M.13.a.8.95. to M.7.b.15.20. and M.7.c.95.80. with H.Qrs at M.7.C.0.7. "B"coy, in command of Capt.D.A.G.Parsons took the line of Outposts. "A" coy, in command of Lieut.J.McAmmond went in support at M.11.d and M.12.c. "C" & "D" coys remained in Brigade support.

[Place column for 8 August: "See O.O. (7) attached".]

## Page 4 — Diary text, 8–10 August 1917

[Typed. "WAR DIARY — 18th (WESTERN ONTARIO) CANADIAN BATTALION. INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY." — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through. Army Form C. 2118.]

### 8 August 1917 (continued) — Brigade support

> Relief was completed without anything unusual happening. Capt.G.W.P.Hodgins returned from course. 4.o.rs rejoined from hospital.

### 9 August 1917 — LAURENT sector

> In conjunction with the 20th and 21st Canadian Battalions the 18th Battalion raided the Enemy's front line trenches. The whole raiding party was under the command of Lieut-Col.L.E.Jones, O.C.18th Canadian Battalion. The frontage raided by the 18th.Battalion was the Enemy front line from N.13.b.1.4. to N.7.d.70.15. Zero hour for this raid was 4.15 a.m. Artillery support was very good. Although no prisoners were captured many casualties were inflicted upon the enemy, and very valuable information as regards to enemy trenches, wire and dug-outs was obtained. Our parties penetrated a distance of 100 yds into the German lines. Our casualties being 4 o.rs Killed and 24 o.rs slightly wounded. All wounded were returned safely to our lines. The German barrage was very scattered. Our raiding party consisted of 35 men of "C"coy under Lieut.H.B.Johnson and 65 men of "D"coy under Lieut.D.Northcombe and Lieut C.J.Spencer. Lieut.Spencer and 7 o.rs, one of whom was wounded, were unable to get back to our lines owing to daylight, remained in a demolished cellar throughout the day. Lieut Spencer and one man found their way in, in the early part of the night, and, assisted by Lieut T.R.Dougall they searched the whole area during the night but were unable to re-locate the cellar in which the remaining 5 o.rs were. Upon the return of Lieut.Dougall and party a report came from the 4th.Brigade H.Qrs that those missing men had come in and had reported to another Regimental Aid post.

[Place column for 9 August: "MAP. Sheet 36 C. See O.O. (8) attached".]

### Night of 9/10 August 1917

> The 4th Brigade was relieved in the LAURENT sector by the 6th.Brigade. "A" "B"coys were relieved by the 27th.Canadian Battalion, and "C" & "D"coys in Brigade support, were relieved by the 31st.Canadian Battalion. Relief was completed at 3.30 a.m. The Battalion moved to billets at BOVIGNY village and the 4th.Brigade came into Divisional reserve. On roll-call at BOVIGNY it was found that the 5 men who had been left in NO MAN'S LAND had not returned, as reported by the Brigade, so a party under LIEUT.DOUGALL again went up to search for them. The party found the cellar in which these men had been but the men were not there. On returning, the search party reported at the Right Coy H.Qrs of the 27th.Canadian Battalion where the missing men were located, having found their own way in. Total casualties as above stated. Nothing to report to-day.

### Night of 14/15 August 1917 (continued) — 15 August 1917

> enemy counter attack, Lieut A.A.McLean was killed. He had gathered a few men together and was leading them up the LENS-BETHUNE road to give what assistance he could and was shot by an enemy sniper and instantly killed. As soon as the attack had gone forward, Lieut T.R.Dougall and some men of the Scout section started forward to make a reconnaissance patrol, and to exploit success, they were held up for an hour by annex enemy plane firing on them to exploit overhead. When this patrol reached the captured enemy position, it went forward approximately 300 yds from our newly won position. ### Evening 16 August 1917

> The 18th Battalion was ordered to attack the enemy trenches between the LENS-GRENAY Railway and LENS-ST PIERRE Railway in conjunction with the 4th.Canadian Division, the Objective being ALOOF TRENCH and thence to connect this trench to CHICORY TRENCH on the left. Two platoons of "A" coy were detailed for this attack, one platoon of "B" coy in support, as this part of the front had not been reconnoitred except by Lieut Dougall, a reconnoitering patrol consisting of Lieuts Dougall, MacDonald and Sloan, and 4 N.C.Os, were sent out to gain knowledge of jumping off point. They covered all the ground necessary. On their way back a bomb was thrown into the party which killed Lieut MacDonald and wounded Lieut Sloan. On Lieut Dougall's report regarding the conditions of the ground it was decided to vary from the plan of attack laid down by the Brigade orders, and instead of making a frontal attack, to push across the Railway cutting from the trenches at N.13.b.2.2 and N.13.b.2.2 Immediately the artillery barrage lifted. This later plan was [uncertain — a word is struck out] sanctioned by the Brigade, Zero hour was set for 4.35 a.m.

### 17 August 1917

> In the early hours of the morning the assaulting platoons were taken forward and it was while giving assistance to the Officers in placing their men, Lieut DOUGALL was mortally wounded by an enemy 5.9 shell. However the attack went forward at the proper time, and occupied COTTON TRENCH from N.13.c.6.9 to N.13.b.2.2 in face of heavy enemy shell fire and the withdrawal of troops on our right, a certain amount of disorganization went forward and succeeded in re-organizing the parties and established posts at N.13.b.35.80—N.13.95.90—N.12.central and N.13.b.4.1. and AMULET TRENCH was found to be untenable. Capt J.B.Bell, Adjt. He sent up further supplies of bombs and left Capt D.A.G.Parsons in charge. The position was completely secure by 7.30.a.m. While this operation was in progress, Lieut W.H.Fenton and a small party went out from our jumping off post at N.13.b.50.35 and entered the Railway cutting and searched the houses in that vicinity and the Railway cutting and searched [uncertain — the sentence is repeated / overwritten] the houses in that vicinity and the Railway cutting [uncertain]. He found that the enemy were working very diligently and building ALOOF TRENCH to the left, to connect with CINNABAR TRENCH. This was reported to the artillery, who took action. During the day the enemy artillery kept up its usual fire on our trenches. From 2 to 4 p.m. our forward posts reported the enemy assembling in houses on either side of the LENS-BETHUNE road from N.13.b.9.2 to Crossroads N.14.c.4.8 and in CHICORY trench.

## Page 8 — Diary text, 17–21 August 1917

[Typed. "WAR DIARY — 18th (WESTERN ONTARIO) CANADIAN BATTALION. INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY." — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through. Army Form C. 2118.]

### 17 August 1917 (continued)

> This was reported to our Artillery, who took very effective action. At 4.00 p.m. a small party of the enemy, estimated at 15 men, attempted to raid our posts at N.13.b.65.40. but were driven off without casualties to us. The remainder of the day and evening were fairly quiet. Lieut. H.B.F.Ralph admitted to hospital, sick.

### 18 August 1917

> At 12.00 midnight 17th-18th, a heavy enemy barrage opened on the whole Brigade front. Our Artillery replied immediately to an S.O.S. call from our left. No enemy was delivered on our front, although some prisoners were thrown. At 2.00 a.m. a similar occurrence happened. It is quite certain that the enemy intended to attack at both of these times but his formations were broken by our Artillery. At 4.15 a.m. the enemy again opened a heavy barrage on the whole Brigade front and succeeded in pushing forward some men into the front trenches on the left of the Brigade, these were immediately ejected. No attack occurred on our immediate front. During the day, nothing of importance occurred beyond the usual desultory shelling.

### Night 18/19 August 1917

> The 18th Battalion was relieved by the 20th.Canadian Battalion taking over the frontage from the LENS-GRENAY Railway to the LENS-BETHUNE Road, and the 27th Canadian Battalion from BETHUNE Road to our left boundary. The relief was completed at 3.45 a.m. and the Battalion proceeded to billets at BULLY-GRENAY, the Brigade being in Divisional Reserve. 23 O.R's arrived as reinforcements, 8 O.R's returned from hospital.

### 19 August 1917 — BULLY-GRENAY

> The Battalion resting today and having bathing parades. LIEUT. T.R.DOU GALL Died of Wounds at No. 6 C.C.S. Berlin, and was buried in the Military Cemetery there. His courage and energy had been a source of pride in the Battalion and his work had been of the greatest value, not only to the Battalion, but also to Brigade and Division. 4 O.R's admitted to hospital, sick.

### 20 August 1917 — BULLY-GRENAY

> No work carried out as Battalion was resting after a strenuous tour. 28 O.Rs arrived as reinforcements and 10. O.Rs returned from hospital.

## Notes on this month

- Casualties: 9 August raid — 4 other ranks killed, 24 slightly wounded (5 men temporarily missing in No Man's Land, all recovered). 15 August (Hill 70 attack) — Lieut A. A. McLean killed by a sniper; Lieuts C. H. Biscoe and L. A. Bis[uncertain]ell wounded; Sgt M. Dunsford severely wounded leading the counter-attack; 65 prisoners taken (165 R.I.R.), plus a complete Minenwerfer crew of 20 men captured by Lieut Dougall's patrol, and 10 more prisoners in the 5 p.m. counter-attack. 16 August — Lieut MacDonald killed and Lieut Sloan wounded by a bomb on a reconnaissance patrol. 17 August — Lieut T. R. Dougall mortally wounded by a 5.9 shell while placing the assaulting platoons; he died of wounds at No. 6 C.C.S. Berlin on 19 August. 21 August — "D" Company sustained 52 casualties on the march from Bully-Grenay, 23 of them fatal, from a single high-velocity shell; the diary puts total casualties for the tour at approximately 220. 5 August — 1 other rank killed and 3 wounded by carrying-party work. No other battle casualties are named; the rest of the month's losses are sickness and hospital admissions.
- Movements: the battalion starts August in billets at Bouvigny in Divisional Reserve, moves into the Laurent sector (Brigade Support, Cité St Pierre) on 4 August, takes the Right Sub-sector outposts on 8 August, is relieved to Bouvigny on the night of 9/10 August, returns to the front line on the night of 13/14 August for the Hill 70 attack of 15 August, is relieved to Bully-Grenay on the night of 18/19 August, marches to Bouvigny Huts on 21 August and to Le Pendu Huts near Villers au Bois on 22 August, and moves to Villers Huts on 31 August.
- Officers named: Lt-Col L. E. Jones (Commanding Officer, in immediate command of the 9 August raid); Capt D. A. G. Parsons; Lieut J. McAmmond; Lieut T. R. - Notable: the month is dominated by the Battle of Hill 70 — the 9 August trench raid (zero hour 4.15 a.m., 100 yards penetration, no prisoners but valuable intelligence), the 15 August attack on Chicory Trench (zero hour 4.25 a.m., objectives taken in 26 minutes, 65 prisoners), the strong German bombing counter-attack at 2.30 p.m. the same day, and the 17 August attack on Cotton Trench. The appendices preserve the full 4th Canadian Infantry Brigade "Instructions for Offensive No. 3" (11 sheets, 29 July 1917) — the brigade-level Hill 70 plan, including barrage timings, German-trap precautions by the 170th Tunnelling Company R.E., gas-shell warnings and contact-aeroplane arrangements. On 27 August the battalion was inspected by Field Marshal Sir Douglas Haig, who complimented it on its good work in the recent operation. The field messages (pp. 43–57) are the original pencil notes from the fighting, including Lieut Fisher's reports from the front line and the complaint that the battalion's own 18-pounders were firing short on Chicory Trench.

## Notes on this part

- Coverage: this part contains appendices only (pp. 58–113 of the August 1917 file) — field messages and notes from the fighting around Lens/Hill 70 on 15–18 August, a retrospective narrative summary of 13–19 August, training syllabuses for 1 and 22–28 August, and trench maps of the Thélus section. There are no diary-text pages in this part; the diary text is in part 1 (pp. 1–57).
- The action: on 15 August 1917 the battalion attacked at zero hour 4.25 a.m. (C and D Companies over to the objective, then a further advance to Chicory), taking prisoners including signallers with telephones. The enemy counter-attacked three times between 9 a.m. and 2 p.m. On 16 August a patrol to Aloof Trench found the enemy strongly held; 2 of the party were killed. On the night of 16/17 August Lt McDonald was killed and Lt Sloane wounded in the hand by Mills grenades thrown by a man in a "C" Company outpost — an apparent friendly-fire incident. On 17 August a German raiding party of about 15 attacked Lt Fisher's (D Company) frontage left of Bethune Road and all but one or two were killed; a 4.25 a.m. minor operation on 18 August to secure a foothold in Aloof Trench failed after Lt Dougall, guiding the raiders, was severely wounded and the party veered onto "C" Company frontage and met great numbers of enemy in hand-to-hand fighting. On the night of 17/18 the enemy put up three heavy barrages and attacked the 21st Battalion front in force; the attackers were of the 55th Regiment and were driven back with heavy losses.
- Casualties: 2 killed and 5 wounded reported in the first D Company note; 15 casualties known to D Company on 17 August (Fisher asks for two stretchers); C Company about 20 casualties "so far as can be ascertained" at the final objective; D Company reduced to 3 officers and 67 other ranks, with 14 and 16 Platoons amalgamated under one officer; 2 of Dougall's Aloof patrol killed 16 August; Lt McDonald killed and Lt Sloane wounded (hand) by friendly Mills grenades; Capt. Dunsford wounded 15 August (Lt Johnson took his place); Lt Russell and another officer wounded 15 August; Lt Dougall severely wounded 18 August; Mr Dunford wounded and a second officer [uncertain] wounded at the final objective; Sgt Woodcock suffering repeated shell shock and Sgt Moore shaken, C Company "in bad shape to hold another night".
- Prisoners and captures: prisoners taken 15 August, including signallers brought in with telephones and other apparatus; 4 prisoners and a machine gun captured by Sgt Routly's patrol north of the Grenay–Lens railway; 1 wounded and 2 [uncertain — "unwounded"] of the 55th Regiment brought in by the 21st Battalion; documents found in a dugout on the cutting, enclosed with the C Company report.
- Movements: the battalion relieved part of the 27th Battalion on the night of 13/14 August, took up assembly positions 14/15 August, attacked 15 August, and was relieved on the night of 18/19 August — the 27th Battalion relieving D Company and the 50th Battalion relieving the right sector — then marched to Bully Grenay. The 102nd Battalion on the right flank withdrew (P.4); the 19th Battalion was on the left, the 21st Battalion on the right during the 17/18 fighting.
- Officers named: Lt.-Col. Jones (addressee, p. 80); Capt. J. S. Bell, Adjutant (signs the syllabuses; addressee of several messages); Lt J. M. Fisher, O.C.   - The narrative summary's "Aug 17" heading (P.4) covers both the 17 August afternoon raid and the 4.25 a.m. minor operation, which the field notes place on 18 August.
  - Spelling varies between notes: "Sgt Routly" (p. 69) and "Sgt Routley" (P3, p. 89); "Chieftory Trench" (p. 80) as written.
  - No diary-text entries for 20–31 August appear in this part; the training syllabuses cover 22–28 August only, and the narrative ends with the move to Bully Grenay on 19 August.
