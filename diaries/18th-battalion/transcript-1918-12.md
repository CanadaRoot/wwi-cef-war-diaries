## Transcription conventions

- Transcribed only from the LAC page images listed above. Spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation are as written in the original.
- The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, "War Diary or Intelligence Summary", with columns Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- `[uncertain]` marks any word or name that could not be read with confidence — names are never guessed. `[struck]` marks text struck through in the original.
- "do" is the diarist's ditto mark; " " (ditto marks) repeat the line above.
- "Battn" = Battalion, "Bn" = Battalion, "Cdn" = Canadian, "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade, "O.R." = other ranks, "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer, "S.B.R." = Small Box Respirator, "H.Q." = Headquarters, "N.C.O." = non-commissioned officer, "M.G." = Machine Gun, "R.A.P." = Regimental Aid Post, "C.C.R.C." = Canadian Corps Reinforcement Camp, "M.M." = Military Medal, "F.G.C.M." = Field General Court Martial, "T.M." = Trench Mortar, "L.G." = Lewis Gun, "O.P." = Observation Post, "Bde." = Brigade, "I.R." = Infantry Regiment (German), "C.S.M." = Company Sergeant-Major, "Q.M." = Quartermaster, "M.O." = Medical Officer, "R.S.M." = Regimental Sergeant-Major, "T.O." = Transport Officer, "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding, "O.C." = Officer Commanding.
- Place names as written: Villers Ste. Gertrude, Barvaux, Heyd, Grandmenil, Odeigne, Courthil, Wallerode, St. Vith, Manderfield (Manderfeld), Dahlem (Dahl), Blankenheim, Tondorf, Munstereifel, Kuchenheim, Duisdorf, Bonn, Hennef, Allner, Happerschoss, Euel, Welderoven, Muschmuhl, Muehlhal, Triesch, Winterscheid, Brohl-Scheid, Heisterschoß, Probach, Cologne.
- Map references are as written, e.g. Marche 9, Germany 1-M, 1-L, 2-L.

## Notes on the filming of this month

- The manifest/filename sequence order is scrambled for this month (e.g. the first file in sequence is p. 4, then p. 1, p. 5, p. 6, p. 3, p. 2). This transcript is in PRINTED page-number order (p. 1, p. 2, … p. 95), sorted by the N in `December_1918_p._N`, not by the page-XXXX sequence number.
- Printed page 67 is MISSING — no file for it exists in the set. None of the four p. 69/p. 70 files is p. 67 (see below).
- Printed pages 69 and 70 each have TWO different files (96 files for 95 printed pages):
  - p. 69 file A (`2006054-page-0171`) is the handwritten cover sheet for Appendix No. 29 (Reference Map, Germany 1-L).
  - p. 69 file B (`2006054-page-0174`) is a map frame of Cologne on the Rhine — a repeat exposure of the same frame as p. 68.
  - p. 70 file A (`2006054-page-0172`) is the same Cologne/Rhine map frame again, framed slightly tighter and lower — another repeat exposure of p. 68.
  - p. 70 file B (`2006054-page-0175`) is a different frame: a tall vertical strip of wooded, hilly country east of the Rhine (incl. Overath).
- Duplicate/repeat exposures elsewhere in the month (all transcribed as filed): pp. 32 and 33 are two different copies of Appendix No. 12 (syllabus for Monday 16 December, with different pay-parade times); pp. 35/36 are two copies of Appendix No. 14; pp. 41/42 are two copies of Appendix No. 19; pp. 56 and 58 are unshaded map versions stamped "REPEAT REPETITION"; p. 61 repeats the p. 60 index map (stamped "REPEAT REPETITION"); pp. 73/74 repeat the p. 72 Sheet 2. L. frame; p. 76 repeats p. 75; p. 85 is a second exposure of the p. 84 sheet 1.M; p. 93 is a second exposure of p. 92; p. 95 is a second exposure of the p. 94 Appendix 28 cover.
- The map appendices (27–30) are large folded sheets photographed in overlapping frames, many in shaded and unshaded versions; small place names on the maps are generally too small to read reliably at this resolution and are marked [uncertain] rather than guessed.
- No diary entry is missing for any date: the diary text (pp. 7–13) covers every day 1–31 December 1918. Note there is no Appendix No. 21 syllabus for Christmas Day, 25 December — Appendix 20 is for 24 December and Appendix 21 is for 26 December.

[Typed Army Form C. 2118. "CONFIDENTIAL" at top left, "23" at top right. Heading: "WAR DIARY 18th CANADIAN BATTALION, WESTERN ONTARIO REGIMENT. INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. (Erase heading not required.)" ### 1 December 1918 — [Place uncertain] 43 75.40. Marche 9

> Dec. 1st. The Battalion moved in full marching order 08.00 hours for VILLERS ST. GERTRUDE, the usual noonday halt was made for dinner. From the Noon Hour on the country became rolling, and hilly and very heavily wooded. The Battalion arrived at the destination at 16.00 hours. Capt. [uncertain] C. Rayward, and Bateman returned from Instructors' Course at Senior Officers School, Aldershot, England. 1 O.R. to Hospital. Appendix No. 1 Order No. D. 265

### 2 December 1918 — Villers Ste. Gertrude

> Villers Ste. Gertrude. 2-C. 95.25 Marche 9. Dec. 2nd. Battalion resting in Billets. 14 O.R's on leave and 2 O.R's returned from leave. 3 O.R's to Hospital and 1 O.R. returned from Hospital.

### 3 December 1918 — do.

> Dec. 3rd. Battalion resting in billets awaiting move order. 2 O.R's to Hospital.

### 4 December 1918 — do.

> Dec. 4th. The Battalion moved in full marching order at 08.30 hours and marched via GRAND MENIL to ODEIGNE. It rained practically during the entire march of the day. A noonday halt for dinner was made, the Battalion arriving at its destination at 16.00 Hours. The march to-day was steadily uphill into the heart of the Ardenne Mountains. Lieut. R. Rouse and 2 O.R's from leave and 4 O.R's to Hospital. Appendix No. 2 Order No. D. 265 A

### 5 December 1918 — Odeigne

> Odeigne 2-I 89.19 Marche 9 Dec. 5th. The Battalion moved off at 08.30 Hours in full marching order for COURTHIL, dropping down the latter part of the march into open country. Battalion arrived at its destination at 10.00 Hours. 20 O.R's to Hospital. Appendix No. 3 Order No. D. 266

### 6 December 1918 — Courthil

> COURTHIL 4-K 98.69 Dec. 6th The Battalion moved off at 08.30 Hours in full marching order for WALLERODE, Germany, crossing the Frontier shortly after 10.00 Hours, and passing through St. Vith about 14.30 Hours, arrived at destination at 16.00 Hours. 6 O.R's from leave and 3 O.R's to Hospital. Appendix No. 4 Order No. D. 267

### 7 December 1918 — Wallerode

> WALLERODE 5-O 89.25 Germany 1-M Dec. 7th. The Battalion moved off at 08.30 Hours for MANDERFELD, arriving there at 15.30 Hours. The country roundabout assuming more of an agricultural aspect. 1 O.R. to Hospital and 2 O.R's returned from Hospital. Appendix No. 5 Order No. D. 268

### 8 December 1918 — Manderfeld

> MANDERFELD 4-H 69.19 Germany 1-M Dec. 8th. Battalion resting in billets. Capt. R. H. Hardy and 7 O.R. from leave. 2 O.R's to Hospital.

### 25 December 1918 — do.

> do. Dec. 25th. "A MERRY CHRISTMAS TO ALL". Christmas Day dawned bright and clear, with just a touch of snow upon the ground, which gave the vicinity a very "Christmas-like" appearance. The members of the Battalion were in excellent spirit and thoroughly prepared for anything the day might bring forth. The Officers of the Battalion had made every preparation for a fine Christmas Dinner and the men were awaiting it with a very keen anticipation. At the appointed hour the tables in the different Company Mess-Rooms, especially selected for the day, were teeming with good things to eat, and they assuredly presented a most pleasant sight. "A" Company Mess-Room was located in a large hall in the Northern section of the town, "C" Company enjoyed its dinner in a picturesque theatre in the centre of the town, while "D" Company had theirs in one of the schools the town affords. "B" Company, being on duty as Outpost Company, had arranged to have dinner in one of the schools in the village of Euel. Each of the different Mess-Rooms was decorated in a most pleasant and "Christmas-like" style to heighten the then very high spirit of the men. The tables were loaded with Roast Turkeys, Oranges, nuts, Ginger Ale Cakes, etc. and from the serving tables in the Rear came most appetizing odours of Turkey, meats, vegetables, Christmas Plum pudding and other items to be served as the dinner progressed. The men took their places at the tables in a very business-like manner, the different platoons being kept together, and then began what was to be their first Christmas dinner and also, in all probability their last one in German Territory. Lt.-Col. L. E. Jones and Staff Officers and the Officers of the different Companies were in attendance at each of the dinners of the Companies and during the progress of the dinner Lt.-Col. Jones made a very felicitous speech of appreciation to the members of the Battalion a very Merry Christmas" and every prosperity for the coming year, which as he remarked would be the one year that would never be forgotten by any one present or by the world in its entirety, being the Anniversary year of the Ending of the War.
