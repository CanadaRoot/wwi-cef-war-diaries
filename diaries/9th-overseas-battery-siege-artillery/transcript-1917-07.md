# 9th Overseas Battery Siege Artillery — Transcript, July 1917

- **Unit:** 9th Overseas Battery Siege Artillery (also titled "MD No. 7: 9th Siege Battery"; "No. 9 o/s Siege Battery, C.E.F.")
- **LAC Item ID:** 2005388
- **RG reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5064, file 985, microfilm reel T-11127
- **Month:** July 1917
- **Pages covered (this file):** manifest pages 133–161 (diary pp. 133–161)
- **Diary date range in this file:** 2–28 July 1917 (weeks ending 7, 14, 21 and 28 July 1917); the covering letter on p. 133 is dated 7 July 1917 and reports on the week ending 30 June 1917, and the special remarks on p. 154 are dated 27 July 1917 and report on the week ending 30 June 1917 (the New River tactical scheme)
- **Location:** Partridge Island, St. John, N.B.
- **Commanding officer / signatory:** Major, C.O. #9 o/s Siege Battery C.E.F. (signature illegible — [uncertain]); the 7 and 14 July sheets are signed by a Captain "for Major" (appears to be Capt. Bennett); the Major signs again from 21 July
- **Note on LAC labels:** the LAC manifest labels these pages "November 1916", but the sheets themselves are dated June–July 1917. Dates below follow the diary sheets themselves.
- **Form:** typed weekly diaries on M.F.B. 316 forms, plus a covering letter on M.F.B. 440. Spelling, abbreviations and punctuation preserved as in the original. Anything illegible or uncertain is marked [uncertain].

## Page 133 (July 1917, p. 133) — covering letter, Weekly Diary of Training, 7 July 1917

[Top left: M. F. B. 440.]
[Stamp, centre right: "HEADQUARTERS OFFICE / NEW BRUNSWICK TROOPS / ST. JOHN, N.B. / JUL 9 1917 / No. N.B. 5-3-21"]
[Stamp, right: "No. 9 O.S. Siege Battery C.E.F. / JUL 7 1917 / ORDERLY ROOM"]
[Number bottom right: 1]

**MILITIA AND DEFENCE.** In reply please quote No. R-3.

Partridge Island, 7th July 1917

From:— The Commanding Officer, No. 9 o/s Siege Battery.
To:— The G.O.C. N.B. Troops, St. John, N.B.

Special Remarks
Weekly Diary of Training.

Sir:—

Regarding the marginally noted subject, I have the honour to state that signalling class during the past week, carried out their work throughout each day, as per schedule drawn up. The recruits' class also worked according to proposed syllabus for the week. The 2nd Class has been employed altogether, during the past week, on the new gun positions, and it is proposed to keep them on this work during the coming week.

I have the honour to be, Sir,
Your obedient servant,

[Signature: [uncertain — signature illegible, appears to read "J. Bennett"]] Capt. for Major.
C.O. #9 o/s Siege Battery C.E.F.

[Note: this letter reports on the week ending 30 June 1917. It does not mention the Tactical Scheme at New River recorded for 26–28 June in the weekly diaries (pp. 130–132), which refer the reader to "Special Remarks"; and its statement that the 2nd Class was "employed altogether" on the new gun positions differs from the 2nd Class diary (p. 131), which records gun drill on Monday and Friday of that week.]

Partridge Island, 27th July 1917

From The Commanding Officer, No. 9 o/s Siege Battery.
To The C. S. O., N. B. Troops, St. John, N.B.

Special Remarks— Weekly Training Diary
Week ending 30th June 1917.

Sir:—

I have the honour to report upon the training carried out during the week ending June 30th.

On Monday afternoon, June 25th, with the permission of the G.O.C., N.B. Troops, the entire Battery left Partridge Island and occupied quarters at Martello Hotel for the night of June 25th. In the morning (Tuesday the 26th) the Battery proceeded via Canadian Pacific Railway to New River Station, Charlotte County. The camping ground was located near the station, fourteen tents were pitched and all arrangements made for the accomodation of the Battery during its stay. Camp kitchens were constructed, latrines were built according to regulations, water facilities marked out and the men generally given instruction in the construction of a model camp. On the same day, the signalling class carried out work in the vicinity; telephone lines were laid and signalling stations established.

On Wednesday, June 27th, the Battery received training in field tactics and company manoeuvres. In the afternoon a route march was held to New River Beach and company drill was carried out at that place.

On Thursday, June 28th, work similar to that of the previous morning was carried out. In the afternoon, camp was struck, the ground entirely cleaned up and left as it was found before the camp was constructed. The Battery entrained at 5 o'clock and reached West St. John in the evening, when it again occupied quarters in the Martello Hotel.

On the morning of June 29th (Friday) the Battery returned to Partridge Island.

In my opinion, the novelty of this trip was much appreciated by the officers and men; practical training in camp life was received; opportunities were given for battery drill and the entire trip was made without any casualties.
