# 9th Overseas Battery Siege Artillery — diary notes

- **LAC record:** Item ID 2005388, "War diaries - MD No. 7: 9th Siege Battery = Journal de guerre - DM no 7 : 9e Batterie de siège", RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5064, file 985, microfilm reel T-11127. 368 page images, 6 November 1916 – 12 October 1918, in two file parts (part 1: 6 Nov 1916 – 31 Dec 1917, manifest pp. 1–216; part 2: 5 Jan – 12 Oct 1918, manifest pp. 217–368).
- **This diary (transcribed):** all 368 pages, 23 monthly transcripts (`transcript-1916-11.md` … `transcript-1918-10.md`; no September 1917 file — no September 1917 sheets exist in the record). Index entries in `INDEX-F.md`.
- **What the diary is:** typed WEEKLY diaries on M.F.B. 316 forms — a "Proposed course of training" sheet and a "Weekly Diary" sheet per week, plus covering letters ("Special Remarks") — not the usual daily Army Form C.2118. The battery was a draft-giving depot battery of the 3rd Regiment, Canadian Garrison Artillery (Military District No. 7), organized June 1916 at West Saint John, N.B., under Maj. P. W. Wetmore. It never went overseas as a unit; the diary is a training diary from New Brunswick, and its men went overseas in drafts. The cover sheets call the run "November 1916 to July 1918 (not complete)" (part 1) and "January 1918 to September 1918 (not complete)" (part 2); the record itself runs to 12 Oct 1918.
- **Where it served:** Partridge Island, St. John, N.B. (Nov 1916 – Oct 1917, with a move to the Martello Hotel, West St. John, 20 Oct 1917 and back to the Island 7–9 Nov); Exhibition Buildings, St. John (from 10 Dec 1917); camp at New River Station (11 Aug – Sep 1917); Red Head Fort camp (from 16 May 1918); the Armouries, St. John (from 20 Sep 1918, ~57 men; ~100 men to Partridge Island 11 Sep 1918).
- **Command:** Maj. P. W. Wetmore (from the week ending 24 Mar 1917; an unnamed Major with an illegible signature commands before that; Capt. Harry S. Bennett signs "for C.O." Nov 1916 – Feb 1917); Capt. Harry S. Bennett as C.O. from 22 Dec 1917; Lieut. F. C. Walker signs as C.O. 2 Feb 1918; Maj. Wetmore again from the week ending 16 Feb 1918 (Walker signs as Major on the 2 Mar 1918 letter). From 21 Sep 1918 a Captain, then a Lieutenant, signs "for Major" (reason unstated).
- **Training:** signalling (Q.M.S. Dean), plotters, layers, gun drill on the 6" Howitzer and 4.7" Q.F. gun, dial sights, clinometers, coast defence, machine-gun instruction (Colt & Lewis) from 27 May 1918, Coast Defence Course on the 4.7" Q.F. 16–21 Sep 1918. Route marches built up to ~20 miles a day. Training suspended March 1917 after a draft of 100 men left, leaving only enough men for guard duties (weeks ending 10–31 Mar all "Nil"); resumed 2 Apr 1917 with graded Recruits'/2nd/3rd Classes. Two 40-pdr Armstrong guns obtained for gun drill (July 1918). Royal Guard of Honour training for Prince Arthur of Connaught's visit (week ending 24 Aug 1918); Brig-General MacDougall inspected the unit 5 Oct 1918.
- **Drafts overseas (the battery's men, not the battery):** draft of 153 all ranks embarked 18 Dec 1917; a draft sailed about 22 Mar 1918 (Classes 1 and 2 did practically no work that week); a draft warned 14 Apr 1918; draft of 75 men selected for overseas 20 July 1918; draft #63 interfered with advanced-class work early June 1918. 23 men on last leave before a draft's departure (week ending 5 Jan 1918).
- **Notable items:** New River tactical scheme 25–29 June 1917 (CPR train, 14 tents, model camp, telephone lines, field tactics; no casualties) and the August 1917 New River camp; St. George march 16 Aug 1917 (16 miles, welcomed by Mayor Lawrence, St. George Band, dinner in St. Marks Church vestry — "first body of troops to be seen in the Town for 30 years"); signalling class final examinations 21–23 Aug 1917 by the Divisional Signalling Officer, M.D. 6; Major Jones's funeral 31 July 1917 (battery paraded; no role or cause of death stated); quarantine late Nov 1917; mumps cases quarantined mid-Mar 1918; inoculation/vaccination 1 Nov 1917, 20 Feb and 26–27 Apr 1918.
- **Casualties:** none recorded anywhere in the diary — no killed, wounded or sick named as casualties (sickness appears only as quarantine/inoculation entries).
- **How the diary ends:** no closing note, embarkation, re-designation or disbandment — the last page (368, diary p. 152) is simply the proposed syllabus for the week ending 12 Oct 1918, and the record stops there. No actual diary sheets exist for the weeks ending 5 or 12 Oct 1918.
- **Gaps / duplicates / misfiled pages (nothing invented):**
  - No September 1917 sheets at all — the diary jumps from the week ending 25 Aug 1917 (p. 180) to October (p. 181).
  - No actual Weekly Diary sheet for the week ending 10 Feb 1917; no proposed programme sheets for the weeks ending 9 and 30 Dec 1916; no Recruits' Class diary for the week ending 4 Aug 1917; no 2nd Class/Recruits' diary for the week ending 11 Aug 1917; no 3rd Week Squad actual diary for the week ending 27 Apr 1918; no diary sheet for the week ending 10 July 1918 (referred to in a 10 July letter); no 4th Week Squad sheet for the week ending 20 Apr 1918; no cover letter for the week ending 3 Nov 1917.
  - Duplicates: p. 48 duplicates p. 47; p. 67 is a typed copy of the p. 66 letter; p. 172 duplicates the 11 Aug letter on p. 168; pp. 192/193 duplicate the week-ending-3-Nov-1917 diary; pp. 200–202 are three copies of the week-ending-17-Nov-1917 diary; pp. 293/294 near-duplicates (the latter headed "May 25th 1913" as printed); pp. 100/102, 112/108, 113/111, 118/116, 119/120, 363/364 near-duplicate/triplicate copies. p. 341 is a blank reverse; p. 297 is an undated HQ file cover sheet.
  - Misfiled pages from another unit: p. 184 is a Training Report letter of the 65th Depot Battery, C.E.F., Woodstock, N.B. (19 Oct 1917, re Petawawa training syllabus); p. 191 is a 65th Depot Battery weekly diary (week ending 3 Nov 1917). Both transcribed and flagged, not indexed as this battery's activity.
  - File order is non-chronological in places: a DND divider (p. 161) states the July–December 1917 run was "Extracted from M.5-3-2 Vols. I & II" and is "not complete"; manifest p. 162 (29 Dec 1917 guard-work letter) sits between the August and October 1917 sheets and is transcribed in its December 1917 place.
  - Manifest filename labels are wrong throughout: pp. 1–216 are all labelled "November 1916" (they run Nov 1916 – Dec 1917) and pp. 217–368 "January 1918" (they run Jan – Oct 1918). Months were assigned from the dates on the pages.
  - The week ending 1 Dec 1917 straddles November/December and is filed under December 1917; other straddling weeks are filed by week-ending date and noted in the transcripts.
- **Uncertain / open items:** 497 [uncertain] flags across the 23 transcripts — mostly the C.O.'s illegible signature on nearly every sheet, overtyped dates/hours, obscured diary page numbers, and the illegible handwritten fragment on p. 269. No names were guessed.

Programmatic verification: all 368 manifest pages appear exactly once across the 23 transcripts, in order, with no gaps or duplicates.

Index built by a separate subagent from the transcripts and verified by the parent run.
