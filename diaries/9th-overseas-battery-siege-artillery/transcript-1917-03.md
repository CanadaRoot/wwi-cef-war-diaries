Partridge Island, 9th March 1917

From The Commanding Officer,
No. 9 o/s Siege Battery.
To The G. O. C., N. B. Troops,
St. John, N.B.

TRAINING
Special remarks, Weekly Diary

Sir:—

With reference to the marginally noted subject, I have the honour report as follows:—

On account of the draft of 100 men leaving this unit recently, only sufficient men are left for guard duties, and therefore no schedule of training can be carried on. However, squad and rifle drill and physical training are given whenever possible.

Under the present circumstances, will it be necessary to submit weekly diary report and proposed syllabus of training, please?

I have the honour to be,
Sir,
Your obedient servant,
[Signature: Harry J. Bennett, Capt.?] [uncertain]
Major.,
C.O. #9 o/s Siege Battery

## Page 54 (March 1917, p. 54) — other (letter / Special Remarks, Weekly Diary of Training) — 24th March 1917, no week-ending date shown

M. F. B. 440.
MILITIA AND DEFENCE.
In reply please quote No. [blank]
[Stamp: No. 9 O. S. Siege Battery, C.E.F., MAR 24 1917, ORDERLY ROOM.]
Partridge Island, 24th March 1917
From:— The Commanding Officer.,
No. 9 o/s Siege Battery.
To:— The G. O. C., N. B. Troops,
St. John, N.B.

Special Remarks
Weekly Diary of Training.

Sir:—

With reference to the marginally noted subject, I have the honour to report, that as the course in coast defence is still being carried on by the Composite Battery, guard and fatigue duties are, on that account, left to No. 9 Siege Battery. As there are only enough men in the latter unit to do the work each day, no regular syllabus of training can be adopted.

I have the honour to be,
Sir,
Your obedient servant,
[Signature: [uncertain — signature illegible, appears to be the new commanding officer, cf. Pages 56–69]]
Major.,
C.O. #9 o/s Siege Battery.
