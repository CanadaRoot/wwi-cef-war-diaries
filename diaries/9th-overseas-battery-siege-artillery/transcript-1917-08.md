## Page 168 (August 1917, p. 168) — special remarks on training, New River, 11 August 1917

[Stamp, centre right: "HEADQUARTERS OFFICE / NEW BRUNSWICK TROOPS / ST. JOHN, N.B. / AUG 13 1917 / 5-3-21"]
[Number bottom right: 55]

New River, N.B. 11th. August 1917

To:— C.S.O., N.B. Troops, St. John, N.B.

**Special Remarks on Training**

With the advantage afforded for special training by the move to New River where the Battery is at present under canvas, great improvement is apparent in all branches of work, particularly in march discipline.

Work is being carried out along the lines practised at Halifax in Artillery training and in addition the preparing of camps and billets and the general care and sanitary arrangements necessary during camp life, as well as choosing gun positions and details necessary for bringing the Battery into action.

Considerable time is being given to Physical Training and Route Marches to get the men into good physical condition while here where there is a much better chance that at Partridge Island.

The health of the men is excellent.

[Signature: [uncertain — signature illegible]] Major,
C.O. No. 9 Siege Battery, C.E.F.

[Note: the Battery has moved from Partridge Island to New River, N.B., and is camped under canvas there. No date for the move itself is given on this page; the move took place between 4 August (the last Partridge Island diary) and 11 August 1917.]

## Page 174 (August 1917, p. 174) — special remarks on training, week ending 18 August 1917

[Stamp, top right: "HEADQUARTERS OFFICE / NEW BRUNSWICK TROOPS / ST. JOHN, N.B. / AUG 20 1917 / No. N.B. 5-3-21"]
[Number bottom right: 67]

**SPECIAL REMARKS ON TRAINING.**

For week ending 18th August 1917

To:— The C.S.O., N.B. Troops, St. John, N.B.

The Signalling class has been carried out under C/Sergt. Dean, O.S.S., and have made good progress.

A discarded telephone line from New River Station to New River Beach a distance of four miles was overhauled and repaired by the class and field wires run from it to the Camp grounds, and the line was connected to the N.B. Telephone Company's line and communication established between the Camp and all points.

For some days one of our field telephones was used, but the telephone Co. have since furnished an instrument, and complimented the class upon the work done in repairing the line.

The examinations of the present class begin on the 21st. inst., and it is hoped that another class may then be started.

The remainder of the Battery have been instructed in general field work during the week, particular attention being given towards physical development of the men by exercises and marches.

The length of route marches have been gradually increased until about 20 miles per day has been reached, and the men instructed in the care of their feet and bodies. Very little fatigue is now shown from long marches, and with the exception of a few blistered heels, no bad results.

On the 16th. inst. the Battery left Camp at 6.50 a.m. and marched to the Town of St. George, a distance of 16 miles, arriving there at 11.20 a.m. having made four stops of ten minutes each enroute.

The march was made on the invitation of the citizens of St. George, who turned out to greet us on arrival, with the St. George Band, and the Mayor tendered us an official welcome to the Town, and the citizens did everything in their power to make our short stay among them most pleasant. The ladies had prepared a most bountiful dinner in the vestry of St. Marks Church. After full justice had been done to the good things provided, addresses were made by the Mayor, Dr. H. I. Taylor, M.L.A., and Rev. Mr Spencer, which were duly replied to.

It was stated that this was the first body of troops to be seen in the Town for 30 years. The Town at one time maintained a Company of Artillery belonging to the 3rd. Regt. C.G.A., the mother unit of this battery.

A half holiday was declared and the Battery manoeuvred about the Town to music furnished by the St. George Band.

Mr Frawley, proprietor of the Imperial Theatre invited the Battery to visit his Theatre where we enjoyed an hour.

We then moved off to the C.P.R. Station where photographs of the Battery were taken, by a local Photographer, and entrained for Camp to the cheers of the people, and the strains of the National Anthem, by the Band.

The weather this week with the exception of today has been all that could be desired, and advantage has been taken of daylight hours for training and sports and marked improvement has been shown in general development. It is hoped that a general inspection may be held while we are under canvas.

[Signature: [uncertain — signature illegible]] O.C. No. 9 Siege Battery

Course Training

- 3/4 P.T. Lieut. Inches
- 2 1/2 Route march, Lieut. Bennett
- 1 Squad Drill, B.S.M.
- 1 Dial Sights & Directors, Lieut. Bennett & B.S.M.
- 1 Company Drill, Lieut Bennett.

**TUESDAY 14th August 1917** — Day of Course.

- 3 3/4 Route march, 12 miles, Lieut. Bennett.
- 1/2 Lecture march discipline
- 1 1/2 Lecture, Dial Sights
- 1 Squad Drill, B.S.M.

**WEDNESDAY 15th August 1917** — Day of Course.

- 2 1/2 Striking and re-erecting Tents
- 2 Route March, Lieut. Bennett
- 1 Company Drill
- 1 Lecture Ammunition.

**THURSDAY 16th August 1917** — Day of Course.

- 4 1/2 Route march to St. George, 16 miles, all officers.
- 1 1/2 Manoeuvre at St. George.
- The Battery was officially welcomed at St. George by Mayor Lawrence. A half holiday was declared and the citizens generally turned out with a band to entertain the Battery, dinner was provided by the ladies in St Marks church. Return to Camp was made by train.

**FRIDAY 17th August 1917** — Day of Course.

- 2 Route March, Lieut. Bennett
- 1 Lecture Directors, Lieut. Bennett
- 2 Squad and Company drill, B.S.M.

**SATURDAY 18th August 1917** — Day of Course.

- Fatigue Work; General Cleaning of Camp.
