## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- The weather for each day is written at the right-hand end of the Summary column; it is shown here after an em dash in the Summary cell.
- Ditto marks (`"`) in the original are reproduced as `"`.
- The Remarks column contains the initialling officer's initials, written in a flowing hand (read as "C.H.L." and "G.H.C." — see Notes).
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; the unit remained at Reninghelst Siding throughout the month.

## Notes — July 1916
- The unit remained at Reninghelst Siding (near Poperinge, Belgium) for the whole month; every day's entry is the same duty line, "Men employed on Railhead Supply Duties", with only the weather varying. No casualties, movements or other events are recorded.
- The place name is written "Reninghelst Siding" on the first row of each diary page (confirmed against the June pages); later rows use ditto marks.
- Change of command, 25 July: Capt. C. H. Lougheed relinquished command and 2/Lieut. Circuitt took over. The 2nd Lieutenant's initials in the entry read as "G. F. L." but the month-end signature reads "G. H. Circuitt" and the Remarks initials from 26 July read "G.H.C.", so the middle initial in the entry is uncertain. The surname is read as "Circuitt" in both the entry and the signature.
- The Remarks initials are written in a flowing hand that at first glance looks like "6A1L" / "G7LC"; they are read as "C.H.L." (Capt. C. H. Lougheed) for 1–25 July and "G.H.C." (2/Lieut. Circuitt) for 26–31 July, matching the change of command on 25 July.
- 10 July weather reads "Fine & rain"; the conjunction between the two words is written unclearly.
- 13 July weather is "Cloudy –" with nothing after the dash.
- No appendices are listed or included for July.
