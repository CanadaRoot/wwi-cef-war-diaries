## Page 3 — Diary text (April 1916, p. 3)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY for month of April" (heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through). Handwritten; headed "9th Canadian Inf. Bde. M.G. Coy.", "VOL. 1", "15/4/16 to 30/4/16".

> Place: B Camp | Date: 1916, April 15
>
> **15 April** — Company organised under Capt W.H. Bothwell. Officers on this date are. O.C. Capt. W.H. Bothwell. Sec./c Lieut J. Mackinnon — Detached on duty as Bde Lewis Offr. Lieut G. Rutherford. Lieut K. Sage. Lieut H. D. Bourne. Lieut L. S. Page. Lieut G. W. Benford. Lieut R. C. [uncertain]. Lieut H. A. Whitaker. [uncertain] H. Lugs. B. Camp from 15.4.16 to 20.4.16 inclusive. Company mobilizing. [Date column shows "15.4.16" with a struck-through date beneath it, reading "20.4.16" — uncertain. A line in the Place column reading "To 20th Camp." is struck through — uncertain reading.]
>
> **20–22 April** — Capt W.H. Bothwell Absent and evacuated Sick. There were no reports from Section Officers on these dates.
>
> [signed] *J. Mackinnon, Lieut, O.C.*

## Page 4 — Diary text (April 1916, p. 4)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY for month of April" (heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through). Handwritten; headed "9th Canadian Inf. Bde. M.G. Coy."

> Place: Hooge | Date: 1916, April 22–25
>
> **22 April** — Lieut J. Mackinnon took command of the Company in the absence of Capt W.H. Bothwell. Owing to lack of accommodation in front line [uncertain] to [uncertain] for guns: Rations are extremely scarce owing to the fact that [uncertain] do not arrive in [uncertain] of the arrival of large parties for reinforcement. Work on M.G. emplacements called for with [uncertain] [uncertain].
>
> **23 April** — Enemy shell front line and HALF WAY HOUSE. No Casualties. Men in front line replaced by men in reserve. Control is difficult owing to different units not yet being merged into one Command.
>
> **24 April** — Disappearing mounts placed [uncertain] in front line, and open emplacements begun. Transport is handsome. Some very incapable drivers have been handed over by battalions, also balky mules. Situation Quiet.
>
> **25 April** — Shrapnel helmets issued to men in front line. Gas and [uncertain] precautions advised. Fairly heavy shelling behind front line.
>
> [signed] *J. Mackinnon, Lieut, O.C.*

## Page 5 — Diary text (April 1916, p. 5)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY for month of April" (heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through). Handwritten; headed "9th Canadian Inf. Bde. M.G. Coy."

> Place: Hooge | Date: 1916, April 27–30
>
> **27 April** — Heavy bombardment and [uncertain] from Hill 60 to YPRES. Dugouts begun in front line and emplacements at [struck through in original: the] YEOMANRY Post.
>
> **28 April** — M.G. in [struck through in original: uncertain] 70 seems to be known to Enemy. Shells and rifle Grenades have been dropped in that neighbourhood. Work parties under sapper Control is too slow, and hours are too short. Front line guns relieved.
>
> **29 April** — Shells drop from time to time at Culvert and behind the lines. Warned to prepare for against Gas attack, with special attention to be prepared for a second cloud of Gas. Situation fairly Quiet.
>
> **30 April** — Town of YPRES is shelled pretty steadily. Line is pretty Quiet, but Enemy snipers and Machine Guns are active.
>
> **30 April, 8 p.m.** — Lieut G. Benford is wounded by shell fire near LILLE GATE at YPRES. Transport routes fairly heavily shelled.
>
> [signed] *J. Mackinnon, Lieut, O.C.*
