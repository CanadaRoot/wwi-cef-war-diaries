## Page 89 — Diary text (January 1917, p. 3)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" (heading struck through). Handwritten.

> Place: ELBE TRENCH | Date: 1917, January 1–8
>
> **1 January** — Our artillery very active, shelling enemy trenches near Th[uncertain]. Our Stokes guns active on left, with light retaliation by [uncertain]. Our machine guns fairly active.
>
> **2 January** — Our artillery & Stokes very active. Enemy trench mortars active during afternoon. Our guns firing as usual at night.
>
> **3 January** — Artillery of both sides active. Usual trench mortar activity in both subsections. Our guns quiet at night.
>
> **4 January** — Our artillery active. Enemy quiet. Trench mortars of both sides active. "A" Section relieved "B" Section.
>
> **5 January** — Artillery very active shelling enemy support line. Trench mortars fairly active.
>
> **6 January** — Our heavy artillery active. Stokes also active. Enemy trench mortars retaliated heavily.
>
> **7 January** — Artillery fairly quiet. Our Stokes guns very active, with little enemy retaliation.
>
> **8 January** — Artillery & trench mortars of both sides very active. Our guns fired [continued on next page].

## Page 90 — Diary text (January 1917, p. 4)

> Place: ELBE TRENCH | Date: 1917, January 8–15
>
> **8 January (contd)** — heavily on new trench north of THELUS ROAD which is being wired at night. "B" Section relieved "C" Section.
>
> **9 January** — Our artillery active as usual. Enemy quiet. Trench mortars of both sides active. Our guns firing as usual.
>
> **10 January** — Our artillery & Stokes fairly active. Enemy quiet.
>
> **11 January** — " " " " Enemy trench mortars active in afternoon.
>
> **12 January** — Usual artillery & trench mortar activity. "C" Section relieved "D" Section.
>
> **13 January** — Our artillery, trench mortars & Stokes quite active. Enemy artillery showed increased activity.
>
> **14 January** — Considerable increase in activity of artillery & trench mortars of both sides.
>
> **15 January** — Usual trench mortar & artillery activity. In view of suspected enemy relief our m. g's fired heavily on communication trenches & trench railways. In conjunction with the 7th & 8th C.M.G. Co's we carried out a shoot on enemy trench mortar dumps.

## Page 91 — Diary text (January 1917, p. 5)

> Place: In the Trenches | Date: 1917, January 16–24
>
> **16 January** — Artillery active. Silenced enemy trench mortar. [uncertain] shoot continued. "D" Section relieved "A" Section.
>
> **17 January** — Heavy snow fall in early hours. Both sides very quiet throughout the day. Rear Headquarters removed from BRAY to a field near ECOIVRE.
>
> **18 January** — More snow. Very little activity on either side.
>
> **19 January** — Very quiet.
>
> **20 January** — Very quiet. "A" Section 9th M.G. Coy relieved "B" Section.
>
> **21 January** — Enemy artillery much more active. At 6 P.M. [uncertain] artillery activity, intense for 30 minutes. Enemy put on TRENCH MORTAR Barrage to which we retaliated vigorously with artillery.
>
> **22 January** — Very quiet.
>
> **23 January** — Very quiet during day. Some enemy retaliation [uncertain] during night.
>
> **24 January** — Quiet in morning. Enemy aeroplanes active. Enemy artillery active on support line between 3 and 5.

## Page 92 — Diary text (January 1917, p. 6)

> Place: [blank] | Date: 1917, January 25–31
>
> **25 January** — Enemy artillery fairly active. Our heavy artillery carried out a shoot from 1 to 4 P.M. Much aerial activity.
>
> **26 January** — Enemy artillery active. Otherwise quiet.
>
> **27 January** — Our machine guns active on account of suspected enemy relief. Otherwise day very quiet.
>
> **28 January** — Day very quiet, with intermittent shelling by both sides.
>
> **29 January** — Relief. 43rd Battn relieved 58th Bn and 52nd Battn relieved 60th Bn.
>
> **30 January** — Very quiet.
>
> **31 January** — Very quiet. Enemy wire cut by 43rd Battn in preparation for raid.
>
> [signed] *A. S. Page Lieut*
> *for O.C. 9th Canadian Machine Gun Co.*
