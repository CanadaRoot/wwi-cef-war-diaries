## Page 155 — Diary text (June 1917, p. 4)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" (heading struck through). Handwritten; headed "Map Ref. 36 C S.W."

> Place: VIMY | Date: 1917, June 1
>
> **1 June** — The night in this Sector was comparatively calm — Lt Soulsby was killed last night (31st/1st of June) while standing at the entrance of his dugout. A.E.B.
>
> 5 a.m. to 7 p.m. The day was quiet round most sections of the line — Usual intermittent shelling of the CULVERT — Enemy also shelled gun pits. Aircraft very active today — At 10.00 am three enemy planes over our positions — We opened fire with our M.Gs from railway embankment + they turned + went towards their own lines — At 10.45 am four enemy planes attacked one of our Scout planes to west of railway embankment + brought it down — it burst into flames as soon as it landed which was just behind our front line. Both airmen were burned to death — We fired with our M.Gs 250 rounds at them — Two retired immediately + the other two retired later + one was seen to land behind the enemy's lines.
>
> 12 to 12.45 p.m. Our front line to East of Railway embankment was shelled quite heavily + from 12.45 p.m. to 1 p.m. CULVERT + Support line shelled quite heavily.
>
> 4.00 p.m. Three Enemy planes over very high.

## Page 156 — Diary text (June 1917, p. 5)

> Date: 1917, June 1–2
>
> **1 June (contd)** — 7.15 p.m. Enemy planes over very high + from 7.30 to 8.45 — Enemy heavily shelled around houses in T19 a with 5.9.
>
> 8.45 p.m. Eight enemy planes over our positions — We opened fire. A.E.B.
>
> **2 June** — The night of the 1st/2nd was fairly quiet — At 2.55 enemy sent up red flares in pairs + immediately machine guns opened up + afterwards front line was shelled — This continued until 3.00 a.m. during which time the enemy sent up all kinds of flares. Single red, double red, single + double green + orange coloured cluster flares. We fired 3,000 rounds on targets which the artillery had engaged during the day. A.E.B.
>
> Enemy engaged the usual targets with his artillery — At 3.00 p.m. to 3.45 p.m. enemy heavily shelled Battery in T13 C 2.4 with 5.9 — over 100 heavy [uncertain] expended + appeared to get one direct hit. Enemy aircraft not very active 3 or 4 between 10 + 12.30 midday flying high — One over at 3.00 p.m. + [uncertain] one at 7 p.m. — We opened fire + the latter turned towards their own lines. A.E.B.

## Page 157 — Diary text (June 1917, p. 6)

> Date: 1917, June 3–4
>
> **3 June** — During the night of the 2nd/3rd the 4th Canadian Bn on our right attacked + the enemy retaliated fairly heavily on our left front with a light barrage on the right front. From 5.30 to 8.00 CULVERT was shelled + was shelled intermittently during the day — VIMY also received some attention — The afternoon was comparatively quiet. Enemy Aircraft was very active during the day — At 5.30 am enemy plane over our position in Railway + was driven back by two of our planes + was forced to land behind his own lines. — We opened fire also with M.Gs. — We opened fire at 8.15 am, 4.00 p.m., 6.30 p.m. + 8.15 p.m. on enemy planes + in three cases forced them to retire behind their own lines. Enemy plane dropped 8 bombs during night [uncertain] near Railway track. A.E.B.
>
> **4 June** — The situation today was generally quiet — Enemy Artillery engaged the usual targets — Enemy planes not very active. One flying very low was engaged by our Machine Guns + retired quickly. A.E.B.

## Page 158 — Diary text (June 1917, p. 7)

> Date: 1917, June 5–6
>
> **5 June** — During night 4th/5th enemy Aircraft dropped bombs in VIMY but without damage. — At 1.15 am enemy sent up a strange flare it spread out in a parachute shape. — At 2.25 Enemy opened up a heavy bombardment. The barrage appeared to be broken into two parts, the first part slightly in rear of our front line — The day was generally quiet — CULVERT and the usual targets were intermittently shelled. We opened fire on hostile aeroplanes upon two occasions with good effect. After dark both artilleries very active. A.E.B.
>
> **6 June** — At 12.30 enemy shelled along front — CULVERT, AVION ROAD with H.E. + whiz bangs. — Enemy Trench Mortars also active with Gas shells. During the night 5/6th we fired 2,500 rounds at targets which had been engaged by the Artillery during the daytime. The day was generally quiet all along our front. During the night of the 6th/7th the Company were relieved in the line by the 8th Can M.G. Coy the relief being completed by 12.30 a.m. After relief the Company moved to Transport Lines. A.E.B. [Remarks: O.O. 62 attached]

## Page 161 — Diary text (June 1917, p. 10)

> Date: 1917, June 16–18
>
> **16 June** — Situation during the day quiet — except for light intermittent shelling of CULVERT — VIMY and Railway tracks with occasional shells in Communication trenches. During the night from 12 to 2 am (16/17) fairly heavy shelling of the front line — The enemy got one direct hit on one of our positions at T14 d 30.80 wounding Sergt. Caldwell + four gunners. During the night we expended 3000 rounds on enemy overland tracks. A.E.B.
>

>
> **18 June** — Situation during the day quiet with the exception of from 2 am to 3 am in afternoon [as written] when the Enemy threw some very heavy shells into VIMY evidently searching for Battery positions. About 7.00 am we attacked along the whole front which was very successful. The definite reports follow. A.E.B.
