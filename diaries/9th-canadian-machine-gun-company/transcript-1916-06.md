## Page 18 — Diary text (June 1916, p. 4)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" (heading struck through). Handwritten.

> Place: B Camp. | Date: 1916, June 1–2
>
> **1 June** — The day was spent in field work and range practice. Early in the morning Enemy planes, which dropped bombs on Poperinghe were fired on by our guns. Range was extreme and not effective.
>
> **2 June** — 10 a.m. While parading for inspection by G.O.C., message arrives to prepare to move at 1 hour's notice. 3 P.m. Warned to be prepared to move at once. 3.20 P.m. Ordered to move at once to Camp E, and await orders. 3.30 Left Camp B to proceed to Camp E.
>
> Place: E. CAMP. | Date: 1916, June 2
>
> **2 June (contd)** — D.A.D.O.S. leave 8 of our guns in their hands; take only 8 guns to advance. 4.45 P.m. Operation order No 19 received from B.H.Q., which read. — "On receipt of this order You will move 4 Guns to occupy 4 positions in LILLE ROAD." 4.55 P.m. Orders issued by O.C. 9th B. M.G.C. To O.C. A Section of that company. "On receipt of this You will move at once and occupy 4 positions in LILLE ROAD. You will report to and come under the orders of G.O.C. 7th C.I.B. [uncertain] Take 20.000 rounds of ammunition with 12 filled belts per Gun."

## Page 21 — Diary text (June 1916, p. 7)

> Place: BUND | Date: 1916, June 6–8
>
> **6 June** — Heavy artillery fire on HOOGE FRONT. Enemy advances in the afternoon on our front line and on to our 2nd [uncertain] line where they were held up. M.G.s proved valuable. Observations:— Front line shell proof Emplacements are impossible to obtain. If made shell proof they are visible. But shell proof dug-outs can be made. Casualties 1 O.R. wounded. [Date column reads 6.6.17 as written; presumably 6.6.16.]
>
> **7 June** — Artillery duel intense. Our artillery is getting very heavy. ZILLEBEKE shelled for 2 hours 5 to 7 P.m. Whole village was razed. Machine Gun Emplacement in Church wrecked. Shelters in ruins rendered untenable. Casualties Nil on this date.
>
> **8 June** — No incident of note. Artillery fire heavy on both sides. We advance two Guns to support the front line in LOVERS WALK and MAPLE COPSE.

## Page 22 — Diary text (June 1916, p. 8)

> Place: BUND | Date: 1916, June 8–11
>
> **8 June (contd)** — Casualties. 1 O.R. wounded 1 O.R. died of wounds.
>
> **9 June** — Heavy artillery duelling continues. Our artillery bombardment is growing very intense. Field batteries in neighbourhood of the BUND and YPRES are subjected to heavy shell fire nearly all day. B.H.Q. at BUND is heavily bombarded. Brigade Staff removed their head Quarters to YPRES. Casualties 2 O.R. wounded. 1 accidentally wounded, while opening a beef can.
>
> **10 June** — YPRES, BUND, Communication trenches receive intense bombardment from early morning till night. Things are calmer [uncertain] in the evening and transport is unmolested. Casualties Nil.
>
> **11 June** — Heavy artillery duel throughout the day, with periods of intense bombardment. Casualties 1 nervous breakdown.

## Page 24 — Diary text (June 1916, p. 10)

> Place: BUND | Date: 1916, June 13–14
>
> **13 June (contd)** — bomb attack by a battalion of 9th Canadian Brigade on HILL 56, RIGO ST. and the old front line of British front along WARRINGTON AVE and BYLAND AVE. Several prisoners are made. On HILL 62 machine guns survived the bombardment and held up the attack. They had to be bombed from the flanks. Recommendation. Deep dug outs be made for all our M.G. Emplacements advanced in front of front line, and this would not be left to M.G. Companies to do as they have not sufficient strength. Casualties NIL.
>
> **14 June** — Whole front is remarkably quiet. Occasional salvoes come from Germans along our Communication trenches and routes. Consolidation is going on. Our men are much exhausted. They have been in now since May 14 with only two days rest. Casualties 1 O.R. wounded.

## Page 25 — Diary text (June 1916, p. 11)

> Place: BUND. | Date: 1916, June 15
>
> **15 June** — Another quiet day. Occasional salvoes from Germans on our Communication trenches, BUND, YPRES, and batteries in the neighbourhood. Relieved by 2nd Guards Brigade M.G. Coy. Guards M.G. Companies are 300 strong and are thus able to do their own work. Relief is accomplished quietly in dull weather. Casualties 1 O.R. wounded.

>

>
> **21 June** — Syllabus of training 6.15 Physical drill 9. Squad drill 10. Limber drill. 11. Entrenchments. [struck words, apparently "Brigade Sports"] held this afternoon and the Company was well represented.

## Page 28 — Diary text (June 1916, p. 14)

> Place: "B" Camp | Date: 1916, June 26–28
>
> **26 June** — Syllabus 6.15 Physical drill 9 Limber drill 10 Ranges 11 Limber packing. At 1.30 P.M there was a company drill under N.C.O.s of the Irish Guards. The Company received twelve Vickers guns to-day. In the afternoon the Brigade horse show was held and this section made a splendid showing.
>
> **27 June** — Syllabus 6.15 Physical drill. The morning was spent on the Vickers gun, cleaning and Instruction. The parade at 1.30 was taken by the Guard N.C.O's.
>
> **28 June** — Syllabus 6.15 Physical drill. The morning was spent on the Vickers gun, special attention being given to Mechanism and Stoppages. One O.R was court-martialled for absenting himself without leave from a work party at the front line.

## Page 29 — Diary text (June 1916, p. 15)

> Place: "B" Camp | Date: 1916, June 29–30
>

Relief complete 11.45 Casualties 2 O.R wounded. Company Hdqtrs at ZILLEBEKE BUND.
>
> Place: ZILLEBEKE BUND | Date: 1916, June 30
>
> **30 June** — Very quiet with exception of a little counter battery work. Few [uncertain] on his front. Info that he is building trenches which are demolished by British fire last night. 9th Canadian Brigade relieves the 7th C. Bde. All reliefs are done with [uncertain] few. Machine Gun fire is hardly known from Enemy. Work. Strengthening Emplacements near BUND. Casualties 1 O.R. wounded.
