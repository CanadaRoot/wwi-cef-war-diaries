## Page 4 — Diary text (December 1917, p. 4)

> Place: BARLIN | Date: 1917, Dec 1–4
>
> **1 December** — Strength of Company 10 Officers - 170 O.R. Attached 1 O.R. (Shoemaker) Weather fine + quite cold. O.C.'s inspection of Company at 9 a.m. Rest of morning spent with squad drill - Gas drill + mechanism of gun
>
> **2 December** — Fine and freezing hard - Company paraded at 9 a.m. to vote Can. Gen. Elections - 150 all ranks polled their votes. Major McFaul acted as Deputy Presiding Officer - There was a pay parade following the voting - Lieut HULBERT joined Can. Corps Gas School near COUPIGNY
>
> [Marginal note, vertical, right edge: for 6 day Course]
>
> **3 December** — fine - very cold - Company paraded at 9 a.m. Spent morning with squad drill - stoppages + immediate action - + gas drill. A special class started today for Officers + N.C.O.'s of the four M.G. Coy's covering a review of training along general lines - [struck through in original: words] Class in BARLIN under supervision of Major WEIR A/D.M.S.C.
>
> [Marginal note, marked *, vertical, right edge: Enemy shelled BARLIN all day. Killed an old woman + a girl - wounded an old woman + two children - used a High Velocity gun with about a 10" Shell - There is apparently no military reason for this shelling as we are the only troops here other than a few "details" and there are no military works - dumps &c - BARLIN is about 8 miles behind the [uncertain] - shelling came from vicinity COURRIERES - 15 miles away - (See next page)]
>
> **4 December** — fine + cold. Company paraded at 8 a.m. Inspection by O.C. followed by Revolver drill. (aiming, snapping practice &c.). Instruction in sanitation + first aid - Gun drill - squad drill - Gas drill. Special class for Officers + N.C.O.'s during afternoon

## Page 5 — Diary text (December 1917, p. 5)

> Place: BARLIN | Date: 1917, Dec 5–8
>
> **5 December** — fine + cold Company paraded at 8 a.m. O.C.'s inspection. morning was spent with squad drill - Instruction in German M.G. Captured by us at PASSCHENDAELE - Gun drill + Gas drill - Company was bathed in afternoon - Officers + N.C.O.'s class as usual.
>
> **6 December** — fine but cloudy - milder - Company paraded at 8 a.m. Spent morning with squad drill - mechanism of gun - Stripping of gun - and gas drill - Lieut BLUNDEN and four N.C.O.'s left to attend the 50th Course M.G. School at CAMIERS Officers + N.C.O.'s class as usual -
>
> **7 December** — fine but cloudy - Company paraded at 8 a.m. and spent morning with squad drill - Gun drill aiming, gas masks - Traversing drill + laying of gun - Gas drill - Company cleaned rifles for 1½ hrs in afternoon - Officers + N.C.O.'s class as usual
>
> **8 December** — showery most of day - Company paraded at 8 a.m. The morning was spent belt-filling. It cleared sufficiently to have also squad drill + gas drill outside - Lieut HULBERT rejoined from Can Corps Gas School -

## Page 8 — Diary text (December 1917, p. 8)

> Place: BARLIN | Date: 1917, Dec 14–17
>
> **14 December (contd)** — recording their vote in the Canadian General Election. 85% of the Company have already voted.
>
> **15 December** — Divine Service was held in conjunction with the 7th, 8th, and 15th M.G. Coy's, in the Y.M.C.A. Hut at 10.30 a.m.
>
> **16 December** — The Company paraded at 9.00 A.M. and carried on with the usual drills. The afternoon was spent cleaning the limbers. The Officers and N.C.O.'s fell in at 1.30 p.m. and after some hours Revolver Drill listened to a lecture on Indication and Recognition of Targets, Fire Orders and Control. 1 O.R., farrier, was attached from 3rd C.D.A.C.
>
> **17 December** — Usual Company Parade at 9 A.M. Pay Parade was held during the afternoon. Early this morning Lieuts. Searles, Rothwell, Rayner, and Newman, proceeded to the Adv. H.Q.'s of the 33rd M.G. Coy. at M6a9.3 (sheet 36 C. S.W.) to look over the ground and positions in anticipation of relieving them at an early date. On their return, they reported the sector as being very quiet.

## Page 10 — Diary text (December 1917, p. 10)

> Place: BARLIN (Dec 22), QUARRIES, Ref. Map. 36 C S.W. 1. (Dec 23–24) | Date: 1917, Dec 22–24
>
> **22 December (contd)** — to Adv. Coy. H.Q.'s of the 250th M.G. Coy., with a view to arranging details concerning relief.
>
> **23 December** — The limbers were packed and Company paraded in full marching order at 8.15 a.m. ready to move out. A, B, and C Sections moved off to the line via BULLY GRENAAY, a halt being made there for a rest and meal before completing the rest of the journey to the trenches. D, Headquarters, and Transport moved off towards MAZINGARBE which was reached about noon. After a few hours rest those going to the trenches again moved off by way of BULLY GRENAAY, MAROC, and LOOS. Company Headquarters was established in the QUARRIES at M6a9.3, Headquarters of the 33rd M.G. Coy, and the following gun positions taken over M18b51.40, N4c35.63, N4c90.00, N8d90.00, N8c08.86, N8c04.92, N9b15.15, N9a34.33, N8a53.49, N9a53.69, N8a49.80, N8d05.05, N4a50.90, N1c40.90, N1d10.95, N1d95.90. The relief was carried through without incident and all complete by 11.00 p.m. The transport stayed overnight at MAZINGARBE and moved next day to permanent lines at LES BREBIS
>
> **24 December** — There was very little activity on either side during the day. During the night one Section carried out harassing fire on enemy dumps and approaches.

## Page 11 — Diary text (December 1917, p. 11)

> Place: QUARRIES (Dec 25), ST LAURENT (Dec 26–30) | Date: 1917, Dec 25–30
>
> **25 December** — Not very much activity. Aeroplanes of both sides active. Finding the present Coy H.Q.'s not quite suitable and too far from the guns another location, at M12b4.4 vacated by the 250th M.G. Coy. was found to be more convenient and we accordingly moved in there.
>
> **26 December** — Our artillery was fairly active, the heavies shelling CITE ST AUGUSTE. Enemy retaliated with heavy Trench Mortars and overhead shrapnel. Our guns fired on enemy approaches during the night.
>
> **27 December** — Both sides more active. Enemy put over a number of Trench Mortars and H.E. on our front and support lines. At night our guns fired on selected targets.
>
> **28 December** — Fairly quiet. Some aerial activity.
>
> **29 December** — Enemy artillery active. Between 9.00 and 10.00 A.M. he put a heavy barrage on our support line. Our heavy artillery retaliated.
>
> **30 December** — The enemy projected gas and bombarded our supports with gas shells and H.E. at 4.50 this morning, the bombardment lasting for 20 minutes. We suffered no casualties. The rest of the day was quieter than usual owing no doubt to the thick haze which made observation extremely difficult.

## Page 12 — Diary text (December 1917, p. 12)

> Place: ST LAURENT | Date: 1917, Dec 31
>
> **31 December** — The enemy artillery was very active all day, firing shells of all calibres over our front support and rear lines. He dropped a few medium shells in close proximity to Coy. H.Qs. but did no damage. Our heavy guns bombarded the enemy lines during the afternoon. Our machine guns carried out the usual night firing programme on enemy dumps and approaches. An Officer of the American Army was attached to this Unit today for instruction. We received a reinforcement draft of 11 O.R., all returned casualties which places our strength at 10 Officers 193 Other Ranks and 2 other ranks attached.
>

*Notes: The contents page lists "Diary Text 9 Pages" (diary text pp. 4–12); there is no appendix for December 1917. All 31 days have entries, and no page is missing from the manifest for December 1917. The marginal note on the 3 December shelling of BARLIN runs across the margins of pp. 4–5. The unit being relieved is written "250th M.G. Coy." in the 22 and 25 December entries, while the 17 and 23 December entries name the 33rd M.G. Coy. — both readings are as written in the original. One word in the shelling note ("8 miles behind the [uncertain]") is obscured by the column rule, and a short passage struck through in the 3 December entry could not be read with confidence.*
