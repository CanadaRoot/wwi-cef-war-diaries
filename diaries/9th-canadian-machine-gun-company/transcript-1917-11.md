## Page 4 — Diary text (November 1917, p. 4)

> Place: ST. JEAN, 'X' CAMP | Date: 1917, Nov 1–2
>
> **1 November** — Company Strength, 9 Officers, 162 O.R., 23 attached. Weather — fine, cool, scattered clouds — Lieut. SEARLES and 18 O.R. reported back to camp in morning from barrage positions, his battery having been relieved by [uncertain] M.M.G. There was a bath parade in afternoon and stores, limbers etc. were got ready to move the next day. The night came up cloudy and there was no bombing. Enemy shelled area however occasionally during night.
>
> **2 November** — Day fine — light clouds — cool — Transport under Lieut. CLARK left lines at 8.15 A.M. for new camping ground at TRAPPISTE CORNR. Company paraded at 8.30 A.M. and marched off at 8.45 A.M. Arrived at entraining point ([uncertain] asylum YPRES) at 9.30 A.M. Entrained at 10.45 A.M. The 7th 8th & 15th M.G. Co's were also on same train as well as the PRINCESS PATS. 49th & 52nd Bn's.

## Page 5 — Diary text (November 1917, p. 5)

> Place: K 24 b. 3.7. Ref. map BELGIUM + FRANCE SHEET 27, 12 d. 2. | Date: 1917, Nov 2–3
>
> **2 November (contd)** — Train left about 11 A.M. + arrived ABEELE. The detraining point at 11.45 A.M. The Company marched from there to a farm at K 24 b. 3.7. about half way between ABEELE + WATOU where billets had been arranged by Lieut RAYNER who had come on by bicycle first thing in morning — The company was housed in tents with exception of B & C sections who were in a barn — (The billets at TRAPPISTE CORNR allotted to us were occupied by Bde, however, our present ground seems to be superior less cut-up + muddy) The transport arrived about 2 P.M. at TRAPPISTE CORNR + owing to change in billets it was quite late in afternoon before all was settled in new camp + evening meal prepared.
>
> **3 November** — Fine — light clouds — no early parades — sections allowed to rest. Muster parade at 11 A.M. Rest of day spent resting and cleaning up.

Acting Sgt to replace casualties in recent operations —

> **7 November** — Physical drill parade at 6.30 a.m. Company paraded at 9 a.m. but was dismissed to tents as heavy rain developed. ## Page 8 — Diary text (November 1917, p. 8)

> Place: [blank] | Date: 1917, Nov 7–9
>
> **7 November (contd)** — [continued from previous page]
>
> **8 November** — Physical drill at 6.30 a.m. Company was paraded at 9 a.m. in fighting kit + inspected. The sections carried on with mechanism + stripping during the morning. One hour was devoted to a thorough inspection of gas respirators + S.A.A. helmets. The rest of day was spent quietly — The two guns were returned from being repaired.
>
> **9 November** — Physical drill at 6.30 a.m. Company paraded at 9 a.m. was inspected — Paraded again at 10 a.m. and joined the 15th Cdn M.G. on their parade ground (adjoining) Both Companies were inspected by Major-General L. J. LIPSETT, C.M.G. G.O.C. 3rd Can. Div. at 10.30 a.m. The General was pleased with the smart appearance of the 9th Coy + congratulated both Companies on their excellent work during the 3rd Divnl attack on BELLEVUE SPUR on the 26th + 30th of Oct. He congratulated the 9th Coy on their successful holding of the new line for some time without infantry support. During the afternoon a warning order came in of a move on tomorrow [struck through in original] — Limbers were packed as far as possible to be in readiness for an early start — During the evening the movement order arrived — we are to move to 'D' Camp — just beyond ST-JEAN (C.27 d.2.) about six [uncertain] from our last camp in that area a week ago — The O.C. Major McFAUL left for England today on leave of absence (14 days). Lieut. DRINKWATER took over the command of the Coy in his absence —

## Page 10 — Diary text (November 1917, p. 10)

> Place: [blank] | Date: 1917, Nov 10
>
> **10 November** — Last night's movement order called for an early start so that everyone was up and about by 4.30 a.m. It had rained heavily through the night and as the Company was marching off about 6.30 a.m. The rain came down again pretty heavily. Rubber sheets helped a little but everyone got pretty damp — We met our convoy of busses on the WATOU-ABEELE road and embussed at K 18 b 3.2. The 7th 8th + 15th M.G. Coy's. also moved by this convoy and the 43rd — 1st C.M.R.'s Bn's — [uncertain] T.M.B. + Bde Hdqrts — By 7.45 a.m. we were all settled pretty comfortably in the busses and made our way to the ASYLUM (in YPRES) where we left our conveyances and marched to our present camp — D Camp was found too small for the four Coy's and E Camp was allotted to us C.27 d.2.6 (SHEET 28) We were housed partly in huts and partly in tents —

## Page 11 — Diary text (November 1917, p. 11)

> Place: C27 d.2.6, Sheet 28 | Date: 1917, Nov 10–11
>
> **10 November (contd)** — The transport left their lines at 9.15 a.m. — in order not to pass the point L 9 c.1.9 ^ before 10 a.m. — and proceeded via (Sheet-27) L10 a 4 b — POPERINGHE — VLAMERTINGHE, SALVATION CORNER to our new camp arriving about 2. P.M. The rain had continued steadily at intervals and the ground about the camp was deep with mud + water — It was very arduous work getting transport + stores into place. Kit was pretty wet — work too — However everyone + everything had got pretty well settled by dark (between 4 + 5 P.M.) and the men had a good hot dinner which was greatly needed — There are many heavy guns in the vicinity + they were pretty active through the night. There was some enemy shelling but a few hundred yds in our rear —
>
> **11 November** — It was fine today with one or two light showers. Accommodation about the camp was made more comfortable + men cleaned up revolvers + rifles during the morning — Enemy shelled area intermittently during day, using H.V. shells + shrapnel. The 15th Cdn M.G. relieved 8 guns each by the 13th + 3rd [uncertain] in Battery No 5 (D11 a 6.9) + Battery No 6 (D5 c 4.0) positions. Four guns of the 8th [uncertain] relieved 4 guns of the 3rd [uncertain] in Battery No 7. D5 c 2.0. and 4 guns of 7th [uncertain] relieved 4 guns of 13th [uncertain] in same battery. Capt. HARTLEY of 15th [uncertain] in command of Barrage guns at CAPITOL. Lieut BURNHAM liaison officer at KORBEK.
