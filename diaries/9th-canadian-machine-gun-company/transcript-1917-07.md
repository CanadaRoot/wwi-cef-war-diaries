## Page 178 — Diary text (July 1917, p. 4)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY" (heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through). Handwritten.

> Place: 3rd Can. [uncertain] Sector | Date: 1917, July 1–3
>
> **1 July** — Company rested except A Section + H.Q. who carried on fatigue during the night 1st/2nd of July. The Commanding [uncertain] Section Commd. [uncertain] out of the line.
>
> **2 July** — The remainder of the Company at TRANSPORT LINES made preparations for again going into the line on the night of the 2nd/3rd relieving the 7th Can. M.G. Coy in the following positions. O.O. 68 attached — 4 Guns A Section 9th Can M.G. Coy relieved 4 Guns A Section 7th Can. M.G. Coy at N32d 20.00 N32d 10.05 T2b 40.12 + T2b 30.35. One gun of B Section 9th Can. M.G. Coy relieved one gun of B Section 7th Can. M.G. Coy. at T2d 3.8 — 4 guns of C Section 9th Can. M.G. Coy relieved 4 guns of D Section 7th Can. M.G. Coy at T8c 30.92; T7b 95.00 T7b 67.42 + T7b 20.10 — One gun of B Section 9th relieved one gun of B. Section 7th Can. M.G. Coy at S12d 47.00 — Relief was complete by 11.30 pm.
>
> **3 July** — From 5.00 am to dusk the day was very quiet indeed very little Enemy Artillery activity — A few Enemy trench mortars in forward area. At night the Enemy Artillery became quite active shelling our Communication Trenches + approaches. [uncertain] Large star shells were also sent up by Enemy during the night.

## Page 179 — Diary text (July 1917, p. 5)

> Place: [blank] | Date: 1917, July 3–6
>
> **3 July (contd)** — forward area. At night the Enemy Artillery became quite active shelling our Communication Trenches + approaches. [uncertain] Large star shells were also sent up by Enemy during the night.
>
> **4 July** — The day was comparatively quiet except for light intermittent shelling. During the night Enemy Artillery more active shelling back area. Communication Trenches + lines of approach. Three thousand five hundred rounds were expended during the night by our Machine Guns firing at Enemy Tracks + Trenches.
>
> **5 July** — The day was comparatively quiet except for light intermittent shelling of Battery Positions. During the night Enemy Artillery more active. We expended 7000 rounds in night firing.
>
> **6 July** — The day was fairly quiet — the Enemy Artillery being more active at night time — During the day we expended 1500 rounds at Enemy Aircraft who on one or two occasions came over our lines flying fairly low. — During the night we expended 7.000 rounds firing at Enemy Trenches + approaches with our Machine Guns.

## Page 181 — Diary text (July 1917, p. 7)

> Place: [blank] | Date: 1917, July 12–15
>
> **12 July (contd)** — The Enemy shelled TRANSPORT LINES with Ten 4.1 shells between midnight + 1 am July 13th 1917 — No damage was done. During the evening an Enemy plane was flying quite low near TRANSPORT LINES firing a machine gun but without effect.
>
> **13 July** — Physical drill at 6.30 am Inspection by O.C. at 9 am Usual Training parades.
>
> **14 July** — Physical drill at 6.30 am Inspection by O.C. at 9 am. Usual training parades — About 5.30 pm. Enemy plane appeared quite suddenly firing two bursts of Machine Gun fire at Observation balloon near our position — The balloon was set on fire but fortunately it was unoccupied. Pay Parade.
>
> **15 July** — The Company spent the day preparing for the line. During the night of the 15th/16th the 9th Can. M.G. Coy relieved the 8th Can. M.G. Coy in the following positions — A Section 9th relieved 4 guns of A Sect 8th Can. M.G. Coy at T7b 20.10. S12d 47.00. S12b 70.50 + S.12a 93.70.

## Page 182 — Diary text (July 1917, p. 8)

> Place: [blank] | Date: 1917, July 15–16
>
> **15 July (contd)** — B Section 9th relieved 4 guns B Section 8th Can. M.G. Coy at T9c 58.85, T2b 60.30, T2b 20.80 + T2b 15.90 — C Section 9th relieved 4 guns C Section 8th Can. M.G. Coy at T8d 65.90 T14a 30.35 (two guns) + T13b 50.15, D Section 9th relieved 4 guns D Section 8th Can. M.G. Coy at T2d 30.30, T8c 30.90, T7b 95.00 + T7b 67.42 — Relief was complete by 12.30 am. During the night Enemy shelled our Communication Trenches + approaches — Our Artillery active against Enemy's defences + Communications.
>
> **15/16 July** — On the night of the 15th/16th Gas was successfully projected on our frontage at 1. am — German retaliation was comparatively light.
>
> **16 July** — Our Artillery very active against Enemy defences in AVION + MERICOURT — The Enemy retaliation light during the day but active against our lines of Communication at night. — During the night HALIFAX + BLUENOSE Trenches + the LENS ARRAS ROAD received attention — During the day Enemy Trench Mortars active in AVION.

## Page 183 — Diary text (July 1917, p. 9)

> Place: [blank] | Date: 1917, July 16–19
>
> **16 July (contd)** — + the LENS ARRAS ROAD received attention — During the day Enemy Trench Mortars active in AVION. One of our planes forced to land near VINY STATION.
>
> **17 July** — Our aeroplanes very active — also our heavy Artillery. Enemy planes over near our front line but out of range of our M. Guns. Heavy shelling by Enemy during the day. Registering on C.Ts + Roads — Some of the Battery positions received attention — Enemy Artillery active at night.
>
> **18 July** — Enemy shelled some of the Battery positions with 5.9s especially in the vicinity of LA CHAUDIERE and Railway Spur. + Bois DE LA CHAUDIERE — Our Artillery active against AVION. — Our aeroplanes active. Enemy Artillery active at night — Lots of Ridge work [uncertain] end of VIMY + PETIT VIMY received attention. We expended 3,500 rounds night firing.
>
> **19 July** — In the morning the Enemy was comparatively quiet.

## Page 184 — Diary text (July 1917, p. 10)

> Place: [blank] | Date: 1917, July 19–22
>
> **19 July (contd)** — All day our Artillery active against AVION — LENS + MERICOURT. In the evening Enemy Artillery more active.
>
> **20 July** — Enemy shelled the usual targets lightly during the day. During the night Enemy shelled our Communications. We expended 7,000 rounds night firing at Targets engaged by our Artillery during the day.
>
> **21 July** — In the early morning much Air activity — Two Enemy planes driven back by our M.G. fire — Between 5 + 6 am two loud Explosions in LENS causing large Clouds of smoke — During the day much activity by our Airmen + Artillery — Several Enemy planes tried to cross our lines between 4 + 9.30 pm. Enemy Artillery active at night against our C.Ts + Roads. We expended 7,500 rounds night firing + against Enemy planes.
>
> **22 July** — During the day Enemy Artillery quiet with the exception of a few heavy shells at intervals at the BOIS DE LA CHAUDIERE. Our Aircraft + Artillery active all day against Enemy defences. At 1 am on the night of 22/23 the 116th Can Battn. made a very successful raid on the German trenches in AVION from Railway Embankment securing 50 to 60 O.R. prisoners + 1 officer — The operation was supported by the 9th + 15th [uncertain] Can. M.G. Companies having 32 guns for M.G. Barrage. — We expended about 25,000 rounds night firing.

## Page 185 — Diary text (July 1917, p. 11)

> Place: [blank] | Date: 1917, July 22–24
>
> **22 July (contd)** — Our Aircraft + Artillery active all day against Enemy defences. At 1 am on the night of 22/23 the 116th Can Battn. made a very successful raid on the German trenches in AVION from Railway Embankment securing 50 to 60 O.R. prisoners + 1 officer — The operation was supported by the 9th + 15th [uncertain] Can. M.G. Companies having 32 guns for M.G. Barrage. — We expended about 25,000 rounds night firing.
>
> **23 July** — In the early morning about 5.30 am. for about half an hour very lively Artillery fighting on both sides. Enemy planes very active flying quite low from 6.30 to 9 am. — The day was quiet — the night was also comparatively quiet. 3000 rounds expended on Enemy Aircraft.
>
> **24 July** — The day was quiet — In the night of the 24th + 25th we were relieved by the 12th Can. M.G. Coy. — After the relief which was completed at 1 am. the Company moved by Sections to TRANSPORT LINES.

## Page 186 — Diary text (July 1917, p. 12)

> Place: GAUCHIN-LEGAL [as written, 26 July]; BURBURE [27 July] | Date: 1917, July 25–31
>
> **25 July** — Company Rested.
>
> **26 July** — At 8.30 sharp the Company moved off to GAUCHIN-LE-GAL. — which was reached within [uncertain] this [uncertain] at 11.30 am. Good billets were obtained for the men + we stayed at the above village for the rest of the day + night.
>
> **27 July** — At 7.15 am sharp the Company moved off to BURBURE which was reached safely at 11.30 am. — The men moved into comfortable billets — Company rested for rest of the day.
>

>
> **29 July** — Pay Parade. Company rested — Owing to wet weather Church Parade was abandoned.
>
> **30 July** — Bathing Parade at 7 am. Usual Training Parades.
>
> **31 July** — Physical drill at 6.30. Inoculation of 108 men. Usual training parade of the remainder.
