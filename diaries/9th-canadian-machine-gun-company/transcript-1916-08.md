## Page 3 — Diary text (August 1916, p. 3)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" ("INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through). Handwritten; headed "VOL. V." and "August 1st to August 31st 1916." Stamped "9TH CANADIAN MACHINE GUN COMPANY." On pp. 3–4 the printed heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through; on pp. 5–15 it is left unstruck. The unit stamp appears at the head of pp. 3–9.

> Place: ZILLEBEKE BUND | Date: 1916, August 1–3
>
> **1 August** — B & C sections 9th M.G. Coy were relieved from OBSERVATORY RIDGE & TUILLERIE POSITIONS respectively overnight Aug 1/2 by two sections 7th M.G. Coy. On completion of relief they came into RESERVE POSITIONS at ZILLEBEKE BUND, MOATED GRANGE, ZILLEBEKE, LAKE SHORE and HELL FIRE CORNER positions, taking over from two sections 8th M.G. Coy who proceeded to rest camp at POPERINGHE. [Remarks: J. McK.]
>
> **2 August** — ZILLEBEKE BUND, ZILLEBEKE and Support trenches shelled lightly during day.
>
> **3 August** — On night Aug 2/3 B & C sections 9th M.G. Coy were relieved by two sections 7th M.G. Coy and on completion of relief moved to MACHINE GUN CAMP. Headquarters was relieved and moved to MACHINE GUN CAMP. B & C section in reserve to and under command O.C. 7th M.G. Coy. [Remarks: J. McK.]

[The words "taking over" in the 1 August entry are inserted in darker ink.]

## Page 4 — Diary text (August 1916, p. 4)

> Place: MACHINE GUN CAMP, ERIE | Date: 1916, August 3–6
>
> **3 August** — Parades as usual 6.15 Physical drill. 9. Care & cleaning of guns. 10.30 General inspections, arms, clothing. [Remarks: J. McK.]
>
> **4 August** — Parades as usual 6.15 Physical drill. 9. Immediate action. 10.30 Limber drill and packing. 11-12. Mechanism. The section to-day received 150 Lee Enfield rifles thus equipping each man with a Lee-Enfield. [Remarks: J. McK.]
>
> **5 August** — Parades as usual 6.15 Physical drill. 9. Immediate action. 10-12 "D" section ranges. 10-11 "A" section Gun drill. 11-12 "A" section Field work.
>
> **6 August** — Parades as under 6.15 Physical drill. 9. Limber drill. 10. Mechanism. 11. Bath parade.

## Page 5 — Diary text (August 1916, p. 5)

> Place: MACHINE GUN CAMP, ERIE | Date: 1916, August 7–8
>
> **7 August** — Parades as usual 6.15 Physical drill. 9. Immediate action. 10-12 "A" section ranges. 10-11 "D" section Limber packing drill. 11-12 "D" section Mechanism. [Remarks: J. McK.]
>
> **8 August** — Parades as usual 6.15 Physical drill. 9 Care & cleaning. 10. Gun drill. 10-12 Stoppages. [Remarks: J. McK.]
>
> Our transport was caught in a cloud of gas from the North and as a result 2 horses had to be destroyed. The gas attack was put on by the enemy North of the MENIN road, and was followed by heavy shelling by both sides. The helmets used by our transport drivers proved satisfactory. Gas densest about HELL FIRE corner and YPRES. None in 9th Bde frontage.

[The gas narrative has no separate date in the original; it follows the 8 August parade entry.]

## Page 7 — Diary text (August 1916, p. 7)

> Place: PROMENADE I 21 b 5.2 | Date: 1916, August 10–12
>
> **10 August (contd)** — and proceeded to MACHINE GUN CAMP ERIE.
>
> **11 August** — Intermittent shelling by the enemy with much heavier retaliation by our batteries. Enemy shelled DORMY HOUSE, COTTING, SQUARE WOOD. 10 O.R. arrived from Base as reinforcements. [Remarks: J. McK.] The 60th Battn 9th C. Bde relieved the 10th Can. Battn, the last Battn of the 1st Canadian Division to be relieved. 8th Can Bde on our right, and the 7th Can Bde on our left.
>
> **12 August** — Very heavy artillery and Trench Mortar bombardment by enemy started at 8.15. At 8.30 intense bombardment concentrated on trenches around RAILWAY COTTING. About 9.45 information received Germans are in trench 38. Bombing is heard but nothing can be seen owing to intense dust.

## Page 8 — Diary text (August 1916, p. 8)

> Place: PROMENADE I 21 b 5.2 | Date: 1916, August 12–13
>
> **12 August (contd)** — 10.40 42nd Battn report dense barrage of 5.9 on routes from VALLEY COTTAGES. Reports trench 46 taken but not confirmed. Communication with front line suspended through some unknown reason. Communication was attempted through 52nd Battn at VERBRANDENMOLEN. 6 runners were despatched to front line but failed to make connection.
>
> 11.35 Communication with front battalion established. 60th Battn report trenches much battered. Feared casualties heavy. Things not serious. Situation well in hand. Two LEWIS GUNS out of commission but replaced by COLT GUNS.
>
> **13 August** — Quiet throughout the day. Machine gun fire not so active. Intermittent shelling by enemy on TRANSPORT during the night.

## Page 10 — Diary text (August 1916, p. 10)

> Place: PROMENADE I 21 b 9.1 | Date: 1916, August 16–18
>
> **16 August** — Enemy artillery active. Trenches in front of hill 60 and the ground behind was shelled. Machine Guns active at night, and also artillery on the front line of centre Sector. B & C Sections in Camp. Programme of work, Firing, practical stoppages, and field training.
>
> **17 August** — Observatory ridge shelled, and neighbourhood of Railway and [struck through in original: the] the DUMP. Sections. A. D were relieved by B, C. Plans of work were handed over for the improvement of dugouts and Emplacements.
>
> **18 August** — Observatory ridge pretty heavily shelled. Railway dugouts also shelled and the batteries in the neighbourhood. Work continues on. Emplacement at S.P.12, HALIFAX Trench and SQUARE WOOD. All Emplacements in our left Group except SQUARE WOOD, have deep Emplacements.

## Page 11 — Diary text (August 1916, p. 11)

> Place: PROMENADE I 21 b 9.1 | Date: 1916, August 18–20
>
> **18 August (contd)** — A. D sections in MACHINE GUN CAMP. Inspection parade only to-day. TRANSPORT ROUTES are shelled at night.
>
> **19 August** — Fairly Quiet in the morning. In the afternoon about 3 Pm. Enemy began heavy bombardment of front line from TRENCHES 38 to 5.4. Very heavy round RAILWAY CUTTING. Casualties were not heavy and there was no attack attempted. Night is very Quiet, with scarcely a shot fired. A. D sections carry on training in Firing and field work. [Remarks: J. McK.]
>
> **20 August** — Enemy artillery fairly active all day. MANOR FARM, and trenches in front of HILL 60, and also OBSERVATORY RIDGE are shelled. Throughout the day the ground in front of HILL 60 has been shelled for 800 yards back. 58th, 52nd Battalions are relieved in the front line by 43rd & 60th battalions. Evening Quiet but wet. [Remarks: J. McK.]

>

> **22 August** — Quiet during the day except for intermittent shelling on HALIFAX ST.
>
> **23 August** — Quiet during day. The 9th Can. M.G. Coy was relieved on the night Aug 23/24 by the 10th M.G. Coy B.C.E.F. [uncertain] after five months in YPRES SALIENT. The 9th Bde is relieved. [Remarks: J. McK.]
>
> Place: MACHINE GUN COMPANY | Date: 1916, August 24–25
>
> **24 August** — Day spent in camp. Inspection and General clean up. [Remarks: J. McK.]
>
> **25 August** — Parades as usual 6.15 Physical drill. 9 B & C section Inspection. A & D section Immediate action. 10. IRON RATION PARADE. 11. FIELD WORK. [Remarks: J. McK.]
