> Place: BURBURE | Date: 1917, August 1–9
>

>
> **6 August** — Usual Training parades including Range practice + revolver drill.
>
> **7 August** — Usual training parades including range practice + revolver drill.
>
> **8 August** — Usual training parades including range practice + revolver drill.
>
> **9 August** — Usual training parades including range practice with revolvers.

> **10 August** — Usual Training parades including range practice with Guns + revolvers.
>

>

## Page 198 — Diary text (August 1917, p. 6)

> Place: Fosse 2 at LES BREBIS, Map Ref. BOIS RASE 1/20.000 | Date: 1917, August 21–22
>
> **21 August** — The Company moved from VILLERS AU BOIS at 10 am for Fosse 2 at LES BREBIS. Camping for the night.
>
> **22 August** — Prepared for the line + marched off at 8.15 p.m. + on the night of the 22/23rd relieved the 1st + 13th Can. M.G. Coys in the following positions. Two guns at H25d 35.95. Two guns H26c 10.80. Two guns H26c 30.30. Two guns H31b 80.60, Two guns H32a 30.30. One gun at H32c 10.80. One gun at H32c 00.50. One gun H31d 95.40. One gun at H31d 80.20. One gun at N1b 50.70. One gun at N1b 90.20. + One gun N1d 95.95. — Relief was completed by 12.30 am. Strength of the Company going into the line was 7 Officers 96 O.R. + 32 Attached men.

## Page 199 — Diary text (August 1917, p. 7)

> Place: [blank] | Date: 1917, August 23–25
>
> **23 August** — Enemy Artillery very active at night. [uncertain] of 22 + 23rd. During the day [uncertain] Air activity by both sides. Enemy Snipers active. — Many German dead lying in forward positions. During the night of the 23/24 Heavy German bombardment on our left. Bombardment to our left with surface detonating fuses. — Artillery on both sides gradually quieter down but increased to a steady bombardment in our right (near LENS).
>
> **24 August** — Considerable air-craft activity, mostly ours. — Dark, heavy bombardment on both sides during the night. Our trenches received many direct hits.
>
> **25 August** — Enemy planes up — We fired 2,000 rounds at enemy aircraft during the early morning. — Enemy Artillery more active during the last 24 hours. At 11 am shelling was fairly heavy in the vicinity of our gun positions. At 7.15 pm enemy laid quite a heavy barrage on our front line.

## Page 200 — Diary text (August 1917, p. 8)

> Place: [blank] | Date: 1917, August 26–28
>
> **26 August** — Enemy planes active. Intermittent shelling by both sides during the day. During the night enemy shelled our Rear Communications. — We expended 2,000 rounds at enemy aircraft. Owing to rain trenches in a very bad state. Considerable sniping on both sides. Our Machine Guns active. The 18th M.G. Coy relieved 8 of our guns on the night of the 26/27th. Trenches in a bad state — Much Mud owing to recent rains.
>
> **27 August** — Both enemy + our Artillery doing Counter Battery work. Enemy shelled our front line + Communication Trenches. Bombing Attack at night. Both Artilleries active in [uncertain] for about an hour. M.G.s also active. — Enemy fired gas shells at our positions. During the night of the 27th + 28th our eight remaining guns were relieved by the 13th M.G. Coy. — Upon completion of relief crews moved to Fosse 2 de BETHUNE.
>
> **28 August** — Strength of Company after coming out of line [continued on next page]
