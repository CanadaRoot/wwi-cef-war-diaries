## Page 4 — Diary text (October 1917, p. 4)

> Place: AUX REITZ | Date: 1917, Oct 1–2
>
> **1 October** — Weather. clear and sunny. Company strength 10 Officers 175 Other Ranks. Company is at present out of the line and is carrying on with preparations and training for coming operations. Reveille 6.00 AM. Physical Drill 6.30–7.00 AM. Breakfast 7.30 AM. O.C.'s inspection 9.00 AM. Training 9–12.15 p.m. Dinner 12.30 p.m. Special parade 2–4 p.m. Tea 4.30 p.m. Tattoo 9.30 p.m. Lights Out 9.45 p.m. This morning all sections were on training under section N.C.Os in immediate action, stoppages and mechanisms. The Commanding Officer took all section Officers to the taped trenches to go over plans for coming offensive. Each sections programme was carefully gone over and all details made clear. All Officers are now familiar with one anothers plans and all out-section Officers are in a position to take over any section in case of necessity.
>
> **2 October** — Weather. clear and sunny. Parades 9–10 a.m. All sections Squad drill 10–11 a.m. All sections Limber packing and preparation for practice over taped trenches in afternoon. 11.30 a.m. sections paraded independently and proceeded to taped trenches. These were 9th Cdn. Inf. Bde manoeuvres with attached machine guns, Trench Mortars and Tanks. The practice was quite successful and several improvements were made on first plans. All ranks were able to see exactly the positions which would be taken up by the infantry and to fix firmly in their minds the relative positions of all trenches, roads, buildings, etc. etc. In addition we were able to ascertain with accuracy the time taken by all guns to reach their allotted positions, also the timing of the Artillery barrage was very clearly shown.

"D" Section paraded to Divisional Gas School to have Gas Respirators Containers exchanged.

> Place: AUX REITZ | Date: 1917, Oct 5–8
>

>
> **6 October** — Weather — cold and rainy. Weather prevented all outside parades. Sections carried on with mechanisms, care and cleaning etc in huts.
>

## Page 15 — Diary text (October 1917, p. 15)

> Place: PASSCHENDAELE AREA | Date: 1917, Oct 26
>
> **26 October** — The attack was launched at 5.40 A.M. by the 8th & 9th Can. Inf Brigade on the PASSCHENDAELE outer defences, including BELLEVUE SPUR. The guns of this company jumped off with the infantry as pre-arranged.
>
> **26 October — 4th C.M.R. frontage** — Lieut SEARLES and two guns jumped off at ZERO and took up positions at D.4. b.0.4. consolidating same. The infantry then retired leaving the two guns isolated and unsupported and the crest of the ridge unoccupied except for these two guns. Lieut SEARLES then went back to find the reason for the retirement and finding no Officer, re-organized the infantry — 4th C.M.R. and led them forward to a commanding position and under his direction the crest was consolidated. When supports came up (4th C.M.R.) he reported over to a company commander.
>
> **26 October (contd)** — Eight machine guns were taken from pill-boxes at D.4. b.0.4. but we were unable to bring them out. This was carried out under very heavy machine gun and rifle fire and the action of Lieut SEARLES and his section was most commendable.
>
> **26 October — SNIPING BATTERY D.4.a.1.7.** — Four guns under Lieut DUTHIE reached the pre-arranged position at D.4.a.1.7. and came into action. When the infantry retired these guns were withdrawn after losing 1 Officer and 2 gun crews. These guns (2) were again advanced under Sgt. LOVE and took positions (defensive) at D.4.a.3.5. later (3 P.M) moving to original positions at D.4.a.1.7. Sgt. LOVE displayed great coolness and bravery throughout the operations.
>
> **26 October — 43rd Bn. Frontage** — On jumping off Lieut ELLIS M.C. was wounded in the shoulder but carried on throughout the morning and got two guns into action at D.4. d.7.5. at 6.00 A.M. Two guns were knocked out before reaching the position.

## Page 17 — Diary text (October 1917, p. 17)

> Place: [blank] | Date: 1917, Oct 26
>
> **26 October (contd) — 58th Bn. Frontage** — Two guns under Lieut MADGETT A.R. jumped off at ZERO and proceeded about 100 yds and waited until the situation cleared before advancing. On the retirement of the infantry, as these guns were in such a position as to be of no use it was decided to retire. This was frustrated by enemy machine gun and rifle fire so a stand was made. Lieut MADGETT and 2 O.R. remained in position until 58th Bn. advanced over them when they withdrew because the guns was out of action.
>
> **26 October (contd) — SNIPING GUNS D.16 b.** — At ZERO hour these guns opened fire on MEETCHEELE and GRAFH WOOD and carried on until +120. On 27th more firing was done again throughout the day on targets of opportunity were engaged because of the poor visibility.
>
> **26 October — Casualties** — Officers Wounded: Capt E. I. H INGS; Lt. M. L. ELLIS; LT. J. H DUTHIE. Reinforcements: 10 O R. O.R Killed 8, Wounded 30.
