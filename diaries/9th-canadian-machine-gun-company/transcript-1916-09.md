## Page 3 — Diary text (September 1916, p. 2)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" ("INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through). Handwritten; headed "9th Cdn. Mch. Gun Coy.", "Sept. 1st to Sept. 30th 1916." Remarks column initialled throughout, appears to read "A. McF."

> Place: STEENVOORDE | Date: 1916, September 1–5
>
> **1 September** — Parades: 6.00-6.45 am Physical Drill; 8.30-9.30 Mechanism & Immediate Action; 9.30-10.30 Limber Drill; 10.30-12.30 Tactical Scheme, using Coy as a whole.
>
> **2 September** — 9 am. — Field day, using limber & field telephone
>
> **3 September** — Parades: 10.15 am. Church Parade; 2.00 pm. Pay Parade.
>
> **4 September** — Parades: 6.00-6.45 am Physical Drill; 8.30-9.30 Mechanism & Immediate Action; 9.30-10.30 Company Drill; 10.30-11.30 Cleaning Guns; 1.00 pm. Tactical Scheme, in conjunction with Infantry.
>
> **5 September** — Parades: 7.30 am. 1½ Sections. Tactical Scheme with Infantry; 12.00 noon. 1½ " " " " As usual for balance of company.

## Page 5 — Diary text (September 1916, p. 4)

> Place: VADENCOURT / POZIERES | Date: 1916, September 14–16
>
> **14 September** — Marched out at 6.30 a.m. and arrived at "The Brick Fields" about 8.15 a.m. The Company came under the command of the O. C. Motor M. G. Bde. Two sections (B+D) went into the line and were located at R 34 central.
>
> **15 September** — The 2nd Canadian Division attacked 6.20. am in the neighbourhood of COURCELETTE. which was very successful. Our 8 guns were employed in putting a barrage on the rear of Enemy's lines, [word struck through, uncertain] COURCELETTE SUGAR FACTORY, and Communication Trenches. CASUALTIES. Lieut McFAUL slightly wounded. Corpl. McGeath wounded. Pte H. Jones. Corp. Bird.
>
> **16 September** — During the evening 2nd Canadian Division took COURCELETTE. Hostile shelling has continued heavy since yesterday's attack. German batteries are [uncertain] to the front line and within range of our guns.

## Page 6 — Diary text (September 1916, p. 5)

> Place: POZIERES | Date: 1916, September 16–17
>
> **16 September (contd)** — Our guns are ordered to engage a German 77. cm battery. Guns are trained on German front line at 6 pm with orders to open fire if S.O.S goes up. Casualties 1. O.R. Killed 1. O.R. wounded.
>
> **16/17 September** — 7 CAN. Bde. attack the ZOLLERN GRABEN at 5. P.m. Under cover of this the 9th Bde had orders to take post. with right wing on the newly captured ZOLLERN GRABEN. and left on our original front line, and at 6.30 pm. they were to attack in a westerly direction and take the ZOLLERN REDOUBT. 7th CAN. Bde is relieved by the 9th. Both had heavy casualties without gaining their objective.
>
> **17 September** — 9th CAN. M.G. Coy relieve the 7th CAN. M.G. Coy in R. 34. Central. 8 guns which were under 2nd Div. were released and returned to Camp at ALBERT. CASUALTIES 2 O.R. wounded

## Page 7 — Diary text (September 1916, p. 6)

> Place: POZIERES | Date: 1916, September 18–19
>
> **18 September** — Guns at R.34 Central are under heavy shell fire most of the time. Three guns were buried today but no damage done to them. Quite a quantity of equipment has been lost by shell fire. Mutual bombardment of front lines is carried on but practically no change of position. Men are very badly shaken. Casualties. 4 O.R. wounded 1 O.R. Sick.
>
> **19 September** — Weather has come on very wet now and the men find it very difficult to dig in the clay soil. Lack of shelter is telling hard on the men. Two guns are advanced to support the front line in R.28 a 30. They cover a gap between the 43rd Can Bn and the [uncertain — word struck through] DORSETS on our left. No incident of note occurred. Casualties. 4. O.R. wounded 5 Sick.

## Page 8 — Diary text (September 1916, p. 7)

> Place: [blank — POZIERES, contd] | Date: 1916, September 20–21
>
> **20 September** — Still wet and heavy bombardment goes on on both sides. Orders came through last night for the 43rd & 58th Bns. to attack a portion of the ZOLLERN GRABEN to improve our position. 58th Bn were to bomb along westward and 43rd were to attack in a southerly direction so that their right flank would join the 58th in the ZOLLERN GRABEN. 58th failed to bomb to their objective. 43rd Bn. got into their objective with few casualties but their communication was cut off and the enemy bombed from both sides. The Company was practically annihilated that engaged in the attack. CASUALTIES. 1 O.R. Killed 2 O.R. Sick and evacuated Lieut Page was evacuated to Rest Station suffering from Muscular Cramps.
>
> **21 September** — Weather continues wet and sickness is very prevalent. The men have little time to rest. Heavy bombardment on both sides but no incident of note [continued on next page]

## Page 9 — Diary text (September 1916, p. 8)

> Place: ALBERT / WARLOY / HERISSART | Date: 1916, September 21–25
>
> **21 September (contd)** — 1. O.R. Evacuated Sick.
>
> **22 September** — Usual shelling goes on but no change of position. The Company was relieved by the 34 British M.G. Coy. and returned to Camp at ALBERT. 1. O.R. Evacuated Sick.
>
> **23 September** — Kit inspection in the morning and the men are allowed to rest for the day. Men got a bath in ALBERT but got no clean underwear.
>
> **24 September** — The Brigade left ALBERT to go into Camp in the WARLOY area. Men sleep in the open owing to unclean condition of billets.
>
> **25 September** — Brigade leave WARLOY for HERISSART. Men have got dry billets and are quite comfortable. Lieuts. CLARK and HENRY reported as reinforcements [uncertain — remainder of this entry is faint and partly illegible; it appears to read "... when in the field he received word 9.35 am that company ..." and continues on the next page]

## Page 10 — Diary text (September 1916, p. 9)

> Place: HERISSART / VADENCOURT | Date: 1916, September 26–28
>
> **26 September** — [continued from 25 September] parades with 52nd Bn who are to be visited by Major General LIPSETT. Commdg 3rd Can. Divn. Impossible to be there. The General addresses the Company about 10.20 am. GENERAL HILL. Commdg 9th C. I. Bde complimented the company on its smart appearance on the march. Casualties. 2. O.R. Evacuated Sick.
> **27 September** — Company drill. Gun drill. Field work. The afternoon was left free for the men. Casualties. 2. O.R. evacuated Sick.
>
> **28 September** — Left HERISSART for VADENCOURT. Men got a bath in the afternoon and clean clothes.
