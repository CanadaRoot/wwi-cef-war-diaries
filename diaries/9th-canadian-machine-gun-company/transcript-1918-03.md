> Place: AVION–MERICOURT SECTOR | Date: 1918, Mar 1–5
>
> **1 March** — Company in line, occupying same positions as on 28.2.18. No change in situation. Intermittent shelling of selected points over area by both sides. Scattered hostile Machine Gun fire over our trenches at night — We carried out night harassing fire on enemy's lines, communication trenches, dumps etc. [struck through in original: "M.G's"] 3 enemy planes over during morning — Work done improving emplacements for M.G's, laying out or checking Battle lines or S.O.S. lines of fire — Rations were brought up as usual by Light Ry. >

>
> **3 March** — Situation normal — Night harassing fire carried on —
>
> **4 March** — Situation unchanged — Pretty quiet — Night firing as usual —
>

## Page 338 — Diary text (March 1918, p. 8)

> Place: " | Date: 1918, Mar 13–14
>
> **13 March** — Situation Quiet. Low visibility on account of heavy ground mist which hung until about noon. Orders received from D.M.G.C. 3rd Cdn. ## Page 340 — Diary text (March 1918, p. 10)

> Place: " | Date: 1918, Mar 16–19
>
> **16 March** — Situation Very Quiet. Low hanging ground mist until noon. Sections employed on usual routine work and on dugout and emplacement maintenance.
>
> **17 March** — Situation Quiet. Exceptionally clear atmosphere. Great air activity by both sides all day. Our A.A. M.G. guns had [uncertain] targets as Hostile planes were kept pretty high. Sections carried out usual work and in addition did collected salvage.
>
> **18 March** — Situation Quiet. Low visibility. To-day all main S.A.A. dumps were overhauled, re-stacked and brought up to strength. Sections carried out usual routine work. Salvage work is proving very successful.
>
> **19 March** — Situation Quiet. Low hanging clouds with intermittent rain showers. Not much outside work possible. Salvage carried on, and usual routine work.

## Page 341 — Diary text (March 1918, p. 11)

> Place: " | Date: 1918, Mar 20–23
>
> **20 March** — Situation very quiet. Cloudy with some rain in morning but sun broke through in afternoon. Relief of the 5th Cdn. M.G. Coy by the 9th Cdn M.G. Coy on night of 21st/22nd March arranged by Major [uncertain] McFAUL. Operation orders issued this evening, copy attached as appendix. Sections carried on with usual routine work and salvage, maintenance of dugouts and emplacements.
>
> **21 March** — Situation unchanged. Warm sunny day — relief was carried out in evening without difficulty. Appendix shows distribution of guns.
>
> **22 March** — Situation unchanged — fine weather continues — Night harassing fire was carried out on various enemy overland routes, trenches etc. A large gas projector discharge was put over into Lens just before midnight from the Givenchy front — Bosch retaliated with artillery —
>
> **23 March** — Normal —
>
> The four M.G. Companies of the 3rd Can. Div. Cease to exist to-day on the formation of the 3rd Can. Divnl M.G. Battalion. The 7 + 8th Coys. Combine to form "A" Coy + the 9th + 5th Coys to form "B" Coy each composed of 32 guns + under the command of Major PARKES + Capt HARTLEY respectively. Major MOORHOUSE Commands the Bn. Each Coy is composed of 4 Batteries with a Capt in charge —
>
> [signed] *R. E. Drinkwater Capt for O.C. 9th Coy.*
>
> [This is the last diary entry. The 9th Canadian Machine Gun Company ceases to exist 23 March 1918, amalgamated into the 3rd Canadian Divisional Machine Gun Battalion.]
