## Page 293 — Diary text (January 1918, p. 7)

> Place: LES BREBIS / HILL 70 Sector | Date: 1918, Jan 8–10
>
> **8 January** — Company training carried on. Brigade Veterinary Officer carried out very thorough inspection of Transport and passed very favorable comments.
>
> **9 January** — Very cold during morning. Sections busy preparing equipment for the line and packing limbers. Weather moderated during afternoon and about 4 p.m. a heavy snow fall commenced which lasted for over three hours. Owing to guides from 8th M.G. Coy. being late our company moved off at 4.45 p.m. instead of 4.15 p.m. to relieve the 8th Coy. in the "Hill 70 Sector". Relief was completed successfully by 8 p.m. Situation normal — no marked activity on either side.
>
> Place: HILL 70 Sector. REF. MAPS Sheets 36 C. N.W. + 36 C. S.W. 2. BENIFONTAINE [as written] | Date: 1918, Jan 10
>
> **10 January** — The enemy seems to devote most of his attention to counter-battery work and pays particular attention to LOOS, shelling it intermittently day and night. His T.M's open up two and three times daily on different parts of the Brigade front and support trenches extending from H.25 63.0. to N.2 d 25 80. His M.Gs sweep our parapets once in a while and one of his guns has an annoying habit of enfilading NETLEY Trench in N.1 a r.6. From 12.30 a.m. to 5.30 a.m. our gun at N.1 a 60 80 fired 2250 rounds at junctions of enemy trenches.
>

## Page 294 — Diary text (January 1918, p. 8)

> Place: HILL 70 SECTOR | Date: 1918, Jan 11–12
>
> **11 January** — Low clouds and rain. No aircraft activity and less shelling. From 2 to 4 p.m. our artillery and trench mortars put down a bombardment in H.25. Company Hdqs and Sections busy making preparations for coming raid by 2nd C.M.R. Battalion.
>
> **12 January** — 3.10 a.m. The 2nd C.M.R. Battalion carried out a successful raid at this hour with the assistance of Artillery, T.M's and Machine Guns. Our part of the task consisted of covering all approaches to the points of entry. 9 of our M.Gs with the assistance of 6 guns of the 1st Motor M.G. ## Page 296 — Diary text (January 1918, p. 10)

> Place: HILL 70 SECTOR | Date: 1918, Jan 14
>
> **14 January** — A snowfall of two or three inches during the early hours of the morning affords a welcome relief to the eyes. The eternal mud is out of sight — for how long? Situation normal. 'A' Battery under LIEUT ADAMS, 1st C.M. M.G. Brigade were relieved by 'B' Battery of the same unit at 6 p.m. under LIEUT TRENCH. All preparations made and calculations checked for coming raid.
>
> The 5th C.M.R. Battalion carried out what was to be a 'stealth' raid at 11 p.m. Raiding party consisted of 2 Officers and 26 O.Rs. They penetrated the German line at H 32 d 75-45; captured 2 prisoners of the 209th R.I.R. and one Machine Gun. They suffered only 1 O.R. wounded. The idea was to cut the wire by hand and carry on without assistance. Failing this a T.M. Rocket (RED-GREEN-RED) was to be fired, calling for a 'Box' barrage from the Artillery — T.M's and M.G's. Specially prepared Bath mats were ready to be thrown over the wire to enable the raiders to cross. The part the M.G's were to perform was to put a hail of bullets into all approaches to the point of entry. ## Page 297 — Diary text (January 1918, p. 11)

> Place: HILL 70 SECTOR | Date: 1918, Jan 14 (contd)–15
>
> **14 January (contd)** — complete their task without assistance. However, promptly on the half hour, the signal rocket went up and all arms immediately responded. We fired 15000 rds in 30 minutes in answer to this call. In view of an anticipated German relief during the night 7750 more rounds were fired on the main avenues of approach.
>
> **15 January** — The snow has gone and the rain and mud are with us. This means more work for the already tired-out gun crews. The trenches in this sector are cut in chalk which is very porous and soaks up great quantities of water. A frost turns this water into ice and the consequent expansion cracks and breaks up the chalk. As soon as it thaws and rains down come the sides of the trenches. ## Page 298 — Diary text (January 1918, p. 12)

> Place: HILL 70 Sector | Date: 1918, Jan 15 (contd)–17
>
> **15 January (contd)** — brooding on 'the horrors of war.' Situation normal. No aircraft activity on account of rain and consequently less artillery fire.
>
> **16 January** — Situation remains unchanged. Enemy continues to pound our front and support lines and communication trenches with various calibre shells and trench mortars. He also keeps up his counter battery work — our batteries in and around LOOS getting the most attention. The M. Guns are becoming more active, sweeping HILL 70, our support and communication trenches. Our M. Guns fired 21000 rounds on selected targets on the other side of the line. The rain let up at noon.
>
> **17 January** — The rain started again in the early hours of the morning and continued all day. All the dugouts are leaking badly, making living conditions miserable and making it very hard at Coy. Headquarters to carry on the Orderly room work. We fired 13000 rds from dusk till dawn on targets given to us thru Division. 100,000 rounds of S.A.A. were brought up from the [uncertain] on the light railway. Captain PERRY and 3 other officers of the 2nd Can. M.G. Coy visited our positions to-day with a view to relieving us shortly.
