## Notes — February–March 1915
- **March 1915 is present.** The volume covers 7 February–31 March 1915; March entries (1–31 March) occupy pp. 10–15 of this set. There is no gap for March.
- **The unit lands in France on 7 February 1915** at Rouen, disembarking from H.M. Transport, and marches north via Neufchâtel, Abbeville and St. Omer to Arques, then Hazebrouck, Outtersteene/Merris, Strazeele, Sailly-sur-la-Lys and Estaires.
- **Strength on arrival:** 4 N.C.O.s and 22 men billeted at Rouen (plus officers).
- **Named people:** Lieut Dickson, A.S.C. (in charge of an ambulance convoy, 10 February). No other personal names are given; the commanding officer signs only as Woodhouse, Major.
- **Casualties:** none recorded in this volume. The work is entirely sanitary: enteric (typhoid) fever enquiries and house-to-house surveys, water chlorination, blanket disinfection (formaldehyde), latrine and refuse work.
- **Disease:** enteric fever among civilians is the recurring concern — surveys at Outtersteene, Merris, Strazeele, Pradeele, Borre, Caestre, Flêtre and Meteren; suspicious houses placed out of bounds for Canadians; a civilian enteric case near Caestre cleared to Bailleul; a child who died presumably of scarlet fever at Sailly (8 March) and measles cases (17 March).
- **Incident:** 10 February, Rouen — the section's motor lorry collided with a motor car (780-Y 4); a mud guard was sprung and a brass axle cap cracked. Repair cost probably 4 francs.
- **Gaps:** no separate entries for 14 February (covered in the 15 February entry), 15 March (covered in the 14 March entry) or 24 February (no summary line; the Flêtre/Meteren survey appears to continue across it). The diary is written up each evening, usually at 7 P.M.
- No appendices are listed or included for this volume.
