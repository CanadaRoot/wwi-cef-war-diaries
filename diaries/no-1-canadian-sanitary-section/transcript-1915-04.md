## Notes — April–May 1915
- **May 1915 is present.** The volume covers 1 April–31 May 1915; May entries (1–31 May) occupy pp. 8–14 of this set. There is no gap for May.
- **Discipline case, 5–9 April:** on leaving Estaires the section found the gas meter in its billet had been broken open; four men were implicated — a corporal (name [uncertain]) placed under arrest and Privates Pilling, Edmonds and Normoyle made prisoners on charges of theft of money. The men were released on D.A.D.M.S. instructions after undertaking to pay the gas company's costs; the company accepted 7 francs and gave a receipt in full.
- **Second Battle of Ypres, 22–26 April:** Poperinghe and Ypres shelled (23 April); the section worked through the nights of 23–25 April dressing wounded and evacuating them for No. 3 Field Ambulance, its motor lorry carrying 21–26 slightly wounded and up to 20 gas cases per trip, active 24 hours removing wounded. On 27 April the unit moved to Watou to assist the Rest Station of No. 1 Field Ambulance.
- **Rest Station, Steenvoorde/Hinges:** opened at Steenvoorde 29 April for No. 1 Field Ambulance (accommodation for 200 sick); at Hinges it held 26 patients on 24 May, rising to 92 on 27 May, and 167 patients in total had been admitted by 31 May, when all were discharged to No. 1 and No. 3 Ambulances on the unit's move.
- **Named people:** Lieut Dickson does not recur. Named in this volume: Privates Pilling, Edmonds and Normoyle; A.D.M.S. Col. Skinner (4 May); General Jones (visited the mess, 11 May); the Prince of Wales (Grenadier Guards colonel who took the officers' billet at Hinges, 19 May).
- **Disease:** measles among reinforcements (16th Battalion, 9 and 11 May; 8 cases by 16 May), cerebro-spinal meningitis (Divisional Supply Column, No. III Field Ambulance, 8th Battalion), typhoid fever from artillery units traced to use of untested water during the Ypres battle, mumps (13th Battalion at Robecq/Hinges), scarlet fever and enteric fever in civilians.
- **Casualties:** none of the section's own men are recorded killed or wounded; the section's men acted as burial squads for mortalities at the hospitals (22 May).
- **Movements:** Estaires → Steenvoorde (5 April) → Vlamertinghe (13 April) → Watou/Steenvoorde (27–29 April) → Bailleul (4 May) → Steenwaerike/Nieppe (5 May) → Robecq (16 May) → Hinges (19 May, with No. II Field Ambulance).
- No appendices are listed or included for this volume.
