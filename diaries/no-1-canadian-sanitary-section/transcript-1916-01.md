## Notes — January 1916
- The unit remained at St-Jans Cappel for the whole month; every entry is timed 7 P.M. All 31 days are covered with no gaps in dates.
- The month's main thread is the diphtheria outbreak at the Convent orphanage at St-Jans Cappel: a suspected case was reported on 8 January by Capt. Gibson (Divisional Headquarters Staff); the patient was given antitoxin, isolated, and sent to the Civilian Hospital at Bailleul, with contacts swabbed and given immunizing antitoxin. The Mobile Lab confirmed the case as diphtheria (10 January). Further cases and carriers were found on 18–20 January, and by 22 January the whole Convent — 100 people — had been immunized. A suspected smallpox case on 16 January proved not to be smallpox.
- Water supply dominates the sanitary work: polluted wells at Westhoff Farm (2 January), the wells above the Hyde Park dressing station (5–6 January), and the unfinished wells at Bulford Camp, which the Engineers were repeatedly pressed to finish, bank up and cover.
- Lectures given during the month included the transmission of typhoid and other intestinal disease (14 January), venereal disease (21 and 24 January), and "Disposal of Camp refuse" (31 January, to the Officers Training School).
- Named people: Capt. Gibson (Divisional Headquarters Staff), Surgeon General Jones (inspected the Hyde Park dressing station, 6 January), Capt. S. [uncertain] (2 January), and a Lt-Col [uncertain] whose vacated room was used to isolate the diphtheria case (20 January). The O.C. is J. A. Amy.
- No combat casualties are recorded in January 1916 and no deaths are recorded. The diary is a sanitary/inspection record, not a battle diary.
- The O.C. repeatedly notes awaiting word from London about a "Pay reduction" / "degradation of pay" (28, 29 and 31 January).
- The cover's "Volume 10" (with "11." written below it) and the contents page's "Volume 16-1" disagree; both are transcribed as written.
- No appendices are listed or included for January 1916. The contents page states "Diary Text 11 Pages" — pages 3–13 of this set.
- The handwriting is difficult throughout; many words are marked [uncertain] rather than guessed. The entries for 3, 10–11, 14, 17–19, 22, 25 and 27–28 January have several illegible passages, and the 5 January entry ends mid-word ("Sub—").
