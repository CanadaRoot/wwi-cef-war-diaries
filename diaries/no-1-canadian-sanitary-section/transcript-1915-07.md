## Notes — July 1915
- **Gas helmets:** on 2 July the G.O.C. and D.M.S. asked the section to stain 200 tents and supervise the re-moistening of gas helmets (masks); a helmet re-moistening demonstration was held at the billet on 5 July, "very well attended".
- **Shelling:** on 24 July the section's billets at Nieppe were shelled by the Germans — one or two shells broke in the yard and split houses with fragments; some convalescent patients in the depot were affected and have since been sent down to Base.
- **Para-typhoid:** the month's main disease work — three civilian para-typhoid cases near Neuve Église investigated by the 2nd Army bacteriologist specialist (21 July); the farmhouse was disinfected and placed out of bounds (23 July). Woodhouse notes 35 cases of illness in the typhoid group, 14 of them among commissioned officers and N.C.O.s, and concludes most infection comes from food prepared or handled by civilians. A suspected enteric case in Gen. Wilson (2nd Artillery) on 1 July proved negative on laboratory investigation (7 of the suspected enteric cases were all found negative, 12 July).
- **Other disease:** a case of diphtheria in the 2nd Battalion (19 July).
- **Hut camps:** from 15 July the section managed the Bulford and Aldershot hut camps near Neuve Église (Corporal [uncertain] with twelve Rest Station men and a squad); milk sold at the camps was boiled in the section's presence each morning, and milk sales at booths near the camps were forbidden on 29 July.
- **Court martial:** a Field General Court Martial was held on 14 July to try a member of the Rest Camp for being absent without leave.
- **Named people:** Gen. Wilson (2nd Artillery, suspected enteric, 1 July); Major Snell (acted in command 22–31 July while Woodhouse was on leave); Sergeant Major Rees does not recur; the Red Cross doctor caring for civilians near Neuve Église (unnamed).
- **Casualties:** none of the section's own men are recorded killed or wounded; the 24 July shelling damaged the billets but no casualties are recorded.
