## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- Place names are as written in the diary ("Dickeybusch", "Patricia", "Connaught", "Scottish Lines", "Dominion Lines", "Abele", "Bedford House", "G 22", "Devonshire Lines"). Map references such as G 22 are trench-map squares as written; no latitude/longitude coordinates are given.
- "W.C.L." in the Remarks column is the officer's initials (W. C. Lawson).

## Notes — June 1916
- No entries are recorded for 4, 9, 18, 24 or 25 June.
- 5 June: Dickeybusch Huts were being shelled, most shells falling around the incinerator, which "from Aeroplane, may look like a Howitzer". The camp was empty, the troops having moved up.
- 15 June: time was advanced one hour by G.H.Q. (summer time).
- 19–20 June: the 2nd Division troops left Dickeybusch Huts and the 3rd Brigade moved in; the 3rd Division moved out of Scottish Lines and the 3rd Brigade moved in.
- 26 June: a reported shortage of chloride of lime is noted, with quantities of lime and cresol issued to the Division (1,080 pounds [uncertain] daily; 6½ drums and 2 cans of lime; 3½ drums of cresol) — the exact wording of the quantities is partly [uncertain].
- Named people: Major Amyot; Genl. Lindsay is not named this month; Cpl. Killen [uncertain]; Cpl. Oram.
- No casualties (killed/wounded) are recorded in June 1916.
- No appendices are listed or included for June.
