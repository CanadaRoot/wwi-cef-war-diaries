## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten in ink throughout).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or phrase that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`. None was observed in December 1915.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The printed heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through on each page.
- Entries are dated in the form "1.XII" etc. and timed "7 P.M."; the year "1915" is written at the head of the Date column on each page.
- The printed form footer reads "T2134. Wt. W708–776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."
- The unit remained at St Jans Cappel throughout the month, apart from the O.C.'s leave 6–16 December and a trip to Boulogne 28–29 December.

## Notes — December 1915
- **Gap in dates:** no entries for 7–15 December. Major Amy went on leave on 6 December (pass to the 15th inst.; his own note at the foot of the 6 December entry says "on leave from this date to Dec 12") and returned to Bailleul at 2 a.m. on 16 December. Capt. Lomer C.A.M.C. took his place in his absence, but no diary entries were made for 7–15 December.
- **Two entries dated 18 December** appear on p. 10 (recreation room; arrangements for mess), apparently written the same evening.
- The cover says "Volume 9" while the contents page says "Volume 15-12"; the contents page's "Diary Text 14 Pages" matches the 14 diary-text pages (pp. 3–16).
- **"hoci":** a word in quotation marks used twice — 2 December ("5 months of awful 'hoci'") and 21 December ("the unavoidable 'hoci' our men are subjected to") — meaning unclear; transcribed as written.
- **"Bulford Camp":** the camp name is written "Bulford" throughout the month; its baths, disinfectors and water system are a recurring theme. The name is transcribed as written.
- **"fireless Cooker":** the cooker tested 2–3 December; the word reads as "fireless" (a cooker that keeps food hot without a fire, for hot drinks in the trenches), though the handwriting could also read "fieldless".
- **Casualties and disease:** 7 cases of trench-feet seen 1 December; Lt. J. A. Davison of the 15th Bn with measles (4 December), with 5 others isolated and isolation continued for 6 days; a measles quarantine was established; a suspected cerebro-spinal meningitis case from 6th Cdn R. reported 24 December (spinal fluid clear, practically no polymorphonuclear cells, culture negative); foot-and-mouth disease in a farm (26 December): 14 cows sick, 2 died, 12 pigs died, 2 human beings affected.
- **Discipline:** two cooks tried 2 December (absence from billet; drunkenness) and let off on suspended sentence; a private tried and dismissed on a drunkenness charge 5 December ("Evidence questionable"); a man tried for insolence to an N.C.O. 24 December and made to apologize publicly before the unit; 2 men punished during Amy's leave.
- **Named people:** Major J. A. Amy (O.C.); Capt. McConnell (No. 1 Fld Ambulance); Col. Firth; Gen. Currie; Gen. Carson; Gen. Alderson; Capt. Lomer C.A.M.C.; Col. Ross; Lt. J. A. Davison (15th Bn); Capt. HANS; Capt. Brydges; a Dr. [uncertain] who gave an address with Amy on 6 December.
- **Places:** St Jans Cappel (unit station), Vlamertinghe, Poperinghe, Dranoutre, Neuve Eglise, Locre, Koutepipe camp, Bailleul, Boulogne, Folkestone, London, Hyde Park Corner, La Hutte.
- No appendices are listed or included for December.
