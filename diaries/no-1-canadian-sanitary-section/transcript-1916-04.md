## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- Place names are as written in the diary ("Dickeybusch", "Poperinghe", "Ouderdom", "Grootebeek", "Abele", "Mont des Cats", "Bailleul", "Scottish Lines", "Canada Lines", "Dominion Lines", "New Dominion Lines"). Map references such as G 21, G 26, H 26, L 22 are trench-map squares as written; no latitude/longitude coordinates are given.
- "W.C.L." in the Remarks column is the officer's initials (W. C. Lawson).

## Notes — April 1916
- No entries are recorded for 2 April or 9 April; the diary jumps from 1 to 3 April and from 8 to 10 April with no explanation.
- 14 April: a case of suspected cerebro-spinal meningitis in a guard at Ouderdom; contacts were isolated and ordered to report daily. The contacts were returned to duty on 19 April. No death is recorded.
- The unit moved from Bailleul (near Flêtre) to map square G 21 on 3 April; detachments were posted to Dickeybusch Huts, Canada Huts and Scottish Lines. Detachments at Dickeybusch and Canada Huts were shelled out of their billets on 7 April and brought into camp at G 21.
- Named people: Capt. W. C. Lawson (O.C.); Sergt. Major [Rees]; Capt. Bond; Sergt. Blondon; Col. Dyer (5th Battalion); Genl. Lindsay (C.R.E.); a man named [uncertain] "Shields" struck through on 13 April (the name appears struck through in the original and may not be the intended reading).
- No casualties (killed/wounded) are recorded in April 1916.
- No appendices are listed or included for April.
