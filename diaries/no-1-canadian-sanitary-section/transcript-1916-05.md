## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- Place names are as written in the diary ("Dickeybusch", "Poperinghe", "Connaught", "Patricia", "Scottish Lines", "Dominion Lines", "Canada Lines", "Abele", "Grootebeek", "Belgium Cafe"). Map references such as G.26.c are trench-map squares as written; no latitude/longitude coordinates are given.
- "W.C.L." in the Remarks column is the officer's initials (W. C. Lawson).

## Notes — May 1916
- Capt. Lawson was on leave of absence from 13 to 21 May; Capt. McLeod (No. 1, 72 Amb[ulance]) acted in his place and returned command to him on 22 May. No entries are recorded for 14–20 May, and none for 7 May or 29 May.
- Named people: Capt. W. C. Lawson (O.C.); Major Amyot; Genl. Lindsay (chairing the Corps Water Board); Capt. McLeod; Capt. Bond (at Abele water tank).
- The Corps Water Board discussed the shortage of water carts in small units and ordered a survey of all wells in the area that might be available for water supply.
- No casualties (killed/wounded) are recorded in May 1916.
- No appendices are listed or included for May.
