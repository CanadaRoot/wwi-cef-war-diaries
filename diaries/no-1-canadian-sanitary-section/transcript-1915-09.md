## Notes — September 1915
- **Change of command:** Major Woodhouse relinquished command at 2 P.M. on 16 September and Major J. Amyot took over (Amyot had reported from England on 12 September). The diary hand changes at that point. Woodhouse's handover entry (16 September) records the section's organisation: the area divided into 4 sanitary areas, each with a corporal or lance-corporal and 4 men; Staff-Sergeant Rees acting Sergeant Major; Staff-Sergeant Osborn acting Quartermaster Sergeant; and a convalescent depot of 66 men under the section's care.
- **Water crisis:** the month is dominated by a water shortage after continued dry weather. Amyot surveyed every well in the district (19–20 September) and found them defective — many dry, many suspect to infection, men and horses drinking from dirty ditches and sump-holes. His report to the A.D.M.S. (21 September) recommended at least two 15–30 ft wells per unit area, curbed and bricked 2 ft above ground and puddled with clay, with efficient pumps; or a drilling apparatus to reach the 150–180 ft stratum. The Bulford spring was developed (26–29 September) with a trench, gravel filter and pipe to a reservoir and distributing tank 300 yards away.
- **Shelling:** a shell dropped on a well at Neuve Église on the night of 22–23 September, destroying the pump and the upper part of the well.
- **Convalescents:** from 16 September the diary records a daily convalescent return (remained / admitted / to duty / to hospital / remaining), rising from 66 on 16 September to 86 on 30 September. From 28 September Amyot held a daily 9 a.m. sick parade for them.
- **Named people:** Major J. Amyot (new O.C.); Major Woodhouse (outgoing O.C.); Staff-Sergeant Rees; Staff-Sergeant Osborn (acting Quartermaster Sergeant); Col. Ross, A.D.M.S. (22 September trench visit); Gen. Mercer, 1st Brigade (raised the question of rat disinfection in the trenches, 21 September); Col. Colborne (supplied a 25-man fatigue for the Bulford spring, 27 September); Capt. [uncertain] of the C.E. 3rd (spring development).
- **Disease:** seven para-typhoid cases are mapped by billet (3 September); no new cases are recorded this month. A diphtheria case is not recorded this month (the 19 July case was in the July volume).
- **Casualties:** none of the section's own men are recorded killed or wounded.
- **Movements:** the section remained at Nieppe all month; the 6th Brigade of the 2nd Canadian Division moved into Aldershot Camp (24 September) and left it in a very unsanitary state (27 September).
