- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.

## Notes — March 1918
- The section moved from Les Brebis to Bruay on 15 March 1918, relieving the 4th Canadian Sanitary Section, and was based at Bruay for the rest of the month, working across a new area: Divion, Auchel, Calonne, Camblain Chatelin, Ferfay, Lozinghem, Marles les Mines, Raimbert and the Civilian Evacuation Camp.
- Main work: inspecting billets and camps; building Solder plants/incinerators (a Solder plant completed at Lozinghem 30 March); water supply investigation and water sampling (a sample from Camblain Chatelin taken to No. 6 Mobile Lab 21 March); estimating sanitary facilities for the Civilian Evacuation Camp (26 March).
- Disease: diphtheria cases and contacts investigated at Lievin, Les Brebis, Angres and Lozinghem, with swabs taken (8 March) and sent to No. 2 Mobile Lab (13 March); a case of C.S.M. (cerebro-spinal meningitis) at Maroe (9 March) and in the 107th Battalion (14 March).
- Named people: Captain Sinclair, of a Canadian Sanitary Section (11 March). The 107th Battalion is named (14 March).
- "Maroe" (1, 9, 11, 14 March) is as written; "embarkation" (16 March) is as written, though the sense after the move to Bruay is unclear; "hey" (27 March) is as written between "Lozinghem" and "Auchel".
- No casualties are recorded in March 1918. No appendices are listed or included.
- No dates are missing: 1–31 March are all covered.
