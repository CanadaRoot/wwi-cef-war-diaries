## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, except for the typed contents page).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word, name or figure that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through on every diary page.
- Every daily entry is timed "7 p.m."; the place is NIEPPE until 22 November, then St JANS CAPPEL (St Jans Cappel).
- Each day's entry closes with a Convalescent Camp return in the form "Remained, Admitted, To Duty, To Fld Amb., Remaining". Where the figures line is cut off at the foot of the scanned page it is noted as not legible rather than reconstructed.
- "Bttn." = battalion; "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "R.C.R." = Royal Canadian Regiment; "A.D.M.S." = Assistant Director of Medical Services; "D.M.S." = Director of Medical Services; "D.A.D.M.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Medical Services; "A.P.M." = Assistant Provost Marshal; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "C.E." = (Royal) Engineers; "Fld Amb." = Field Ambulance; "S.O.S." does not occur this month.
- Place names as written: NIEPPE (Nieppe), DRANOUTRE (Dranoutre), BULFORD (Bulford) Camp, ALDERSHOT (Aldershot) Camp, LARK HILL (Lark Hill), BAILLEUL (Bailleul), NEUVE EGLISE (Neuve Eglise), St JANS CAPPEL (St Jans Cappel), BERTHEN (Berthen), WESTOUTRE (Westoutre), WOODS CAMP, COUR DREVE.

## Notes — November 1915
- The month is Volume 8 of the diary (cover), though the typed contents page calls it "Volume 15-11" — the two volume numbers disagree in the original. The contents page says "Diary Text 18 Pages", which matches pp. 3–20 (18 diary-text pages) of the 20 pages in this month's set.
- The cover states "With Appendices 1 - to - 5." Appendices 1 and 2 are cited in the remarks column on 14 and 15 November, Appendix 3 on 17 November, and Appendices 4 and 5 on 20 November. No appendix pages are included in this month's 20-page set — the appendices themselves (the louse-campaign outline, the fireless-cooker efficiency data and drawings, and the camp-stove drawings) are not in the images transcribed here.
- The diary is handwritten throughout (except the typed contents page). The handwriting is small and in places faint or cramped; many individual words are marked [uncertain] rather than guessed. The Convalescent Camp figures for 1–7 November are cut off at the foot of the scanned pages (pp. 3–5) and are recorded as not legible.
- Two separate entries are both dated 9.XI.15 — one at the foot of p. 6 (M. Bonquet, the well-digger) and one at the head of p. 7 (disinfector material handed to the Engineers). Both are transcribed as written.
- The Convalescent Camp return figures do not always reconcile day to day (e.g. the "Remained" figure for 10 and 11 November reads 86 although the previous day's "Remaining" was 87); the figures are transcribed as read and the discrepancy is the original's.
- After the Convalescent Camp was handed over to Lieut Hirsch on 22 November, no further daily convalescent returns appear in the diary (19–30 November have no figures).
- The 27 November entry is timed "7 a.m." as written, unlike every other entry in the month, which is timed "7 p.m." — possibly a slip of the pen in the original.
- Named people in the month: Gen Wood; Col Ross; Col Frith; Col Lindsay; Gen. Alderson (inspected the section 23 November); Capt. [uncertain] Hirsch (HIRSCH), who took charge of the Convalescent Camp 15/22 November; Lieut Hirsch (same officer, 22 November); Sgt Maj Ross; M. Bonquet, the district well-digger; Sgt. [uncertain] (made the sketch for the fireless cooker); the D.M.S., A.D.M.S., D.A.D.M.S. and D.D.M.S. (unnamed).
- The month's dominant work: sanitation of the Nieppe / Bulford / Aldershot / Dranoutre camp area — bath-houses and Foden disinfectors, the louse plague (lectures to battalions, 100 oil sprayers of the "Cow-horn" type, a "fire-less cooker" tested 19 November and adopted by Col Frith), and the troubled Bulford Camp water supply — wells repeatedly caving in, curbs slipping, and a new centralised well-and-gallery scheme sketched 20 November (p. 14).
- Health in the month: 14 men isolated with measles at Lark Hill (12 November); a measles quarantine at Cour Dreve lifted 24–25 November with no further cases in 14 days; one man sent to No. 1 Field Ambulance with a temperature of 102.4° F on 29 November ("Hope it is only a 'cold'"). No battle casualties are recorded — the unit was behind the lines at Nieppe and later St Jans Cappel.
- The unit moved from Nieppe to St Jans Cappel on 22–23 November (six lorry loads; the first load broke down half a mile out and was towed in by the D.M. Workshop). The Convalescent Camp moved separately to farm billets on Neuve Eglise Road above Bulford Camp.
- Weather: rain and mud early in the month, frost from 18 November, snow and rain 26 November, and the coldest weather yet on 28 November (ground frozen 3 inches, not thawing in daylight).
- The printed form footer differs between the early pages — "(73989) W4141—463. 400,000. 9/14. H.&J. Ltd. Forms/C. 2118/10." (pp. 3–5) — and the later pages — "T.2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S." (pp. 6–20).
