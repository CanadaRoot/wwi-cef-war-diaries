## Notes — October 1918
- The section was based at St Sauveur near Arras until 23 October, then moved to Auberchicourt on 24 October, taking the rear half of the Canadian Corps area while No. 5 Canadian Sanitary Section took the forward area.
- The area was considerably enlarged on 1 October, extending to the Canal du Nord and taking in the area formerly worked by No. 5 Canadian Sanitary Section; by 31 October it had been reduced again, bounded on the south by the Canal Sensee and the Canal d'Escaut.
- Disease: several cases of dysentery at Queant (7–8 October), apparently not contracted in the area; a diphtheria case at Anouchicourt (30 October).
- Auberchicourt, like the other recently captured villages, was filthy — houses and yards littered with refuse, drawers and cupboards emptied and strewn about the floors; dead German horses to bury; flies very numerous; water points tested, with no cases of poisoning found so far.
- The Motor Cycle Rider became a casualty on 29 October (no name given, cause not stated).
- The D.O.M.S. (Sanitation) of First Army called on 15, 21 and 28 October; the A.O.M.S. (Sanitation) of First Army called on 15 October.
- No signature appears at the foot of the 9–18 or 19–25 October pages.
- No military casualties of the section itself are recorded in October 1918 apart from the Motor Cycle Rider (29 October). No appendices are listed or included.
- No dates are missing: 1–31 October are all covered.
