## Notes — September 1918
- The section was based at St Sauveur near Arras for the whole month, working the forward area along the Cambrai Road toward Monchy, Cherisy, Wancourt, Guemappe and Bucquoy.
- Early September (2–6 September) the men helped clear wounded from the light railway at St Sauveur to the ambulances; later in the month (27–30 September) 14 men were sent to assist at the dressing station at Bucquoy, still helping to clear wounded.
- Main work: locating bodies and horses forward of Monchy and reporting them to the Burial Officer; testing wells and springs for poison and signposting them; burying dead horses (the majority buried by 21 September); building a workshop to construct latrines; inspecting the area occupied by the 3rd Canadian Division at the request of its A.O.M.S. (9 September), including Rumancourt, Saudemont and Ecourt St Quentin.
- The area boundaries are set out in full on 7 September, with map-sheet references (Sheet 51 B) and grid references (P.26.d.7.2., Q.10 central, M.20.a.a.o., U.12.d.6.4., W.7.a.0.5).
- The section's box car had not returned since the evacuation two weeks before (13 September); the workshop staff were brought from Arras, where they had been constructing operating rooms and assisting the 2nd Canadian Field Ambulance (12 September).
- No signature appears at the foot of the 1–7, 7–12 or 13–22 September pages; only the final page (23–30 September) is signed by Major Chapman.
- No military casualties of the section itself are recorded in September 1918, but many British and German bodies and horses lay in the forward area. No appendices are listed or included.
- No dates are missing: 1–30 September are all covered.
