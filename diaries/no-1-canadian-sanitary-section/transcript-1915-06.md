## Notes — June 1915
- **New area:** the section arrived at Vendin-lez-Béthune on 31 May and remained there until 26 June, when it moved to Nieppe.
- **Disinfector trouble:** the Thresh disinfector's wheel collapsed on 3 June; Woodhouse indented for two complete wheels and reported the machine wrecked. He records his view that the machines are good for stationary work but of practically nil disinfecting value in a division as to vermin, since blankets and all wearing apparel must be sterilised simultaneously — cleansing and vermin powder are more useful in his experience.
- **Trench sanitation:** on 9 June the D.M.S. 1st Army addressed the division's medical men on unsanitary conduct in the trenches (urination, defecation and food waste in connecting trenches and in front of parapets). The A.D.M.S. told Woodhouse the division "had the name of being the dirtiest division in the army" — Woodhouse notes in the margin that the General of the 8th Division, which followed them at Sailly-sur-la-Lys, wrote that they were the cleanest he had followed: "(This is the thanks we get for cleaning after our predecessors.)" On 11–12 June Woodhouse and the A.D.M.S. visited the trenches and reported the state to G.H.Q., blaming "absolute negligence of Commanding, Company Officers and N.C.O.s".
- **Named people:** Sergeant Major Rees (11 June); Lieut Tennant (14 June); Seely's detachment (17 June — [uncertain] spelling).
- **Disease:** measles continues (16th Battalion, 4th Battalion — a reinforcement case developing within 24 hours of joining, 21 others segregated); a suspected typhoid case in No. II Field Battery Brigade (5 June). Anthrax at a farm near Nieppe: six of eight cattle developed the disease and six died, last case about 18 June.
- **Casualties:** none of the section's own men are recorded killed or wounded.
- **Lorry:** the section's lorry needed a spring — sought at St. Omer without success (20 June), obtained at Hazebrouck (21 June).
