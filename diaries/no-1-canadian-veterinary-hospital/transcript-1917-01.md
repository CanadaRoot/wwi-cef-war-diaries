## Notes

- The month's diary is a single diary page (p. 3) of daily horse and mule tallies: numbers admitted, died, shot (destroyed), sent to butcher, transferred, and discharged. No narrative entries, no named men, and no other places besides Havre are recorded in January 1917.
- The abbreviation "Cfs" appears at the end of most daily entries, exactly as written; its expansion is not given in the diary.
- The cover page (p. 1) gives the volume as "Volume 21", while the inner title page (p. 2) gives "Volume 17-1". Both are transcribed as written.
- The handwritten "No" (with a raised "o", i.e. the abbreviation for "Number") in the top right corner of the cover page is transcribed as seen; its meaning is not stated on the page. The same mark appears on the February and March covers.
- Day-by-day attribution: the diary page carries one tally line for each day, 1–31 January, presented here in the order written. The day numerals in the left-hand ("Hour, Date, Place") column are unevenly spaced — the numerals 2, 3, 4 and 5 are bunched tightly at the top of the column — and in places they do not sit exactly level with the tally line for the same day, so the day attribution follows the order of the entries rather than the precise vertical position of each numeral. The first entry sits on the "January 1st 1917. Havre" line and the last entry sits immediately above the signature.
- The signing officer is Major C. G. Saunders, commanding officer of the Canadian Veterinary Hospital. His signature on the January diary page is flourished and hard to read on its own; the name is confirmed by the typed title sheet in the February diary (p. 137 of this file), which reads "WAR DIARY OF MAJOR C. G. SAUNDERS, CANADIAN VETERINARY HOSPITAL."
- No dates are missing: entries are present for every day 1–31 January 1917. No duplicate pages and no blank pages in this month's set.
- Notable entries: very large intakes on 3 January (162 horses, 38 mules), 10 January (284 horses, 8 mules) and 18 January (268 horses, 28 mules); on 9 January, 100 horses were transferred to No. 2 Veterinary Hospital; on 23 January the hospital was inspected by the D.D.V.S. and D.D.M.S.
