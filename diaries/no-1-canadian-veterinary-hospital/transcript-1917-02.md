### Remarks column (page 138)

[Written in the "Remarks and references to Appendices" column, opposite the early part of the month:]

The high mortality of animals during the month is attributable to the intense cold and the extreme state of emaciation and exhaustion of the animals when admitted Cfs The water supply was quite inadequate during the period of cold & shelter for animals the results might have proved disastrous Cfs

[At the foot of the Remarks column:]

23.2.17
Base Commandant decided that no more men to be disposed of Cfs

## Notes

- The month's diary is a single diary page (p. 4) of daily horse and mule tallies, plus a substantial entry in the Remarks column explaining the month's high animal mortality. The typed title sheet on p. 3 names the diarist as Major C. G. Saunders, Canadian Veterinary Hospital — this identifies the signing officer of the January diary as well.
- The abbreviation "Cfs" appears at the end of most daily entries and twice in the Remarks column, exactly as written; its expansion is not given in the diary.
- The cover page (p. 1) gives the volume as "Volume 22", while the inner title page (p. 2) gives "Volume 17-2". Both are transcribed as written.
- The handwritten "No" (with a raised "o", i.e. the abbreviation for "Number") in the top right corner of the cover page is transcribed as seen; its meaning is not stated on the page. The same mark appears on the January and March covers.
- The 1 February entry writes the number out in words: "Ten horses died 2 shot Cfs."
- Day-by-day attribution: the diary page carries one tally line for each day, 1–28 February, presented here in the order written. As in January, the day numerals in the left-hand ("Hour, Date, Place") column are unevenly spaced and in places do not sit exactly level with the tally line for the same day, so the day attribution follows the order of the entries rather than the precise vertical position of each numeral. The first entry sits on the "Feb 1 1917 Havre" line and the last entry sits immediately above the signature. The numerals "15" and "25" in the date column are followed by a dash ("15-", "25-"), as written.
- The 7 February entry reads, as written, "8 horses 4 mules admitted 2 horses 1 mule died 8 horses 1 shot Cfs" — the phrase "8 horses 1 shot" is reproduced exactly as written; its sense is unclear.
- The large figures in the early-February tallies (for example 26 shot on 3 February, 15 shot on 5 February, and 57 shot with 56 horses and 31 mules discharged on 6 February) are transcribed as written; they are consistent with the Remarks column's account of high mortality from intense cold.
- The Remarks column's sentence about the water supply and shelter is transcribed exactly as written, including its compressed grammar ("during the period of cold & shelter for animals the results might have proved disastrous").
- The final Remarks entry ("23.2.17 — Base Commandant decided that no more men to be disposed of Cfs") is transcribed exactly as written; the word "men" is as written, though its sense in a veterinary-hospital context is unclear.
- The signature block as written repeats "O.C." ("Major O.C." / "O.C. Can Vet Hosp.").
- No dates are missing: entries are present for every day 1–28 February 1917. No duplicate pages and no blank pages in this month's set.
- Notable entries: very high mortality in the first week of February (26 shot on 3 February, 15 shot on 5 February, 57 shot on 6 February), attributed in the Remarks column to intense cold, emaciation and exhaustion of animals on admission, and an inadequate water supply; inspections by the Auditor, Canadian Pay Office (14 February), the D.D.V.S. (16 February), the A.A. & Q.M.G. (26 February) and the Base Commander (27 February); on 23 February the Base Commandant decided that no more men were to be disposed of.
