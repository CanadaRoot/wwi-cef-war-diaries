## Page 6 — Appendix: Report on work done in the laboratory, May 1916 to June 1917 (page-0155-June_1917_p._6.jpg)

REPORT ON WORK DONE IN THE LABORATORY OF THE CANADIAN VETERINARY HOSPITAL FROM MAY 1916 to JUNE 1917.

o-o-o-o-o-o-o-o-o-o-o

Bacteriological work commenced on May 19th.1916 and has been carried on continuously ever since.
The range of work has comprised the diagnosis of infections, the determination of Causative bacteria their isolation and the manufacture of Vaccines, the examination of scrapings for mange parasites, blood examinations for micro-organisms, and percentages of leucocytes.
Agglutination tests have also been carried out for the confirmation of cases that have given suspicious reaction to the Mallein Test.
4800 doses of autogenic, 7600 polyvalent and 690 Univalent vaccines have been prepared and administered for the treatment of suppurating wounds, quittors, poll evils, Ulcerative Cellulitis and catarrh, making a total of 13,290 doses of vaccine prepared and administered. In all 1262 animals have received vaccine treatment from vaccine prepared in the laboratory.
The causative micro organism of Cellulitis which for some time has been confused with Nocards disease has been determined and a curative vaccine prepared. One case of equine piroplasmosis has been diagnosed and blood cultures from the blood of animals suffering from Shipping fever have revealed the streptococcus equi of Schutz. Further work in this direction is proceeding and will be the subject of a further report.
1,000 examinations have been made for acari, 298 cases were Sarcoptic, 65 Psoroptic and 7 symbiotic acariasis, 630 were negative.
The Bacillus Mallei has been isolated from the lesions of every case of glanders destroyed, thus verifying the mallein test and the macroscopic lesion discovered P.M.
The Laboratory staff deserve great credit for the zeal, energy and initiative which they have displayed, under adverse conditions, of housing, limited appliances and apparatus, but they are well repaid for their devotion in seeing the excellent results of their labors. From the results obtained the laboratory has more than justified its institution, as the outlay in installing it and cost of running it, sinks into insignificance besides the saving of expense to the country by getting animals fit for duty weeks ahead of time required under ordinary [word struck through] treatment. This is especially marked in cases of catarrh, the average cases with vaccine treatment clearing up in four days, without, three weeks.

Sgd. [signature] Major. C.A.V.C.
Officer Commanding.
Canadian Veterinary Hospital.

## Page 7 — Appendix: Notes on Mange (page-0156-June_1917_p._7.jpg)

Notes on Mange

Some interesting facts have come to light in the course of the treatment and cure of 2337 cases of Mange in this Hospital which have led to the routine now adopted here for handling this disease.
1000 microscopical examinations have been made. Diagnosis is now, except in cases of advanced Sarcoptic Mange, made entirely by microscopical methods, other diagnostic methods, such as scratching with a stick, or hands, to obtain response from the animal, by movement of the lips, having been proved by actual experiment to be worthless and misleading.
In this respect 370 cases of positive Sarcoptic Mange were taken at random and the "scratching test" applied, and only 68 responded. On the other hand, in hundreds of cases of Pruritis which responded to this test, no parasite could be found even after repeated scrapings and microscopical examinations and, furthermore no eruptions, depilations, or other symptoms of Mange subsequently developed. In particular, six exceedingly itchy horses, received from Remounts, were taken and used in the Unit's Transport service, they were dirty, out of condition, and debilitated. They [word struck through] received no treatment whatever and are still at work, in fair condition, and with no sign of Mange, and this after a lapse of three months. Itchiness, in so far as it relates to response to scratching is, in our opinion, solely the result of want of, or poor, grooming, insufficient exercise, and errors in diet ie: poor stable management. On the other hand, itchiness, as evinced by the animal biting, scratching, and rubbing itself, has been found to be diagnostic, as a general rule, either of pediculosis or acariasis.

Technique of diagnostic methods.
Several scrapings are obtained from a suspected animal, the skin being scraped until blood oozes. The scrapings are spread on an Arnold's Mange dish and examined with a magnifying glass. If live parasites are detected one or more are transferred to a drop of water on a slide and examined under a microscope. This preliminary examination saves much time and labour and even with the Acarus Sarcoptis has proved reliable in detecting live Acari. If the magnifying glass reveals nothing the scraping is then collected, treated in the usual manner with K.H.O, centrifuged, slided, and examined. It has been found, even after only one dipping in Calcium Sulphide solution for two minutes, that live Acari cannot be found so that two dippings, nine days apart, should be sufficient to effect a cure.
It has also been determined that dead Acari may persist in or on the skin for at least one month after conclusion of treatment when the skin, to all appearances, is normal. This does, and will probably continue to do so, often cause animals to be regarded as "mangy" and sent into hospitals either as new cases, or relapsed ones. After making many examinations and conducting a number of experiments with the action of K.H.O solution, on live and dead Acari, we have come to the conclusion that it is easy to determine whether a case is "active" or cured.
In the case of live Acari the K.H.O solution has little or no effect the parasite appearing [uncertain — word partly obscured] complete with legs, suckers, etc, the dead Acari, on the other hand, appear more opaque and are practically never complete, but minus legs or suckers or both. This is probably due, not to the action of the K.H.O but to that of the Calcium Sulphide solution and the consequent dissociation of the parasite after death rendering it more brittle and liable to injury during the scraping process and subsequent manipulation.
We have found that the Calcium Sulphide solution exerts a prolonged parasiticidal action and that better and quicker results are obtained if the animals are not groomed between dippings and for at least four days after the last dip. Sweating and grooming should then be proceeded with in order to restore the skin to its normal condition and to remove dead parasites, etc. We maintain continuous stable disinfection by means of blow lamps, grooming kits and blankets (when used) are disinfected daily.

1st. That itchiness, unassociated with cutaneous eruption, is not diagnostic of the disease and that the microscope is the only true

## Notes — June 1917

- **Page numbering gap:** the image files for June 1917 are labelled p. 1, p. 3, p. 4, p. 5, p. 6, p. 7, p. 8. There is no file labelled "June 1917 p. 2" in the folder. No diary content appears to be missing as a result: p. 3 is the title page and p. 4 is the complete 1–30 June daily summary, so the absent "p. 2" was most likely a blank or unnumbered leaf, but this cannot be confirmed from the files supplied.
- **No duplicate pages** in the June set.
- **No dates missing** from the daily summary: 1–30 June 1917 are all listed, though the line-to-date alignment is uncertain around 23–25 June, where the cramped lines appear to run on (see the daily entries above).
- **Place:** the daily summary is headed "June 1st 1917. France" (no more precise place is given).
- **Appendices:** the June volume carries two substantial typed appendices — the laboratory report (May 1916–June 1917) and the "Notes on Mange" (2337 mange cases treated). Both are signed by Major C. G. Saunders.
- **Arithmetic discrepancy in the original:** the laboratory report's vaccine figures (4800 + 7600 + 690) total 13,090, while the report states a total of 13,290 doses. Transcribed as printed.
