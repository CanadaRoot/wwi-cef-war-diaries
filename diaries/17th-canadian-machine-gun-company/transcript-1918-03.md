## Notes on March 1918
- March 1918 is a fresh volume — "Volume 1" — and is **typed**, not handwritten. It covers only 25–31 March 1918: there is **no diary for 1–24 March 1918** in this file, and no February 1918 diary at all (see `transcript-1918-01.md`).
- The company is now in **France**: it landed at Le Havre on 25 March 1918 with 10 officers and 177 other ranks, travelled by motor lorry via Dieppe and Abbeville to Verdrel, and on 29 March split into "A" and "B" Batteries which moved into the line, while the remainder went to Villers du Bois and then Gordon Camp.
- Officers named in March: Capt. G. Black (O.C. of Batteries), Lieuts. R. Leary and W. Murray (Battery Commanders), Lts. J. F. McLennan and R. W. Chipman (2nd in Command of Batteries). Col. Brutinell (spelled "Brutinell" in the diary) and General Currie inspected the 17th, 18th and 19th Canadian Machine Gun Companies at Verdrel on 28 March.
- The 28 March entry's sentence "They spent in making preparation for same" is garbled in the original (an object, presumably "the evening", is missing); transcribed as typed.
- The March volume is signed by a Captain whose signature is illegible (initials appear to be "J. E."), as A/O. C. of the company.

## Page 97 — Diary text, 25–29 March 1918 (March 1918, p. 3)

Army Form C. 2118 (typed form 2449). Stamped: CAMP COMMANDANT 2 APR 1918 CANADIAN CORPS. The "or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is struck through.

### 25 March 1918 — Le Havre — 8.00 A.M.

> Arrived here this morning at 9.00 o'clock with 10 officers and 177 other ranks and transport consisting of 7 riding horses, 47 light draught, 1 mess cart, 1 water cart, 1 H. Q. limber and 12 fighting limbers fully and properly loaded. Devoted balance of morning to unloading transport, after which company paraded and moved off at 2.15 for British Rest Camp where we spent the night.

### 26 March 1918 — Le Havre — 8.00 A.M.

> Devoted morning to cleaning equipment generally. Entrained in motor lorries at 2.45 p.m., taking fighting equipment only. Transport ordered to remain behind and follow later. Travelled continuously until 1.00 a.m. following morning, when we arrived and encamped in tents on outskirts of Dieppe.

### 27 March 1918 — Dieppe — 8.00 A.M.

> Company paraded at 7.45 and moved off in lorries at 8.00 a.m. sharp, stopping at Abbeville for lunch. Owing to delay, waiting for rations and gasoline company did not pull out until nearly 5.00 p.m., when we left for Verdrel, via St. Paul, arriving at the latter place after midnight. We were billeted in huts.

### 28 March 1918 — Verdrel — 8.00 A.M.

> 6 p.m. Black given to understand, by Col. Brutinell, that the company would likely be called upon to take up positions in the line at any moment. They spent in making preparation for same. Late in afternoon General Currie and Col. Brutinell made inspection of 17th, 18th and 19th Canadian machine gun companies. About 5.00 p.m. orders were issued by Col. Brutinell for all ranks to be fed and stand to ready to move off at any time after 6.00 a.m. Friday morning.

### 29 March 1918 — Verdrel — 8.00 A.M.

> Two Batteries — "A" and "B" Batteries — each consisting of a personnel of 2 officers and 46 other ranks (Lieuts. R. Leary and W. Murray, as Battery Commanders, respectively, and Lts. J. F. McLennan and R. W. Chipman as 2nd in Command of Batteries, respectively), moved off at 2.00 p.m. to take up their positions in line. Capt. G. Black, as O. C. of Batteries. The remainder of the 17th Cdn. Mach. Gun Coy., paraded and moved off at 3.15 p.m. for Villers du Bois, where they encamped for the night. Coy transport coming from Le Havre joined Coy just before moving off.

[The last sentence is handwritten at the foot of the page.]

## Page 98 — Diary text, 30–31 March 1918 (March 1918, p. 4)

### 30 March 1918 — Villers du Bois — 8.00 A.M.

> The company paraded at 8.45 a.m. and moved off at 9.00 a.m. for destination not definitely known, camped at Madagascar Corner on Lens-Arras Road for lunch, where we remained until 5.00 p.m. awaiting further instructions regarding billeting. Moved off at 5.15 p.m. for Roberts Camp where we arrived in due course only to be informed that our billets had been changed to Gordon Camp, to which latter place we at once proceeded and arrived at, at 9.00 p.m., where we were billeted for the night. Because of unforeseen and unavoidable circumstances, considerable delay and inconvenience were suffered in securing billets.

### 31 March 1918 — Gordon Camp — 8.00 A.M.

> No parade ordered for to-day, time being devoted for cleaning of equipment and straightening up generally. No official word has as yet been received as to the location of "A" and "B" Batteries, which moved into the line on the 29th instant.

Signed at foot of page: [signature illegible — initials appear to be "J. E."], Captain, A/O. C. 17th Canadian Machine Gun Company.
