## Page 9 — Diary text, 11–18 March 1917 (March 1917, p. 3)

Army Form C. 2118. Entries initialled in the right margin "T.C.B. major" (Major T. C. Bamfield).

### 11 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 1 p.m.

> The Coy attended Divine Service at 9.45 a.m. with 128th batt'n.

### 12 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> From 6.30–7 a.m. Dummy bombing practice was held, and the day was occupied in the digging area, constructing dug-outs and emplacements

### 13 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> Early morning parade — Bombing. And from 8–12.30 and 1.30–4.30 p.m. — Entrenching.

### 14 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> The parades for the day were identical with that of the preceding day.

### 15 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> Three N.C.O's and twenty men were detailed to attend a 3-day Bombing Course under the Bombing Officer of the 202nd batt'n.

### 16 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 1 p.m.

> From 6.30–7 a.m. — short route march. At 8.30 a.m. the draft paraded in full marching order for O.C's inspection, followed by a Kit inspection. From 9.15–12 Bombing Class, and from 1.30–4.30 p.m. Bombing. From 2.30–3.30 p.m. LT McPHERSON gave P.T., and from 3.30–4.30 p.m. LT. JUNOR gave a lecture on Active Service Conditions, for Machine-Gunners.

### 17 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 1 p.m.

> All available men were utilized for Barrack fatigues, cleaning quarters etc. until 12.30 p.m.

### 18 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 1 p.m.

> The Co paraded for Divine Service at 9.45 a.m. and proceeded to the Mess room of the 128th Battalion

## Page 10 — Diary text, 19–24 March 1917 (March 1917, p. 4)

### 19 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> 6.30–7.00 a.m. Dummy Bombing Practice was held. 8.00–12.30 and from 1.30–4.30, Day was spent on the digging area, constructing dugouts and emplacements.

### 20 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> 6.30–7.00 a.m. No I Section employed on Dummy Bomb throwing. Remainder of Co. went for short route march. The remainder of the day was spent on the digging area on further construction of emplacements and dugouts.

### 21 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> 6.30–7.00 a.m. No II Section employed in Dummy bomb throwing — remainder of Co on morning route march. Two hours of the morning were spent on anti gas instruction, one hour on physical training and one hour lecture on Machine Gun Characteristics. Afternoon was spent on digging area constructing dugouts and emplacements.

### 22 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> 6.30–7.00 a.m. No III section Bombing — remainder route march. Remainder of the day was spent as follows — Two hours on anti-gas drill — one hour on physical drill. Two hours on visual training and judging distance — a further two hours on Range Cards, defence and attack.

### 23 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> 6.30–7.00 a.m. No IV section Bombing — remainder on route march. From 8.00 a.m. until 7.30 p.m. the company were employed on the construction of dugouts and emplacements.

### 24 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 1 p.m.

> 6.30–7.00 a.m. No I. on Bomb throwing — remainder on short route march. Until 12.00 noon the Co was employed on fatigues — cleaning huts and drawing stores.

## Page 11 — Diary text, 25–28 March 1917 (March 1917, p. 5)

### 25 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 1 p.m.

> The company attended Divine service. The R.C.s attended No 3. Y.M.C.A Hut from 9.00 a.m. Other denominations paraded with the 128th Batt at 11 a.m.

### 26 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> 6.30–7.00 a.m. No II section threw bombs and remainder went on a route march. The forenoon was spent on dugouts and emplacements. The afternoon was spent in passing through the gas chamber.

### 27 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> 6.30–7.00 a.m. No III Section were employed in bomb throwing. The remainder were employed in the morning route march. One hour of the remaining day was employed on physical drill and the remainder on dugouts and emplacements.

### 28 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> The 17th M.G.Co. per W.O. No 66-8-1 [uncertain] [struck through in original: illegible] ceases to exist from this date. Divisional Orders — 1136. 27th March 1917. Transfers of five officers — six sergeants — two corporals — one Lance Cpls — twenty nine other ranks to the 18th M.G. Co and of five officers — one C.S.M. — five sgts — one corporal — two lance cpls. and sixty other ranks to the 19th M.G.Co. and one sergeant, four Signallers — ten short rain [uncertain] drivers — twenty two trained men to the Yukon Machine Gun Co. and fourteen Lewis Machine Gunners to the 128th C.I. Battalion.

## Page 12 — Diary text, 29–31 March 1917 (March 1917, p. 6)

### 29 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> By D.O. No 1137 — 27/3/17 The Yukon Machine Gun Co will be the 17th M.G.Co and from this date is shown as such in this diary. The day was spent in reorganization.

### 30 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 9 p.m.

> Under Maj. T.C. Bamfield the Yukon M.G.Co the new 17th M.G.Co. commenced training as follows... one hour physical training — two hours fire orders — one hour — choice of gun positions practical — two hours use of ground and cover — one hour judging distance.

### 31 March 1917 — Witley Camp, Surrey — 1 p.m.

> The morning was spent as follows. 8–9. a.m. Physical Drill 9–10 Lecture on Machine Gun Organization. 10–11 Fire Orders and Signals Practical. 11–12 Definitions of Machine Gun Terms.

Signed at foot of page: T. C. Bamfield, Major, O.C. 17th Machine Gun Coy.

## Notes on March 1917
- **28–29 March — the company is broken up and re-formed:** the original 17th M.G. Co. (formed 6 February) ceases to exist on 28 March under Divisional Order 1136; its men are transferred to the 18th and 19th M.G. Companies, the Yukon Machine Gun Company and the 128th Battalion. By Divisional Order 1137 the Yukon Machine Gun Company becomes the (new) 17th M.G. Co. from 29 March, still under Major T. C. Bamfield. The diary continues without a break under the new unit.
- This differs from the LAC guide's summary ("Yukon Infantry Company reorganized at Whitley on 9 February 1917 as 17th Canadian Machine Gun Company") — the diary itself is the primary source and is transcribed as written.
