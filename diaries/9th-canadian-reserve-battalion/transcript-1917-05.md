# War Diary — 9th Canadian Reserve Battalion

## Source
- **Unit:** 9th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Alberta) — organized at Bramshott 4 January 1917 under Lieut.-Colonel W. C. G. Armstrong, absorbing the 9th, 194th and 209th Battalions; it reinforced the 10th and 49th Battalions and was absorbed by the 21st Canadian Reserve Battalion 15 October 1917
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4950, file 476, part 1; microfilm reel T-10761
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004677 — "War diaries - 9th Reserve Battalion", 90 pages
  - Record: https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=2004677
- **Page images:** pages 39–52 of 90 in this file (`page-0039-01-05-1917_cover.jpg` … `page-0052-29-05-1917.jpg`)
- **Commanding officer:** Lieut.-Colonel W. C. G. Armstrong (in command throughout May 1917; the diary is signed by him at the end of May — see note below)
- **Note on the record:** this LAC record also contains an unrelated 1914–15 diary of the original 9th Battalion on pages 1–25 and 83–90; those pages are NOT transcribed here. This chunk covers only the Reserve Battalion diary, May 1917 (pages 39–52).

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- The "Hour" column is used only on pages 40–41 and 45–46; elsewhere it is blank. The Remarks column is blank throughout this volume.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.
- "S.O.S." = struck off strength; "T.O.S." = taken on strength; "O.R." = Other Ranks; "Net Decrease" is the diary's own wording for the daily fall in Other Ranks strength.
- The printed form footer ("M. & S. ... D.D.A.I. Form C/2118/14") is not transcribed in full.
- All entries are at Bramshott unless stated otherwise.

## Notes and gaps
- Pages 41–48 are all dated 5 May 1917: they are not appendices but the diary sheets for the 5 May practice mobilization — the mobilization orders and narrative (pp. 41–46), the "Stand by" entry and officer/cadet movements (p. 47), and the strength returns for 5–14 May (p. 48). The eight pages labelled 05/05/1917 are consecutive diary sheets, not repeated or duplicate sheets.
- The 5 May practice mobilization: the Test Mobilization ordered for 5 May was postponed by Home Forces and replaced by a practice mobilization (p. 41). The battalion formed a Composite Battalion with another unit (p. 43, "Composite Battalion"), marched in two halves from Bramshott to Bordon Station — Right Half under Maj. [uncertain], Left Half under Lt.-Col. W. C. G. Armstrong — entrained, and marched back the same day (pp. 43–46). Right Half reached camp at 5 p.m., Left Half at 6 p.m. (p. 46).
- The 3 May emergency warning (p. 40) is the trigger for the mobilization sequence: warnings from A.H.Q. and Brigade, Signallers warned, all available men to be fully equipped, "A" & "B" men able to march 5 miles.
- The 5 May "Stand by" entry (p. 47) records that weather was very bad, that the men stood the test remarkably well, and that the Left Half men were not in condition to undergo such a test, having recently arrived from Canada and returned from leave about 2 a.m. on the day of mobilization.
- 29 May 1917: H.R.H. the Duke of Connaught and staff visited the battalion, inspected the men's mess, and watched a bombing-raid demonstration supervised by Lieut. J. W. Hunter, Battalion Bombing Officer (p. 52).
- No diary entries for 4 May, 24 May or 28 May 1917 — the diary jumps from 3 May to 5 May (p. 40 to p. 41), from 23 May to 25 May (p. 50 to p. 51), and from 27 May to 29 May (p. 51).
- The 12 Other Ranks S.O.S. line is repeated twice on 3 May (p. 40), as written — not corrected.
- "Part II Orders" and "Routine Order" numbers are not cited in this volume; the diary is almost entirely strength returns after 5 May.
- The 14 May entry (p. 48) reads "Lt.-Col. W. C. G. Armstrong [uncertain] S.O.S. on return to Canada", but the diary is signed "W. C. G. Armstrong, Lieut.-Colonel, Comdg. 9th Reserve Battalion (Alberta)" at the end of May (p. 52) and again at the end of June — the surname on the 14 May line may be misread, or the signature may be by another officer; left as written with the uncertainty flagged.
- The monthly strength return at 31 May (p. 52) gives 118 [uncertain] Officers and 786 Other Ranks; the leading figure is uncertain — it may read "18".
- The cadet list on page 47 (taken on strength from O.T.C.) names about 17 cadets; most surnames could not be read with confidence and are flagged [uncertain] rather than guessed.
- No casualties (killed or wounded) are recorded in May 1917 — the diary is a Bramshott training/reserve battalion record of drafts, transfers and strength returns.

[uncertain], Lieut. R. F. H. Dewar [uncertain], Lieut. W. H. Edwards [uncertain], Lieut. R. E. Pearson, Lieut. J. Keaney, Maj. H. Lowery [uncertain], Capt. R. W. Hale, Lieut. C. R. Martin, Lieut. J. W. Hunter (Battalion Bombing Officer), Lieut. J. B. [uncertain], and the commanding officer Lieut.-Col. W. C. G. Armstrong.
