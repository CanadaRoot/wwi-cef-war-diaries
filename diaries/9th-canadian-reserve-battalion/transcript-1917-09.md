# War Diary — 9th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Alberta)

## Source
- **Unit:** 9th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Alberta) — organised at Bramshott 4 January 1917 under Lieut.-Colonel W. C. G. Armstrong, absorbing the 9th, 194th and 209th Battalions; reinforcing the 10th and 49th Battalions
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4950, file 476, part 1; microfilm reel T-10761
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004677 — "War diaries - 9th Reserve Battalion", 90 pages
  - Record: https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=2004677
- **Page images:** pages 73–78 of 90 in this file (`page-0073-01-09-1917_cover.jpg` … `page-0078-20-09-1917.jpg`)
- **Commanding officer:** Lieut.-Colonel W. C. G. Armstrong (the September sheet is signed at the foot by the commanding officer — signature illegible, see note below)
- **Note on the record:** this LAC record also contains the 1914–15 diary of the original 9th Battalion on pages 1–25 and pages 83–90; those pages are NOT transcribed here. This chunk covers only the Reserve Battalion diary, September–October 1917 (pages 73–82).

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- The "Hour" column is blank throughout this volume — no hours are recorded.
- The Remarks column is blank throughout September; in October it carries a short annotation on most lines (see October notes).
- "Alta Reg Depot" is the Alberta Regimental Depot; "recd" = received; "attd" = attached; "O.S." = overseas; "Offrs" = Officers.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; the place throughout is Bramshott, recorded as written.
- The diary is a strength-and-postings record: almost every entry is an officer taken on or struck off strength, or a net increase/decrease of Other Ranks. No training, weather or narrative entries appear in these two months.

## Notes and gaps
- Dates with no entry in September: 2, 9, 16 and 23 September. No other pages are missing; the sheets run continuously 1–30 September.
- The 20 September "Net Increase — Net Decrease 2 Other Ranks" is written as shown: the writer appears to have begun "Net Increase" and corrected it to "Net Decrease 2 Other Ranks" — not corrected in the transcription.
- The September cover is headed "Vol. 7" (the October cover is "Volume 8").
- Uncertain readings, with best guesses where any could be made (none of these are certain enough to transcribe without the flag):

  - 10 September, Lieut. C. G. [uncertain] — surname unreadable; possibly "Drace".

  - 20 September, Capt. J. M. Baldwin — a small word is interlineated above "attached" and could not be read; "Baldwin" itself is the best reading of the surname.

- Notable items:
  - Reinforcements sent overseas: 60 Other Ranks to the 10th Battalion on 10 September; officers to the 10th Battalion (3 September), the 50th Battalion (20 September) and the 49th Battalion (six lieutenants, 20 September).
  - Two lieutenants (C. Crocker and W. F. Miller) left for the Royal Flying Corps on 11 September.
  - Lieut. D. W. Mackay was transferred to the 1st Quebec Regiment on 3 September; Lieut. W. A. Hardisty arrived from a Saskatchewan regiment on 14 September.
  - Lieut. W. B. [uncertain] was struck off strength on being admitted to hospital on 7 September.
  - Unit strength at 30 September 1917: 60 officers, 760 Other Ranks.
  - No casualties (killed/wounded) are recorded in September — the diary records postings and strength changes only.
  - The whole month was spent at Bramshott; no movements.
