# War Diary — 9th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Alberta)

## Source
- **Unit:** 9th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Alberta) — organised at Bramshott 4 January 1917 under Lieut.-Colonel W. C. G. Armstrong, absorbing the 9th, 194th and 209th Battalions; reinforcing the 10th and 49th Battalions; absorbed by the 21st Canadian Reserve Battalion 15 October 1917
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4950, file 476, part 1; microfilm reel T-10761
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004677 — "War diaries - 9th Reserve Battalion"
  - Record: https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=2004677
- **Page images:** pages 34–38 of the LAC file (`page-0034-01-04-1917_cover.jpg` … `page-0038-27-04-1917.jpg`)
- **Commanding officer:** Lieut.-Colonel W. E. Forbes (taken on strength 1 April 1917, from the 145th Battalion)
- **Note on the record:** this LAC record also contains the 1914–15 diary of the original 9th Battalion on pages 1–25 and pages 83–90; those pages are NOT transcribed here. This chunk covers only the Reserve Battalion diary, April 1917 (pages 34–38).

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word, name or figure that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" (the "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" line is struck through on every sheet), handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- The Place column reads "Bramshott" throughout; the "Hour" column is blank throughout April.
- "S.O.S." = struck off strength; "O.R." = Other Ranks; "tfr." = transfer; "acct." = accounted (for rations, quarters and duty); "Genl. List" = General List; "C.A.M.C." = Canadian Army Medical Corps; "C.A.S.C." = Canadian Army Service Corps.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.
- The Remarks column is blank throughout.

## Notes and gaps

- **9 April 1917 has no entry.** The diary jumps from 8 April to 10 April. 9 April 1917 was the first day of the Battle of Vimy Ridge; the diary itself gives no reason for the missing day.
- All other April dates have entries (1–8, 10–30 April, all at Bramshott).
- **21 April:** the first reinforcement figure reads as "256 O.R." — this reconciles exactly with the stated net decrease of 283 (256 + 27 = 283), which supports the reading.
- **22 April:** the entry records 24 O.R. struck off to the 10th Battalion but gives the decrease as "20 O.R."; the figure is flagged [uncertain] as the two do not reconcile.
- **27 April:** the two Lieut.-Colonel entries share the same illegible surname (it appears to read "McNaught" but this is not certain). The parenthetical figures "(25th)" and "(4th)" are also uncertain.
- **25 April:** the parenthetical after "Hon. Maj. W.K. [uncertain]" reads as "(12th)" but is uncertain.
- **No casualties are recorded in April 1917.** Every entry is a strength return, a posting, a transfer, or a reinforcement draft overseas. The month is dominated by large drafts to the fighting battalions: 200 O.R. on 13 April (100 each to the 10th and 49th Battalions), 283 O.R. on 21 April (256 to the 49th, 27 to the 10th), 50 O.R. on 25 April (40 to the 10th, 10 to the 49th), 24 O.R. on 22 April (to the 10th), plus officers to the 49th Battalion on 25 April and to the 50th Battalion on 27 April.
- **No month-end strength return is given for April** (unlike March, which closed with 133 officers / 1,390 other ranks on 31 March). The April sheet ends with the 30 April decrease and the commanding officer's signature.
- **The month-end signature is illegible** (a flourish followed by "Lt. Colonel, Comdg. 9th Reserve Battalion (Alberta)"), the same flourish as the March month-end signature.
