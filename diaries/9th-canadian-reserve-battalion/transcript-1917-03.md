# War Diary — 9th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Alberta)

## Source
- **Unit:** 9th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Alberta) — organised at Bramshott 4 January 1917 under Lieut.-Colonel W. C. G. Armstrong, absorbing the 9th, 194th and 209th Battalions; reinforcing the 10th and 49th Battalions; absorbed by the 21st Canadian Reserve Battalion 15 October 1917
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4950, file 476, part 1; microfilm reel T-10761
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004677 — "War diaries - 9th Reserve Battalion"
  - Record: https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=2004677
- **Page images:** pages 26–33 of the LAC file (`page-0026-01-03-1917_cover.jpg` … `page-0033-27-03-1917.jpg`)
- **Commanding officer:** Lieut.-Colonel W. C. G. Armstrong to about 9 March 1917; Lieut.-Colonel W. E. Forbes thereafter (see note on the 9 March entry)
- **Note on the record:** this LAC record also contains the 1914–15 diary of the original 9th Battalion on pages 1–25 and pages 83–90; those pages are NOT transcribed here. This chunk covers only the Reserve Battalion diary, March 1917 (pages 26–33).

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word, name or figure that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" (the "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" line is struck through on every sheet), handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- The Place column reads "Bramshott" throughout; the "Hour" column is blank except for the timed entries of 24–26 March.
- "S.O.S." = struck off strength; "O.R." = Other Ranks; "tfr." = transfer; "acct." = accounted (for rations, quarters and duty); "Genl. List" = General List; "Divl." = Divisional; "Emer." / "Em." = Emergency.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.
- The Remarks column is blank throughout.

## Notes and gaps

- **Every date in March 1917 has an entry** (1–31 March, all at Bramshott). No dates are missing.
- **10 March:** the date figure in the Date column is not clearly legible on the image; the entry is placed at 10 March because it falls between the 9 March entry (page 28) and the 11 March entry (page 29).
- **9 March — change of command:** the entry appears to record Lieut.-Colonel W. C. G. Armstrong ceasing in command and Lieut.-Colonel W. E. Forbes succeeding him, but the exact wording is illegible in both lines. Forbes is confirmed as commanding officer by the 1 April entry ("Lt. Col. W.E. Forbes (145th Bn.) taken on strength") and by the month-end signature on page 33.
- **1 March:** the letters after "R. Belcher" read as "C.M.G." but are not certain.
- **5 March:** Pte Adams's regimental number reads as "A33120" but the first character is unclear (it may be "433120").
- **13 March:** the diary gives "4 Other Ranks taken on strength & 6 O.R. struck off strength. Net Decrease — 0 O.R." — the figures do not reconcile (4 in, 6 out would be a net decrease of 2); the net figure as written is flagged [uncertain].
- **17 March:** the increase figure is unclear; "12" is the best reading, and it reconciles with the stated net increase of 10 (12 − 2 = 10).
- **24–26 March — Emergency call-out:** the battalion formed part of a 3rd Emergency Battalion under Col. Rattray, O.C. 6th Canadian Reserve Brigade. Timed entries record the recall of officers at 6.30 p.m. on 24 March, the organisation of Emergency parties, reports of 200 O.R. unfit but available for fatigue and 218 (Emergency) complete, dismissal orders at 9.15 p.m. (men to take off boots but not clothes), a 2 p.m. parade and route march on 26 March, and notification at 3 p.m. on 26 March that the need for vigilance had ceased and normal conditions prevailed. Much of the wording in these entries is illegible; the sentence beginning on page 30 ("notified that the Emergency …") continues onto page 31.
- **29 March:** Lieut. R.J. Belcher's promotion entry is partly illegible; the gazette date as read ("20/10/17") post-dates the entry and is flagged [uncertain].
- **31 March:** the list of officers taken on strength from the 21st Reserve Battalion names 10 officers, but the entry states a net increase of 11 officers; one name may be illegible. The month-end signature is illegible (a flourish followed by "Lt. Colonel, Comdg. 9th Reserve Battalion (Reserve)").
- **No casualties are recorded in March 1917.** Every entry is a strength return, a posting, a transfer, a commission, or a reinforcement draft. The largest single movement is the 50-O.R. draft overseas to reinforce the 10th Battalion on 5 March.
