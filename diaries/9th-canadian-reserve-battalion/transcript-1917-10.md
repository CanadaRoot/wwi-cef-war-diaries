# War Diary — 9th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Alberta)

## Source
- **Unit:** 9th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Alberta) — organised at Bramshott 4 January 1917 under Lieut.-Colonel W. C. G. Armstrong, absorbing the 9th, 194th and 209th Battalions; reinforcing the 10th and 49th Battalions
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4950, file 476, part 1; microfilm reel T-10761
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004677 — "War diaries - 9th Reserve Battalion", 90 pages
  - Record: https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=2004677
- **Page images:** pages 79–82 of 90 in this file (`page-0079-01-10-1917_cover.jpg` … `page-0082-12-10-1917.jpg`)
- **Commanding officer:** Lieut.-Colonel W. C. G. Armstrong (the final sheet is signed at the foot by the commanding officer — signature illegible, see note below)
- **Note on the record:** this LAC record also contains the 1914–15 diary of the original 9th Battalion on pages 1–25 and pages 83–90; those pages are NOT transcribed here. This chunk covers only the Reserve Battalion diary, September–October 1917 (pages 73–82). September 1917 is in `transcript-1917-09.md`.

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- The "Hour" column is blank throughout this volume — no hours are recorded.
- The Remarks column carries a short annotation on most October lines, apparently reading "Sick" [uncertain] — its meaning is unclear; it may be a clerk's tick or initials rather than the word "Sick". It is not transcribed line by line.
- "Alta Reg Depot" is the Alberta Regimental Depot; "recd" = received; "attd" = attached; "O.S." = overseas; "Offrs" = Officers.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; the place throughout is Bramshott, recorded as written.
- The diary is a strength-and-postings record: almost every entry is an officer taken on or struck off strength, or a net increase/decrease of Other Ranks. No training, weather or narrative entries appear in these two months.

## Progress
- **September 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 73–78): cover, diary sheets 1–30 September 1917. Transcribed 2026-10-27 (Eighth F). See `transcript-1917-09.md`.
- **October 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 79–82): cover, diary sheets 1–15 October 1917, ending with the battalion's absorption by the 21st Reserve Battalion on 15 October 1917. Transcribed 2026-10-27 (Eighth F).
- **This is the end of the 9th Canadian Reserve Battalion diary.** The battalion was absorbed by the 21st Canadian Reserve Battalion on 15 October 1917 and ceased to exist as a separate unit; the diary's final entry (15 October) records the transfer of its officers and men. The LAC guide's date range for this diary (1 March – 15 October 1917) is confirmed: the last sheet is dated 15 October 1917, not 12 October as the file manifest label suggested.

## Notes and gaps
- Dates with no entry in October: 5, 6 and 14 October. The final sheet (page 82) carries only the single 15 October entry — the absorption of the battalion. The manifest label "12/10/1917" for page 82 is misleading: the sheet's only dated entry is 15 October 1917, and the cover (page 79) states the volume runs "1st Oct. to 15th Oct. 1917 (inc.)". The diary's true end date is **15 October 1917**, matching the LAC guide.
- Uncertain readings, with best guesses where any could be made (none of these are certain enough to transcribe without the flag):
  - 1 October, Lieut. J. B. Armour "while on command to [uncertain] Ranges" — the range name could not be read; possibly "Hythe".
  - 1 October, Lieuts. J. W. [uncertain] and W. F. Oliver — the first surname could not be read; possibly "Berge". Both were in Bramshott Military Hospital.
  - 1 October, Capt. E. [uncertain] — the surname could not be read at all.
  - 2 October, Capt. W. J. [uncertain] (10th Bn) — surname unreadable.
  - 2 October, Lieut. C. J. [uncertain] — surname unreadable.
  - 3 October, Capt. C. A. D. [uncertain] — surname unreadable; possibly "Buchanan".
  - 4 October, Lieut. C. A. [uncertain] — surname unreadable.
  - 10 October, the three lieutenants to Canada as instructors: surnames unreadable; best readings "Watt" (C. W.), "Rankin" (A. H.) and "Kepler" (J.). The place after "C. T. S." could not be read.
  - 12 October, Lieut. W. [uncertain] Ewen — the middle initial could not be read; "Ewen" is the best reading of the surname.
  - 12 October, Lieut. W. J. [uncertain] — surname unreadable; possibly "Stefferman".
  - 15 October, the commanding officer's signature at the foot of the final sheet is an illegible scrawl; the printed "Lt. Colonel" and "Comdg. 9th Reserve Battalion (Alberta)" are legible.
  - The Remarks-column annotation on most October lines apparently reads "Sick" but could be a clerk's tick or initials; its meaning is unclear.
- Notable items:
  - **The battalion's end:** on 15 October 1917 the 9th Reserve Battalion was absorbed by the 21st Reserve Battalion — 54 officers and 757 Other Ranks were struck off strength on posting to the 21st Reserve Battalion, with further attached personnel (5 officers, 4 officers, 54 Other Ranks) becoming attached to the 21st, and 78 Other Ranks returning to the Alberta Regimental Depot. This is the last entry in the diary.
  - Reinforcements sent overseas in October: Major C. Y. Weaver, Lieut. A. E. Edwards and a third lieutenant, with 20 Other Ranks, to the 49th Battalion on 4 October.
  - Three lieutenants left on 10 October to proceed to Canada as instructors, via a C. T. S. course.
  - Capt. N. B. Pearson was posted on 13 October to the Department of the Controller of Salvage (Imperial) in France.
  - Lieut. J. W. [uncertain] and Lieut. W. F. Oliver were struck off strength while in Bramshott Military Hospital on 1 October (Oliver had already been struck off once, on 20 September).
  - No casualties (killed/wounded) are recorded in October — the diary records postings and strength changes only.
  - The whole month was spent at Bramshott; no movements.
