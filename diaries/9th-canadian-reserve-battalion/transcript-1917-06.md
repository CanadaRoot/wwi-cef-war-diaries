# War Diary — 9th Canadian Reserve Battalion

## Source
- **Unit:** 9th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Alberta) — organized at Bramshott 4 January 1917 under Lieut.-Colonel W. C. G. Armstrong, absorbing the 9th, 194th and 209th Battalions; it reinforced the 10th and 49th Battalions and was absorbed by the 21st Canadian Reserve Battalion 15 October 1917
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4950, file 476, part 1; microfilm reel T-10761
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004677 — "War diaries - 9th Reserve Battalion", 90 pages
  - Record: https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=2004677
- **Page images:** pages 53–60 of 90 in this file (`page-0053-01-06-1917_cover.jpg` … `page-0060-30-06-1917.jpg`)
- **Commanding officer:** Lieut.-Colonel W. C. G. Armstrong (the diary is signed by him at the end of June — see note below)
- **Note on the record:** this LAC record also contains an unrelated 1914–15 diary of the original 9th Battalion on pages 1–25 and 83–90; those pages are NOT transcribed here. This chunk covers only the Reserve Battalion diary, June 1917 (pages 53–60).

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- The "Hour" column is blank throughout this volume's June sheets. The Remarks column is blank throughout.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.
- "S.O.S." = struck off strength; "T.O.S." = taken on strength; "O.R." = Other Ranks; "Net Decrease" is the diary's own wording for the daily fall in Other Ranks strength.
- The printed form footer ("M. & S. ... D.D.A.I. Form C/2118/14") is not transcribed in full.
- All entries are at Bramshott unless stated otherwise.

## Notes and gaps
- June 1917 is entirely strength returns — officers and other ranks taken on strength, struck off strength, transferred or posted to the Sea. Reg. Depot, the 10th, 49th and 50th Battalions, the 21st Reserve Battalion, the Canadian Forestry Corps, the Canadian Railway Troops and the C.A.P.C. — with no narrative entries, no training notes and no casualties recorded.
- The largest single draft of the month: 100 Other Ranks S.O.S. on proceeding overseas to the 49th Bn. on 21 June (p. 58), with a Net Decrease of 96 Other Ranks that day.
- Other drafts overseas in June: 10 Other Ranks (Signallers) to the 49th Bn. (5 June); Lieut. A. H. [uncertain] and Lieut. J. Keaney to the 10th Bn. (13 June); Capt. J. B. [uncertain] to the 10th Bn. (21 June); Capt. J. [uncertain], Capt. J. W. [uncertain], Lieut. J. D. [uncertain], E. C. King, R. H. [uncertain] and E. J. [uncertain] to the 49th Bn. (23 June); 25 [uncertain] Other Ranks to the 49th Bn. and 14 [uncertain] Other Ranks to the C.E.F. (26 June); Lieut. W. H. Burgess to the 50th Bn. (20 June).
- No diary entries are missing in June: every date from 1 to 30 June 1917 is covered. 19 June appears on both page 57 and page 58.
- The 14 May entry in the May volume reads "Lt.-Col. W. C. G. Armstrong [uncertain] S.O.S. on return to Canada", but the diary is signed "W. C. G. Armstrong, Lieut.-Colonel, Comdg. 9th Reserve Battalion (Alberta)" at the end of June (p. 60) as well as at the end of May — the surname on the 14 May line may be misread, or the signature may be by another officer; left as written with the uncertainty flagged.
- The monthly strength return at 30 June (p. 60) gives 86 Officers and 745 Other Ranks, down from 118 [uncertain] Officers and 786 Other Ranks at 31 May (p. 52).
- Officers named (best readings; surnames flagged [uncertain] where doubtful): Pte. W. H. Brooks (appointed to a commission in the Imperial Army, 2 June), Lieut. A. D. Richards, Lieut. J. E. Dickens, Lieut. E. J. Van [uncertain], Hon. Capt. M. J. [uncertain] (Q.M.), Lieut. J. M. Taylor, Lieut. J. Weir, Lieut. H. J. Richards, Maj. C. L. [uncertain] (seconded to the Ministry of Munitions), Lieut. J. Keaney, Capt. E. [uncertain] (to No. 1 Detachment, C.A.P.C.), Capt. W. C. [uncertain] (Paymaster), Lieut. W. H. Burgess, Capt. Nicholson (to C.A.P.C.), E. C. King, Lieut. C. A. Richardson, and the commanding officer Lieut.-Col. W. C. G. Armstrong.
- "Sea. Reg. Depot" and "Sea. Regtl. Depot" are transcribed as written; the abbreviation is not expanded, as its exact expansion could not be established from the diary alone.
