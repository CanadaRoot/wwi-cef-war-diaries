### Appendix 2 — Translation of French newspaper report of the Franco-Canadian (Dominion Day) (LAC pp. 47–48)

#### Page 9 — Translation, sheet 2 (LAC p. 47)

> TRANSLATION OF FRENCH NEWSPAPER REPORT OF THE FRANCO-CANADIAN (DOMINION DAY) PUBLISHED IN THE "L'AVENIR DE L'ORNE" PUBLISHED THURSDAY 4TH JULY, 1918.
>
> [uncertain — this sheet is heavily overtyped in the original, with words struck through and retyped; some words are hard to read]
>
> "Dominion Day" celebrated at Alençon, on Sunday, was a splendid and memorable "Franco-Canadian" success. The fellowship existing between our esteemed Allies and the people of Alençon was manifested in such striking fashion that it will long remain a cherished memory to both. For Alençon was en fête Sunday.
>
> For nearly four years, ever since that memorable exhibition of flying by an aeroplane at the race course on the eve of the world wide catastrophe which has since filled our hearts with anguish — Alençon, had maintained a reserve for which the long list of bereavements and the seriousness of events were ample justification. It remained for our Canadian friends to rouse the people of Alençon from that reserve. The crowd made its way to the Manoeuvres ground, on Sunday, in thousands and one has to go back to the days of Barnum to form an idea of the transformed appearance of the Le Mans Road on that day. How many of us were there on that sunny field last Sunday? 12,000, 15,000 may be 20,000. But in any case it was a human ants nest that surrounded the vast sports grounds; with the light coloured dresses of the ladies and the kaki uniforms of our Canadian, English and American Allies predominating in the picture. The Canadians had done things well and it was with outstretched hand and that peculiar smile which still leaves the face agreeably grave that they welcomed their numerous guests. Amongst the notabilities present were our sympathetic Prefect of the Orne, Mr. J. Desmars; our no less sympathetic and devoted deputy, Mr. Adrien Dariac; General Francois commanding the Garrison; Mr Aveline, Mayor of Alençon, etc. Lieut-Colonel Carew received them with charming courtesy and conducted them to the tribune of honour. The American Red Cross was represented by Mr Du Bellay. It is unnecessary to enumerate the various interesting and diverting events comprised in the programme. It may be that no event constituted an outstanding attraction in itself yet the whole formed a magnificent tableaux in which skill and strength vied for first place. We cannot, however, pass over in silence the splendid English band whose performance was so much appreciated by all nor must we overlook that cavalcade of Red-skins, the thrilling stunts of that English aviator and the exploits of the clown and his faithful steed, and the brave Scotch piper also deserves a word.
>
> The "Entente Cordiale" spirit of the day was emphasized in the speeches delivered by Lieut-Colonel F. J. Carew and the Prefect of the Orne. Lieut-Colonel Carew's speech, made in English, was as follows:— "Ladies and Gentlemen, French, Americans, Belgian and British:— We are all assembled here to celebrate the 51st anniversary of the "Confederation of Canada" Dominion Day! A day very dear to the hearts of all Canadians.
>
> It will be 51 years tomorrow since the great provinces of Canada were united as one single Dominion. Since then, Canada has made rapid progress and we all know that in the near future, she will take her place in every sphere, amongst the greatest countries of the world.
>
> Hereafter and already
> "The Rose and the Thistle, the Shamrock and the Lily,
> Bloom all together
> Under the giant maple?"
>
> The Rose is England, the [Thistle struck through in the original] Thistle is Scotland, the Shamrock is Ireland and the Lily is France."

#### Page 10 — Translation, sheet 3 (LAC p. 48)

> Continuation of translation from French Newspaper. Sheet No. 3.
>
> [uncertain — the opening lines of this sheet are heavily overtyped and partly illegible]
>
> The Maple, emblem of Canada, [uncertain] then all blooming around her to witness the various races of which her people in common.
>
> Canada, as you know, is England's greatest Dominion. This is why as Britishers and France's Allies we experience such keen pleasure in having you all with us, today, on our national holiday.
>
> It is the first opportunity we have had to express our heartfelt thanks to our faithful friends and Allies: the French, for the magnificent hospitality with which they have made us welcome from the first day of our arrival in their beautiful country, more especially in this Department and this city of Alençon. In the name of my Officers, N.C.O's and Men allow me to tell you that the warm welcome you have extended us has touched our hearts more deeply than you will ever know.
>
> Today, however, is a day of sports and not of speeches and you shall not have many of the latter, let me assure you but I wish to take advantage of the opportunity to ask Mr Desmars, Prefect of the Orne, to address a few words to you.
>
> Before finishing, however, I should like to tell you all how much the Prefect and all the Authorities of Alençon have contributed by their ready and energetic assistance to make this celebration such an imposing success.
>
> Allow me, Ladies and Gentlemen, to call on Mr Desmars, Prefect of the Orne to address you!
>
> The Prefects' reply was in English, which he speaks with considerable facility and was afterwards translated by him into French. We deem it advisable to give the text of his speech in both languages:—
>
> "Gentlemen;— In the name of the Government of the Republic, in the name of the Department of Orne, in the name of the people of Alençon, I am anxious to heartily welcome the courageous soldiers of the gallant army of Canada, the faithful Dominion of the British Empire, and the devoted friend of France.
>
> It is on behalf of you all that I am anxious to express to you our very sincere gratitude for having been kind enough to invite us to your magnificent celebration of today. This "Dominion Day" which unites all our hearts in remembrance of your generous Motherland, is also, allow me to say, the feast of your French friends, as it gives us the welcome opportunity to affirm once more our sentiments of deep and sincere friendship. The bond that binds us is too strong for anything to break. Our blood has closely mixed on the battlefields of Right; our hearts are closely united in the same love for Liberty; our souls beat with the same hope in a victory which will be our common work, and will constitute the definite triumph of Holy Liberty in the world.
>
> Gentlemen, such are the sentiments that are sure to be the true interpretation of whole population, I was anxious to express to you in a few words, while addressing you my hearty welcome, and my sincere thanks. To these sentiments I am pleased to join the eminent representative of the powerful British Empire present amongst us today.
>
> Long Live Canada — Long Live the British Empire — Long Live France"!
>
> We in turn also wish to heartily thank our Canadian friends for the splendid holiday we had. They will understand that our happiness would have been complete if our absent heroes had been there to participate in it with us.

- **Other notes:** 1 July — 3,000 francs, proceeds of the Dominion Day celebration (25,000 spectators), donated to the French Red Cross; operations finished on the Blanche Lande property. 25 July — first arrival of American wounded in Alencon; assistance given by H.Q. Staff. 26 July — No. 38 Company made a record cut, the Scotch Mill producing 126,175 F.B.M. in a 10-hour shift. The Hour column carries a handwritten entry on every line, read throughout as 7 A.M.
