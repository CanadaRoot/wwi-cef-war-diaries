## Appendix A — Letter of appreciation from the Director of Timber Operations, 19 March 1919 (LAC p. 83)

A copy of a letter from Colonel J. B. Donnelly, Director of Timber Operations, Headquarters Canadian Forestry Corps, to Lieut.-Col. F. J. Carew.

> Copy.
>
> [uncertain — file reference at the head of the page is faint; appears to read "C.F.C. 25-6. A.D. 19514."]
>
> TO: Lieut-Col. F. J. Carew,
> Headquarters, No. 1 District. C.F.C.
>
> My dear Lieut-Col. Carew.
>
> On the eve of your departure from France I wish to express to you, the Officers, N.C.O.'s and Men of your Headquarters, and the District, my deep appreciation of the splendid work done by the District during the past two years.
>
> The excellent production of the District, in common with the rest of the Corps in France, has been a factor of prime importance in the glorious victory which has come to our Armies.
>
> We have the Commander-in-Chief's word for it that without the timber produced by the Canadian Forestry Corps, the progress of the Armies would have been rendered much more difficult, if not impossible. It may interest you to know that the total production of the Corps in France is as follows:—
>
> Sawn Timber 1,245,993 tons.
> Round Timber 244,340 tons.
> Fuelwood. 631,147 tons.
>
> I wish to express my appreciation also of the very satisfactory manner in which the operations of the District have been brought to a conclusion, a result which reflects the greatest credit upon all concerned. The satisfaction of the French Forestry and other Officials, as expressed in the various letters, copies of which have been forwarded these Headquarters, indicate that you leave the area with the good will and esteem of our Allies, a result, the importance of which cannot be over-estimated.
>
> Wishing you, your Officers, Non-Commissioned Officers and Men a safe and speedy return to your homes in Canada.
>
> I remain,
>
> Yours faithfully,
>
> Sgd. (J. B. Donnelly) Colonel.
> Director of Timber Operations.
>
> Headquarters, Canadian Forestry Corps.
> B.E.F. France.
> March 19th. 1919.

## Coverage notes

- **Dates with entries:** all 31 days of March 1919.
- **Dates with no entry:** none.
- **Pages that are covers/contents rather than diary text:** LAC p. 79 (cover, "CONFIDENTIAL", annotated "With appendix 'A'"), p. 80 (contents — "Diary Text 2 Pages" and "Appendix A"). Diary text is on LAC pp. 81–82 (2 pages, Army Form C. 2118). Appendix A is on LAC p. 83: a copy of the letter of appreciation from Col. J. B. Donnelly, Director of Timber Operations, dated 19 March 1919.
- **Casualties:** none recorded in March 1919.
- **Weather:** recorded every day — fair 1–10, 12–13, 15–18, 21–27 March; wet 11, 14, 19–20, 29–31 March; fine 28 March.
- **Movements:** the final demobilization of the District — 11 March, authority received to send a portion of Headquarters and a portion of 42 Company to Havre on 24 March; 15 March, Lieut.-Col. Carew proceeded on duty to Bordeaux, rejoining 21 March; 21 March, authority received to despatch the remaining personnel of No. 42 Company and Headquarters No. 1 District to Havre on 27 and 31 March respectively; 27 March, 4 Officers and 23 Other Ranks proceeded to Havre for demobilization.
- **Other notes:** How the diary ends — there is no closing statement. The last diary entry is 31 March 1919, a bare "Weather–Wet." The 31 March departure of the remaining Headquarters personnel to Havre, ordered on 21 March, is not itself recorded as an entry; the month simply closes with the Major's signature for the Lieut.-Colonel O.C. (signature illegible — see the [uncertain] flag at the foot of the month). 14 March — Headquarters 76th Labor Group occupied part of the Headquarters offices at 32 Rue Bretagne, Alençon, Orne. 19 March — the attached copy of the letter of congratulation from the D.T.O. (Director of Timber Operations) was received; it is Appendix A, and it gives the Corps' total production in France as 1,245,993 tons of sawn timber, 244,340 tons of round timber and 631,147 tons of fuelwood. 1 March notes "Billets unchanged during month." The place column reads "Alençon" on diary p. 1 and "Alençon, Orne." on diary p. 2.
