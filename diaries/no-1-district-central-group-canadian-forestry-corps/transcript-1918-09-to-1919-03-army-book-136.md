- **Unit:** Headquarters, No. 1 District, Canadian Forestry Corps (Central Group)
- **Period:** 1 September 1918 – 31 March 1919
- **LAC Item:** 2004987
- **Page range:** LAC pages 84–128 (LAC labels "Septembre 1918, p. 1" – "p. 45")
- **Volume:** Vol. 2
- **Form:** Army Book 136 (handwritten field diary)
- **Place:** Alençon, Orne, France (District Headquarters); operations in the Senonches Forest, Forêt de Bourse and Forêt d'Écouves
- **Commanding Officer:** Lieut.-Col. F. J. Carew, O.C. No. 1 District (named as O.C. in the typed monthly diaries)

Transcribed faithfully from the digitised diary pages. Original spelling, abbreviations and figures are kept as written. F.B.M. = foot board measure, as written in the diary. "do" = ditto. O.Rs = Other Ranks. N.C.Os = Non-Commissioned Officers.

## What this is

This 45-page set is **neither appendices nor a duplicate of the September 1918 diary**. It is the **original handwritten field diary — Army Book 136, Vol. 2, "#1 District, Sept 1918 – March 1919"** — the rough day book kept at District Headquarters from which the typed monthly diaries (Army Form C. 2118, Vols. 14, 16, etc., LAC pp. 52–83) were compiled. The LAC labels call all 45 pages "Septembre 1918" because the book starts in September 1918, but the book itself runs through 31 March 1919. The September 1918 portion (book pp. 1–7) is the handwritten original of the typed September diary on LAC pp. 52–55; the October 1918–March 1919 portions are the handwritten originals of the typed diaries for those months. The wording and figures differ in places from the typed fair copies (e.g. the 28 September shipment figures), and the field book contains entries and details not in the typed versions. Two typed slips are pasted into the book (28 September and 24 October). One January page (book p. 36) was started, crossed out with a large X marked "Cancelled", and restarted.

## Coverage notes

- **What this set is:** the original handwritten field diary (Army Book 136, Vol. 2), 1 September 1918 – 31 March 1919 — not appendices and not a duplicate of the typed September diary. The typed monthly diaries (Army Form C. 2118) on LAC pp. 52–83 were compiled from this book. File named `transcript-1918-09-to-1919-03-army-book-136.md` rather than the `transcript-1918-09-appendices.md` / `transcript-1918-09-duplicate.md` names suggested in the task, because neither fits what the material proved to be.
- **Dates covered:** every day from 1 September 1918 to 31 March 1919 has a dated heading, except that 23 November is the second "Nov. 22" heading on LAC p. 113 (read as 23 November from the sequence). Most days carry only a weather note; substantive entries are listed below.
- **Days with substantive entries:** 2, 4, 12, 16, 17, 18, 20, 21, 26, 28, 30 September; 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 11, 13, 15, 19, 21, 23, 24, 25, 28, 30, 31 October; 1, 7, 8, 11, 12, 15, 27, 29, 30 November; 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 11, 12, 13, 15, 25, 27 December; 1, 9, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16, 27, 31 January; 1, 10, 12, 14, 15, 20, 26, 27, 28 February; 11, 14, 15, 21, 27 March.
- **Casualties:** none recorded in this book. The only injury-related note is the influenza epidemic: 28 October — Alençon placed out of bounds to British troops because of the serious influenza epidemic.
- **Movements / drafts of note:** 2–4 October — urgent draft of 6 N.C.Os and 150 men to the Canadian Corps Reinforcement Camp, with 150 O.Rs held at Headquarters "to stand to" for a further call; 12 September — drafts to a Depot Group in England and to No. 3 District, C.F.C., on account of surplus strength; December–March — demobilization drafts: 1–2 December (5 officers, 196 O.Rs to Conches, en route to Havre); 4 December (1 officer, 135 O.Rs of No. 2 Canadian Construction Company); 7 December (3 officers, 142 O.Rs to No. 6 C.B.D. for return to Canada); 9–16 January (44 Coy, 43 Coy, 30 Coy, 41 Coy, 54 Coy evacuated to CFC Sunningdale via Le Havre); 15 February (3 officers, 74 O.Rs of No. 44 Coy to Havre); 28 February (orders for 3 officers, 70 O.Rs to Havre on 1 March); 27 March (4 officers, 28 O.Rs to Havre). 30 September — H.Q. and half of 2nd Eastern Coy, N.C.C. moved to a new camp site near Bellême.
- **Fires:** 11 December — fire broke out at No. 20 Coy caused by spontaneous combustion; 12–13 December — 50 men from No. 42 Coy, 20 teams from Nos. 42 and 43 Coys, and 50 men from No. 30 Coy sent to fight it; no damage to stock or mill.
- **Production records:** 16 September — new record for a day's cut, 402,066 F.B.M.; 28 September — record month's production and shipment (typed slip: 25,284 tons produced; 6,652,952 F.B.M. sawn material; 20,282 tons shipped; 1,210 trucks); 31 October — record production and shipments (30,428 tons produced, 32,496 tons shipped, 76 complete trains of 2,314 wagons). Note the handwritten 28 September entry gives slightly different figures (16,364 tons, 6,658,276 F.B.M.) from the pasted-in typed slip (16,363 tons, 6,652,952 F.B.M.).
- **Visits of note:** 21 September — Brig. Gen. Atkinson visited Senonches operations; 11 October — Brig. General Ross and a party including Col. J. B. White, D.S.O., visited the District and inspected Pay Records at Alençon; 7–8 November — Gen. Turner, Col. J. B. White and Col. Cooke inspected the District, 41 and 54 Coys at Senonches, and addressed the men; 24 October — Col. L. A. Dowey (Auditor General), McKinnon and Mr. McLelan visited Headquarters and the Senonches Forest camps; 13 October — a party visited to investigate establishing the "Khaki College" educational scheme in the area.
- **Armistice:** 11 November — advice by phone from Paris at 11:30 that Germany had accepted the terms of Armistice; 12 November — general holiday throughout the District.
- **Other notes:** 17 September — No. 24 Coy awarded the "Diploma of Honour" by the Société d'Horticulture et de Viticulture d'Eure-et-Loir, Chartres; 1 November — new band mill at Senonches commenced operations; 15 November — new lighting plant completed at the District Detention Hospital; 30 November — electric lighting plant at the Officers' Mess and Headquarters offices, and a steam engine and boiler at Headquarters Camp; 15 December — football match in Alençon between No. 1 and No. 4 District ended in a draw; 25 December — Christmas Day a holiday throughout the District; 27 December — dinner of all officers of No. 1 District; February–March — sawmills sold to French purchasers (2 at Senonches on 1 February; Nos. 20 and 38 Coys' Scotch mills on 20 February; No. 30 Coy's on 26 February; Nos. 20 and 43 Coys' on 27 February); 31 January — letter from the Préfet of the Department of [uncertain] Eure regarding good feeling between the French population and District personnel; 14 March — Headquarters and 6th Labour [uncertain] Camp occupied part of Headquarters offices, 34 Rue de Bretagne, Alençon.
- **Weather:** recorded almost every day, mostly "Wet" in the winter months; "Fair" predominates in March 1919.
- **Peculiarities of the book:** the 2 September entry is struck through; the 28 September and 24 October entries are typed slips pasted in; the first attempt at January 1919 (LAC p. 119, left-hand page) was crossed out with a large X marked "Cancelled" and restarted on the right-hand page; the District stamp "No 1 DISTRICT, CENTRAL GROUP, C.F.C., FRANCE" appears at 30 September, 30 November, 31 January and 31 March; the O.C.'s name is read as "Carew" throughout — Lieut.-Col. F. J. Carew is the O.C. named in the typed monthly diaries, which corroborates the reading, but the handwriting itself is not always clear, so each occurrence is flagged [uncertain].
