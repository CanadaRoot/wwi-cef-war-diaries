# War Diary — 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Nova Scotia)

## Source
- **Unit:** 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Nova Scotia)
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4951, file 479, part 2; microfilm reel T-10767
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004685 — "War diaries - 17th Reserve Battalion"
- **Page images:** pages 175–182 of the LAC record, saved alongside this file (`page-0175-...jpg` … `page-0182-...jpg`).
- **Commanding officer:** Lieut.-Colonel W. H. Muirhead (signs the month's diary 1 June 1918).
- **Place:** Bramshott, Hants., England, throughout the month.

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", typewritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- "do." in a column means "ditto" (same as the entry above).
- N.S.R.D. = Nova Scotia Regimental Depot; B.R.D. = [uncertain — as written]; O.R. = Other Rank(s).
- In the May sheets the typist renders "Part II" as "Part II" and "Part II. Orders" in varying forms; transcribed as written where legible.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.

## Notes and gaps
- No dates are missing from the May diary sheets: 1–31 May 1918 are all covered across the five sheets (pages 176–180).
- Notable event: Inspection by His Majesty King George V on 8 May 1918, between 11.00 a.m. and 12 noon.
- Three deaths are recorded in May, all unnamed in the diary: one Other Rank "having died" (7 May), one "having died" (16 May), and one "having died" (27 May).
- The unit won three of four competitions in the Bramshott Monthly Musketry Competition for April 1918 (reported 2 May).
- 24 May was a holiday: area sports during the day and a band concert in the evening; no orders published.
- From 26 May the battalion was Duty Battalion for the week, with all available men used on duties.
- Reinforcements proceeding overseas in May (per the efficiency report, p. 181): 20 to the 25th Battalion, 70 to the 85th Battalion, 25 to the Royal Canadian Regiment — 115 in total.
- Unit strength at 31 May 1918: Officers 78; Other Ranks 1045 (down from 63 officers / 1424 Other Ranks at 30 April, reflecting the drafts sent overseas and the move of men to Frensham Pond Segregation Camp).
