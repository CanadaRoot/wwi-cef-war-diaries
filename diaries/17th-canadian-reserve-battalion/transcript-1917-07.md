# War Diary — 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion

## Source
- **Unit:** 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Nova Scotia) — formed 23 January 1917 at Bramshott by the amalgamation of the 17th, 193rd and 219th Battalions; part of the 5th Canadian Reserve Brigade, reinforcing the 85th and 185th Battalions
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4951, file 479, part 2; microfilm reel T-10767
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004685 — "War diaries - 17th Reserve Battalion", 293 pages
  - Record: https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=2004685
- **Page images:** pages 106–111 of 293 in this file (`page-0106-01-07-1917_cover.jpg` … `page-0111-30-07-1917.jpg`)
- **Commanding officer:** Lieut. Colonel W. H. Muirhead (signs the 31 July sheet)
- **Note on the record:** this LAC record also contains an unrelated 1914 diary of the 17th Battalion on pages 1–79; those pages are NOT transcribed here. This chunk covers only the Reserve Battalion diary, January–December 1917 (pages 80–139).

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", typed in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- Entries begin with the day of the week, and dates are written "July 1st", "2nd", etc.
- The "Hour" column is blank throughout this volume — no hours are recorded.
- The Place column gives "BRAMSHOTT CAMP, HANTS, ENGLAND." on the first entry of each sheet and is blank (ditto) thereafter.
- The Remarks column is initialled on each line (initials not legible — see per-page notes).
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.
- "Part II Orders" and "Routine Order paras." are cited by number only; the orders themselves are not in this record.

## Notes and gaps
- Strength: 66 officers and 724 other ranks at 30 June; 55 officers and 651 other ranks at 31 July.
- 1 July: 1 officer and 16 other ranks represent the battalion at a Memorial Service in London. 2 July: declared a holiday by the G.O.C. Canadians in the British Isles; Divisional Sports.
- 5 July: a draft of 80 other ranks proceeds overseas to the 85th Battalion. 11 July: a further 24. 14 July: 12 officers proceed overseas for attachment to Imperial Forces.
- 20 July: inspection by the Overseas Minister of Militia and the General Officer Commanding Canadians in the British Isles. The same day: "1 Other Rank struck off strength, having died" — no name is given; the third death noted in the diary.
- 23 July: 1 other rank struck off strength, "being illegally absent since 9th March, 1917" — the diary's only mention of a deserter.
- 24 July: the "B.B.B." Team is presented with prizes by His Majesty the King on the Ash Ranges, Aldershot. 18 July: the battalion team had won second place in the "B.B.B." Competition at Aldershot.
- 26 July: the battalion is re-organised into a 4-Company unit under L.R.O.2079 of 25 July 1917 (it had been re-arranged from eight to five companies on 20 June). The same day the Musketry and Bombing Teams win first place respectively in the Brigade Competition.
- 18 July: the Routine Order numbers are struck through and illegible, and no Part II Order was issued ("Part II Orders Nil.").
- Abbreviations as typed and not expanded: "C.O.M.P., London" and "C.O.C., Ashford" (12 July); "14th Reserve Battalion, Dibgate" (5 July); "C.P.C., Bunningdale" (27 July).
- No individual names of other ranks are given anywhere on these sheets; only numbers and the units they join or leave.
