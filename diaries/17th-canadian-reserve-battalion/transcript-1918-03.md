# War Diary — 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Nova Scotia)

## Source
- **Unit:** 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Nova Scotia)
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4951, file 479, part 2; microfilm reel T-10767
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004685 — "War diaries - 17th Reserve Battalion"
- **Page images:** pages 159–167 of the LAC record, saved alongside this file (`page-0159-...jpg` … `page-0167-...jpg`).
- **Commanding officer:** Lieut.-Colonel W. H. Muirhead (signs the month's diary and training note, 1 April 1918).
- **Place:** Bramshott, Hants., England, throughout the month.

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", typewritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- "do." in a column means "ditto" (same as the entry above).
- N.S.R.D. = Nova Scotia Regimental Depot; B.R.D. = [uncertain — as written]; O.R. = Other Rank(s).
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.

## Notes and gaps
- No dates are missing from the March diary sheets: 1–31 March 1918 are all covered across the six sheets (pages 160–165).
- Operation Orders Nos. 31, 32, 37 and 38 are cited in the diary (route marches on 1, 8, 15 and 22 March) but are NOT filed with this month's pages — they are not in the page sequence 159–167, so they could not be transcribed here. The 22 March route march was cancelled (too many men on Divisional Duties).
- The German spring offensive (Operation Michael) began 21 March 1918; the diary's only direct reflection is the 24 March "Demand for reinforcements received at 8.30 a.m." and the heavy drafts of 29 March (111 Other Ranks to the 25th Battalion, 69 to the Royal Canadian Regiment). Good Friday (29 March) was worked as a normal training day "owing to excessive drafts being called".
- British Summer Time came into effect 24 March 1918 (2.00 a.m.; "All trains and watches moved one hour ahead").
- New training syllabus from 27 March: reveille 6.00 a.m., training 8 a.m.–12 noon and 1.30–5.00 p.m. daily including Sundays; men completing the 9th week of training counted as "Fully Trained".
- Reinforcements proceeding overseas in March (per the efficiency report, p. 167): 173 to the 25th Battalion, 161 to the 85th Battalion, 146 to the Royal Canadian Regiment — 480 in total, all passed all tests, all Lewis Gunners and Rifle Bombers.
- Unit strength at 31 March 1918: Officers 79; Other Ranks 1077.
