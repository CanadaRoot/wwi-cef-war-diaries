# War Diary — 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion

## Source
- **Unit:** 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Nova Scotia) — formed 23 January 1917 at Bramshott by the amalgamation of the 17th, 193rd and 219th Battalions; part of the 5th Canadian Reserve Brigade, reinforcing the 85th and 185th Battalions
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4951, file 479, part 2; microfilm reel T-10767
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004685 — "War diaries - 17th Reserve Battalion", 293 pages
  - Record: https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=2004685
- **Page images:** pages 89–94 of 293 in this file (`page-0089-01-04-1917_cover.jpg` … `page-0094-27-04-1917.jpg`)
- **Commanding officer:** Lieut. Colonel W. H. Muirhead (appointed Commanding Officer 2 April); Major G. S. Harrington in command during Muirhead's absence at the Senior Officers' Course, Aldershot (9–14 April); Major J. C. Johnstone briefly in command 10 April; Harrington signs the 30 April sheet
- **Note on the record:** this LAC record also contains an unrelated 1914 diary of the 17th Battalion on pages 1–79; those pages are NOT transcribed here. This chunk covers only the Reserve Battalion diary, January–December 1917 (pages 80–139).

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", typed in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- From April the entries begin with the day of the week, and dates are written "April 1st", "2nd", etc.
- The "Hour" column is blank throughout this volume — no hours are recorded.
- The Place column gives "BRAMSHOTT CAMP, HANTS, ENGLAND." on the first entry of each sheet and is blank (ditto) thereafter.
- The Remarks column is initialled on each line (initials not legible — see per-page notes).
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.
- "Part II Orders" and "Routine Order paras." are cited by number only; the orders themselves are not in this record.

## Notes and gaps
- Volume 2 (April) opens with a new cover and title page; the title page dates the volume "From April 1st to May 1st, 1917", while the cover says "April 1st 1917 to April 30th 1917".
- Command: Lt. Col. W. H. Muirhead is formally appointed Commanding Officer 2 April, but leaves for the Senior Officers' Course at Aldershot on 9 April; Major G. S. Harrington commands in his absence, Major J. C. Johnstone commands for a day on 10 April while Harrington is at Bexhill-on-Sea, and Harrington resumes command 14 April and signs the 30 April sheet.
- 6 April (Good Friday): the diary quotes in full a signal from Headquarters, 5th Canadian Reserve Brigade, that Good Friday "will be treated as Sunday by the troops in this area; attendance of Church Parades will be voluntary."
- 20–21 April: 2 officers and 100 other ranks taken on strength from the 246th Battalion on 20 April; 100 other ranks struck off on proceeding overseas to the 85th Battalion on 21 April.
- 14 April: the battalion band is shown on command at Brighton from this date. 15 April: the battalion is Duty Battalion for the Brigade for the week.
- Strength falls from 1,148 other ranks at 31 March to 928 at 30 April; officers remain at 66.
- Typist's errors transcribed as written: 19 April "frem to N.S.R.D."; 20 April "Seorncliffe" (for Shorncliffe, spelled correctly earlier in the same entry). "Part II Orders No.82" is struck through in the 6 April entry and the number re-used on 7 April.
- Routine Order paragraph ranges have small gaps (e.g. 1319, 1447–1448, 1507–1511, 1587), as typed.
- No individual names of other ranks are given anywhere on these sheets; only numbers and the units they join or leave.
