# War Diary — 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion

## Source
- **Unit:** 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Nova Scotia) — formed 23 January 1917 at Bramshott by the amalgamation of the 17th, 193rd and 219th Battalions; part of the 5th Canadian Reserve Brigade, reinforcing the 85th and 185th Battalions
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4951, file 479, part 2; microfilm reel T-10767
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004685 — "War diaries - 17th Reserve Battalion", 293 pages
  - Record: https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=2004685
- **Page images:** pages 121–125 of 293 in this file (`page-0121-01-10-1917_cover.jpg` … `page-0125-26-10-1917.jpg`)
- **Commanding officer:** Lieut. Colonel W. H. Muirhead (signs the 31 October sheet, dated Bramshott Camp, 2 November 1917)
- **Note on the record:** this LAC record also contains an unrelated 1914 diary of the 17th Battalion on pages 1–79; those pages are NOT transcribed here. This chunk covers only the Reserve Battalion diary, January–December 1917 (pages 80–139).

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", typed in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- Entries begin with the day of the week, and dates are written "October 1st", "2nd.", etc.
- The "Hour" column is blank throughout this volume — no hours are recorded.
- The Place column gives "BRAMSHOTT CAMP, HANTS, ENGLAND." on the first entry of each sheet and is blank (ditto) thereafter.
- The Remarks column is initialled on each line (initials not legible — see per-page notes).
- From October the typist writes "Part 11 Orders" (Arabic "11") instead of "Part II Orders" — transcribed as typed.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.
- "Part II Orders" and "Routine Order paras." are cited by number only; the orders themselves are not in this record.

## Notes and gaps
- Strength: 50 officers and 618 other ranks at the end of September; 79 officers and 1,661 other ranks at 31 October — the jump follows the absorption of the 26th Canadian Reserve Battalion.
- 15 October: the 26th Canadian Reserve Battalion (37 officers, 986 other ranks) is absorbed into the 17th under letter A.G.21-2-40 of 3 October 1917. The 17th is now the reinforcing unit for the 25th Battalion, the Royal Canadian Regiment, and the 85th and 185th Battalions — "which comprise all the Nova Scotia Units in the Field". Reveille moves to 6.00 a.m. and Retreat to 4.30 p.m. from this date.
- 20 October: air raid warning received 11.45 p.m. on 19 October; all clear sounded 12.17 a.m. on 20 October — the diary's only air-raid entry.
- 23 October: presentation of Military Medals to Sergeant Stewart and Private Doucette, and the Good Conduct Badge and Long Service Medal to L/Cpl. Palmer, by Brigadier-General P. S. Meighan. ("L/Cpl. Palmer. A." is as typed — presumably L/Cpl. A. Palmer.) The same day 1 other rank is taken on strength "from Desertion" — a returned deserter.
- 11 October: muster parade at 7.00 a.m.; the battalion wins both the Brigade Bombing Competition and the Brigade Rapid Wiring Competition (total points 65). 25 October: second in the Brigade Rapid Wiring Competition and first in the Brigade Rifle Grenade Competition (score 20 points).
- 14 October: brigade duties at [uncertain] Bordon for the week; a party of 25 other ranks proceeds to Mychett to fire the General Musketry Course.
- The 10 October entry is cut off at the foot of page 122; its continuation is not in the record (page 123 begins with 11 October).
- "Part 11 Orders" (Arabic numerals) is used throughout October in place of "Part II Orders" — transcribed as typed.
- Abbreviations as typed and not expanded: "C.A.M.C. Bisboro" (19 October); "Canadian Machine Depot" (9 October).
- No individual names of other ranks are given anywhere on these sheets, except the medal recipients named on 23 October; only numbers and the units they join or leave.
