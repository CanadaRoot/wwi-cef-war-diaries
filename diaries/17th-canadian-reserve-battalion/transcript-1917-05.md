# War Diary — 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion

## Source
- **Unit:** 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Nova Scotia) — formed 23 January 1917 at Bramshott by the amalgamation of the 17th, 193rd and 219th Battalions; part of the 5th Canadian Reserve Brigade, reinforcing the 85th and 185th Battalions
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4951, file 479, part 2; microfilm reel T-10767
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004685 — "War diaries - 17th Reserve Battalion", 293 pages
  - Record: https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=2004685
- **Page images:** pages 95–100 of 293 in this file (`page-0095-01-05-1917_cover.jpg` … `page-0100-30-05-1917.jpg`)
- **Commanding officer:** Major G. S. Harrington in command to 19 May, when he hands over to Major J. C. Johnstone on proceeding to General Headquarters, Canadians; Johnstone signs the 31 May sheet
- **Note on the record:** this LAC record also contains an unrelated 1914 diary of the 17th Battalion on pages 1–79; those pages are NOT transcribed here. This chunk covers only the Reserve Battalion diary, January–December 1917 (pages 80–139).

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", typed in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- Entries begin with the day of the week, and dates are written "1/5/17", "2", etc.
- The "Hour" column is blank throughout this volume — no hours are recorded.
- The Place column gives "Bramshott Camp, Hants." on the first entry of each sheet and is blank (ditto) thereafter.
- The Remarks column is initialled on each line (initials not legible — see per-page notes).
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.
- "Part II Orders" and "Routine Order paras." are cited by number only; the orders themselves are not in this record.

## Notes and gaps
- No strength return is given at the end of May (unlike March and April).
- Command: Major G. S. Harrington hands over to Major J. C. Johnstone on 19 May, on proceeding to General Headquarters, Canadians. Lt. Col. D. D. Cameron — the battalion's first commanding officer — is struck off strength to Canada on 14 May.
- 29 May: visited by His Royal Highness, The Duke of Connaught, on tour of inspection.
- 18 May: "1 other rank died" — no name is given. This is the second death noted in the diary (the first was 9 February).
- 17 May: muster parade at 6.00 a.m.; 2 other ranks struck off on proceeding overseas to France.
- 9 May: Junior N.C.O.'s Club formed; 100 other ranks go away on leave. 7 May: the brass band proceeds on command to Hastings.
- 2 May: 42 other ranks struck off to the Forestry Corps and 12 to the Canadian Engineers — the largest single-day transfers of the month. 28 May: 59 other ranks proceed overseas to reinforce the 85th Battalion.
- Part II Order No.125 is not cited (the sequence jumps from No.124 on 26 May to No.126 on 28 May) — transcribed as written.
- Abbreviations as typed and not expanded: "1 to C.S.M., Shorncliffe" (1 May), "1 struck off strength to C.M.S., Shorncliffe" (12 May), "1 other rank transferred to H.Q.S.S., 3rd Can.Res.Bde. Seaford" (28 May).
- Routine Order paragraph ranges have small gaps (e.g. 1766, 1969), as typed.
- No individual names of other ranks are given anywhere on these sheets; only numbers and the units they join or leave.
