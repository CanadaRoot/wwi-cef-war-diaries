# War Diary — 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion

## Source
- **Unit:** 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Nova Scotia) — formed 23 January 1917 at Bramshott by the amalgamation of the 17th, 193rd and 219th Battalions; part of the 5th Canadian Reserve Brigade, reinforcing the 85th and 185th Battalions
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4951, file 479, part 2; microfilm reel T-10767
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004685 — "War diaries - 17th Reserve Battalion", 293 pages
  - Record: https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=2004685
- **Page images:** pages 126–132 of 293 in this file (`page-0126-01-11-1917_cover.jpg` … `page-0132-monthly_report.jpg`)
- **Commanding officer:** Major A. W. P. Weston (assumed command 9 November 1917, vice Lieut. Colonel W. H. Muirhead, on duty in London; signs the 30 November sheet and the training and efficiency reports, dated Bramshott, 2 December 1917)
- **Note on the record:** this LAC record also contains an unrelated 1914 diary of the 17th Battalion on pages 1–79; those pages are NOT transcribed here. This chunk covers only the Reserve Battalion diary, January–December 1917 (pages 80–139).

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", typed in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- Entries begin with the day of the week, and dates are written "November 1st", "2nd.", etc.
- The "Hour" column is blank throughout this volume — no hours are recorded.
- The Place column gives "BRAMSHOTT HANTS ENGLAND." on the first entry of each sheet and is blank (ditto) thereafter.
- The Remarks column is initialled on each line (initials not legible — see per-page notes).
- From October the typist writes "Part 11 Orders" (Arabic "11") instead of "Part II Orders" — transcribed as typed.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.
- "Part II Orders" and "Routine Order paras." are cited by number only; the orders themselves are not in this record.

## Page 131 — Training note (November 1917)

Army Form C. 2118, as above. The Place, Date and Hour columns are blank. Signed at the foot by Major A. W. P. Weston, "Major. Commanding 17th. Canadian Reserve Battalion." "Bramshott: 2-12-17." is written at bottom left.

> TRAINING
>
> Training is based on a 14 weeks syllabus; the class numbers representing the week of training.
>
> A soldier on arrival from Canada, is considered a recruit and invariably commences in the 3rd. or 4th. week. In the case of men transferred from here from Other Units, who have already done some training in this Country, they are placed in the various classes, according to their efficiency. Overseas Casualties are placed in Class 8.
>
> After completing 8 weeks training, the men proceed to the Ranges to fire the G.M.C. (Class 9 & 10.)
>
> On returning from the Ranges, the men are required to pass tests. After passing these tests, they are fully trained and available for draft, but continue to complete their 14 weeks [struck through: "training"] training with a view to further improving the standard attained, special attention being paid to the Specialist knowledge of these men, in Rifle-bombing and Lewis Gun, Anti-gas practice and platoon in attack.

## Notes and gaps
- Strength: 79 officers and 1,661 other ranks at 31 October; 59 officers and 1,052 other ranks at 30 November — the fall follows the month's large drafts overseas.
- 1 November: air-raid warning ("Field Marshalls warning, 2.30 am. All Clear, 8.45 a.m."); "C" Company is absorbed by "A" Company; the battalion again wins the Brigade Wiring and Bombing Competition.
- 9 November: Major A. W. P. Weston assumes command, vice Lieut. Colonel W. H. Muirhead, on duty in London.
- 11 November: the month's largest draft — 250 other ranks to the 85th Battalion in the Field, 150 to the Royal Canadian Regiment in the Field, 20 officers to the 85th and 3 officers to the Royal Canadian Regiment. 25 November: a further 105 other ranks to the 25th Battalion, 75 to the 85th and 42 to the Royal Canadian Regiment.
- 20 November: 1 other rank struck off strength, "having died in Hospital" — the fourth death noted in the diary (after 9 February, 18 May and 20 July).
- 20 November: inspection of lines, hutments and training by General Turner, General Murray and Staff at 10.30 a.m. 22 November: brigade inspection by Brigadier General P. S. Meighen, C.M.G., at 3.00 p.m. 23 November: inspection by Lieut. General Sir Archibald Murray, G.C.M.G., K.C.B., C.V.O., D.S.O., Commander-in-Chief, Aldershot Command — "Brig General Murray and Staff expressed great satisfaction with general routine of training and cleanliness of lines, hutments, etc., and especially Messing and Recreation arrangements."
- 7 November: the 17th, 20th and 25th Canadian Reserve Battalions form the 1st Composite Battalion and parade as a brigade ("G" to "L" Scheme).
- 19 November: 2 other ranks taken on strength "from Desertion" (returned deserters); 1 other rank to No. 1 Cadet Wing, St. Leonards.
- Deserters: 9 November 1 other rank struck off as a deserter; 11 November 1; 13 November 1; 16 November 2; 30 November 2.
- The 8 November entry on page 127 is struck through in full and re-typed on page 128 (the re-typed version omits the Route March clause).
- Operation Order numbers are left blank in the 2, 23 and 30 November entries ("Operation Order No. [blank] attached") — transcribed as blank.
- The efficiency report (page 132) repeats the draft strength figures (155 / 332 / 192) down rows (2), (4), (5), (6) and (7) as typed; no men are recorded as failing the tests (row 3: NIL).
- "Part 11 Orders" (Arabic numerals) is used throughout November in place of "Part II Orders" — transcribed as typed.
- No individual names of other ranks are given anywhere on these sheets; only numbers and the units they join or leave.
