# War Diary — 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Nova Scotia)

## Source
- **Unit:** 17th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Nova Scotia)
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4951, file 479, part 2; microfilm reel T-10767
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2004685 — "War diaries - 17th Reserve Battalion"
- **Page images:** pages 168–174 of the LAC record, saved alongside this file (`page-0168-...jpg` … `page-0174-...jpg`).
- **Commanding officer:** Lieut.-Colonel W. H. Muirhead (signs the month's diary 30 April 1918).
- **Place:** Bramshott, Hants., England, throughout the month.

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", typewritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- "do." in a column means "ditto" (same as the entry above).
- N.S.R.D. = Nova Scotia Regimental Depot; B.R.D. = [uncertain — as written]; O.R. = Other Rank(s).
- In the April sheets the typist renders "Part II" as "Part 11" in places; transcribed as "Part II" throughout.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.

## Notes and gaps
- No dates are missing from the April diary sheets: 1–30 April 1918 are all covered across the five sheets (pages 169–173).
- No operation orders are filed with this month's pages (none in the sequence 168–174); the 12 April route march is recorded in the diary but its order is not included.
- Notable visitors: Brigadier-General F. S. Meighen inspected the camp on 2 April; Lieutenant-General Sir R. E. W. Turner, V.C., K.C.M.G., D.S.O., Commanding Canadian Forces in British Isles, inspected training on 3 April; Sir John Gibson, late Lieutenant-Governor of Ontario, with the G.O.C. Overseas Military Forces of Canada, visited on 19 April.
- Easter Monday (1 April) was worked as a normal training day — no holiday granted.
- Large draft arrivals from Canada: 436 Other Ranks on 24 April, and a draft of 284 Other Ranks at Frensham Pond Segregation Camp on 28 April.
- Reinforcements proceeding overseas in April (per the efficiency report, p. 174): 85 to the 25th Battalion, 113 to the 85th Battalion, 118 to the Royal Canadian Regiment — 316 in total.
- Unit strength at 30 April 1918: Officers 63; Other Ranks 1424 (up from 79 officers / 1077 Other Ranks at 31 March, reflecting the big April drafts from Canada).
