## Page 3 (February 1918, p. 3) — [diary text]

WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. (Erase heading not required.) 5th BATTERY C.G.A.

### LIEVEN. — Feb 1st to 4th.

During this period traing was continued at Camp in VILLERS AU BOIS. At 10.a.m. Feby 4th advance party of 3 Officers and 10 Other Ranks proceeded to Lieven to take over Battery from 1st Canadian Siege Battery, who in turn relieved a Battery of the 91st H.A.G. then proceeding to Camp for training. The remainder of the Battery left Camp at 3.p.m. by Light Line Railway reaching Lieven by 5.p.m. The Battery was then split into 3 sections Two Guns forming a silent section at Augres (M33a 18.44) and M33c 15.78. two other guns were placed in Cite Calonne at M14d 00.07 and M14a 95.20 and the remaining two guns in old position in LIEVEN at M28a 60.07 and M27a 65.95. The headquarters of the Battery were also in Lieven. Staff Sgt F.C. Smith and A/B.S.M. Woolley were dispatched to Calais to take over two new guns to complete our establishment to a 6 Gun Battery.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 5th

Visibility during early part of the day was poor but cleared up a bit towards noon. No shooting was done by this battery. Hostile shelling was normal being confined mostly to Woolley Bears and a few H.E's.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 6th

During the early morning Hostile Artillery was very active. From 3.30.a.m. to 3.50.a.m. a heavy bombardment was put down on our lines from Green Crasier to Hill 70 and Heavy T.M. Barrage on our front lines, Batteries in this vicinity, however were not engaged and we received no S.O.S. Call. Visibility was very poor throughout the day and very little shooting could be done. At 3.35.p.m. we attempted a shoot on H.B. N.Z.16 in N22b59.30 by aeroplane observation but had to be discontinued after 19 Rounds on account of bad visibility. Target being obscured by Clouds.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 7th

NIL

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 8th

Visibility was very good during the early part of the day and good work was done in getting Guns of the North Section registered. At 12.55.p.m. we fired 10 Rounds N.E. without observation on H.B. NX 63 in N16 b 98.05. From 12.00.p.m. to 1.15.p.m. 18 Rounds were fired on Puits 2 in N 10 b 65.25 to [uncertain] register No 2 Gun. results were very satisfactory. Hostile shelling in the vincinity of the Battery position was NIL.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

## Page 4 (February 1918, p. 4) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN. — Feb 9th

Howitzer No 186 was received from Workshop and placed in position at Angres in M33a 13.42. This gun was on charge to 1st C.S.B. A destructive shoot was successfully carried out by Aeroplane observation on H.B. NZ 16. Fire was opened at 3.40.p.m. and 21 Rounds expended ranging during which we obtained 5 Y's, 2 Z's and 7 A's. During fire for effect Battery shot remarkably well one shell causing large explosion in No 1 Pit. 50 Rounds in all were expended on this shoot. Hostile shelling was at times very heavy, mostly to the right of battery position. Positions towards Avion were subjected to a heavy bombardment from 10.15 a.m. to 11.a.m.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 10th

Howitzers No 279 and 303, both new guns from Ordnance Calais were removed from Railhead to Siege Park until such time as they could be placed in position.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 11th

Nil

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 12th

Nil.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 13th

At 3.a.m. we opened fire on H.B. O.W 17 in O7c 36.23 in support of Infantry. Neutralizing Fire was also opened on H.B. NX 17 and NX 57, a total of 86 Rounds being fired. At 3.20 a.m. 8 Rounds were fired on H.B. NX 63 in N16b 98.05. Visibility was good during the day and Hostile shelling in the vicinity of the battery position Nil. During the morning we engaged enemy T.M.s in M34c 77.34 by ground observation. 9 Rounds were expended for registration and 10 Rounds on task results were satisfactory, guns getting well on to the target.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 14th

From 6.40 a.m. to 6.45 a.m. we opened up with Neutralizing fire on Hostile batteries NZ 16 and NZ 35. Five rounds being fired on each battery. Visibility was very poor during the day and no shooting could be carried out.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 15th

Visibility very bad and nothing unusual occurred. Hostile shell ng was normal in the vicinity of position but some heavy shelling on our right took place during the afternoon. At 19.45.a.m. we opened fire on H.B. NZ 3 with five rounds neutralizing fire, H.B. NZ19 was also neutralized 15 Rounds being fired on this target.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

## Page 5 (February 1918, p. 5) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN. — Feb. 16th

Visibility was fairly good during the day. At 10.45 a.m. we commenced a shoot on H.B. NZ 19 by aeroplane observation, shoot was very successful, 3 O.k's being obtained and considerable damage done to gun pits. A N.F. Call was received at Noon in reply to which we fired 10 Rounds on H.B. NZ 46. Only 1 G was given by plane. Considerable shelling of battery positions in this district took place during the day and in order to silence Hostile Batteries we opened up on NX 63 and NX 64. 20 Rounds were fired after which hostile shelling ceased and did not reopen again during the day.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 17th

At 9.a.m. we opened fire on H.B. NX 63 by aeroplane observation. During 43 Rounds ranging we obtained 3 Z's and 14 A's. General position of Hostile Battery only was visible to observer so damage could not be ascertained but 1 M.O.K. was obtained during fire for effect. Enemy shelled positions with Woolley Bears and a few H.E's fell near the battery position.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 18th

A Successful shoot by aeroplane observation was carried out on H.B. NZ 30 at 9.25 a.m. during 26 Rounds ranging we obtained, 1 Y, 7 Z's, 7 A's, 5 B's and 6 C's. During fire for effect 1 M.O.K. was obtained. Airman reported "Battery shot well and quickly and got well onto target. General position only visible." An excellent shoot was carried out by Lieut D.J. Maxwell for 10th Infantry Brigade on M.G.E. in N20 c 90.46. 4 O.K's were obtained which knocked a hole in the emplacement, the last shell was observed to fall in the emplacement and bursting inside completely destroyed it. Visibility was good and hostile shelling below normal.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

## Page 6 (February 1918, p. 6) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN. — Feb 24th

H.B. NZ 16 and N22b69.30 was engaged at 1.35.p.m. shooting by balloon and aeroplane observation. Shoot was satisfactory, 2 M.O.K's and 3 O.K's being reported. At 4.55.p.m. all guns concentrated on NX63, 20 Rounds being expended. Visibility was good and E.A. active but hostile shelling was practically nil, being confined mostly to W.B's and shrapnel.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 25th

At 8.a.m. we were relieved at out rear position, Angres. by a section of the 1st C.S.B. who took over Guns No 162, 186 and No 7. our section formerly at that position were removed to North Section Cite Calonne.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 26th

Visibility good in early morning. Enemy and [uncertain] Balloons observing and one was engaged by a 6" Mk 19 Battery near our position, enemy retaliated with 4.1 Gun and a lively artillery duel was kept up for about an hour, several shells falling in our position. Enemy opened up harassing fire on Main Road through Lieven at Dusk, Continual shelling being kept up until 8.p.m.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 27th

Visibility good and hostile batteries active. Considerable shelling of the town and Battery positions near here in the afternoon and evening by 5.9" and 4.1f. gun. A shoot by aeroplane observation was started at 1.45 p.m. on H.B. NZ16. Shoot was successful, 34 Rounds were ex ended ranging, during which we obtained 5 Y's, 9 Z's, and 8 A's. During fire for effect 2 M.O.K.s were rep rted and an explosion caused close to No 2 Pit. General position only visible and damage not ascertained. Hostile shelling above normal.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN.] — 28th

No firing was done by battery during the day. Hostile batteries were active during the afternoon Harrassing fire being kept up on roads and positions in Lieven during the evening.

D. J. Maxwell Lieut.
Actg. O.C. 5th Can. Siege Batty.

## Transcriber's notes — February 1918

- **Date gaps (days with no entry):** none — 1–4 February are covered by a single period entry, and every other day 5–28 February has its own entry, including "Nil" entries on 7, 11, 12, 19, 20, 22 and 23 February.
- **Entries spanning pages:** none. Each entry is contained on a single page (1–4 Feb. on p. 3; 9–15 Feb. on p. 4; 16–23 Feb. on p. 5; 24–28 Feb. on p. 6).
- **Remarks column:** every entry bears the same repeated handwritten mark in the Remarks column. It is not legible enough to read with confidence and is flagged [uncertain] throughout; it appears to be a pair of initials with a flourish. (The January volume's Remarks mark was read as "Lom".)
- **1–4 February entry:** the battery returned to the line on 4 February, taking over from 1st Canadian Siege Battery, and was split into three two-gun sections: a silent section at Angres (spelled "Augres" in the entry), two guns at Cité Calonne, and two guns at the old Lieven position. Staff Sgt. F. C. Smith and A/B.S.M. Woolley were sent to Calais to collect two new guns to bring the battery up to a six-gun establishment. "91st H.A.G." is reproduced as written.
- **8 February entry:** a word before "register No 2 Gun" is struck through in the typescript and is flagged [uncertain]. "N.E." (in "10 Rounds N.E. without observation") is reproduced as written.
- **9 February entry:** "Howitzer No 186" is reproduced as written; the 25 February entry names guns "No 162, 186 and No 7" at Angres. "ranging" is typed "ranging" with the "g" overstruck in the original; "50 Rounds in all were expended on this shoot" is reproduced as written.
- **13 February entry:** "H.B. O.W 17 in O7c 36.23" is reproduced as written. "T.M.s" (trench mortars) is reproduced as written.
- **15 February entry:** the time "19.45.a.m." is reproduced as written; it appears to be a typographical error in the original. "Hostile shell ng" (a broken word, "shelling") is reproduced as written.
- **16 February entry:** "A N.F. Call" and "Only 1 G was given by plane" are reproduced as written.
- **18 February entry:** the airman's report is quoted in the diary: "Battery shot well and quickly and got well onto target. General position only visible." "M.G.E." (machine-gun emplacement) is reproduced as written. Lieut. D. J. Maxwell conducted this shoot for the 10th Infantry Brigade.
- **25 February entry:** "out rear position" is reproduced as written (apparently "our rear position"). "Guns No 162, 186 and No 7" is reproduced as written.
- **26 February entry:** a word before "Balloons observing" is unclear and is flagged [uncertain]; it may be a number. "4.1 Gun" is reproduced as written.
- **27 February entry:** "4.1f. gun" is reproduced as written (the "f" may be a mis-struck inch mark). "ex ended" and "rep rted" are broken words in the typescript, reproduced as written.
- **Signature:** the volume closes with the handwritten signature "D. J. Maxwell Lieut. Actg. O.C. 5th Can. Siege Batty."
- **Casualties:** none recorded in February 1918. Several shells fell in the battery position on 26 February, but no casualties are mentioned.
- **People named:** Staff Sgt. F. C. Smith; A/B.S.M. Woolley; Lieut. D. J. Maxwell (Acting O.C.).
- **Places:** Lieven (battery position and headquarters), Villers au Bois (rest camp), Angres (silent section), Cité Calonne (North Section), Calais (ordnance), Green Crasier, Hill 70, Avion, Lens, Puits 2, Fosse 13 (from January, re-registered in February).
