## Page 3 (April 1918, p. 3) — [diary text]

WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. (Erase heading not required.) 5th CANADIAN SIEGE BATTERY

### LIEVEN. — APRIL 1st

Visibility in the early morning was very poor but cleared up a little towards noon. Harassing fire was carried out in the early morning on Hostile Batteries NZ35 and NZ 18 in N24b 40.27 and N36b 86.29. During the evening neutralization fire was carried out on Hostile Batteries NX63 in N16b 90.01. Nothing of unusual interest occurred during the day.

### [LIEVEN.] — 2nd

No 2 Gun was shifted today to a new position further forward, the operation being carried out by using light line railway. A wire was received from 42nd C.C.S. stating that Major T.W.F.MacDonald had died of wounds received on March 29th. Harassing Fire was carried out on Hostile Battery positions during the early morning. At 5.25 a.m. an S.O.S. call was received and we opened fire on T4b 48.83 6 rounds being fired. 8 Rounds were fired at 7.30.a.m. to neutralise Hostile Battery [uncertain] in N24b 40.27. A successful shoot was carried out by aeroplane observation on H.B. NX 63 in N16b 88.05. during which we obtained 1.O.K. and 2 M.O.K's. During the night we fired upon Hostile Billets in N28c 5.7 – 90.85 and also on Hostile Battery NZ38 in N24b 40.27. Hostile shelling was normal during the day. Main Roads through Lieven being subjected to harassing fire, mostly shrapnel.

### [LIEVEN.] — 3rd

Major T.W.F. MacDonald was buried today at Aubigny, all Officers and Men not actually on duty attended. Visibility was decidedly bad, heavy rains and low misty clouds making any kind of observation impossible. An S.O.S. call was received at 5 a.m. in response to which we fired 6 Rounds on T4b 48.83. Hostile shelling was heavy during the day and at night Battery position and vincinity was subjected to heavy Gas Bombardment. H.E's were freely mixed with the gas. Main roads were also under harassing fire. No serious casualty occurred however Gunner H.G. Turnbull was wounded by shrapnel and 2/AM Lightfoot R.F.C. was admitted to Hospital suffering from Gas poisoning. During the afternoon 10 Rounds were fired on Fosse 13 for registration. During the night we fired 40 Rounds in N.F. Calls, neutralization, and harassing fire on billets in N28c 50.70 – 90.85 and Hostile batteries NZ31. NZ35. and NZ18. Visibility considerably improved towards evening.

## Page 4 (April 1918, p. 4) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN. — April 4th

Visibility was again spoiled by heavy rains and mist which prevented any good work being done. Hostile artillery was fairly active and main roads through Lieven were again subjected to harassing fire. During the night we carried out a program of harassing fire on Billets in N28c 5.7 to N28c 90.85.

### [LIEVEN.] — 5th

Visibility poor and hostile shelling below normal. Nothing of unusual interest occurred during the day. In the afternoon we neutralized Hostile Battery NZ38 in N24b 40.27 and a program of harassing fire was carried out during the night on enemy Headquarters in T5b 20.10.

### [LIEVEN.] — 6th

Hostile shelling was more active during the early part of the day in vincinity of battery positions. Maud Villas in N36c 77.00 –36d 10.15 were subjected to harassing fire during the night.

### [LIEVEN.] — 7th

Visibility was fairly good during the day although early morning was very misty. Hostile shelling was normal. Hostile Battery NX9 in N18b 37.95 was neutralized during the afternoon and Maud Villas were again subjected to harassing fire. Neutralization and Counter Battery programmes on NZ38 and NX71 were carried out during the night.

### [LIEVEN.] — 8th

Harassing fire was carried out in the early morning on Rose Cottage in N36a 35.29. and Noyelles Road and approaches were also subjected to harassing fire. Enemy put up heavy Gas Bombardment at 4.30a.m. lasting until 4.58 a.m. during which we neutralized H.B.'s NX71 and NZ37, the shoot being conducted in S.B.Rs. Noyelles was harassed during the afternoon and Tramways in N35b 10.35 and N246 were subjected to harassing fire during the night.

### [LIEVEN.] — 9th

Hostile batteries NZ38 in N36d 80.55 was neutralized at 3.20.p.m. and harassing fire was carried out on Noyelles and approaches during the evening. Hostile shelling was below normal and nothing of unusual interest occurred. 5 Rounds for neutralization was fired on H.B. NZ40 in N30b 81.19.

## Page 5 (April 1918, p. 5) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN. — APRIL 10th

Maud Villas were subjected to a heavy harassing fire during the early morning, also an enemy Headquarters in N30b 35.75 was fired on during the evening. 30 Rounds being put over on this target. Enemy shelling was normal some scattered shelling of the roads with shrapnel and a few H.E's.

### [LIEVEN.] — 11th

Nil.

### [LIEVEN.] — 12th

From midnight to 8 a.m. we fired 20 Rounds harassing fire on Railway in N35d 00.00. A very successful shoot was carried out during the afternoon on Dumps at Rose Cottage in N36b 38.30. the shoot being observed from Alice O.P.. The 29th Round caused a very large explosion apparently at the dump followed shortly after by a series of smaller explosions which continued for several minutes. At the 46th Round a large fire was started which quickly spread to between 75 and 80 yards in extent and burned for a considerable time. Visibility failed at 7.15 p.m. and the last report from Alice O.P. stated both dump and Rose Cottage to be still burning. Shoot was continued without observation until the allotment of 75 Rounds had been expended.

### [LIEVEN.] — 13th

Day was exceptionally quiet and nothing of unusual interest occurred. Harassing fire was opened up on Billets in N15b 77.77. Cross Roads in N22b 78.96 and Rose Cottage.

### [LIEVEN.] — 14th

Hostile shelling was below normal but a full days program of Harassing fire was carried out by us. 170 Rounds being expended in Harassing fire on selected targets and neutralization of Hostile Batteries.

### [LIEVEN.] — 15th

Nil.

### [LIEVEN.] — 16th

Rear section of Battery left Neuville St Vaast and relieved 10th R.G.A. at Cite Calonne taking over 3 Guns. 90 Rounds were expended during the day as harassing fire and H.B's NX91 and NZ19 were neutralized Hostile shelling was below normal and nothing of unusual interest occurred.

## Page 6 (April 1918, p. 6) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN. — 17th April

Lieut R.L. Smith was discovered in an unconscious condition in bed and although all methods of resuscitation were tried for over two hours he failed to recover consciousness and died. The Medical Officer giving his verdict as Heart Failure. He was buried same afternoon at Ablain St Nazaire all members of his sub-section who could be spared attended. Two S.O.S. calls were received in the evening. The first being at 10.18.p.m. in reply to which we fired 5 Rounds on S.O.S. lines St Emile. The second call was received at 10.27.p.m. and a further 5 Rounds put over on the same line. At 10.30.p.m. we fired 10 Rounds as per C.B. program on H.B. NX65. Harassing fire was kept up for the remainder of the night on N17c 50.00 and searching and sweeping around N35d 10.30.

### [LIEVEN.] — April 18th

90 Rounds were fired during the day as Harassing fire on NX65 in N12c 86.57 and NZ45 & NZ35. Hostile shelling was practically Nil.

### [LIEVEN.] — April 19th

Day was exceptionally quiet and nothing of unusual interest occurred. 8 Rounds were fired at 10.09 p.m. on N15a and b in reply to an S.O.S. call. A program of harassing and neutralizing fire was carried out during the night.

### [LIEVEN.] — April 20th

Enemy troops were observed on Canada Road in N21d 9.7. at 2.25p.m. and 6 Rounds were fired into them. A Shoot by aeroplane observation was opened up at 1.p.m. on NX65 in N12c 86.57 but had to be abandoned after the 15th Round on account of Mist obscuring the target.

### [LIEVEN.] — April 21st

A successful shoot by aeroplane observation on H.B. NZ65 in N12c 86.57 opened up at 10.19.a.m. 150 Rounds being expended. During the evening 20 Rounds were fired on NX41 in N16a 85.15 successfully neutralizing it. the shoot being conducted by balloon observation. 65 Rounds were fired during the night as harassing fire on selected targets. Hostile shelling at times was above normal. Two destructive shoots being carried out by the enemy on Battery Positions in Lieven.

## Page 7 (April 1918, p. 7) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN. — APRIL 22nd

Hostile shelling in vincinity of Battery position was normal and nothing of unusual interest occurred during the day. The usual program of Harassing fire was carried out during the night, 60 Rounds being expended.

### [LIEVEN.] — 23rd

Nil.

### [LIEVEN.] — 24th

One subsection of battery was sent to prepare reserve Gun positions at Gouy Servins. Nothing of unusual interest occurred and only programs of Harassing fire and neutralization on NZ63 and NZ24 were carried out.

### [LIEVEN.] — 25th

Visibility very poor throughout the day and nothing of unusual interest occurred. From 6 p.m. to 8.p., we fired 10 Rounds on Noyelles and 10 Rounds on Loison. During the night 129 Rounds were expended as harassing fire on selected targets.

### [LIEVEN.] — 26th

Nil

### [LIEVEN.] — 27th

Hostil Shelling was nil. Visibility very poor throughout the day. 30 Rounds were fired as Harassing fire on Canada Road in M 30b and N22d 7.9. during the evening and 10 Rounds neutralizing fire was put over on H.B's NZ93 and NX 95.

### [LIEVEN.] — 28th

A demonstration was held before Brig. Gen. Morrison and Brig. Gen Massie by a detachment under Lieut. E.S. Bushell in loading and unloading a 9.2" How. on Light Line Railway. The demonstration was very successful and the following letter was received from the G.O.C. Canadian Corps. thru the Brigade Commander:- "The conduct of the demonstration of loading a 9.2" Howitzer on light line railway, by the detachment of the 5th Canadian Siege Battery was most excellently carried through. It reflects great credit on the Unit and especially those members directly concerned, I want you to convey to Lieut Bushell, N.C.O's and Men of this detachment my thanks for, and appreciation of, their excellent work. Signed E.W.R, Morrison Brig. Gen. G.O.C. R.A. Can.Corps.

## Page 8 (April 1918, p. 8) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN — APRIL 28th

Hostile shelling during the day was practically nil and we fired 95 Rounds harassing fire on selected targets.

### [LIEVEN] — 29th

Day was very quiet and nothing of unusual interest occurred during the day. 20 Rounds were fired during the night on Harassing fire.

### [LIEVEN] — 30th

Visibility was poor and [uncertain] throughout the day and nothing of interest occurred. From Noon and throughout the day 50 Rounds harassing fire was put over on selected targets.

N.P. MacLeod Capt.
O.C. 5th C.S.Bty.

## Transcriber's notes — April 1918

- **Duplicate 28 April entry:** the diary carries two entries dated 28 April — the demonstration before Generals Morrison and Massie (with the quoted letter from Brig.-Gen. E. W. R. Morrison) on p. 7, and a separate entry recording the day's harassing fire (95 rounds) on p. 8. Both are transcribed as written.
