## Page 2 (March 1919, p. 2) — [diary text]

WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. (Erase heading not required.)

### Jemappes, Belgium — March 1st – 20th

During this time Battery remained in billets in Jemappes and nothing of interest occured. The football Team were constantly in training for Canadian Corps Sports which were to be held in Brussels on 22nd inst. The football team together with three other entries for flat racing eventually proceeded to Brussels and did very fine work dispite he [uncertain] disappointments and hardships caused by decidedly bad management. They were eventually beaten in the semi finals.

### Jemappes, Belgium — 21st

Orders were received today to collect all Qm Stores and equipment ready for shipment to the base. This work was put in hand at once and all details completed by evening.

### Jemappes, Belgium — 22nd

6 Caterpillars and 10 lorries reported at the battery at 8.30.a.m. to move guns and stores to Mons. The guns were loaded at Mons station in the afternoon and a party consisting of Lieut C.S.Bennett, Lieut E.A. Leslie, The B.Q.M.S. and 6 other ranks left the same evening for Calais where the guns etc were to be handed over.

### Jemappes, Belgium — 23rd –29th

The Battery personnel were to pull out on the 29th and proceed to Le Havre for embarkation to Canada via England but on the evening of the 24th a slight case of sickness occured in a billet occupied by a New Zealand Traffic Policeman and situated about 1/4 mile from the billets occupied by the 1st Brigade CGA. As this was "Suspected" to be a case of smallpox the entire town of Jemappes was put out of bounds to all soldiers and moving orders cancelled. This was the 4th time our departure had been cancelled and after a stay of nearly 4 months in such a dull town the moral of the troops was stretched to breaking point. However the men, although grumbling, remained quiet and orderly. The case was taken before the proper authorities and after due consideration and investigation the ban was removed and moving orders again received for the 27th.

### Jemappes, Belgium — 28th

Moving orders were again cancelled and postponed until the 29th, the reason being the difficulty experienced in getting trains placed in time.

## Page 3 (March 1919, p. 3) — [diary text]

### Jemappes, Belgium — March 29th (continued)

The Battery proceeded to Mons leaving Jemappes in lorries at 4.30.p.m. The Civilian population giving the boys a hearty sendoff. The battery entrained at Mons station at 5.p.m. and pulled out at 7.10.p.m. for Le Havre. The accommodation consisted of Box Cars but these were made comfortable with stoves and a good supply of straw, the journey was made more pleasant by free issues of Biscuits, Chocolates, Tinned Fish and cigarettes which Major MacLeod had thoughtfully provided for the journey. Le Havre was reached at 10.30.a.m. on the 30th and the battery proceeded to Camp to await transportation to England.

### [No place given] — 31st

Battery paraded in the morning and was put through a medical exam, Bathing and Delousing process, and embarkation cards prepared for the trip to England.

[Signed:] N. P. MacLeod Major, O/C 5th Canadian Siege Battery
