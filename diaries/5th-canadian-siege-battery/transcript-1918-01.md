## Page 3 (January 1918, p. 3) — [diary text]

WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. (Erase heading not required.) 5th Battery C.G.A.

### LIEVEN. — Jan 1st.

There was considerable activity during the day, which, altho misty had occasional bright intervals, and enemy aircraft took full advantage of these, several flying over Battery position in the morning and again during the afternoon. At 4.2.p.m. we were ordered to open fire on Hostile Battery N.Z. 19 at N29b 90.16. 5 Rounds neutralizing fire being put over. At 6.30.p.m. enemy attempted to raid our trenches near Mericourt under cover of a heavy bombardment. We received S.O.S. and opened fire on S.O.S. lines "2" in T5c 35.60 and T5d 68.00. Three parties of the enemy dressed in white attempted to enter our trenches but were disorganized by our artillery fire, some of our patrols who were out in "No Man's Land" at the time engaged these parties, and, after hard fighting, succeeded in overcoming them, capturing three prisoners. At 7.p.m. the enemy attempted a raid on Hill 70 but was repulsed with heavy loss. At 9.50.p.m. another enemy party attempted to rush one of our posts in AVION, but was driven off. Our men taking one prisoner. No S.O.S. calls were received in these last two raids, the Infantry handling both raids with rifle and machine Gun Fire.

[Remarks: Lom]

### [LIEVEN.] — 2nd

Everything was quiet throughout the day. Visibility was very poor and no shooting was done. Work was carried on at new gun positions and nothing unusual occurred.

[Remarks: Lom]

### [LIEVEN.] — 3rd

Visibility cleared up a bit about 10.a.m. and we took advantage to register 2 Guns. The target was Hill Farm in N29c 56.26. No 2 Gun was satisfactorily registered, but light failed before No 1 Gun could be satisfactorily registered. A Shoot on Hostile Battery NX 63 in N16b 96.05 was successfully carried out by aeroplane observation during the afternoon. 75 Rounds were fired the pilot reporting 2 O.K's, 13 Y's 14 Z's and 10 A's. Hostile shelling was below normal and nothing unusual occurred during the day.

[Remarks: Lom]

### [LIEVEN.] — 4th

Visibility was very poor with occasional bright intervals. Some houses in N27a 95.65, which obstructed the view from the front line had to be demolished and a shoot was started at noon, working Nos 1 and 2 Guns with a view to getting a good registration. No 2 Gun was not registered satisfactorily on account of the light failing and shoot had to be stopped after the 17th Round. A very interesting shoot was started on 12.45.p.m. on some gas projectors in N14b 9.9. As two other batteries had tried to destroy these projectors without success, it was very important that our shoot be successful. A plane was sent up to observe for us [uncertain] unfortunately low clouds

[This entry continues on the next page.]

## Page 4 (January 1918, p. 4) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN — Jan 4th Contd.

Clouds and heavy mist obscured the target and hindered the work of the pilot. Only one burst was seen which was given as C12. 7 Other rounds were fired but were unobserved and the pilot gave C I mist at 1.26 p.m.

[Remarks: Lom]

### [LIEVEN] — 5th

There was very little activity on this front during the day, mostly confined to Trench Mortar activity N.W. of Lens. There was no firing by this battery during the day.

[Remarks: Lom]

### [LIEVEN] — 6th

Day was very quiet. Low clouds and misty rain prevented any shooting during the day. Nothing of unusual interest occurred during the day.

[Remarks: Lom]

### [LIEVEN] — 7th.

NIL.

[Remarks: Lom]

### [LIEVEN] — 8th

Visibility was bad in early morning owing to heavy snowstorms, but cleared up a bit later. Some houses in N27a 95.65 were again selected for registration. Shoot was started at 11.20 a.m. Lieut E. Scott observing from Lawson O.P. Shoot was twice stopped on account of visibility failing but was finally finished at 3.15 p.m. Shoot was satisfactory, the houses being completely demolished and a good registration of Nos 1 and 2 Guns obtained. From 10 a.m. to 10.30 a.m. we fired 30 Rounds without observation on some dugouts in N28a 06.86. Nothing of unusual interest occurred during the day and hostile shelling in the vicinity of the battery was NIL.

[Remarks: Lom]

### [LIEVEN] — 9th

From 2.05 a.m. to 2.31 a.m. we opened fire on three Hostile Batteries OY18, in N25d 65.90 OY36 in N35c 20.60 and NZ 19 in N29b 90.05. 16 Rounds were fired on each Battery without observation. Nos 5 and 6 Guns were satisfactorily registered during the morning on Fosse 13 in N28a 50.70. Aeroplane called at 10.46 a.m. to observe for us on H.B. NZ 35 in N25b 91.30. L was put out almost immediately but plane did not acknowledge it. Pilot came down as he had mistaken the Map spotting for our ground strip but on this matter being rectified he went up again at 12.50.p.m. 1st G was sent at 1.04 p.m. First shots were rather good, getting 4 Z's and 6 A's after which the light faded and at 1.56 p.m. pilot was obliged to C I on account of mist. The last 11 Shots were unobserved.

[Remarks: Lom]

## Page 5 (January 1918, p. 5) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN — Jan 10th

As soon as visibility permitted a shoot of 30 Rounds was started by ground observation on Puits No 2 in N10b 66.18 to finish registration of Guns. The results were very satisfactory All Guns being now registered. Orders were received from H.Q. that we would be relieved by 1st C.S.B. tomorrow and proceed for three weeks rest and reorganization. All Guns and stores were to be left behind.

[Remarks: Lom]

### [LIEVEN] — 11th

At 4.p.m. we were relieved at Battery Position by 1st C.S.B. and proceeded to Camp at Villers au Bois for rest and reorganization. Men were marched to the Camp and stores and kits proceeded by Light Line Railway. Villers Au Bois was reached about 11 p.m. and the battery went into Billets and hutsments.

[Remarks: Lom]

### Period Jany 11th to 31st.

During this period extensive training was carried out in all branches. Gun drill was carried out on a 9.2" How. mounted on the parade ground and classes in musketry and Machine Guns, Lectures were held each afternoon for Officers and N.C.O's and in all our work we received very high compliments from General Currie, and Brig Gen. Morrison also from Major W.B. Prowse O.C. 2nd Brigade C.G.A. During the week experiments were carried out in loading and unloading a 9.2" How. on Light Line Railway. This proposition had not been undertaken before and this Battery was detailed to carry out the experiment, which in its early stages presented almost insurmountable difficulties. Lieut. E.S. Bushell and Q.M.S. J.A. Ware D.C.M. set themselves to the task and after various plans had been tried, invented and adopted a ramp, placed on both sides of the track. The Gun and carriage on transporting timbers were hauled by drag ropes up to this ramp, and the railway trucks then run underneath them. A Complete drill was organised and the men practised this until all were thoroughly conversant with the part each man had to do. After three days it was found possible the gun could be loaded in 15 Minutes and unloaded in 10 Minutes. This was considered by G.H.Q. Authorities a most important advance in gun transportation, as up to this time a 9.2" How. has always been obliged to keep to, or close to, a main road. With the new system it was clearly seen that by laying short spurs of Light Line a gun could be run into places where under the old system of caterpillar transport, it was never possible to take them.

[Remarks: Lom]

## Page 6 (January 1918, p. 6) — [diary text]

### Jany. Contd.

On Jany. 31st a demonstration was given before General Currie Brig Gen. Massie and Brig. Gen. Morrison. The demonstration was perfectly satisfactory, the entire operation of loading and unloading taking less than 25 Minutes. All ranks received the highest praise for their smart drill and excellent manner of handling the guns.

## Transcriber's notes — January 1918

- **Date gaps (days with no entry):** none as such — 1–10 January have daily entries, 11 January is the relief and move to Villers au Bois, and 11–31 January are covered by a single period entry (training and the light-railway loading experiment), closed by the 31 January demonstration on p. 6.
- **Entries spanning pages:** the 4 January entry runs from p. 3 onto p. 4; the period entry of 11–31 January runs from p. 5 onto p. 6.
- **"Lom":** the Remarks column on every entry bears the same repeated handwritten mark, read here as "Lom". Its meaning is unclear — it may be initials or a file reference.
- **1 January entry:** the time of the order to open fire is typed "4.2.p.m." and is reproduced as written; it may be "4.20 p.m." The target references are given in the diary's own abbreviated form (e.g. "N29b 90.16.", "T5c 35.60"). "S.O.S. lines '2'" is reproduced as written.
- **3 January entry:** the pilot's report "2 O.K's, 13 Y's 14 Z's and 10 A's" is reproduced as written. "Hostile Battery NX 63 in N16b 96.05" is reproduced as written.
- **4 January entry:** a word between "observe for us" and "unfortunately low clouds" at the foot of p. 3 is unclear and is flagged [uncertain]; the entry continues on p. 4. The pilot's report "C12" and "C I mist" are reproduced as written.
- **9 January entry:** "1st G was sent at 1.04 p.m." is reproduced as written ("G" may abbreviate "Group" or "Gun"). "L was put out almost immediately but plane did not acknowledge it" is reproduced as written. "Map spotting" is reproduced as written.
- **10 January entry:** "Puits No 2 in N10b 66.18" is reproduced as written.
