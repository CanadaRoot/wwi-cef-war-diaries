## Page 3 (July 1918, p. 3) — [diary text]

WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. (Erase heading not required.) 5th CANADIAN SIEGE BATTERY

### FIELD. — JULY 1st.

Corps sports were held to-day at Tinques and all available men who could be spared were dispatched there in the Morning by Lorries. H.R.H. the Duke of Connaught visited First Army including Canadian Corps Headquarters and sports. During the night and early morning our North Section positions at Lieven were subjected to a very heavy concentration of gas, but fortunately no casualties were reported. Very little shooting was carried out during the day. Hostile Battery in N28d.08.00 was successfully neutralized at 11.45.am and a Hostile Battery in O2b.49.43 neutralised at 9.30.pm with good effects. Harassing fire was put over during the night on Hostile Batteries in [uncertain] Enemy opened up heavy Gas Bombardment in Lieven, our North Section position coming in for a good deal of attention. Further photographs taken of H.B. O.Y.62 engaged by us on 29th ulto shows H.B. was well onto target and position considerably damaged.

### [FIELD.] — 2nd.

During last nights Gas Bombardment at Lieven 2 —5.9" Yellow Cross Gas Shells fell in No 1 Gun pit and several fell around No 2 Pit. Some very fine shooting was carried out by this section during the day. Despite large concentrations of gas in and around the pits they undertook a destructive shoot by aeroplane observation on H.B. at O.14.c.23.62. All ranks were obliged to wear S.B.R's and anti-gas gloves nevertheless altho considerably handicapped the shoot was very successful, obtaining [uncertain] 6 Y's, 8 Z's, 6 A's, 4 MOK's A large explosion was caused at Z3 and much damage believed to have been done. Enemy artillery has [uncertain] shown considerable activity. Battery positions at [uncertain] and N27d in Lieven receiving considerable attention. Both our positions were heavily shelled between 2 and 3 pm. with 150.mm and 105.mm and again about midnight short bursts of 77.mm Gas fell in the positions. A destructive shoot by aeroplane observation on UW.63. resulted in 9.Z's. 11.A's. 1.MOK. and an explosion [uncertain] E1 and No 2 pit received a direct hit. Harassing fire was put over during the night on Cross Roads at U.6.d.60.45.

## Page 4 (July 1918, p. 4) — [diary text]

### FIELD — JULY 3rd Contd.

this morning. 67th Brigade. RGA. The section of 9th Canadian Siege Battery with your Brigade was gassed during the night of 30th June and again during the night of 1/2nd July. The section was ordered to do a Counter-Battery Aeroplane shoot on the morning of 2nd July. The conditions were such that the detachments had to work wearing their Small Box Respirators. The shoot lasted for two hours and during the shoot 6Y's, 5Z's, 6A's 4MOK's and a large explosion at Z3 observations were received and much damage inflicted. In spite of the difficulties under which they were working the shoot proceeded so well that the observer was unaware that the Battery was shooting under any but normal conditions. This is a very good example of what can be done under the circumstances and is a proof of the training and steadiness of all ranks under trying conditions. The B.G. R.A. congratulates all ranks concerned and trusts that their example will be emulated by all units of the Corps Heavy Artillery should they have the misfortune to be placed in a similar condition. It is only by continual training, discipline and forethought that this can be obtained. Sgd. H.G. Kenyon. Major. R.A. Brigade Major. VIII Corps. R.A. July 2nd 1918.

## Page 8 (July 1918, p. 8) — [diary text]

### FIELD. — JULY 9th

Enemy artillery normal, the morning being very quiet. In the evening some neutralizing fire was put over on our position by a 10.5.cm gun apparently firing from near Algoma. During the night Lieven was shelled with H.E. and gas from 150.mm Hows but was apparently in the nature of Harassing fire. A considerable nu ber of H.E's being mixed with the gas. Two other ranks were admitted to Field Ambulance gassed. A destructive shoot on UW 60 in U9a by our South Section was conducted by Lieut H.M.Stairs and observed by Lieut Calvert RAF from 10.35.am to 12.47.pm. 1st G at 10.57.am and in 32 rounds ranging obtained 1.O.K. 9.Y's, 3 Z's, 10 A's. 4.B's, and 5 C's. During the evening H.B.s UW 81 and UW 85 in U14b were successfully neutralized.

### [FIELD.] — 10th

Enemy artillery activity was in no way above normal. North Section in Lieven was shelled with 150.mm between 8.pm and 9.pm the remainder of the night being fairly quiet with the exception of some scattered shelling in the vincinity of Cite de Roillencourt. At 8.30.am Lieut H.M.Stairs conducted a destructive shoot on HB. UW 35 in U3c. Lieut West RAF observed this shoot which was in every way satisfactory. 1st G was given at 8.42 am and 29 rounds ranging obtaining 2 Y's, 7 Z's and 15 A's Fire for effect was excellent. At the North Section Lieut E S.Bushell carried out a destructive shoot on HB OY 93. 1st G at 7.2.pm 24 rounds were expended ranging during which we obtained 4Y's 3Z's 7A's 6B's and 2 OK's A small explosion followed by a fire occurred near OK pin point. Plane gave CI on account of low clouds making observation difficult. During the night we carried out harassing fire on U27b and U1b.

### [FIELD.] — 11th

Enemy artillery activity normal during the day. Position in Lieven was neutralized during an Aeroplane shoot in the evening which had to be abandoned after 50 rounds. Battery position in Lieven had been subjected to a great deal of attention and in and around the position shelling has taken place almost every day, in addition to which a considerable amount of harassing fire is put over during the night. The whole valley in which the position is located reeks with gas and not one member of the section can speak louder than a whisper, all being affected, more or less, by the poisonous fumes despite every possible precaution that could be taken.

## Page 11 (July 1918, p. 11) — [diary text]

### Field — July 16th Contd.

Heavy shelling took place during the afternoon of position in Lieven, several shells falling behind No 1 pit wounding 2 men. No 314726 Gnr Ballantyne W.M. was seriously wounded, a shell splinter penetrating the neck just behind the jugular vein. This man's life was undoubtedly saved by the smart action of No 346944 Cpl Forsythe W. who stayed by the man under heavy shell fire staunching the flow of blood with his hands until First Aid could be applied.

### [Field] — 17th

Nil

### [Field] — 18th

Battery position in Lieven was subjected to severe harassing fire by 10.5.cm Hows at 6.am and again at 3.15.pm. A further severe burst of fire was also placed on the position at 7.pm.

### [Field] — 19th

2nd Brigade CGA was relieved during the night by 16th Brigade RGA and 2nd Brigade less 5th and 9.2" Batteries relieved 63rd Brigade RGA which then proceeded to G.H.Q. reserve.

### [Field] — 20th

During the afternoon one section at Neuville St Vaast was relieved by 69th Siege Battery RGA The section at Lieven were relieved during the night and we proceeded to take over positions of 69th S.B. Guns stripped of all stores were left in situ. The exchange of guns was certainly to the advantage of 69th S.B. as although their guns were very little older than our own they had not been kept in the same excellent condition. The guns taken over in Thelus position will have, before any good shooting can be done, have to be pulled out and relaid and much work will also have to be done to the other positions if we are to uphold the excellent reputation which we at present hold.

### [Field] — 21st

This Battery is now split into three sections and spread over a fairly wide front. One section under Lieut F.R. May went into position at Thelus (A.17.a.) and one section under Lieut H.M.Stairs to position at St Nicholas at G.16.b. The remaining section and Battery Headquarters took up positions at Ecurie at A.21.c. The three sections being called Left (Thelus) Centre(Ecurie) and Right (St Nicholas) The position at Ecurie was fairly open, accommodation being provided in Nissan huts and tin shacks, which was a decided change after 8 months of deep dugouts and damp cellars with positions as far advanced as Field Guns (In Lieven 18 pdr batteries were behind us.)

## Page 12 (July 1918, p. 12) — [diary text]

### FIELD. — JULY 21st Contd.

The sudden change from daily and almost continuous shelling to open country and few shells made this position very like a rest camp and this was badly needed as the men were feeling played out after the strenuous time they have lately gone through.

### [FIELD.] — 22nd

The section at St Nicholas came under the 19th Brigade RGA for Tactical purposes but were still administered by the 2nd Brigade CGA.

### [FIELD.] — 23rd

During the night our position in Ecurie and surrounding districts were subjected to harassing fire by 5.9" H.V.Guns. This took the form of short bursts of from 6 to 8 shells at intervals of half-an-hour. Fortunately all fell in the open and no huts occupied by our men were hit, altho several shells fell dangerously near the Billets. During the day H.B. CA 54 in C6d was successfully neutralised and harassing fire kept up throughout the night on C16b and C5d.

### [FIELD.] — 24th

During the afternoon enemy made determined attempts to shoot down the Captive Balloon at Ecurie with H.V. Guns. 14 shells being fired. The balloon was damaged and occupants compelled to make a descent by parachute. The district was again harassed during the night by H.V. Guns of 4.1 and 5.9" calibre. Four Hostile Batteries were taken on today for harassing and neutralising. The harassing fire took the form of salvoes at long intervals and an occasional burst of fire. H.B's engaged were CA.93. and 99. IA.93.and 96. Harassing fire was also kept up throughout the night on C29a.

### [FIELD.] — 25th

Nil.

### [FIELD.] — 26th

Harassing fire on roads, tracks etc in this district again received a considerable amount of attention from the enemy. Considerable movement of enemy troops was observed in C.15.b. during the afternoon which was dispersed by a concentration of fire, this battery firing five rounds. 10.30.pm we fired 15 Rounds on C25c in support of a moonlight raid by the Infantry.

- **16 July entry:** Gnr. W. M. Ballantyne (No. 314726) was seriously wounded by a shell splinter in the neck, just behind the jugular vein; his life was saved by Cpl. W. Forsythe (No. 346944), who staunched the bleeding with his hands under heavy shell fire until first aid arrived. A second man was also wounded (unnamed).
- **19–21 July entries:** the battery left Lieven and Neuville St Vaast and took over the 69th Siege Battery's positions, splitting into three sections — Left at Thelus, Centre at Ecurie (Battery Headquarters), Right at St Nicholas — the first open-country positions after eight months in deep dugouts at Lieven.
- **24 July entry:** enemy guns tried to shoot down the captive balloon at Ecurie; the balloon was damaged and its occupants descended by parachute.
- **29 July entry:** a 4th Division theatrical party performed "Camouflage" at Ecurie; 25 men of the battery attended each evening.
- **30 July entry:** Canadian Corps Heavy Artillery H.Q. pulled out, relieved by 17th Corps R.G.A.; the battery now came under 17th Corps for administration and tactics.
- **Signature:** the volume closes with the handwritten signature "N.P. MacLeod Capt. O.C. 5th C.S.Bty."
