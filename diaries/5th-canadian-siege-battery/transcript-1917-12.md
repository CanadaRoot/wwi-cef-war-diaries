# War Diary Transcript — 5th Canadian Siege Battery, December 1917

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Siege Battery (originally 165th Canadian Siege Battery), Canadian Garrison Artillery
- **LAC Item ID:** 2004798, RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4976, file 572, reel T-10804, finding aid 9-52
- **Month / Volume:** December 1917, Volume 16 (covers 1 – 31 December 1917)
- **Pages in this file:** LAC images pp. 87–94 (8 pages: cover, contents, 6 diary text pages)
- **Diary text date range:** 1 – 31 December 1917
- **Commanding officer / signatory:** The volume is signed at the foot of the last diary page by Lieut. H. M. Stairs, "for O.C. 5th Can. Siege Battery". Lieut. D. J. Maxwell was in command for most of the month (he was presiding officer for the election voting on 4 December); Major T. W. F. MacDonald does not reappear after his admission to hospital on 28 October.
- **Transcription note:** The diary is typewritten on Army Form C. 2118/14 ("War Diary or Intelligence Summary"); the printed heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through. The "Hour" column is blank throughout; the Remarks column is blank throughout. The Place column reads "YPRES" for 1–13 December and "LIEVEN" (Liévin) for 24–31 December, and is otherwise blank. Target references are given in the diary's own abbreviated form (e.g. "W19d 70.60", "M28a 47.58"). "S.O.S." is the distress call for defensive fire; "H.V." is high velocity; "R.F.A." is Royal Field Artillery; "A.A." is anti-aircraft; "I.O.M." is written as in the diary; "How" is howitzer. "Hazebruick" and "Morbecque" are spelled as written in the diary.

## Page 3 (December 1917, p. 3) — [diary text]

WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. (Erase heading not required.)

5th CANADIAN SIEGE BATTERY

### YPRES — Dec 1st

5.10.a.m. to 5.45 a.m. we were firing on Barrage in "K" and "M" lanes at W19d 70.60, W20a 60.20. W19c 50.85 and W19b 30.40. 48 Rounds being expended. During the day we fired at slow rate on Farm and Roads in W19b 40.50 to W13d 85.20. This shoot was still in progress at 4.30.p.m. when we were ordered to switch to lane K, 1300 yards east of R.F.A. line, 24 Rounds were fired and we then switched to lane "M" 1100 Yards east of R.F.A. lines. A registration shoot was carried out during the afternoon farm in V17c 25.10. Two guns being registered. During the night harrassing fire was kept up on Roads in W19b 40.50 and W13d 85.20+ Hostile shelling was normal throughout the day, 9 H.V. shells fell in the battery position but there were no casualties.

### YPRES — 2nd

Two heavy barrages were opened up in the early morning. From 2.10.a.m. to 6 a.m. 250 Rounds were fired on farms in W13d 90.20. and W20a 30.50. After completing these shoots we returned to our night task of harrassing fire on Roads and Farms in W 19b 40.50. We fired 70 Rounds which completed the task and finished at 7.25.a.m. We opened fire again at 9.50.a.m. on the same target and at 11.10.a.m. after having fired 50 Rounds were ordered to cease fire. S.O.S. call was received at 4.15 p.m. and opened fire on W19a 60.50 and ceased fire at 4.35 p.m. after firing 47 Rounds. At 5.20.p.m. we received a second S.O.S. call and opened fire on the same target, firing 27 Rounds. During the night we fired 60 Rounds on Farm and Roads in W19b 40.50 and W13d 85.20. Hostile shelling was below normal but considerable bombing of our rear billets took place during the evening.

### YPRES — 3rd

At 3.10. a.m. No 346903 Bdr E.J. Marshall was guide to an ammunition party from Manners Jct to Battery Position, Having deposited the ammunition he walked back alone to the rear billets. He had covered about half the distance when he noticed a man walking ahead of him and attempted to overtake him in order to have company back to the billets. The man refused to be approached and turning sharply to the right slipped into an old pill-box. Bdr Marshall then noticed the man was wearing a German pattern steel helmet so followed him into the pillbox and flashing his light around discovered a German soldier hiding in the corner, He immediately took the man prisoner

## Page 4 (December 1917, p. 4) — [diary text]

### YPRES — 3rd (continued)

prisoner and marched him back to the Corps Cage, It was then discovered that the prisoner had been wandering around our lines for 5 days with very little food and explained that he was scared to give himself up to the Canadians as his officers had told him what he might expect at our hands if taken prisoner. He was very much surprised and relieved to find conditions so very different to what he had expected.

From 6.15.a.m. to 7. a.m. we fired in "Bursts of Fire" on Roads and Farms in W19b 40.50 and W13d 85.20, 56 Rounds being fired. During the day we fired intermittently on McCulloch and Sorby Farms. Hostile shelling was normal throughout the day. A few gas shells and H.V. gun shells fell in and near the Battery Position.

### YPRES — 4th

At 11.a.m. we started voting for the Canadian General Election. Lieut D.J. Maxwell being the presiding officer. Great enthusiasm prevailed amongst the men who were all anxious to poll their votes in favour of the Government.

### YPRES — 5th

Received instructions from Group H.Q. that 1st and 2nd Heavy Groups and all Canadian Batteries would shortly be pulling out of the line and to hold ourselves in readiness for pulling out at very short notice. During the day we fired on area McCulloch Farm, Sorby Farm and Nicholson Cross Roads in W13d 90.20, W20a 30.80 and W20a 08.55. The same targets and to 350 yards east were taken for a night task, 85 Rounds being fired. Some hostile shelling of the battery position occurred during the afternoon but never at any time reached large dimensions.

### YPRES — 6th

During the day Harrassing fire was carried out on Roads W20a and searching 350 yards east of Nicholson Cross Roads. From 7.p.m. and continuing throughout the night Fire was kept up on Venison Farm in V 24d 30.90 and Nicholson Cross Roads in W20a 10.60. 100 Rounds being expended on the night task. During the evening a Hostile raiding party flew over and bombed our back areas and rear billets in Ypres, one plane became separated and was brought down by A.A. Guns. The pilot discharging his cargo of bombs whilst falling, The machine crashed between Ypres and Poperinghe, the pilot being killed.

## Page 5 (December 1917, p. 5) — [diary text]

### YPRES. — Dec 7th

At 6.a.m. we opened up in "Crashes" on area W16d 30.15, W26c 10.20 to E1b 39.55 18 Rounds being expended. Our day task was again on Venison Farm and Nicholson Cross Roads. Starting at 10.a.m. we continued to fire at slow rate until 4.p.m. when we opened up in "Crashes" on same area as in the early morning shoot. Throughout the night harrassing fire was kept up on Nicholson Cross Roads, Sorby and Vulture Farms. 150 Rounds being fired during the night.

### YPRES. — 8th

From 5.30.a.m. to 5.45 a.m. crashes of fire were opened up on area W26a 00.70. W26a 50.10 W26c 30.40 and W26c 00.40. This was again repeated at 6.30.a.m.

### YPRES. — 9th

A special artillery attack opened up at 5.45 a.m. and lasted until 5.55.a.m. we fired 15 Rounds in this attack on area W26b 00.90 to W21c 00.20. and W26b 00.65 to W21f 00.00. At 6.30.a.m. this programme was repeated. Our task for the day was harrassing fire which was kept up at slow rate on Roads in W19c 70.25. 50 Rounds were fired during the night on Velvet Copse (V18d 05.25) and Vulture Farm (V24b 60.55) From 6.30.p.m. to 11 45.p.m we were shelled by a 4.1" H.V. Guns. The enemy firing in salvos of three about every 10 Minutes, no damage, however, was done to either personnel or material.

### YPRES. — 10th

Started taking down guns ready for removal. How No 230 went out of action during the night Hows No 482, 251 and 249 were pulled in to the morning and taken to Siege Park.

### YPRES. — 11th

Arrangements were made for 16 lorries to withdraw ammunition from position, but at 9.a.m. enemy opened up on the battery position and it was then impossible to get lorries up to position beyond St Jean. Hostile shelling was kept up until Noon, when ammunition was loaded and finally got away by 6.p.m. Hostile shelling was resumed almost immediately after last lorrie had left. The ammunition was taken to Dump and permission was given by Group to unload it there and to proceed without it.

### YPRES. — 12th

The morning was taken up by the men casting last votes for the election. Poll was completed by 2.p.m. and remainder of the day was occupied by packing ready for removal on the morrow.

## Page 6 (December 1917, p. 6) — [diary text]

### YPRES — Dec 13th

Left YPRES at 10. a.m. and proceeded to M.T. Supply Column. Party was completed here and we proceeded by lorries to Morbecque via Poperinghe and Hazebruick. We arrived at Hazebruick about Noon and found the town was being heavily shelled by Naval Guns, our route had to be diverted several lorries having very narrow escapes from being hit by falling masonry and pieces of shell but fortunately the danger zone was passed without any casualties. A halt was called for the night on main road outside Morbecque. Men sleeping in the lorries.

### [place not stated] — 14th

A start was made at 8.a.m. proceeding via Morbecque to Bethune where we spent the night, the men being billeted in the barracks and lorries parked in the town square.

### [place not stated] — 15th

Left Bethune at 8 a.m. and came back to Lillers, reaching that place about noon and were then sent on to the village of Ham-en-Artois where we went into Billets. The Guns were parked at Barlin under guard.

### [place not stated] — 20th

Men reviewed by General Currie at Billets in Ham-en-Artois. Two sections with Hows No 230 and 251 moved up to new position at Lieven.

### [place not stated] — 21st

Two guns were placed into position and got ready for firing.

### [place not stated] — 22nd

Two guns were ready for action early in the morning, their positions being M28a 47.58 and M28a 30.47.

### [place not stated] — 23rd

Took over position from 62nd Siege Battery and remaining sections with two guns arrived. We also took over two Hows No 162 and 7 in position at Angres (M33a) These two guns were to form a silent battery and only to fire in case of S.O.S. Forward position of Four guns to be active. 62nd S.B. left position at 6.p.m. and we took over their billets.

## Page 7 (December 1917, p. 7) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN — Dec 24th

Working to get Hows No 249 and 482 into Position.

### LIEVEN — 25th

All guns ready for action. Fired 1 shot from How No 251 for registration, Shell exploded in Barrel throwing Gun forward on to the earth box and splitting carriage. No one was hurt, but gun was completely out of action and later condemned by I.O.M. and sent back to base.

### LIEVEN — 26th

Plane called at 2.30.p.m. to observe for us on Hostile Battery (Z1) shoot was very successful, the pilot reporting 2 O.K's, 4 Y's, 7 Z's and 2 A's. 75 Rounds in all being fired. During this shoot the enemy ranged on us with woolly Bears by aeroplane observation and at 4.40.p.m. commenced a destructive shoot on our position with 5.9" H.E.. Shells were falling at the rate of 14 per minute and 250 Rounds in all were dropped into the Battery position. How No 230 was struck by a shell and put out of action. Ten Men were wounded in the cookhouse whilst lining up for supper, The shelling continued until 5.40.p.m. and then ceased and the casualties were then hurried to the dressing station, one man later returning to duty.

### LIEVEN — 27th

How No 230 was pulled out ready for removal. The I.O.M. instructed Barrel to be sent to Woolwich and carriage to Base. There was no shooting today and no hostile shelling, Lieut R.L. Smith proceeded to 11th Brigade to act as Liaison Officer.

### LIEVEN — 28th

Morning was very misty with occasional snow flurries and very bad visibility but cleared up for a short time in the afternoon. At 3.p.m. we fired 14 Rounds on a snipers post in a building near Fosse St Louis in M20c 30.90. This was observed by Lieut F.R. May and was successful. 2 O.K's being registered.

### LIEVEN — 29th

A shoot by aeroplane was started at 9.25 am on Hostile Battery MX 59 in M18b 80.50. This was a very difficult target, being partly concealed by clumps of trees. Visibility was very poor and shoot unsuccessful, 38 Rounds were fired and plane gave C.I. at 11.30. a.m. During this shoot we were ordered by group to concentrate on Trench Mortars in Railway Embankment in M34c. Plane was given H.Q. and 10 Rounds rapid fired at the T.M's after which we resumed shoot with plane. At 2.p.m. we fired 10 Rounds rapid in Cemetery at M33b, without observation and at 3.p.m. 10 Rounds on M33d 90.90 also without observation. How No 251 was removed to Railhead during the night Gas alarm was given at 12 Midnight and all precautions immediately taken but no gas came over position.

## Page 8 (December 1917, p. 8) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN — Dec 30th

Day was very quiet and nothing unusual occurred. Visibility was very bad in morning but cleared later in the day. A few snow flurries fell in the afternoon. There was no shooting done.

### LIEVEN — 31st

Started preparing two more positions at RED MILL in rear of present position. Work was carried on under ideal conditions, light mist and clouds, we were therefore not interfered by hostile aircraft. Day was very quiet and there was no shooting.

[Signed, bottom right:] H. M. Stairs, Lieut.
for O.C. 5th Can. Siege Battery

## Transcriber's notes — December 1917

- **Date gaps (days with no entry):** none — every day 1–31 December is covered, though 16–17 December are covered by "Men in Billets at Ham-en-Artois" and "do."
- **Entries spanning pages:** the 3 December entry runs from p. 3 onto p. 4 (the German prisoner story).
- **Printed diary-page numbers:** the printed page numbers at the head of the diary sheets do not run in sequence (the first two diary text pages are numbered 59 and 58); the entries nevertheless run continuously from page to page.
- **3 December entry:** Bdr E. J. Marshall (346903), guiding an ammunition party from Manners Junction, captured a lone German soldier hiding in an old pill-box; the prisoner had been wandering around the British lines for five days with very little food and said he had been afraid to give himself up to the Canadians because his officers had told him what to expect if taken prisoner — he was "very much surprised and relieved to find conditions so very different to what he had expected". He was marched back to the Corps Cage.
- **4 and 12 December entries:** the battery voted in the Canadian General Election (the 1917 federal election); Lieut. D. J. Maxwell was presiding officer on 4 December, and the poll was completed on 12 December. The diary records "Great enthusiasm prevailed amongst the men who were all anxious to poll their votes in favour of the Government."
- **5 December entry:** orders received from Group H.Q. that the 1st and 2nd Heavy Groups and all Canadian Batteries would shortly be pulling out of the line.
- **6 December entry:** a German raiding party bombed the back areas and rear billets in Ypres; one plane was brought down by A.A. guns and crashed between Ypres and Poperinghe, the pilot being killed.
- **10–13 December — pulling out of Ypres:** the guns were taken down on 10 December (Howitzers Nos. 482, 251 and 249 taken to Siege Park; Howitzer No. 230 went out of action during the night); ammunition was withdrawn by lorry on 11 December under shelling; the battery left Ypres by lorry on 13 December, travelling via Poperinghe and Hazebrouck (heavily shelled by naval guns) to Morbecque, then Bethune (14 December) and Lillers (15 December), going into billets at Ham-en-Artois with the guns parked at Barlin.
- **20 December entry:** the men were reviewed by General Currie at Ham-en-Artois.
- **23 December entry:** the battery took over the position at Liévin from the 62nd Siege Battery, including two howitzers (Nos. 162 and 7) in position at Angres (M33a) which were to form a silent battery, firing only in case of S.O.S.
- **25 December entry:** Christmas Day — a registration shot from Howitzer No. 251 exploded in the barrel, throwing the gun forward onto the earth box and splitting the carriage; no one was hurt, but the gun was condemned by the I.O.M. and sent back to base.
- **26 December entry:** the enemy ranged on the position with "woolly Bears" and then fired a destructive shoot of 250 rounds of 5.9" H.E. at 14 rounds a minute; Howitzer No. 230 was put out of action and ten men were wounded in the cookhouse while lining up for supper (one man later returned to duty). The ten men are not named in the diary.
- **29 December entry:** "Plane was given H.Q. and 10 Rounds rapid fired at the T.M's" — reproduced as written; the sense of "H.Q." here is unclear. Howitzer No. 251 was removed to Railhead during the night; a gas alarm at midnight proved false — no gas came over the position.
- **Casualties:** 26 December — ten men wounded in the cookhouse by shelling (unnamed; one later returned to duty). No deaths are recorded in December. No other casualties are recorded in the month.
- **People named:** Bdr E. J. Marshall (346903); Lieut. D. J. Maxwell (presiding officer for the election voting; reconnoitred the new position, 18 December); Lieut. F. R. May (reconnoitred the new position, 18 December; observed the sniper's post shoot, 28 December); Lieut. R. L. Smith (Liaison Officer to 11th Brigade, 27 December); General Currie (reviewed the men, 20 December); Lieut. H. M. Stairs (signed the diary for the O.C.).
- **Places:** Ypres, Poperinghe, Hazebrouck, Morbecque, Bethune, Lillers, Ham-en-Artois, Barlin, Liévin, Angres, Red Mill, Manners Junction, Venison Farm, Nicholson Cross Roads, McCulloch Farm, Sorby Farm, Vulture Farm, Velvet Copse, Fosse St Louis, St Jean.
- **Units mentioned:** 62nd Siege Battery (taken over from, 23 December); 1st and 2nd Heavy Groups; 11th Brigade; Group H.Q.
- **Equipment:** Howitzers Nos. 230, 249, 251, 482 (and Nos. 162 and 7 taken over at Angres). Howitzer No. 251 condemned after a barrel burst on 25 December; Howitzer No. 230 put out of action by a shell on 26 December, its barrel sent to Woolwich and carriage to base.
