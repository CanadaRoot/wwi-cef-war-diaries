## Page 3 (June 1918, p. 3) — [diary text]

WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. (Erase heading not required.) 5th CANADIAN SIEGE BATTERY.

### LIEVEN. — June 1st.

The carriage and cradle of Howitzer No 482 having been condemned, the gun was pulled out during the day and removed to Workshops. Enemy activity was active during the morning. Battery positions in and around Lieven being shelled for a short time by 10.5 cm Hows. During the afternoon a Destructive shoot of 60 Rounds with aeroplane observation on NX 46 was successfully carried out, obtaining 5 Y's, 9 Z's, 16 A's and 2 M.O.K's. A second shoot with aeroplane observation was started on OY 44 in O25b 60.64 but was discontinued after the 18th Round as target was too far away for our guns to do any effective shooting.

### [LIEVEN.] — June 2nd.

From Midnight Lieven and Battery positions were heavily shelled for half an hour with 10.5 c.m. and 15 c.m. [uncertain] hows. A considerable number of gas shells being mixed up with the H.E. Some scattered shelling of our positions at Cite Calonne also took place shortly after midnight, mostly by H.V. guns. A destructive shoot with aeroplane observation was started at 11 a.m. on H.B. NZ63 in N30b resulting in 2 Y's, 5 Z's, 7 A's and 2 M.O.K's. A fire was also caused at O.K. pin point. Airman reported fire for effect very good. During the night Harassing fire was put over on selected targets in N35b.

### [LIEVEN.] — 3rd

Our North Section position at Cite Calonne was lightly shelled by 10.5 cm Hows at 1 p.m. The shelling lasting about 40 minutes. Enemy activity was below normal during the day and nothing of unusual interest occurred. There was no shooting by this battery during the day.

### [LIEVEN.] — 4th

The day was unusually quiet and nothing of interest occurred. The evening and during the night was more active. At 10.30 p.m. the enemy put over harassing fire in and around positions in Lieven, mostly 15 cm shells. [uncertain] Harassing fire was also put over by the enemy from [uncertain] guns and was kept up intermittently throughout the night. One O.R. was admitted to Field Ambulance suffering from gas poisoning which brings our total casualties from the recent gas bombardment up to 39. There was no shooting by this battery during the day.

## Page 4 (June 1918, p. 4) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN. — June 5th

Lieven being heavily shelled by 15 cm hows and at 4.30.p.m. our North Section at Calonne was shelled by a 10 cm or 15 cm how. Information was received today that it had been decided to rearm this battery with a new equipment of Mark II Howitzers. There was no shooting by this battery during the day.

### [LIEVEN.] — 6th

During the early morning Lieven and Calonne were intermittently shelled by 10.5 cm Hows but no damage was caused to either material or personnel. Captain N.P. MacLeod and 16 Other ranks left for Le Havre to take charge of new equipment of Mark II Hows. Hostile activity was below normal during the day and nothing of unusual interest occurred.

### [LIEVEN.] — 7th

A burst of hostile fire was placed on Calonne at 2 a.m. but the shelling was not maintained and no damage was done to personnel or material. Nothing of interest occurred during the day. At 11 p.m. and at intervals during the night Lieven was shelled by 10.5 cm Hows.

### [LIEVEN.] — 8th

Hostile activity was much below normal and there was nothing of interest to report. At 4.30.p.m. we received an N F call in answer to which we fired 10 Rounds on N35b 9.4.

### [LIEVEN.] — 9th

Hostile shelling was normal during the day and only a slight shelling around the battery positions took place during the afternoon mostly by 5.9" Hows. Harassing fire was put over by us during the night on Railway Embankment in N35a 05.55.

### [LIEVEN.] — 10th

At 3.05 a.m. Lieven was subjected to some gas shelling but there was no pronounced bombardment the remainder of the day was quiet and nothing of interest occurred. There was no shooting by this battery.

### [LIEVEN.] — 11th

Hostile artillery quiet and nothing of interest to report.

### [LIEVEN.] — 12th

There was no shooting done today and enemy activity was comparatively quiet but considerably increased between 11 pm and 2 a.m. which appears to be the enemy's favorite time for harassing fire, which took place in short bursts. A few Blue Cross shells were also fired over with H.E's

## Page 5 (June 1918, p. 5) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN — June 13th.

Captain N.P. MacLeod and party returned to Battery today having drawn New equipment which was left at Workshop for Breech fittings and overhauling. A successful shoot was pulled off at 8 p.m. on enemy Headquarters at N R14 60.83 using both balloon and ground observation, 90 Rounds being fired. During the night Lieven was subjected to harassing fire by 10.5 cm Hows but not with any great intensity.

### [LIEVEN] — 14th

Hostile activity was practically nil. A demonstration of Aeroplane S.O.S. was carried out on on this front about 6.p.m. S.O.S. smoke bombs being dropped from the plane to demonstrate the procedure during a hostile attack.

### [LIEVEN] — 15th

Between 3 a.m. and 8 a.m. Hostile artillery showed very marked activity, Lieven coming in for a good deal of attention. Short bursts of fire were also put down during the afternoon. About 6.p.m. we put over 30 rounds on OY 46 The shoot being observed by No 10 Kite Balloon section who reported shooting good and [uncertain] persistent on the target.

### [LIEVEN] — 16th

Enemy activity was not marked nor at any time during the day very intense. Most of the shelling took place in the early morning and was principally directed against our battery position in Lieven. From 4 a.m. to 6 a.m. several parts of Lieven were persistently shelled by 10.5 cm and 15 cm Hows and intermittently afterwards up to 1.30.p.m. The afternoon was quiet until 6.p.m. when the shelling of Lieven, mostly near and around our No 1 gun, was resumed until 6.p.m. with a series of bursts. No casualties occurred altho 1 Dugout was blown in and some material damage caused. During the afternoon we fired 30 Rounds on NX66, corrections being given by 30 Sq R.F.C. the reported Battery was well onto target.

### [LIEVEN] — 17th

Nil

### [LIEVEN] — 18th

Nil

### [LIEVEN] — 19th

It had been decided that This Battery less two sections would relieve the 79th S.B. 16th Brigade R.G.A. at position at Neuville St Vaast, they taking over four guns now in position at Calonne.

## Page 6 (June 1918, p. 6) — [diary text]

### LIEVEN — June 19th

In preparation of this exchange we removed two platforms to new positions at [uncertain] How No 209 was also pulled out and removed to Lorrie park ready for shipment to the base. But No 230 was pulled out but on account of the caterpillar failing to turn up it was not possible to remove it as had previously been arranged.

### [LIEVEN] — 20th

During the evening two new Mk II Hows Nos 616 and 617 were received from Shops and placed in position at [uncertain] Neuville St Vaast. [uncertain] Mark II Guns Nos 285 and 286 The old Mark I pieces being removed to lorrie park for shipment to base. A shoot of 120 Rounds with aeroplane observation was successfully carried out during the day on NX 60, airman reported 2 Y's, 13 Z's, 21 A's and 2 O.K's position well shelled and a fire caused.

### [LIEVEN] — 21st.

Handing over of [uncertain] with H.Q. was completed during the evening and 2nd Brigade C.G.A. [uncertain] the line from [uncertain] relieved the 16th Brigade R.G.A. Fully O.P. was taken over by us at 7. P.M.

### [LIEVEN] — 22nd

The Headquarters of the Battery was removed to Neuville St Vaast today, 2 sections under Lieut E.S. Bushell at Lieven. [uncertain] the section was under the 2nd Brigade C.G.A. for administration and 67th Brigade R.G.A. for tactics. The 4 sections at Neuville St Vaast being under the control of 2nd Brigade. C.G.A.

### [LIEVEN] — 23rd.

From 3.10.p.m. to 4.30.p.m. we fired 30 Rounds at a Battery at [uncertain] 68.00 using ground observation. During the evening we also fired 10 Rounds on light line railway at [uncertain] 90.30 and corrections were given by Balloon. At intervals during the night harassing fire was put over on targets in N27a 70.70.

### [LIEVEN] — 24th

3 Other Ranks were removed to hospital today suffering from a new form of fever which had begun to spread in this district with alarming rapidity. The symptoms resembled Influenza combined with high temperature. Neutralizing fire was put over during the morning on UW 20 at U 21b 90.08 and UW 1 at U19d 0.5 70 Rounds were expended during the evening as harassing fire on selected targets in [uncertain]

## Page 7 (June 1918, p. 7) — [diary text]

### NEUVILLE ST VAAST. — June 25th

On account of the prevalence in the district of the new fever, which owing to its short duration has been termed "The three Days Fever" a special hospital was opened in the battery Position for the care of men affected. This was a large and commodious dugout fitted with almost every available comfort and placed under the charge of two Other ranks who had had previous experience in Hospital work with the C.A.M.C. This hospital has proved a great success and several men going sick with the fever were placed therein, kept warm and given lots of hot drinks, were able to resume their duties at the end of four days. During the day Lieven was subjected to very severe shelling from 10.5 cm Hows. From 1 p.m. to 3.40.p.m. we conducted a destructive shoot on NX 99 at [uncertain] 66.90 100 Rounds being fired with aeroplane observation. The shoot was satisfactory obtaining 2 M.O.Ks 1 Y, 2 Z's and 12 A's. The North Section at Lieven also carried out a destructive shoot with aeroplane observation on OY 60 obtaining 3 Y's, 4 Z's, 9 A's, and 2 MOK. No 1 Pit was also reported damaged. Harassing fire during the night consisted of 30 Rounds on Railway at [uncertain] 93.70.

### [NEUVILLE ST VAAST.] — 26th

Howitzers Mark II No. 305 and 304 were brought up and placed in position at [uncertain]. Hostile activity in front of south section position normal but North Section position in Lieven came in for a good deal of attention during the morning. Later on between 10 p.m. and midnight Lieven was again heavily shelled. A destructive shoot by aeroplane observation was successfully carried out on UW 85 at U9b 15.18 during the evening, 120 Rounds being fired, resulting in 1 Y, 4 Z's and 12 A's. A destructive shoot with aeroplane observation was also carried out by the North Section resulting in 7 Y's, 12 Z's and 17 A's.

### [NEUVILLE ST VAAST.] — 27th

The evening was very lively for our North Section position. Lieven and Battery position being heavily shelled by at least one [uncertain] battery. Neutralization fire was opened up in which a few rounds of gas shell were used and apparently proved effective. A shoot with aeroplane observation on OY 60 in O25a 60.30 was successfully carried out from 3.8.p.m. to 6 p.m. 120 Rounds being fired resulting in 1 Y, 3 Z's 7 A's and 1 MOK. A destructive shoot of 120 Rounds carried out by our North Section position with aeroplane observation resulted in 3 Y's, 9 Z's and 19 A's.

## Page 8 (June 1918, p. 8) — [diary text]

### NEUVILLE ST VAAST. — JUNE 28th

10.45 a.m. to 10.55 a.m. UW 1 at U19d 65.60 was neutralized. 10 Rounds being put over with satisfactory results. A shoot with aeroplane observation on UW 10 at U 16b 39.08 was started at 1 40.p.m. but had to be abandoned at 6.20.p.m. after 11 Rounds had been fired on account of clouds hindering the work of the airman. During the night 30 Rounds harassing fire was put over on O 22a 2.2.

### [NEUVILLE ST VAAST.] — 29th

Lieut C.A. Bennett joined Unit from 3rd R.A.C. to replace Lieut E.J. Maxwell M.C. on duty in England. At 9.40 p.m. North Section at Lieven was subjected to bursts of fire from 15 cm Hows. Enemy also shewed considerable activity in front of South Section position using a large number of gas shells but causing no casualties. A destructive shoot on OY 60 was successfully carried out by our North Section using aeroplane observation. 120 Rounds were fired obtaining 3 Y's, 10 Z's, and 6 A's. Airman reported shooting excellent throughout. 6 p.m. to 6.10 p.m. neutralizing fire was put over on UW 20 and at 8.10.p.m. we opened up on enemy movement in [uncertain] Firing 6 Rounds. 3 Rounds neutralizing fire was also put over on UW 20.

### [NEUVILLE ST VAAST.] — 30th.

Lieven was again shelled by 10.5 cm Hows at 2.40.a.m. During the evening, the remainder of Mk I and the non-interchangeable stores were loaded at Aubigny railhead and despatched to base 6.45 a.m. to 9.15 a.m. we fired 30 Rounds and successfully neutralized UW 60. 20 Rounds harassing fire was also put over during the night on [uncertain]

## Transcriber's notes — June 1918

- **Date gaps (days with no entry):** none — every day 1–30 June has its own entry, including "Nil" entries on 17 and 18 June.
- **Entries spanning pages:** the 5 June entry runs from p. 3 onto p. 4; the 19 June entry runs from p. 5 onto p. 6.
- **Legibility:** the June diary pages (pp. 3–8) are printed with a very heavy typewriter ribbon, so much of the text is over-inked and hard to read. The transcriptions of 4, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23 and 24 June are best-effort readings; map references and connective wording that could not be read are flagged [uncertain]. The 4 June entry in particular is largely obscured.
- **1 June entry:** Howitzer No. 482 had its carriage and cradle condemned and was pulled out to the workshops.
- **4 June entry:** one Other Rank was admitted to Field Ambulance suffering from gas poisoning; the diary says this brought the battery's total casualties from the recent (May) gas bombardment to 39 — the May volume's own monthly total was 35, so four further gas casualties (including this man) appear to have been counted after the May volume closed.
- **5 June entry:** it was decided to rearm the battery with new Mark II howitzers.
- **6 June entry:** Capt. N. P. MacLeod and 16 Other Ranks left for Le Havre to take charge of the new Mark II equipment; they returned on 13 June.
- **13 June entry:** "N R14 60.83" is reproduced as written (the map reference as typed is unclear). "A demonstration of Aeroplane S.O.S." (14 June) and "No 10 Kite Balloon section" (15 June) are reproduced as written.
- **15 June entry:** a word in "who reported shooting good and [uncertain] persistent on the target" could not be read.
- **16 June entry:** the shelling of Lieven "was resumed until 6.p.m." is reproduced as written. Corrections for the shoot on NX66 were given by 30 Sq. R.F.C.
- **19 June entry:** the battery (less two sections) was to relieve the 79th Siege Battery, 16th Brigade R.G.A., at Neuville St Vaast, taking over four guns at Calonne.
- **20 June entry:** two new Mark II howitzers (Nos. 616 and 617) were received; the old Mark I pieces were sent to the lorry park for shipment to base.
- **21 June entry:** the handing-over with Headquarters was completed and the 2nd Brigade C.G.A. relieved the 16th Brigade R.G.A.; the battery's O.P. was taken over at 7 p.m. Much of this entry is illegible and is flagged [uncertain].
- **22 June entry:** Battery Headquarters moved to Neuville St Vaast; two sections under Lieut. E. S. Bushell remained at Lieven, under 2nd Brigade C.G.A. for administration and 67th Brigade R.G.A. for tactics. A word in this entry could not be read and is flagged [uncertain].
- **24 June entry:** three Other Ranks were removed to hospital with a new fever spreading rapidly in the district — symptoms resembling influenza combined with high temperature. This is the "three Days Fever" of the 25 June entry.
- **25 June entry:** a special hospital was opened in a large dugout at the battery position for men with the "three Days Fever", run by two Other Ranks with previous C.A.M.C. hospital experience; men were kept warm, given hot drinks, and returned to duty after four days.
- **29 June entry:** Lieut. C. A. Bennett joined from 3rd R.A.C. to replace "Lieut E.J. Maxwell M.C." on duty in England — reproduced as written; the officer is named "D. J. Maxwell" in the February–April volumes.

Nil." (the June volume records no battle casualties, though men were hospitalised with the fever and one gas-poisoning case was admitted on 4 June).

One O.R. admitted to Field Ambulance with gas poisoning on 4 June (bringing the May gas-bombardment total to 39); three O.R.s hospitalised with fever on 24 June, with more cases following (the 25 June entry describes a special fever hospital in the battery position).
