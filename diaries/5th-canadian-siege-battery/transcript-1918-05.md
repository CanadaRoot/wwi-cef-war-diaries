## Page 3 (May 1918, p. 3) — [diary text]

WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY (Erase heading not required.) 5th CANADIAN SIEGE BATTERY

### May 1st

Nothing of unusual interest occurred during the day, Hostile shelling was practically nil, 20 Rounds were fired during the night as harassing fire on T.12a 8.8. and T 5b 7.9.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 2nd

Orders were received this morning for battery to move out into reserve with 2nd Brigade C.G.A. All guns were dismounted and placed into position ready for travelling. But orders were received at 9.15 p.m. to cancel moving orders and put guns back into position for firing. This was done immediately and all guns were ready for action by 2 a.m.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 3rd

Hostile artillery was below normal and nothing of unusual interest occurred during the day. Starting at 3.p.m. we conducted a destructive shoot by aeroplane observation on H.B. NZ 24 at N 35b 90.28. 256 rounds were fired with satisfactory results. During the night harassing fire was kept up on N 5d 6.9. and N 35c 9.8.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 4th

During the day two guns were registered on Houses in N 21c 91.02. 23 Rounds being fired and registration satisfactory.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 5th

Nil.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 6th

Day was very quiet and nothing of interest occurred, as a registration for Nos 1 and 2 Guns we fired 22 rounds on embankment at N 16d 80.00.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 7th

Nil.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 8th

No shooting was done by this battery today. Hostile shelling was above normal, a considerable amount of 5.9" H.E's and shrapnel being thrown into Battery positions in and around Lieven. No material damage was done and no casualties were reported amongst personnel.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

## Page 4 (May 1918, p. 4) — [diary text]

### May 9th

A shoot was carried out at 9.43 a.m. on H.B. NX 68 in N18c 20.99. 120 Rounds being fired with aeroplane observation, obtaining 6 Y's, 10 Z's 10 A's, explosion and fire on pin point of No 1 Gun pit, explosion in No 2 pit. Houses all around target much knocked about. Airman reported Battery shot well and shelled target thoroughly. During the evening a second aeroplane shoot was carried out on OY 45 in O25c 22.62. 120 Rounds being fired with satisfactory results. Hostile shelling during the day was normal.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 10th

Nil.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 11th

Hostile shelling normal and nothing of unusual interest occurred during the day. 10 Rounds neutralizing fire was put over on NZ 42 at N28b 82.57 and 5 Rounds on NZ 81 at N17a 06.72.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 12th

A shoot with aeroplane observation was carried out from 5.30.p.m. to 7.15.p.m. on OY 46 in N20b 4.6. 120 Rounds were fired with satisfactory results. A N.F. call was received from plane at 8.20.p.m. and 21 rounds fired on N30 B 4.6. During the night 30 rounds general neutralizing fire was put over on H.B's NZ 95 and NZ 24 in N36c 18.14 and N25b 90.24. Hostile shelling was normal and nothing of unusual interest occurred during the day.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 13th

At 5.42 a.m. we started a destructive shoot on H.B. NX 65 in N12c 88.67 by aeroplane observation 120 Rounds being fired with satisfactory results. At Noon we fired 9 rounds on Windmill in N11d 72.86 using ground observation to check line of No 1 gun, which was found O.K. During the afternoon we also fired 4 rounds on movement in N9d 60.80. Hostile shelling was normal and nothing of unusual interest occurred during the day.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 14th

18 rounds were fired at noon on IVY COTTAGE in T6d 30.15 for registration of No 2 gun, using both ground and balloon observation. Destructive shoot on Headquarters in N30b 40.70 with aeroplane observation was started at 2.04.p.m. 120 rounds were fired obtaining 1 Y, 8 Z's, 7 A's and 3 M.O.K's. Houses badly knocked about causing 2 large and 1 small explosion.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

## Page 5 (May 1918, p. 5) — [diary text]

### May 14th Contd.

A second shoot with aeroplane observation (MQNF) on H.B. NX66 in N16b 98.05 was carried out during the evening, 52 rounds being expended, resulting in 1Y, 5 Z's 5 A's and 1 MOK. Bridge near No 3 pit believed destroyed and one explosion caused.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 15th

Hostile shelling was at times above normal. Two destructive shoots being carried out by the enemy during which 3 O.R's were wounded. During the morning we fired 15 rounds on NX 28 in N5d 20.10 using balloon observation. A shoot with aeroplane observation on H.B. NX 66 in N12d 34.11 was carried out during the evening, 120 rounds being fired with satisfactory results.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 16th

During the day a 120 round shoot on H B. NX 50 with aeroplane observation resulted in 1 Z, 6 A's, 2 MOK's. airman reported position well shelled

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 17th

Destructive shoot with aeroplane observation on H.B. OY 42 in O25a 50.30 was commenced at 10.05a.m. 120 rounds were fired resulting in 5 Z's, 11 A's and 5 MOK's. One large explosion occurred in buildings at A12, a large explosion at A7, one in buildings at B3 and large explosion caused in No 2 pit, pit evidently destroyed. Shooting was excellent during fire for effect. Hostile shelling was very active during the night but no damage was done.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 18th

During the early hours of the day 20 Rounds harassing fire was put over in N39b 65.50. A 120 round shoot with aeroplane observation was carried out on NX 25 with following results:- 2 O.K's 11 Y's 11 Z's, Battery reranged obtaining 1 MOK. Nos 2 and 3 pits appeared utterly destroyed. No 1 pit probably destroyed. No 4 pit fell at edge of Carvin Road (B3) and caused several explosions and a fire, evidently ammunition dump.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 19th

Harassing fire was put over on N39a 65.50 during the early morning. A shoot of 120 rounds on NX66 at N12d 34.11 was carried out with aeroplane observation at 9.a.m. to noon. resulting in 3 Y's, 9 Z's, 8 A's and 2 MOK's. also causing small fire in Battery position. Hostile shelling of our battery positions and vincinity was at times very heavy.

## Page 6 (May 1918, p. 6) — [diary text]

### May 19th Contd.

During the afternoon 70 Rounds were fired on bridge at N21a 8.5 with satisfactory results. Harassing fire was put over during the night on T6b 2.5.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 20th

A shoot by aeroplane observation was started at 7.50 a.m. on H.B. NZ 60 in N36a 25.35. a second shoot by aeroplane observation was commenced at 10.55 a.m. on NZ59 in N29d 53.35 resulting in 1Y, 5Z's 8 A's and 6 MOK's. Several small explosions in general position, which was well shelled. Hostile battery appeared to have two forward and two rear pits, the latter being doubtful. During the night harassing fire was put over on N29d 6.1 and N36b 5.2. Hostile artillery more active in back areas and vincinity of battery position shelled with H.E's and woolley Bes.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 21st.

Nil.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 22nd.

From 11.19 to 11.20 a.m. 10 Rounds were fired on OY 45 in O25c 2.7. as neutralizing fire. At 11.42 a.m. a shoot by aeroplane observation was started on OW 46 in O7c 40.20. 200 rounds were fired obtaining 6 Y's 9 Z's 13 A's (includes ranging) and 4 MOK's.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 23rd

From 9.03 a.m. to 11.10 a.m. a shoot of 120 rounds on H.B. OY 44 in O25b 62.60 by aeroplane observation was successfully carried out. During the night 30 Rounds were fired at intervals as harassing fire on N29d 18.98.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 24th

Nil.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 25th

From 11.15 to 1 p.m. we carried out a shoot with aeroplane observation on NZ 63 in N30b 77.66 obtaining 4 Y's, 7 Z's, 8 A's, and 2 MOK's. Two large explosions were caused in houses near target. Hostile artillery was active during the night harassing roads and approaches thru Lieven.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

## Page 7 (May 1918, p. 7) — [diary text]

### May 26th

At 4.35 p.m. 10 Rounds neutralizing fire was put over on H.B. OY 40 in P25a 16.68 and at 5.28p.m. we started an aeroplane shoot on NX 92 at N18a 96.90 obtaining 3 Y's, 9 Z's, 15 A's and 2 MOK's also causing a small explosion and fire.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 27th

From 1.57 a.m. to 2.57 a.m. Battery position and vincinity were subjected to heavy Gas bombardment of Yellow and Blue cross gas from 15 cm and 10 cm Howitzers and 77 mm guns. The gas shells were freely mixed with 5.9" H.E's. On the first alarm being given gas curtains in every dugout were lowered and S.B.R's adjusted. In consequence of the smart action and precautionary measures taken only two casualties were reported, these being two signallers who were working on the signal lines and continued with their work throughout the bombardment. over 1000 shells were fired in a hour. All gas shell holes were treated with chloride of lime and filled in with earth and every endeavour made to clear out the gas from around battery position. A 120 round shoot on NX37 in N6d 50.41 with aeroplane observation was successfully carried out during the morning.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 28th

Hostile shelling was fairly heavy during the early part of the day, mostly 5.9" H.E's with an occasional burst of 4.1 H.V. Gun Shells. A shoot with aeroplane observation on NX65 in N12c 88.67 was successfully carried out. 120 Rounds being fired. 27 men were reported today as gas casualties. These casualties were caused by the blast of the guns stirring up dust which had been contaminated with mustard gas during the recent bombardment Everything possible was done to eliminate the possibility of further casualties through the same causes, Bottles of Bi-carbonate of soda were placed in each dugout for bathing the eyes of men who were believed to have passed through the affected areas, and the greatest care was exercised by all ranks on account of the persistency of the gas.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [May] 29th

During the night the 2nd Brigade, C.G.A. went out into reserve and this Battery together with 4th C.S.B. were taken over by 67th Brigade. R.G.A.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

## Page 8 (May 1918, p. 8) — [diary text]

### MAY 29th

From Midnight to 5 a.m. we fired 70 Rounds on Fosse 4 in N29d 7.5. as harassing fire. Hostile shelling was kept up intermittently all day, enemy using 5.9" Hows and a 4.1 H.V. Gun. No material damage was done and only one man wounded. No 339545 Gnr Swenson A being hit in chest with piece of shell.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [MAY] 30th

Nothing of unusual interest occurred during the day, and hostile shelling was normal.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [MAY] 31st.

A shoot with aeroplane observation on OY40 in O25a 16.68 was successfully carried out during the morning, 120 Rounds being fired. Harassing fire was also put over on Cross Roads at N29b 90.52.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

Total Rounds fired during Month. == 3387.

Total Casualties for Month. == 35.

S. T. Layton, Maj.
O.C. 5th Can. Siege Bty.

## Transcriber's notes — May 1918

- **Date gaps (days with no entry):** none — every day 1–31 May has its own entry, including "Nil" entries on 5, 7, 10, 21 and 24 May. The Place column is blank throughout May (no place is given); the battery remained at Lieven.
- **Entries spanning pages:** the 14 May entry runs from p. 4 onto p. 5; the 19 May entry runs from p. 5 onto p. 6; the 29 May entry runs from p. 7 onto p. 8.
- **Remarks column:** every entry bears the same repeated handwritten mark in the Remarks column. It is not legible enough to read with confidence and is flagged [uncertain] throughout; it appears to read "S.T.L." — possibly the initials of the O.C., Major S. T. Layton.
- **Form change:** the May volume is printed on a different form stock from January–April: the foot imprint reads "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12." (the earlier volumes read "A6945 Wt. W11423/M1160 350,000 12/16 D. D. & L. Forms/C.2118/14."). The diary text is also typed in a smaller typeface.
- **2 May entry:** the battery was ordered to move out into reserve with 2nd Brigade C.G.A., dismounted all guns for travelling, then had the order cancelled at 9.15 p.m. and put the guns back into action by 2 a.m.
- **14 May entry:** "(MQNF)" is reproduced as written. A word before "Bridge near No 3 pit believed destroyed" is struck through in the typescript.
- **15 May entry:** a map reference before "N5d 20.10" is struck through in the typescript. "3 O.R's were wounded" — the three men are not named.
- **19 May entry:** the last line on p. 5 ("During the afternoon 70 Rounds were fired on bridge at N21a 8.5 with satisfactory results. Harassing fire was put over during the night on T6b 2.5.") is struck through in the typescript, and the same sentences are retyped at the head of p. 6 as the continuation of the 19 May entry; they are transcribed once, from p. 6.
- **27 May entry:** heavy gas bombardment (yellow and blue cross gas, over 1,000 shells in an hour) from 1.57 to 2.57 a.m.; only two casualties at the time, both signallers working on the signal lines. "over 1000 shells were fired in a hour" is reproduced as written. "S.B.R's" (Small Box Respirators) is reproduced as written.
- **28 May entry:** 27 men reported as gas casualties — caused by gun blast stirring up mustard-gas-contaminated dust from the previous night's bombardment. "Bi-carbonate of soda" is reproduced as written.
- **29 May entry:** the battery (with 4th C.S.B.) was taken over by 67th Brigade, R.G.A., as 2nd Brigade, C.G.A. went into reserve. Gnr. Swenson A (No. 339545) was wounded in the chest by a shell fragment.
- **Monthly totals:** the diary itself records "Total Rounds fired during Month. == 3387." and "Total Casualties for Month. == 35." The casualties itemised in the daily entries sum to 33 (3 wounded on 15 May, 2 gas casualties on 27 May, 27 gas casualties on 28 May, 1 wounded on 29 May) — two casualties counted in the monthly total are not itemised in the text.
- **Signature:** the volume closes with the handwritten signature "S. T. Layton, Maj. O.C. 5th Can. Siege Bty."
- **Casualties:** 15 May — 3 Other Ranks wounded (unnamed) in enemy destructive shoots; 27 May — 2 signallers (unnamed) gassed; 28 May — 27 men (unnamed) gas casualties from mustard-gas-contaminated dust; 29 May — No. 339545 Gnr. A. Swenson wounded in the chest by a shell fragment. Monthly total as recorded: 35.
- **People named:** Major S. T. Layton (O.C.); Gnr. A. Swenson (No. 339545).
- **Places:** Lieven (battery position), Ivy Cottage, Carvin Road, Fosse 4.
