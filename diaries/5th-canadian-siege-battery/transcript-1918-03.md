# War Diary Transcript — 5th Canadian Siege Battery, March 1918

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Siege Battery, Canadian Garrison Artillery (originally 165th Canadian Siege Battery)
- **LAC Item ID:** 2004798, RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4976, file 572, reel T-10804, finding aid 9-52
- **Month / Volume:** March 1918, Volume 19 (covers 1 – 31 March 1918)
- **Pages in this file:** LAC images pp. 107–113 (7 pages: cover, contents, 5 diary text pages, numbered 71–75 in the diary's own pagination)
- **Diary text date range:** 1 – 31 March 1918
- **Commanding officer / signatory:** Major T. W. F. MacDonald until 29 March, when he was severely wounded; Capt. N. P. MacLeod arrived the same day and took up duties as O.C. The volume is signed "D. J. Maxwell Lieut. for O.C. 5th Can. Siege Batty."

The "Hour" column is blank throughout. The Remarks column bears the same repeated handwritten mark on every entry (see Notes).

## Page 3 (March 1918, p. 3) — [diary text]

WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. (Erase heading not required.) 5th CANADIAN SIEGE BATTERY.

### LIEVEN — March 1st.

Visibility was poor throughout the day, Low clouds, mist and occassional snow flurries prevented any work being done.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN] — 2nd

Day was fairly quiet and nothing unusual occurred until evening. At 5.30. p.m. Enemy put down heavy barrage of all calibres on our trenches in N 13c. At 5.35 p.m. in reply to two split green flares Hostile barrage increased and range lengthened taking in Battery position and also batteries in our rear. Effective retaliation was carried out in reply to this bombardment, during which we fired 31 Rounds on various targets. All was quiet by 6 P.M. and remained comparatively quiet for the remainder of the night.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN] — 3rd

Visibility was poor during the early morning. Howitzer No 279 was removed from Siege Park and placed in position in Cite Calonne at M14d 15.59. Bursts of fire were opened up from 1.07 to 1.12.p.m. 5 Round being fired on N Z 67 in N18c 51.65. This was repeated at 1.50.p.m. on N Z 37 in N24a 14.50.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN] — 4th

At 5.55.a.m. enemy opened up very heavy barrage in support of hostile raid, during which Battery position and vincinity was heavily shelled. Despite heavy shelling all ranks answered smartly to S.O.S. and guns were in action by 6.a.m. The S.O.S. fire was kept up continously until 7.27.a.m. during which we fired 131 Rounds. Only one casualty occurred in the Battery. No 1250127 Gunner Kelsall J. being struck by a splinter and instantly killed. The enemy raid was unsuccessful two prisoners being left in our hands. 7.32 a.m. to 7.37 a.m. we fired 5 Rounds on H.B. NZ 37 in N24a 14.50. without observation.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [LIEVEN] — 5th

Gunner Kelsall was buried this morning at Cabaret Rouge. A destructive shoot by aeroplane observation was successfully carried out during the afternoon on H.B. NZ 19, 28 Rounds were fired ranging during which we obtained 2 Y's, 7 Z's, 8 A's, and 5 B's. During fire for effect a small explosion occurred in buildings at E5 from target, and a second explosion occurred near pin point of target. General position of hostile Battery only visible and pilot was greatly hampered in his work by low clouds.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

## Page 4 (March 1918, p. 4) — [diary text]

### Lieven — March 6th

Shoot was successful, 75 rounds being expended. Hostile shelling in vincinity of battery position Nil.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 7th

Nothing of unusual interest occurred today. Visibility was poor in early morning owing to heavy ground mists. 5 Rounds were fired at 9.35 p.m. on H.B. NZ 95 in N29a 20.85. Neutralizing fire.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 8th

Visibility was fairly good today and day was exceptionally quiet. No shooting was done by this battery and hostile shelling was Nil.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 9th

From 8.07.a.m. to 8.12 a.m. 5 Rounds were fired to neutralize H.B. NZ 95 in N29a 20.85. From 2.30.p.m. to 4.34 p.m. 75 Rounds were fired in a destructive shoot on H.B. NZ 37 in N24a 14.50. by aeroplane observation, shoot was successful. 5 Rounds neutralizing fire was put over at 5.30.p.m. in N30a 50.83. During the evening Harrassing fire was carried out in N30a 50.53 and on road Junction in N18c 20.91. Visibility was very good during the day and hostile shelling normal, being confined mostly to Woolley Bears.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 10th

A few hostile shells, mostly 5.9's fell in the battery position but no damage was done either to material or personnel. No shooting was done by this battery. Hostile shelling increased during the evening in and near position.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 11th

Visibility good. At 12.26 p.m. a shoot by aeroplane observation was opened on H.B. NX 41 in N16b 85.05. 55 Rounds being expended, after ranging shoot had to be stopped on account of hostile shelling which became very heavy about 1 p.m., shoot was eventually finished at 6.p.m. Hostile shelling was again resumed during the evening and lasted about 1½ hours, principally around No 2 Gun but no damage was caused.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 12th

A successful shoot was carried out by aeroplane observation at 2.10.p.m. on H.B. NZ 35 in N22b 79.83 during which we obtained 1.O.K. and 2 M.O.K's. Visibility was fairly good. Hostile shelling in vincinity of battery position was heavy between 1 p.m. and 2.30.p.m. and slacked off during the afternoon to an occasional shell and a few Woolley bears.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

## Page 5 (March 1918, p. 5) — [diary text]

### Lieven. — March 13th

Nil

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven.] — 14th

From 10.45 a.m. to 1.08 p.m. we carried out a destructive shoot on H.B. NX 56 in N17a 07.71 by aeroplane observation which was successful. Enemy attempted twice to register on Battery position principally at No 2 Gun. Hostile shelling was at times exceptionally heavy but no damage was done to either personnel or material.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven.] — 15th

From 5.34 a.m. to 6.35 a.m. 35 Rounds were fired on Target U W 1 in U1a 57.93. Visibility was fair and nothing of unusual interest occurred during the day. Hostile shelling Nil.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven.] — 16th

A successful shoot by aeroplane observation was carried out at 10 a.m. on O Y 36 in O25c 22.62 during which 2 O.K's were obtained. Hostile artillery was active during the day and position was shelled during the afternoon with 5.9's. 8.5 p.m. to 8.30 p.m. 15 Rounds were fired in response to an S.O.S. call.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven.] — 17th

From 9.5 a.m. to 10.35 a.m. 60 Rounds were fired on Purdy Bridge by ground observation, and during the afternoon 15 Rounds were fired on Puits 2 and 13 Rounds on South Fosse Cite du Grande Conde in N9d 02.05. Both these shoots were observed by Lieut D.J. Maxwell F.O.O. Hostile shelling today was below normal.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven.] — 18th

9.21 a.m. to 11.95 a.m. 3 Rounds neutralizing fire was put over on H.B. NX67 in N18c 52.62. Hostile shelling was heavy during the afternoon, No 2 Gun coming in for a good deal of attention Main roads through Lieven were continually Harrassed with 5.9's, Woolley bears and a 4.1" Gun. No damage was caused to battery stores or personnel.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven.] — 19th

Nil.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven.] — 20th

NIL.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven.] — 21st

S.O.S. call was received at 5.52 a.m. in response to which 35 Rounds were expended on S.O.S. lines. During the afternoon 110 Rounds were fired on NX 68 in N17d 33.04 and 20 Rounds fired into Enemy Headquarters in N3d 70.90. Day was quiet and hostile shelling below normal.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

## Page 6 (March 1918, p. 6) — [diary text]

### Lieven — March 22nd

Nil

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 23rd

Visibility good, during the day we fired 20 Rounds on H.B. NZ 9 in N24a 45.86 and 10 Rounds on H.B. NZ 85 in N22b 80.93. Hostile shelling was below normal and nothing of unusual interest occurred.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 24th

From 4.05 a.m. to 4.15 a.m. 20 Rounds were fired on H.B. NX13 in N23c 75.68. This was repeated at 5.0a.m. 15 Rounds were expended at 5.50.a.m. in response to an S.O.S. call. From 11.02 a.m. to 1.14 p.m. 105 Rounds were fired with good results on H.B. NX 69 in N17d 33.04. 15 Rounds neutralizing fire was fired from 11.45 p.m. to 12 midnight on NX 39 in N11c 77.58. Hostile shelling during the day was practically nil.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 25th

No shooting was done by this battery during the day. Hostile shelling heavy in the afternoon. in and around positions in Lieven.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 26th

NIL.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 27th

Nil.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 28th

15 Rounds were fired during the afternoon on TX 30 and 22 in T 6a. Hostile shelling normal near battery position but a great deal of shelling of back areas by H.V. Gun.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 29th

Orders were received today that our North Section at Cite Calonne would be relieved by 10th S.B. and that we would take over their guns now at rest. A new position was to be occupied near Neuville St Vaast. Major T.W.F. MacDonald and Lieut D.J. Maxwell proceeded to New Position to look over position for guns. Road was under shell Fire by H.V. Gun. One shell fell near the Car killing driver and severely wounding Major MacDonald. Capt N.P. MacLeod arrived at Battery today and took up duties as O.C.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

### [Lieven] — 30th

Battery position and cross Roads were heavily shelled during the day but no damage was done. Guns No 208, 212 & 230. were taken over from 10th Siege Battery our own guns being taken over by them.

[Remarks: [uncertain]]

## Page 7 (March 1918, p. 7) — [diary text]

### Lieven. — March 31st.

Four sub sections were removed to new positions at Neuville St Vaast. Two guns and personnel together with Battery Headquarters remaining at Lieven. During the afternoon a destructive shoot by aeroplane observation was successfully carried out, 75 Rounds being expended.

D. J. Maxwell Lieut.
for O.C. 5th Can. Siege Batty.

## Transcriber's notes — March 1918

- **Date gaps (days with no entry):** none — every day 1–31 March has its own entry, including "Nil" entries on 13, 19, 20, 22, 26 and 27 March.
- **Entries spanning pages:** the 6 March entry runs from p. 3 onto p. 4.
- **Remarks column:** every entry bears the same repeated handwritten mark in the Remarks column. It is not legible enough to read with confidence and is flagged [uncertain] throughout; it appears to be a pair of initials with a flourish. (The January volume's Remarks mark was read as "Lom".)
- **4 March entry:** the battery's first recorded casualty of 1918 — No. 1250127 Gunner Kelsall J., struck by a splinter and instantly killed during the enemy barrage and raid in support of which the battery fired 131 rounds on S.O.S. lines. The enemy raid was unsuccessful, with two prisoners left in British hands.
- **5 March entry:** Gunner Kelsall was buried at Cabaret Rouge. "pin point of target" is reproduced as written.
- **6 March entry:** "shoot was succ" at the foot of p. 3 continues as "Shoot was successful, 75 rounds being expended" on p. 4.
- **17 March entry:** "South Fosse Cite du Grande Conde in N9d 02.05" is reproduced as written. Lieut. D. J. Maxwell observed both shoots as F.O.O. (Forward Observation Officer).
- **18 March entry:** the time "11.95 a.m." is reproduced as written; it appears to be a typographical error in the original.
- **21 March entry:** the German spring offensive (Operation Michael) opened on this date; the diary records an S.O.S. call at 5.52 a.m. and heavy firing (110 rounds on NX 68, 20 rounds into enemy headquarters), but otherwise describes the day as quiet.
- **29 March entry:** Major T. W. F. MacDonald was severely wounded and the car's driver killed (unnamed) by a high-velocity gun shell while reconnoitring the new position near Neuville St Vaast; Capt. N. P. MacLeod arrived the same day and took over as O.C. "H.V. Gun" is reproduced as written.
- **30 March entry:** "Guns No 208, 212 & 230." is reproduced as written; the battery took these guns over from 10th Siege Battery in exchange for its own.
- **31 March entry:** four sub-sections moved to new positions at Neuville St Vaast; two guns, personnel and Battery Headquarters remained at Lieven. No target is given for the 75-round destructive shoot that afternoon.
- **Signature:** the volume closes with the handwritten signature "D. J. Maxwell Lieut. for O.C. 5th Can. Siege Batty."
- **Casualties:** 4 March — Gunner J. Kelsall (No. 1250127) killed instantly by a shell splinter; 29 March — the car driver (unnamed) killed and Major T. W. F. MacDonald severely wounded by an H.V. gun shell near Neuville St Vaast.
- **People named:** Gunner J. Kelsall (No. 1250127); Major T. W. F. MacDonald; Lieut. D. J. Maxwell; Capt. N. P. MacLeod.
- **Places:** Lieven (battery position and headquarters), Cité Calonne (North Section), Cabaret Rouge (burial ground), Neuville St Vaast (new position), Purdy Bridge, Puits 2, South Fosse Cité du Grande Conde.
- **No appendices** are listed or included with this volume; the contents page lists only "Diary Text — 5 Pages".
