## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", typed in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" line is struck through on each page, with "(Erase heading not required.)" beneath it.
- The Hour column is blank throughout this volume; on pages 4–6 the "Hour" heading itself is crossed out.
- The Place column is blank from page 4 onwards (the battalion remains at Witley; the place is not repeated).
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.
- "do." does not occur in this volume.

## Page 2 — Diary text, 19 January–7 February 1917 (January 1917, p. 2)

Army Form C. 2118. Heading: "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. 185th Canadian Infantry Battalion." Handwritten "ORIGINAL" at top right. ### 19 January 1917 — Witley

> On the 19th day of January, 1917, the 12th (Nova Scotia Highlanders) Training Brigade becomes the 15th Canadian Infantry Brigade. This battalion (185th Cape Breton Highlanders Battalion) as one of its units is now designated the 185th Canadian Infantry Battalion.

### 21 January 1917 — Witley

> Ten officers are transferred to-day from the 193rd Battalion.

### 22 January 1917 to 29 January 1917 — Witley

> During this period no events of importance have obtained. The battalion is training hard by companies. The Specialists—Machine Gun Section, Scouts & Snipers, Bombers and Signallers—are all devoting their energies in their own branches of training.

### 29 January 1917 — Witley

> Lieut Col Day returns from France and resumes command.

### 28 January 1917 — Witley

> Two more officers are transferred from the 193rd Battalion. (Major Legallais & Capt. Baird)

Note: the 29 January entry appears above the 28 January entry in the Date column, out of chronological order.

### 30 January 1917 — Witley

> The number of cases of mumps has increased to seventy-one. All in staff-sergeants hut were quarantined to-day.

Signed at foot of page: F. P. Day, Lieut. Col., Comdg. 185th Bn., C. E. F. [Cape Breton Highlanders].

### 5 February 1917 — Witley

> A change in the system of training commences today. Now we are having battalion schools for the companies to attend. The seven schools (under the supervision of our own officers and N.C.O's) are as follows:- Physical Training & Bayonet Fighting: Engineering; Bombing: Drill; Musketry; Gas; and Company School (including Route marches and night work).

### 7 February 1917 — Witley

> The afternoon has been devoted to sports. Contests in all the branches of the first six mentioned schools have been held. "D" Company won by points.

## Page 3 — Diary text, 10–20 February 1917 (January 1917, p. 3)

Army Form C. 2118. Heading as on page 2. The Remarks column carries the same repeated handwritten initials, appearing to read "R.E.D." [uncertain].

### 10 February 1917 — Witley

> The 85th Battalion (the only other existing unit of the Nova Scotia Highland Brigade) left at a very early hour this morning, for France. A close relationship has ever existed between the two battalions. They assisted us in our organization and furnished us with many officers and non-commissioned officers. They were in camp with us for about five months and were well trained. Their band which has gained a good reputation, went with them.
>
> This evening a Scotch concert was held in the Men's Mess Room. Songs in the Gaelic tongue were enjoyed and understood by no small portion of the audience. (It may be noted that the 185th Battalion departed from Canada with more Gaelic speaking men than any other unit of the Canadian Expeditionary Forces.) Scotch Plaids were also accepted with applause.

### 11 February 1917 — Witley

> Owing to a temporary shortage of coal, electrical power is very weak and tonight we are burning candles. Number under quarantine for mumps 341.

### 12 February 1917 — Witley

No entry is written against this date.

### 13 February 1917 — Witley

> Commencing to-day the following is the organization of the 15th Canadian Infantry Brigade:—
>
> 104th Battalion, Can. Inf.
> 119th Battalion, Can. Inf.
> 185th Battalion, Can. Inf.
> 199th Battalion, Can. Inf.
> 19th Machine Gun Company.

### 14 February 1917 — Witley

> To-day we had an inspection by General Howard. About 2 a.m. the power house (which furnished all the electricity for the camp) burned down. Now we are issued with lamps and oil.

### 15 February 1917 — Witley

> Three additional officers (Lieuts Christie, Cunningham, & Lowther) taken on strength from 17th Bn.

### 18 February 1917 (Sunday) — Witley

> Number under quarantine— — 18. [uncertain]

### 19 February 1917 — Witley

> Commencing to-day the training for the next fortnight, at least, will be by platoons. Section and Platoon leaders will be trained in regard to their individual responsibility and in Section drill. Bombing and first aid training are both being carried on. One chap commenting upon the days work remarked that it was rather funny "showing a fellow how to blow a man to pieces in the forenoon, and then in the afternoon showing how to patch him up."

### 20 February 1917 — Witley

> All of the Battalion available (about 300, owing to quarantine, etc.) are in the hands of the Senior Officers' School Aldershot.
