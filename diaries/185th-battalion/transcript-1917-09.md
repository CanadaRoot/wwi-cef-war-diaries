## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", typed in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | (Weather report) | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "Hour" column is struck through on the form and a "(Weather report)" column is used instead; no hour is recorded for individual entries.
- The Remarks column carries repeated handwritten initials on each row (illegible); they are not transcribed.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written.
- "do." in the Place column means "ditto" (same place as the entry above).

## Page 58 — Diary text, 1–13 September 1917 (September 1917, p. 2)

Army Form C. 2118. Heading: "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY of the 185th Canadian Infantry Battalion. (Cape Breton Highlanders) Volume VII". Orderly Room stamp dated OCT 2 1917.

### 1 September 1917 — WITLEY

> Four hours individual training. Half Holiday.

Weather: Showery.

### 2 September 1917 — WITLEY

> Church Parade at Witley Park in morning. "A", "B", and "C" Companies inoculated in the afternoon.

Weather: Fine.

### 3 September 1917 — WITLEY

> "D" Company inoculated. No parades on account of 48 hours exemption from duty, following inoculation. Musketry Party of third class shots returned from MYCHETT.

Weather: Fine.

### 4 September 1917 — WITLEY

> No parades on account of inoculation.

Weather: Fine.

### 5 September 1917 — WITLEY

> Morning: 4 hours individual training. In the afternoon no parades were held. Battalion paraded at 10 p.m. when it was to occupy THURSLEY Sector 15th Brigade Trenches. On account of the rain (and the recent inoculation) occupation of trenches was postponed until morning of 6/9/17.

Weather: Rain.

Remarks: See Appendix "A" I.

### 6 September 1917 — WITLEY

> THURSLEY Sector of trenches were occupied at 8.45 a.m. in accordance with instructions issued for the 6th instant.

Weather: Showery.

### 7 September 1917 — WITLEY

> The battalion remained in trenches until 8 a.m. Individual training was carried on for four hours in the afternoon.

Weather: Rain.

### 8 September 1917 — WITLEY

> Four hours individual training. Half-holiday.

Weather: Fine.

### 9 September 1917 — WITLEY

> On account of the inclemency of the weather, church parade was held in Men's Mess Room at 10 a.m.

Weather: Dark and misty.

### 10 September 1917 — WITLEY

No summary recorded.

Weather: Fine.

### 11 September 1917 — WITLEY

> Battalion Scheme.

Weather: Fine — thunder showers during evening.

Remarks: See "A" Appendix.

### 12 September 1917 — WITLEY

No summary recorded.

Weather: Fine.

### 13 September 1917 — WITLEY

> Morning devoted to cleaning equipment. Divisional Half-holiday.

Weather: Dark with showers.

## Page 64 — Appendix "B" to Volume VII (September 1917, p. 8)

> Appendix "B" to Volume VII
>
> 185th Canadian Infantry Battalion War Diary. September, 1917.
>
> WITLEY 17/9/17. Battalion marched to Mytchett Canadian Musketry Camp, near Farnborough and Aldershot, and went into hutments there, for five days. The Battalion passed starting point at 8 a.m., having dinner at 11 a.m. and arriving at MYTCHETT at 2.45 p.m.
>
> Fine.
>
> MYTCHETT 18/9/17. 600 men fired 5 rounds application and 5 rounds rapid; firing at 200 yards to get accustomed to the actual training which commenced with an individual field practice in Box Respirators. 338 hits were made giving an average of .5 hits per man for 10 rounds rapid in 45 seconds. This was considered very fair.
>
> Fine.
>
> 19/9/17. On account of the weather, the battalion did not shoot until the afternoon. Night firing, which had been on the schedule, was cancelled. Collective field practices were in battle order. Collective practice of sections of 1 N.C.O. and 16 men advancing from 200 to 200 yards, engaging six dummy enemy scouts which were moved from cover across 30 yards of exposed ground to a point of observation where they got cover again. Scouts then appeared for 4 seconds, four times and two crouching attempts to move back to their starting point with information were made. When hit they fell into the butts. In spite of the mist which prevailed, the scouts seldom got back. 656 men shot, scoring 620 hits. Average .957.
>
> Wet and Misty.
>
> 20/9/17. The practice held was platoon in attack. 1 officer and 32 men shot and the hitting average was 1.412. Sections started off from 500 yards behind cover, and advanced to the assault which was delivered against sandbags. Targets appeared five times during advance and were exposed 10 – 15 seconds each time. This was considered the best practice fired and of most value to all ranks. Night firing commenced at 7.30 p.m. Very lights were used and details of 50 men fired at figure targets of various sizes from 200 yards point. 373 men fired, making 641 hits. Average 1.111.
>
> Fine.
>
> 21/9/17. On Friday morning the battalion proceeded to WITLEY CAMP stopping at No 4 range (the range used for all practices) to fire "Scouts in Motion" practice over again. On account of the absence of mist the average was greatly improved. After "C" and "D" Company had completed their firing and were at the hot water boilers, cleaning rifles, No 282592 Pte A. Robichaud had his left foot shot through by a bullet from his own rifle; the bullet entered and shattered the ankle bone on the inside of the foot and came out through the centre of the sole of the foot. A Court of Enquiry, appointed to investigate the cause of the accident arrived at the decision, that, as the rifle had been examined by the platoon sergeant of the injured man, after firing had been completed, the accident was due to the carelessness of the injured man, himself. Battalion had dinner and proceeded to WITLEY CAMP arriving at 4.30 p.m.
>
> Fine.
