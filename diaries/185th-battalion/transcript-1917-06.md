## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", typed in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- The "Hour" column is struck through on these forms and carries no entries; no hour is recorded for the daily entries. The weather is recorded at the end of each day's summary ("Fine.", "Rainy.", etc.), under the heading "(weather report)".
- The Remarks column on each diary-text page carries the same handwritten initials against every entry; the initials could not be read with confidence and are not transcribed per entry (see Uncertain items).
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written. The appendices use Aldershot map sheet references (e.g. H2c7.6) as written.
- "do." in the Place column means "ditto" (same place as the entry above).

## Page 8 — Bombing Instructions (June 1917, p. 8)

> BOMBING INSTRUCTIONS.
>
> Supplies and Equipment – Other Ranks. Each Other Rank, except as noted below, will carry two Mills Bombs [uncertain] one in each breast pocket. These are not to be used except with the permission of an Officer, and will be collected in the objectives or from forward grenade dumps.
>
> Bombing Sections. Each Rifleman will carry: Rifle and Bayonet; [uncertain]; 40 rds. S.A.A. The thrower and the two carriers will each carry 2 Mills and 1 smoke grenade in a carrying bucket.
>
> Rifle Grenadiers. As for Bombing Sections, except: No smoke bombs, and 3 Mills Rifle Grenades instead of hand grenades. In addition to the [uncertain] [uncertain] special for grenade firing, 6 [uncertain] rods, and 1 [uncertain] attachment per man.
>
> Moppers Up. Rifle, Bayonet, and Equipment; Each Pocket and 12 Mills bombs (pocket [uncertain] in reality), 2 smoke bombs, and 2 [uncertain] rods. A white band worn on right forearm.
>
> Issue of. The Battalion Bombing Officer will issue the necessary bombs and equipment from the Transport Lines at the following times.
> "A" Coy. 11.00 a.m. "B" Coy. 11.20 a.m.
> "C" Coy. 11.40 a.m. "D" Coy. 12.00 noon.
> Companies will arrange to have a fatigue party of 1 N.C.O. and at least 10 men report to him at these times, and the B.O. will inform him of the exact number of Other Ranks from the Company to take part in the attack, exclusive of the Bombing Sections, Rifle Grenadiers, and Moppers Up.
>
> Replenishment of. The Battalion Bombing Officer will be responsible for the supply of bombs during the attack. He will site Company Dumps in the captured trenches, and Company Commanders will be responsible for the transportation of bombs from the Battalion to the Company dumps.
>
> Bombing and L.G. Sections: Organization of. The Bombing Section of each Platoon will be organized as follows:
> 1 N.C.O. In charge.
> 2 Other Ranks Bayonet Men.
> 3 Other Ranks Throwers and Carriers.
> Rifle Grenade Section:
> 3 O.R.'s Rifle Grenadiers.
>
> Forward Dumps: Location of. Immediately a Platoon has attained its objective, the Bombing Section will collect the bombs carried by each O.R. and form them into a Platoon dump, bearing in mind the necessity of easy access to this store, by means of C.T.'s or otherwise.
>
> All ranks are again reminded of the vital necessity of conserving the supply of the expensive and heavy grenades, the transportation of which is so difficult. Bombs are only to be used for dislodging an enemy from behind cover; they are not to be used against men in the open, except in exceptional circumstances. "Never use a bomb when a rifle will do the work" is a good rule to remember and on which to act.
