## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", typed in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- The "Hour" column is struck through on these forms and carries no entries; no hour is recorded for the daily entries. The weather is recorded at the end of each day's summary ("Fine.", "Rainy.", etc.), under the heading "Weather Report".
- The Remarks column on each diary-text page carries the same handwritten initials against every entry; the initials could not be read with confidence and are not transcribed per entry (see Uncertain items).
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written. The appendices use Aldershot map sheet references (e.g. F 27 a 0.8) as written.
- "do." in the Place column means "ditto" (same place as the entry above).

## Page 5 — Appendix I: 19th Machine Gun Company and 185th Battalion Scheme (July 1917, p. 5)

> Appendix I to Volume V of 185th Battalion War Diary.
>
> (The scheme described hereunder was carried out on the 5th and 6th of July instead of the 4th and 5th as mentioned therein).
>
> 19th Machine Gun Company and 185th Battalion SCHEME
> Wednesday 4th July and Thursday 5th July, 1917.
>
> GENERAL IDEA.
>
> The country North of the RIVER WEY is RED.
> The " " South " " " " is BROWN.
>
> At the outbreak of war on Tuesday, 3rd July, RED has only one battalion mobilized and BROWN has one Battalion and a Machine Gun Coy.
>
> SPECIAL IDEA
>
> On Tuesday the 3rd inst the RED Battalion was defeated by BROWN at WITLEY and commenced withdrawing towards its own frontier at ELSTEAD, fighting stubborn rearguard actions all Tuesday and Wednesday on every favourable piece of ground.
>
> By 5.00 p.m. on Wednesday the 4th July the BROWN advance guard has forced the RED Rear guard across the RIVER WEY at ELSTEAD.
>
> During the construction of a temporary bridge the BROWN advance guard lost touch with the RED rear guard and the O.C. BROWN advance guard on reaching the INN at CHARLESHILL at 8.00 p.m. decided to halt and verify the information he received from its inhabitants that the BROWN rear guard had retired North to SEALE by the SEALE – CHARLESHILL road.
>
> On the arrival of the O.C. BROWN FORCE at 8.30 p.m. he decides to halt for the night of the 4th/5th July at [uncertain] CHARLESHILL owing to his troops being very fatigued and his ignorance of the country.
>
> BROWN outposts are worried during the night of the 4th/5th July by RED snipers and patrols.
>
> On 7.00 a.m. on the 5th July BROWN Scouts report the RED FORCE about 800 infantry and some Vickers guns to have entrenched themselves on a line running roughly from the H of CROOKSBURY HILL to the C of CULVERSWELL HEATH.
>
> The O.C. BROWN force decides to attack at once.

## Pages 10–12 — Battalion Standing Orders, 16–18 July 1917 (July 1917, pp. 10–12)

### Cowdray Park, 16 July 1917 (page 10)

> (Copy)
>
> Battalion Standing Orders,
> by Lieut Col F.P.Day,
> Commanding 185th Canadian Inf. Bn.
> (Cape Breton Highlanders).
> COWDRAY PARK 16-7-17.
>
> Detail. 1. To be orderly officer to-morrow,– Lieut [uncertain] T. Andrews
> Next for duty,– Lieut D.N. McDonald
>
> Alarm post 2. The Battalion Alarm post will be the open space north of the Battalion lines, between our lines and those of the 19th M.G.Co.
>
> Camp Boundaries. 3. The boundaries of the Camp will be the bounds of Cowdray Park. Enclosed portions will be out of bounds. No men will be allowed outside the camp boundaries without a pass countersigned at Brigade headquarters.
>
> Water. 4 Drinking water carts will fill as follows:—
> 185th Battalion at stand pipe in park near LODGE at 7.00 p.m. The C.R.E. will supply the required length of hose for attachment to hydrant and stand pipes. Horses will water in park at BEN BO... [uncertain] F 27 d 5.4. Buckets will be used as horses must not be permitted to walk into the pond.
>
> Guides. 5 A guide for baggage and supply wagons will be detailed from the Scouts by O.C. Scouts to do duty during the scheme. Times and places at which he is to report will be notified to units daily.
>
> Practise Casualties 6 Units will detail classified casualties as follows:
> 185th Canadian Inf. Bn. – 10 casualties on Tuesday 17-7-17. The Battalion M.O. will be held responsible to see that these men are collected by representatives of the 14th Canadian Field Amb. Their rations for mid-day and evening meal will be delivered to the section 14th Fd. Amb. on Tuesday morning. Casualties will be returned to the unit Tuesday evening;
> Actual casualties will be evacuated through 14th Canadian Fd. Amb. and necessary alterations made in the Daily Distribution State.
>
> Disposal of waste. 7 Immediately upon arrival at a bivouac area steps will be taken to arrange for the disposal of manure and swill to local farmers, etc., All waste disposed of in this way must, however, be removed, before the departure of troops from the area. If such arrangements cannot be made this fact will be communicated at once to Staff Captain Q, when instructions will be issued as to disposal by burning or deep pits.
>
> Guard. 8 The battalion will mount a quarter Guard of 1 sergeant, 1 corporal, 1 Bugler, and 12 men, and supply one sentry for each side of the Battalion Bivouac area.
>
> Latrines. 9 Latrines for Officers and Other Ranks are 150 yards in front of Battalion Headquarters.
>
> 10 Men will move to front and rear by left flank of camp, under no circumstances will they move on right flank of camp.
>
> (sgd) J.W. MACDONALD Major & Adjutant, 185th Canadian Inf. Bn.
