### I.8.a.4.4., Sheet 28 N.W., Belgium — 3-11-17

Weather. Dull. South wind.
Message received from G.O.C. 1st Canadian Corps congratulating Canadian Corps on success of operations carried out by them.
Great difficulty is experienced on the work in the forward area in pushing forward the bathmat walk Track No 5. The great darkness and depth of the mud renders the grading and laying of bathmats very difficult. Also long sections of the track already laid are frequently removed presumably by infantrymen desiring to build bivies or on which to sleep. Last night 260 yards of the track in front of Abraham Heights was torn up and the mats removed. The great distance that the mats have to be carried by hand, in some cases nearly four miles, adds to the difficulty of the work.
Lieut. Baldock with 11 Sappers and party from the 4th Battalion carrying bathmats See report [Remarks column: Report #5]
A guard of 1 N.C.O. and 3 Sappers placed on bathmats.

(Continued)

Signed: W.R.R., Major.

### Vlamertinghe, Belgium — 18-11-17

Weather: Dull. North wind
The Company's transport accompanied by Sapper brakemen moved off at 9 a.m. and was clear of Busseboom (the starting point) by 11 a.m., and moved via Godewaersvelde to the Caestre area. The transport of the 7th Field Coy C.E. and 123rd Canadian Pioneer Battalion accompanied this Coy's transport under Capt. Ross's orders.
The remainder of the Company remained at Vlamertinghe during the day and inspection of kit was held.

Signed: W.R.R., Major.

### Havreskerque, France — 19-11-17

Weather. Cloudy and dull.
The Company fell in under Major Robertson and moved off at 8:15 a.m. and marched by way of Vlamertinghe-Ypres Road to Kruisstraat where they embussed on lorries with other units of the 7th Infantry Brigade group. Nine lorries being allotted to this Company.
Leaving about 12 noon the Company proceeded by way of Dickebusch, Kemmel, Locre, Bailleul, and Merville to Havreskerque where it debussed and marched to billets in farm houses on the Merville Road just west of le Sart.

(Continued)

Signed: W.R.R., Major.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** 30 dated diary-text entries for 1–30 November 1917 (no date gaps; every day has a diary-text entry). Diary text pp. 4–26 (23 pages). Appendix R pp. 27–56 (cover + 29 sheets: Report #1, 1 Nov, and sheets 2–27, 31 Oct–29 Nov, including the training program for 21–27 Nov on p. 54).
- **No-entry dates (diary text):** None. **Date gaps (appendix sheets):** No sheet dated 1 November 1917 (other than Report #1, dated 1 Nov, on p. 28); no sheets dated 12 November; no sheets dated 17–20 November; no work-report sheets dated 21–27 November (only the training program, p. 54); no sheet dated 30 November 1917.
- **Contents-vs-pages discrepancy:** The contents page (p. 2) states "Diary Text — 25 Pages", but the diary text as filed runs pp. 4–26, i.e. 23 pages. It also describes Appendix R as "Work report issued by 9th Field Co. 1-11-17 to 29-11-17", which matches the sheets as filed (31 Oct–29 Nov) except that the first sheet is dated 31 October 1917.
- **Split entries:** Diary entries split across page breaks: 3, 5, 6, 7, 10, 11, 12, 14, 16 and 19 November.
- **Report-number discrepancy:** Report #26 is cited in the remarks column for both 28 November (Lieut. Murdie, rifle ranges) and 29 November (Lieut. Murdie, rifle ranges continued); the number is transcribed as written on both pages. The Schedule of Training cited on 21 November is entered as Report #25, but its report number is left blank in the text ("See copy attached Report No ").
- **Battalion discrepancy (1 November):** The diary text (p. 4) names the infantry party with Lieut. Baldock as the "1st Battalion" (interlineated), while appendix Report #1 (p. 28) records the same carrying party as 308 men of the 13th Batt. Both are transcribed as written and not reconciled.
