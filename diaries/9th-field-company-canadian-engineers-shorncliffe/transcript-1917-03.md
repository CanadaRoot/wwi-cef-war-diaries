[Handwritten, top:] Original — 9th Field Coy Canadian Engineers — 3rd Canadian Division

### France, Auchel — 1-3-17

Weather: Dull and cold in forenoon. Clear in afternoon.
Two Sections of the Coy marched to Marles-les-Mines in the forenoon and two in the afternoon where they engaged in target practice on the rifle ranges.
Drivers Section engaged in Riding Exercise, Wagon drill and usual routine work.

Signed: W.R.R., Major.

### France, Auchel — 2-3-17

Weather: Cloudy and cool.
Company engaged in Section Drill, Bayonet Fighting, Range Finding, Field Works, and Gas Drill. Also No 1 Section Demolitions, No 2 Sec. Pontooning; No 3 Sec. Wiring; No 4 Sec. Taping trenches. Drivers Sec Riding Exercise and Wagon Drill.

Signed: W.R.R., Major.

### France, Auchel — 3-3-17

Weather: Cold and dull.
Company engaged in Company Drill: Physical Drill, Bayonet Fighting, Box Respirator Drill and Field Works. Also No 1 Sec. Demolitions

(Contd)

### France, Auchel — 3-3-17 (continued)

No 2 Section. Pontooning, No 3 Section wiring. No 4 Section taking out trenches. Drivers Section. Wagon Drill and Riding Exercise.

3rd Canadian Divisional Operation Order No 21 Copy #4 received today reciting Canadian Corps Orders that the 2nd Canadian Division would be relieved by the 3rd Canadian Division on the front from De la Fauche (exclusive) (A.4.a.5.6) to La Salle (inclusive) [uncertain], together with the rear Area "A" Division. Further the 9th C.I.B would relieve the 5th C.I.B on the front above mentioned. Relief to be complete by Mar. 12, 1917. and further directing that one Field Coy. 3rd Canadian Division would relieve the Field Coy of the 2nd Canadian Division working on the front to be taken over. Details to be arranged between the C.R.Es of the two Divisions. The Field Coy to be under orders of the 3rd Canadian Division for work, and under the tactical orders of the 2nd Canadian Division

Signed: W.R.R., Major.

## Notes

- **Structure:** 90 LAC images. Cover (p. 1), contents (p. 2), inner title page (p. 3), diary text pp. 4–33 (30 pages, matching the contents page's "Diary Text — 30 Pages"), Appendix "J" cover (p. 34), and 56 daily work-report sheets (pp. 35–90, numbered 1–56 at top right).
- **Contents-page discrepancy:** The contents page describes Appendix J as "Work report issued by 9th Field Co. 15-3-17 to 20-3-17", but the appendix as filed actually contains work reports dated 13–30 March 1917 (sheets 1–56).
- **Date gaps:** None in the diary text — all dates 1–31 March 1917 are present, in sequence. The 27th March has no stand-alone appendix report; it is covered only inside the two 27/28-3-17 reports (pp. 81, 86).
- **Split entries:** The 3 March entry is split across pp. 4–5; 8 March across pp. 8–9; 9 March across pp. 9–10; and the 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 28 and 29 March entries are likewise split across page breaks, each marked "(Contd)".
- **Appendix sheet numbers irregular:** Sheets are not in chronological order and their numbers are irregular — two sheets numbered 1 (pp. 35 and 41), two numbered 13 (pp. 46 and 47), p. 40 reads "5" over "6", p. 57 reads "20 over 3" (sequence suggests 23), and several sheets have a small extra digit beneath the main number (pp. 37, 48, 53, 55, 56, 59). Report numbers also run out of chronological order against their dates in the later sheets (e.g. report 51, p. 85, is dated 28-3-17 but follows report 50, p. 84, dated 29-3-17).
- **Casualty discrepancies:**
  - Spr Cranton, J.J.: p. 31 gives his number as #504480 [uncertain] (wounded 29-3-17); p. 33 gives No. 504430 [uncertain] (died of wounds 29-3-17). One number is miswritten in the original.
  - Spr Crowley, E. (#445695) is listed sick on both 13 March (p. 13) and again on 14 March (p. 14), transcribed as written.
  - The diary's only other named casualty is #500758 Spr Corp. M. [uncertain] (wounded 29-3-17, p. 31). The appendix's only casualty line is p. 90: "CASUALTIES: – Two wounded" (no names). Appendix sheets pp. 46 and 50 record "CASUALTIES: Nil"; p. 42 records "CASUALTIES:— Nil."
  - One saddle horse killed and one horse wounded by shell fire, 26 March (p. 28).
- **Name-reading discrepancies across the month (same hands read differently):** The officer signing the work reports is read "H. H. Robertson", "H. D. Robertson" and "H. W. Robertson" on different sheets — the initials are indistinct; the diary text consistently names him Lieut. H.D. Robertson. The trench-cleaning officer is read "W. C. Murdie" on most sheets but "W.C. Munder" on pp. 76, 79, 82, 85, 87. The diary (p. 11) names a Lieut. "Muroie" [uncertain], evidently the same officer. The O.C.'s cursive signatures on pp. 31–33 are indistinct and read as "N.R.P." / "N.R. Robertson" [uncertain], while the title page (p. 3) gives the name in full as "W. R. Robertson, Major". The officer signing "A. G. [uncertain]" on pp. 36, 39, 41, 45 could not be identified.
- **Form heading flip:** Pp. 31–32 are headed "WAR DIARY" (with "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through); p. 33 is headed "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" (with "WAR DIARY" struck through).
- **Times on work reports:** Sometimes written with a "th" suffix (e.g. "12th", "7th") — transcribed literally; these probably mean 12 (midnight) and 7.
- **Other notes from the pages:** p. 25 contains a heavily overwritten/interlineated passage about Dvr. Moscrip (10 days Field Punishment No. 1 for abandoning his mule); the interlineated words are illegible. The diary writes "M.T.M." on some pages and "H.T.M." on others against the usual "T.M." (trench mortar); transcribed as written. P. 71 includes small diagrams of emplacement layouts in the Amount Done column. P. 80 is the only non-tabular appendix report (narrative form, faint writing) and is the hardest to read. Signatures on pp. 82 and 85 are partly cut off at the foot of the page. P. 77: the report date appears corrected — 28-3-17 written over 26-3-17. P. 76 heading reads "Daily – Work – Party"; p. 72 reads "Daily – Report – Work"; p. 55 reads "Daily – Report – Work. 18/19-3-17".
- **Uncertain items:** See the [uncertain] flags throughout the transcript — 315 in the page transcriptions (318 in the file including the three mentions in this Notes section). The heaviest uncertainty is on the faint pencil work reports, especially pp. 49, 80, 84 and 88.
