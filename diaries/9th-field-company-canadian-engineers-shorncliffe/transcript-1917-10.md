### C.27.b.2.3. Sheet 28 N.W., Belgium — 19-10-17 (continued)

Major Robertson accompanied O.C. 4th Field Coy N.Z. Engineers over the works of this Coy in the forward area. This consists in the construction of a double infantry track of trench mats from the neighbourhood of St. Jean Easterly, passing south of Hill 35 and Hill 37 to the top of Abraham Heights on the Gravenstafel Ridge; approximately a distance of 5 miles.
Work includes maintenance of portion already constructed. parts of which are frequently destroyed by enemy shell fire.
It was noted by Major Robertson that our present front line as being taken over from the N.Z. Division by the 9th C.I. Bde, is in the same locality and approximately 100 to 200 yards ahead of the old front line trenches which were occupied by the 10th Infantry Battalion in April 1915 just prior to the German attack on April 22. 1915. and [faint interlineation, partly legible: "in these trenches he was working on with [uncertain] of the 2nd F.C.E." — see Notes]
These trenches were being worked upon by Major Robertson, then a Lieut. in the 2nd Field Coy C.E.

Signed: W.R.R. Major.

### I.8.a.4.4. Sheet 28 N.W., Belgium — 26-10-17

Weather: Cold with rain. East wind.
The 9th C.I. Bde. with 43rd and 58th Battalions attacked the Bellevue Spur at 5:40 a.m. and got part of their objectives.
The 8th C.I. Bde. also attacked on the left and the 4th Canadian Division on the right, each taking some objectives.
Lieut. Jones with Sappers from Nos. 1, 2, and 3 Sections assembled last night with 100 Infantry from the 116th Battalion prepared to push forward with the building of bathmat walk No 5. Party waited all night and this morning the G.O.C. 9th C.I. Bde decided that in view of the heavy fighting and casualties sustained by his Brigade in the attack and the objectives gained, that it was not advisable for this party to go forward.
This supports the view that large parties cannot work in view of the enemy in daylight immediately after an attack unless the attack has progressed so deep as to disturb the enemy guns.
Lieut Baldock with 7 Sappers and party from 42nd Battalion carrying bathmats and signs. [Remarks column: Report #23]
Lieut Murdie with 8 Sappers and party from R.C.R. carrying and laying bathmats [Remarks column: Report #24].
Lieut Walley returned from on leave. [Remarks column: Report #25]

The work to be, constructing bathmat walk, "track No.5."

Major Robertson and Capt. Ross attended the funeral at Brandhoek. Funeral was largely attended by Os.C. Field Companies including three Os.C. of Field Companies (Major N.R. Robertson being one) who had served under him as Lieutenants.
