### L.25.C.2.4 — Sep. 1st 1916

Weather, — Fair and cool.

Company carried on training under Section Officers as per syllabus. Numbers 1. 2. 3 & 4 Sections engaged in gas alerts drill, inspection of gas helmets, trestle bridging, pontooning and practical work laying out and wiring of strong points. Mounted Section engaged in mounted drill under Captain HARRISON.

C.R.E. and O.C. inspected Brick Fields, LA BOISELLE and other sites in SOMME area yesterday for possible billets and obtained general information as to area and interviewed Engineer and Pioneer Officers of the AUSTRALIAN Corps, obtaining information as to works etc.

Casualties. — No. 502777 A/L/Cpl. ESSERY F. sick and sent to No. 9 Field Ambulance.

[N R R Major]

### L.25.C.2.4 — Sep. 2nd 1916

Weather. — Fair and cool.

Company carried on work as per syllabus under O.C. and Section Officers.

No. 1 Section, — Physical exercise, squad drill, map reading, lectures on gas, demolitions and consolidation.

No. 2 Section. — Physical exercise, infantry drill, map reading and survey, putting in strong point and lecture on map making.

No. 3 Section. — Physical exercise, section drill with arms, siting defences of wood and making out two strong points; field level, prismatic compass work and map reading.

No. 4. Section. — Physical exercise, infantry training, M. G. Emplacements, topography (N.C.O.'s) and practice in signalling.

Mounted Section — Riding and driving exercise, branding horses and usual routine work under Captain HARRISON.

O.C. returned from SOMME area to camp, in evening.

[N R R Major]

### Domqueur (Somme area) — Sep. 8th 1916

Weather. — Fine and clear

Train moved off from Cassel Station with Company at 3.51 A.M., also French Mortar Battery of Lahore Division on same train, Major Robertson O.C. train, proceeded via BOULOGNE to Candas Station, SOMME area, arriving 1.55 P.M. Horses were watered and fed en route on the train. Company detrained and moved off passing through VIENVILLERS, halted on road East of BERNAVILLE where Company had tea and horses fed. March continued through [uncertain], FRANSU to DOMQUEUR, arriving at 9.20 P.M.

Numbers 1 and 2 Sections with mounted section billeted on East side of road leading North from centre of village; balance of Company with transport billeted on West side of same road, Officers in billets B 8 as arranged by billeting party which was met as the Company marched in. None of the men fell out en route.

[N R R Major]

### FIEFFES (Somme area) — 10/9/16

Weather, Fine and cool.

Pursuant to 3rd Canadian Divisional Operation Order No 42 and 8th C.I.B. Operation Order No 37, the Company, being in the 8th Brigade group for billeting purposes, moved off from Domqueur at 6 a.m. and passed the starting point of the Brigade march at 9.36 a.m., being junction of the LANCHES-GERMEUIL [uncertain] and the Domart-Berneuil roads. The company following the 3rd Canadian Pioneer Battalion and preceding the 8th Canadian Field Ambulance unit, Marched with Brigade via MONTRELET to FIEFFES, a distance of approximately 10 miles, and billeted overnight in a field in FIEFFES; some of the Sappers occupying billets on either side of the field. None of the company fell out during the march. The full march to LAVICOGNE area was not carried out upon verbal orders of 8th C.I.B. Staff Captain.

3rd Canadian Divisional Operation Orders No 43 received. Company to proceed from Vicogne area to Senlis preceding the 8th C.I.B. group

The whole of the transport of each unit is to arrive at the entraining Station 3 hours before the train is due to leave. All Infantry Battalions are to be at entraining Station 1½ hours prior to the departure of train.

The entrainment of units must be completed ½ hour before the time of departure of the train.

C.O. Units, or portion of unit, on arrival at entraining Station will report to the Staff Officer superintending entrainment.

Each unit will proceed with its authorised establishment of transport and animals as shown in the annexed Table "C". There will be no accommodation for any other vehicles.

A Marching Out State showing the number of Officers, other ranks, animals, 4 wheeled vehicles, 2 wheeled vehicles and bicycles entraining with the unit, will be sent to the Station with the first portion of each unit and handed to the R.T.O. A copy of this State is to be sent to this Headquarters.

7. Train Vehicles.

The Train Supply and Baggage wagons will entrain with the Battalion or unit which they serve.

Each unit will entrain with the current days rations, or unused portion, and rations for the following day are to be carried in the Train Supply wagons which accompany the unit. Water carts are to be filled.

10. Breast Ropes.

Breast ropes for horse trucks are to be provided by units. Ropes for lashing vehicles on flat trucks are provided by the Railway. Heel ropes will serve as breast ropes.

11. Brake Van.

No personnel or stores will be allowed in the brake van at the end of each train.

12. Picquets.

Each battalion will detail a picquet of 1 Officer and 25 N.C.Os. and men, and each other unit a picquet of 1 Sergeant, 2 Corporals and ten men. These picquets will report to the entraining Officer at the entraining Station immediately the unit arrives there. They will be on duty during the whole period of the journey. These picquets will be posted at the entraining Station under instructions of the entraining Officer to prevent men wandering away or leaving the train after entrainment is completed.
Immediately the train stops at an intermediate stopping place, a part of the picquet will be posted at each end of the train and elsewhere as necessary, to prevent men leaving the train except for the purposes of nature; and to ensure that men leaving for this purpose rejoin at once.

COMPANY ORDERS BY MAJOR J.B. DUNBAR OFFICER COMMANDING 7TH FIELD COMPANY, CANADIAN ENGINEERS. 6TH SEPTEMBER, 1916.

365. PARADES FOR THURSDAY, 7TH SEPTEMBER.

Reveille — 7.00 a.m.
Stables — 7.15 to 7.45 a.m.
Breakfast — 8.00 a.m.
*Parade — 9.00 a.m. to 12.00 noon.
Dinner — 12.00 noon.
**Parade — 1.00 p.m.
Tea — 5.00 p.m.
***Parade — 11.00 p.m.

*The 9.00 a.m. parade will be in clean fatigue dress, and to pack all wagons possible and to clean camp.
**The afternoon parade will be the same. During the parade sections will be practised in placing packs in wagons, in taking them out, adjusting them quickly, and in demolishing rack extensions and packing them on top of tool carts and G.S. wagons.
***This parade will be in light marching order. All packs are to be placed in wagons by 8.00 p.m. and all wagons fully loaded by that time.

The Company will march to ESQUELBECQ; it must arrive there by 4.30 a.m. The Company will then entrain.

Each section will first unload packs and demolish rack extensions. They will then pile arms and place packs by their arms.
They will then load on their tool and forage carts. Any men remaining over will load on the remaining vehicles.

The drivers. The drivers will remove saddles and harness and load the horses into the cars. The MOUNTED SERGEANT will see that breast ropes are available. Two drivers per car will be detailed by the MOUNTED SERGEANT; they will remain with the animals. As soon as wagons and horses are loaded all men except those detailed for other work will fall in, in sections, in line. The men will then be detailed to cars. No personnel or stores will be allowed in the brake van at the end of each train.

A picquet consisting of 1 Sergeant, 2 corporals and ten sappers will be detailed from No 3 section; their duties will be as under:—
They will report to the entraining officer at the entraining station immediately the unit arrives there. They will be on duty during the whole period of the journey. They will be posted at the entraining station under the instructions of the entraining officer to prevent men wandering away or leaving the train after the entrainment is completed. Immediately the train stops at an intermediate station a part of the picquet will be posted at each end of the train, and elsewhere as necessary, to prevent men leaving the train except for the purposes of nature, and to ensure that men leaving for this purpose rejoin at once. On arrival at the detraining station the picquet will be posted to prevent men wandering away from the precincts of the station.

The Company will detrain at CONTEVILLE. The procedure there will be the exact opposite of entraining. After detraining the Company will march to CRAMONT and will billet there.

For the journey each man will carry in his haversack as much food as possible, in addition to the emergency rations.

[signed] J. B. Dunbar
Major,
O.C. 7th Field Company, Canadian Engrs.

Day Work Lts Worsley & Kennedy with one Section placed signs in REGENT ST, OXFORD ST, HESSIAN TRENCH, ZOLLERN GRABEN & FABECK GRABEN.
REGENT ST appears to run from about R.28.c.5.5 1/2 to about R.28.a.5 1/2 6 1/2 [uncertain] and then curves North Easterly to HESSIAN TRENCH.
OXFORD ST appears to run from about R.28.B.2.1 to the ZOLLERN [struck: TRENCH] GRABEN at about R.28.B.3 1/2.7 1/2. It then goes northerly for about 200 yds and then swings North Westerly to join REGENT ST. about 100 yds [uncertain] South of HESSIAN TRENCH.
Casualties — NIL.
Night Work — NIL

[signed] J. B. Dunbar, Major,
O.C. 7th Fld Co. C.E.

29.9.16
