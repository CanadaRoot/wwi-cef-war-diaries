### La Tiemand — 1-12-17

Weather: Dull with showers in afternoon. West wind
Company, consisting of No 4 Section and part of No 3 Section marched to rifle ranges, south of this village, and carried on target practice.
Voting under the Military Service Act of Canada commenced today. Major N.R. Robertson [uncertain] as O.C. the Company acted as Deputy Returning Officer for the Company, being sworn in by the O.C. 2nd C.M.R. C.S.M. Samways, A.E. being sworn in by Major Robertson acted as Poll Clerk, and Capt. Bruce Ross and #502761 Cpl Peppers, W.W. acted as Scrutineers for the Opposition and Union Government respectively, both being duly sworn in by Major Robertson. Votes were taken at the Horse Lines, Estree Blanche and at the Company Billets, La Tiemand. 72 votes in all were polled.
Lieut Murdie with 13 N.C.Os and Sappers and party of 25 O.R. from the R.C.R cleaning up around rifle ranges at T.15.a.

[Remarks column: Appendix "S"]

Signed: W.R.R., Major.

### La Tiemand — 18-12-17

Weather: Clear and cold N West wind.
Company loading wagons, cleaning equipment preparatory to moving.

### Verquin, France — 20-12-17

Weather: Clear. S.W. Wind.
Company fell in and moved off under the O.C. at 8.15 a.m., passing starting point of 9th C.I. Bde in l'Eclere [uncertain] at 9 a.m. and marched via Busnettes, Chocques, Fouquereuil, and Fouquieres, to Verquin arriving at about 11.45 a.m.
The Company billeted in houses in the town. Horses in standings under cover.
Major Robertson rode to 9th C.I. Bde Hqrs. in Fouquieres and interviewed Acting Bde. Major re. policy of work to be followed on the line. Same to be one of defensive wiring.
9th C.I. Bde Operation Order No 134 Copy No 19 dated 20-12-17 received today.
Under orders from C.R.E. Company to move to Mazingarbe tomorrow.
3rd Canadian Divisional Engineers Operation Order No 47, copy No 3 received whereby inter alia the 9th Field Coy C.E. will take over work in 33rd Brigade 11th British Division from the 68th Field Coy R.E. and portion of 34th Brigade Sector from the 86th Field Coy R.E. Relief to be complete by 6 a.m. 23rd Dec. 1917.

(Continued)

Signed: W.R.R., Major.

### Mazingarbe, France — 21-12-17

Weather: Clear and cold N.W. Wind.
Company fell in and marched off under the O.C. at 8.30 a.m. and marched by way of Noeux-les-Mines to Mazingarbe where Company billeted in a chateau for the night.
Two enemy shells, H.V., landed near the column as it marched through Noeux-les-Mines.
The O.C. and Lieut Jones attended the Rear Billets of the 68th Field Coy R.E. at les Bechis and made final arrangements re taking over. It having been ascertained that 68th Field Coy R.E. were moving from Advance Billets today although relief not to be completed until morning of the 22nd, party of 2 N.C.Os and 8 men from 3 and 4 Sections proceeded to Advanced Billets and reported to Lieut West to hold billets etc and to be there in case of emergency.

Weather: Clear and cold. N.W. Wind.

The Adjutant, Major Shaw, attended the Coy Rear Billets today with copy of C.R.E. 15-29 issued in conjunction with policy of work as laid down in 3rd Canadian Division G.492, dated 20-12-17 which comprises mostly a system of defensive wiring of forward trenches.

## Notes

- **Contents-page discrepancies:** The contents page (p. 2) states "Diary Text 28 Pages", but only 18 diary text pages are present in this file (pp. 4–21). The contents page also describes Appendix S as "Work report issued by 9th Field Co. 25-12-17 to 29-12-17", but the appendix sheets actually run from 23 December to 29 December 1917 (Reports No. 1–14, pp. 23–36), covering 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28 and 29 December — two reports per day, one each from No. 3 Section (Lieut F. S. Jones) and No. 4 Section (Lieut A. G. West [uncertain]).
- **Diary-text date range:** 1–31 December 1917 with no date gaps. Diary text occupies pp. 4–21; the month's diary is complete.
- **Place-name spellings:** "La Tiemand" is spelled thus throughout the diary (also "la Tiemand"); "les Bechis" / "Les Bechis" is spelled thus throughout; "Clacques" (pp. 5, 10) and "l'Eclere" (pp. 11, 12) are as written. "FERFAY" is written in capitals on pp. 5–6 and as "Ferfay" on p. 16.
- **Officer names:** The O.C. is signed "W.R.R., Major" throughout; the title page and final signature read "W. R. Robertson". The diary text on 1 December gives the initials as "N.R. Robertson" [uncertain]. The C.S.M. / Poll Clerk is written "Samways" on 1 December and "Samway" on 5 December. Capt "Miller Duff" (27 December) is as written. Lieut Murdie's leave on 30 December is written with an illegible word before "leave".
- **Casualties:** No men of this Company were killed or wounded in action during December 1917. The only casualty in the diary text is #172841 Spr Howard J.W., accidentally injured on 25 December and sent to hospital. The diary records "Some Infantry casualties. None in this Coy" after the enemy gas projector attack of 30 December. All appendix work reports record "Casualties — Nil" except Report No. 13 (p. 35), where the casualties line is illegible.
- **Appendix form:** Unlike March 1917, the December work reports are not on the tabular Daily Work Report form; they are free-form handwritten notes on squared paper, with a "Work / Men / Time / Work done" or "Party / Work / Casualties" layout, and the No. 4 Section sheets are the hardest to read.
- **Notable events:** Voting under the Military Service Act of Canada, 1–5 December (72 votes polled at La Tiemand; No. 2 Section voted at Clacques, No. 1 Section at Ferfay); inspection by Maj.-Gen. Lipsett, G.O.C. 3rd Canadian Division, on 4 December, who thanked the Company for the bathmat track and bridges in front of Bellevue Spur in the Ypres Sector and passed on Gen. Sir Douglas Haig's congratulations to the 3rd Canadian Division; the Company moved from La Tiemand to Busnes (19 December), Verquin (20 December), Mazingarbe (21 December) and les Bechis (22 December), taking over from the 68th and 86th Field Companies R.E. in the 33rd and 34th Brigade sectors of the 11th British Division; the main work from 23 December was defensive wiring of the reserve line (double apron and knee-high entanglements) with parties from the 58th, 116th and 43rd Battalions; two enemy H.V. shells landed near the column at Noeux-les-Mines on 21 December; enemy gas shells fell near the Advanced Billets nightly from 29 December; a German gas projector attack of about 600 projectors struck the front line on the morning of 30 December.
- **No duplicate photographs:** No page in this file is a duplicate photograph of another page.
