# 9th Field Company, Canadian Engineers (Shorncliffe) — war diary

## Unit and source

- **Unit:** 9th Field Company, Canadian Engineers (Shorncliffe), 3rd Canadian Division.
- **LAC guide reference:** War diary, 20 May 1916 – 25 May 1918, RG 9 III-D-3, vols. 4998–4999, folders 668–669 ("Field Companies and Battalions, Canadian Engineers" guide, p. 63). Organized at Shorncliffe in February 1916 under Major N. R. Robertson; arrived in France 21 May 1916, 3rd Canadian Divisional Engineers; absorbed by 9th Battalion, Canadian Engineers in May 1918.
- **Cataloguing trap (verified 2026-10-27):** No LAC Collection Search record titled "War diaries - 9th Field Company, Canadian Engineers" exists — exact-phrase searches for that title (English and French) return 0 results. Field company diaries are catalogued under their later battalion titles. The diary is split across three file-level records, all titled **"War diaries - 9th Battalion, Canadian Engineers"**:

## What the diary is

Handwritten Army Form C.2118 diary of a field company of the 3rd Canadian Division's engineers — trench work around Ypres (Hooge, Sanctuary Wood, Mount Sorrel, Hill 60 sector), then the Somme (Regina Trench / Courcelette, October 1916) and the march south to the Vimy sector. Each month is cover + contents + diary text + a large appendix of nightly Officers' Work Reports (Appendices B–E) with sketches and maps. O.C. throughout this part: Major N. R. Robertson — but the O.C.'s initials as written vary by month (N.R., H.R., W.R., H.P. Robertson) and are transcribed as written, not reconciled. The Remarks column mark reads "C.S.W." in May; the diary text cites work-report sheet numbers in the Remarks column.

## Notable content, May–October 1916

- **20–21 May:** company sails Shorncliffe → Southampton → S.S. Anglo-Canadian → Le Havre; 25 May reaches Poperinghe and camps at map ref. H.13.d.3.7 (Sheet 28 N.W.); sections move up to advanced billets at Ypres from 27 May, relieving the 8th Field Company.
- **Casualties (diary text):** first death 6 June — Spr. Grove F. J. (#502825) killed by H.E. shell; Spr. Campbell J. (#502770) wounded, died of wounds 8 June; Dvr Woodward J.M. (#541970) killed 29 June by H.E. shell with the water cart at advanced billets (buried Brandhoek 30 June); Spr Fox R.E. (#502624) killed 30 June by shell fire close to his billet. 2 July — Spr. Burns W. (#502441) and Spr. Cunningham F. B. (#641720) killed by the same H.E. shell (buried Brandhoek Cemetery 4 July). 23 August — Sapper Proctor R. (#2152) wounded by machine-gun bullet at Sunken Road Dump, died minutes later; buried Brandhoek Military Cemetery 24 Aug. 7 October — Spr. Clark W. H. (#434445) killed by shell fire (buried Tara Hill Cemetery 8 Oct). 10 October — L/Cpl. Gilmour W. (#541920) killed in action. Many wounded/sick to hospital throughout (shell, shrapnel, rifle fire, gas alerts 18–22 July, influenza not yet named).
- **Mount Sorrel, June 1916:** company takes over the Left Brigade Sector from the 8th Field Company (Operation Order #2, 1 June); trench repair and wiring at Mount Sorrel (trenches 46–52), Hill St., the Culvert.
- **July:** 9 July the company takes over a new front-line area (Yeomanry Wood to Gourock Rd., C.R.E. Operation Order No. 10); work on the named "streets" (Savoy, Bond, New Oxford, Regent, Leinster, West, Grafton, Cork, China Wall), Menin Road culverts, Zouave Wood; gas alerts 18–22 July; chronic rubber-boot shortage.
- **August:** move to new billets 26 August (place column changes to L.25.c.2.4, Sheet 27); night of 11–12 August (Report #32) — torpedoes placed in the old crater to blow in German tunnels exploded at 2 a.m. from the eastern lip of the old crater.
- **September:** diary text 1–30 Sep; appendices include much 7th Field Company material (orders, training summaries, work reports signed by Major J. B. Dunbar, O.C. 7th Field Coy.) filed with this diary — indexed as a Diary note, not as 9th Field Company activity.
- **October:** 1 Oct attack on Regina Trench west of Courcelette (2nd Division and part of 3rd captured it, then were driven back); 8 Oct Canadian Corps attack penetrated the enemy trench but retired; company marches south 11–23 Oct (Senlis → Harponville → La Vicogne → Outrebois → Bonnières → Houvin-Houvigneul → Chelers → Maroeuil) and relieves the 2/4 London Field Coy R.E. at 8 a.m. 24 Oct; trench-warden system from 27 Oct.

## Notable content, November 1916–March 1917

- **November:** company at Maroeuil, France, all month; trench-warden system, trench cleaning/draining/revetting, bath mats, tramway work (Glasgow Dump line, Vistula branches). Divisional Orders 30-10-16 awarded the Military Medal to six men for the Somme (Sgt. Emrey D.J., Cpl. Fletcher J.F., L/Cpl. Brown K.N., Spr. Feltham R.F., A/L/Cpl. Coyle J.J., Spr. Sinker H.); General Plummer presented the medals at Bray on 30 November, four of the six on parade. No men killed in the diary text; one man accidentally wounded (#500790 Cpl. Mann E.W., 5 Nov). O.C. on ten days' furlough from 18 Nov; diary signed by Lieut. E. J. Young, senior officer present.
- **December:** company at Maroeuil; no company men killed or wounded in the diary text. Appendix: one infantryman blown to pieces 8/9 Dec (unnamed); work interrupted by shelling 16 Dec with [uncertain] men of a working party killed (number illegible). Dvr. Flood J.C. heard by the C.R.E. 7 Dec for disobedience of orders, remanded for Field General Court Martial.
- **January:** based at Maroeuil 1–17 Jan; marched Maroeuil → Ecoivres via Bray 18 Jan under 3rd Canadian Divisional Engineers Operation Order No. 18; based at Ecoivres 18–31 Jan. Spr. Cox Wm (#505165) killed 21 Jan (H.E. head wound, died at the dressing station; buried Ecoivres Cemetery 22 Jan). Gas alarm stand-to 11:30 a.m.–5:00 p.m. 10 Jan. Capt. J. W. Harrison signs as O.C. on the closing page (31 Jan).
- **February:** relieved by the 4th Field Company C.E. under 3rd Canadian Divisional Engineers Operation Order No. 19; handover completed midnight 12/13 Feb; company marched Ecoivres → Maisnil-les-Ruitz (14 Feb) → Auchel (15 Feb), training at Auchel to 28 Feb. No killed or wounded in the diary text. Sgt. C. A. H. Carson granted a commission in the Canadian Engineers (11 Feb). Appendix work reports cover only 1–12 Feb and end mid-series at Report 36.
- **March:** at Auchel 1–8 March (training); marched to Villers-au-Bois 9 March and took over the La Folie sector from the 5th Field Company under Operation Orders 21/22, assuming responsibility from 6 p.m. 12 March; work handed over to the 7th/8th Field Companies from 6 p.m. 22 March, the company then supervising rear-area trenches and continuing trench-mortar emplacement and subway work in the Grange/Goodman tunnels. Spr. Cranton J.J. wounded 29 Mar, died of wounds the same day (number given as #504480 on one page and No. 504430 on another). One saddle horse killed and one wounded by shell fire 26 Mar.

## Notable content, April 1917–May 1918

- **April 1917 (Vimy):** the Battle of Vimy Ridge dominates — 9 April, zero hour 5:30 a.m., the company digging communication trenches ("Y" route, Vernon–Artillerie Weg) and running tunnel traffic while the 3rd Division took its objectives by 7:40 a.m.; wells in Vimy reported poisoned with arsenic 13 April; then road repair, water supply and tramline work around La Folie Wood. Three company men killed: A/L/Cpl Sherriff, G. (5 Apr, premature from a 60 pdr gun), 2/Cpl Downes, Albert Ernest (24 Apr, shell fire on the Arras-Lens road), Spr Tierney, W.J. (30 Apr, bomb accidentally exploding on the works). C.R.E. Col. T.V. Anderson seriously wounded in the arm 8 April.
- **May–July 1917:** light railway/tramline work on Vimy Ridge; relief of the 8th Field Company 20 May and front-line consolidation (wiring, jumping-off trenches, dugouts) ahead of proposed operations, including the "pineapple" gas bomb incident of 25 May. June: relieved by the 7th Field Company 5–6 June, Brown line and tramline work, tunnel under the Lens–Arras road completed 26 June; 58th/43rd Battalions attacked Avion trench 28 June. July: Resistance line at Chateau-de-la-Haie; gas projected on enemy lines 15 July; Spr Leslie J.A. died of wounds night of 16–17 July; marched to Marles-les-Mines 25 July.
- **August–November 1917:** training and manoeuvres at Marles-les-Mines, then Bully-Grenay from 21–22 August (Corp. Douglas, R.C., killed 26 Aug); September: march to Aux Reitz Corner 6 Sept, jumping-off and communication trench work (Totnes, Quebec, New Brunswick), 2/Cpl Sinker killed 22 Sept, Dvr Scarrott died of wounds 25 Sept, enemy raid on Totnes trench 17/18 Sept. October: training at Beugin, then Ypres 19 Oct — bath-mat Track No. 5 and Ravebeek bridges for the Passchendaele offensive; 9th C.I. Bde attacked Bellevue Spur 26 Oct; Lt-Col. T.C. Irving, C.R.E. 4th Canadian Division, killed by an enemy bomb 29 Oct. November: Track No. 5 work at Ypres; Passchendaele taken 6 Nov; march to la Tiemard (First Army Reserve) 20 Nov; Canadian Election preparations.
- **December 1917–February 1918:** training at La Tiemand and voting under the Military Service Act (December); move to les Bechis 19–22 Dec, defensive wiring of the reserve line; German gas projector attack (~600 projectors) 30 Dec. January: wiring the reserve ("blue") line at les Brebis; move to Annezin/Lambres 22 Jan; hut-building (Nissen huts) from 24 Jan. February: hut-building at Annezin, move to La Targette 18–19 Feb, Avion-sector wiring; the reorganization of Field Companies into Engineer Battalions announced 27 Feb.
- **March–May 1918:** Avion-sector trench work; the German spring offensive — company stood to from 26 March, rear billets shelled 28 March, move to Fort George. April: Bully-Grenay, Bully Switch wiring and Blue Line work; relief by the 103rd Field Company R.E. completed 2 May. May: training at Burbure while the reorganization was planned; 9th Canadian Engineer Battalion's composition settled 23–24 May (Major N. R. Robertson commanding); **the Field Company diary ends 25 May 1918** with the march Burbure → Auchy-au-Bois. No company men killed in 1918.

## Gaps and discrepancies (see each transcript's notes)

- **3 July 1916:** no diary entry; no work report for the night of 3–4 July either.
- **June:** no work report for the night of 19–20 June. Appendix B sheets cited as #41–#75 in the diary text are not the sheets bound at pp. 40–56 (numbered 1–12, first week of June) — a separate, earlier sequence.
- **July:** Appendix C report numbers not always in date order; duplicate photographs (pp. 48, 57 repeat pp. 47, 56).
- **August:** p. 132 photographed upside down, content largely bleed-through — no report content recoverable from it. Report #80 (p. 158) near-duplicates #79 (p. 157). Appendix D contents-page date range (1–26 Aug) is incomplete — reports run 31 Jul–1 Aug to 24–25 Aug.
- **September:** contents page lists Diary Text 22 pages and appendices A1–A5 / B.1–B.5, but 96 pages are present (diary text pp. 4–26; A1–A6 pp. 28–73; B1–B15 pp. 75–96).
- **October:** contents page lists Diary Text 59 pages, but the diary text is 40 pages (pp. 4–43) and the appendix 49 pages (pp. 44–92); work reports cited as #31–#33 (28–31 Oct) are not among the appendix pages. Printed form page numbers run one behind the LAC image page numbers throughout.
- **Robertson/Robinson:** both spellings occur in June ("Lieut Robertson" mostly, "Lieut Robinson" 4, 7, 16 June) — transcribed as written; whether one man or two is not stated in the diary.
- **May:** "C.S.W." in the Remarks column — meaning unclear (not the O.C.'s initials).
