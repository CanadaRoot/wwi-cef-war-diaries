# War Diary Transcript — 9th Field Company, Canadian Engineers (Shorncliffe), January 1918

- **Unit:** 9th Field Company, Canadian Engineers, 3rd Canadian Division
- **LAC Item ID:** 2005998, RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4999, file 669, microfilm reels T-10841–T-10843
- **Month / Volume:** January 1918, Volume 21
- **Pages in this file:** LAC images pp. 797–889 (93 pages: cover, contents, inner title page, hut-lining diagram plate, 20 diary text pages, appendix cover + duplicate diagram plate + 63 work-report sheets, two Operation Orders, and printed Nissen bow hut diagrams and instructions)

The appendix work reports are handwritten on squared paper, in pencil in places, and are faint or crowded in places. Page numbers in this transcript are the LAC image page numbers.

### les Brebis, France — 2-1-18

Weather: Clear in forenoon, cloudy in afternoon.
9th C.I. Bde. G.39-35 received setting out policy and allotment of work in 9th Bde area whereby garrisons of front line maintain trenches and wire of front line and communication trenches back to reserve trench. 123rd Cdn Pioneer Battalion to wire and construct a support trench as set out. 9th Field Coy C.E. and attached details (Infantry) are allotted the work of constructing the reserve or blue line, wire and trenches from the Brigade Southern boundary to the Brigade Northern boundary.
Pursuant to Divisional Orders, 9th C.I. Bde G.39-15 permanent working party of 100 other ranks from the 9th C.I. Bde reported today to live with the Coy at Advanced Billets. This party is composed of 25 men from each of the four Battalions of the 9th Brigade and to work direct under orders of O.C. 9th Field Coy C.E.

### les Brebis, France — 3-1-18

Weather: Clear and warm. N.W. wind.
The work of wiring the reserve line is continuing satisfactorily but a great deal of work is required in thickening in places as the existing at present forms a very poor obstacle.
The heavy artillery and trench mortars had a concentrated shoot today on the suspected location of the enemy gas projecting apparatus. Judging by the enemy retaliation it was successful.
Lieut Walley with No 2 Section and parties from the 116th and 58th Battalions Wiring, fixing gas curtains and carrying material.

INSTRUCTIONS FOR ERECTING NISSEN BOW HUTS, MARK III.

1. Lay outer bearers. Unless the ground is flat, it will be quicker to level up by inserting pickets, packing of wood or bricks under the bearers, rather than to level the ground. All that will be necessary in this respect is to fill in any hollows which may allow water to collect under the hut.

2. Lay and level up centre bearers.

3. Lay floor joists and skew-nail at ends to bearers.

4. Bolt up rib sections. Raise and bolt to sills.

5. Lay floor panels on joists, notching where necessary around bows.

6. Fix top purlin only to ribs, taking care that the shank of the hook-bolt is tight up against the web of T iron, and that the washer is in position between the purlin and the bow. Hammer the hook end of the bolt into the underside of purlin, to prevent it slipping when tightening up the nut. Cut off the bolt above nut. Some purlins may be inaccurately bored and should be remedied.

7. Strut bow temporarily with floor joists from other huts or any timber handy.

8. Fix corrugated iron lining, after first straightening out all dents in both sheets and slides. Commence with the bottom sheet in each bay by placing it in position on edge between the webs of the T ribs; secure this in position by inserting nails in the two lower holes of ribs, provided for this purpose. To join the next sheet spring back the top edge of the lower sheet and slip on the slide from the end; slip in the second sheet and place down on ribs in position; insert further nails so that the lower sheet and the lower portion of the second sheet are secured. Add another slide in a similar manner and then the third sheet. The lower purlin should now be placed and bolted up after which the remaining two sheets and purlins can be added similarly.

To complete lining at the top, lay another sheet over the opening and secure with nails through holes in ribs—no slides are required.

9. Fix the remaining purlins in a similar manner to operation 6.

10. Erect end panels. If made inaccurately, get the two window panels fixed accurately first, equally distant from the centre, and alter outer panels if they do not fit. The door to be adjusted.

11. Fix the corrugated iron outer sheeting. Commencing with two bottom sheets, at one end of hut, place them in position to allow for an overhang of 8 inches at the gable, and so that the sheets overlap by two corrugations. Fix with eye-screws and diamond washers on the ridge of corrugations, at the bottom end of sheets only, one eye-screw through the overlap, which is held down by the diamond washer. The eye-screw to be screwed into purlin. Then fix the remaining bottom sheets similarly and successively, giving also an overlap laterally of two corrugations to each sheet.

Place the top covering corrugated iron sheets similarly, overlapping the bottom sheets. Fix with diamond washers and eye-screws, the eye-screws passing through both overlaps, i.e., four thicknesses of iron, and screwed into purlin (one eye-screw passing through both overlaps). The eye-screws at the gable ends pass through one overlap only. No eye-screws are provided or required for fixing the bottom row of corrugated iron sheets to the lower purlin.

12. Each bow should be examined before erection to see whether there are any inaccuracies of manufacture, in case the angle plates are out of true. Unless this is remedied it may be impossible to get in the lining.

13. The edges of the floor board panels at each end of hut must project five inches beyond the batten to obtain correct fitting.

14. Whitewash corrugated iron lining.

15. The boards for Shelf and Rifle Rack are not included in the shipment so must be provided locally—See drawing on page 30 for details.

- **Notable events:** Concentrated heavy-artillery/trench-mortar shoot on a suspected enemy gas-projector site, 3 January ("judging by the enemy retaliation it was successful"); a 100-man permanent working party from the 9th Brigade attached 2 January; reserve-line ("blue line") wiring finished with three belts of wire, 19 January; taping out of the Red Line tactical scheme (1,800 yds), 17 January; relief of the 9th by the 1st Field Company C.E. at Le Brebis on 21/22 January (Operation Order, 18 January); the company move on 22 January under First Army orders — Nos 2 & 3 Sections to Annezin (First Army R.E.
