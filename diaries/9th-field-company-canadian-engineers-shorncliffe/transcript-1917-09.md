### France, Bully-Grenay — 1-9-17

Weather: Cool and cloudy with showers. West wind.
3rd Canadian Divisional Engineers Operation Order No 43 Copy No 3. dated 1-9-17 received whereby the 1st Canadian Division will relieve the 3rd Canadian Division from the Lens-Bethune Road, exclusive, to Nestor trench inclusive. Relief to be complete by 6 a.m. 6-9-17. and particularly that the 9th Field Company C.E. and Brigade Wiring party now working with this Coy will be relieved by the 3rd Field Coy C.E. Relief to be completed by 6 a.m. 6-9-17: also that the 9th Field Coy C.E. and the Brigade wiring party will relieve the 210th Field Coy R.E. in the Right Brigade area of the 21st English Division, Neuville-St-Vaast area. Relief to be complete by 6 a.m. 6-9-17. 9th Field Coy C.E. to march to that area on that date, being clear of present billets by 9 a.m.
Lieut Steeves with one Sapper attended at Hqrs 52nd Battn and explained to Officers and men of that Battalion the method

- Page 1 is the cover; page 2 the inner title page; page 3 the contents page. Diary text runs on pages 4–34 (31 pages, matching the contents page "Diary Text — 31 Pages"). Page 35 is the appendix cover. Pages 36–62 are 27 Officers' Work Report sheets (reports #1–#27).
- Diary text covers 1–30 September 1917 with no date gaps. The diary text is complete for the month within these pages; the appendix continues beyond this part (reports #28–90 are on pages 63–125, another agent's assignment).
- The appendix sheets carry sheet numbers 1–27 at top right, EXCEPT the four wiring reports (pages 43, 46, 55, 59), which carry no visible sheet number. Their diary-text references are reports #8, #11, #20 and #24 respectively.
- Contents-page note: the contents page names the unit "9th Field Company C.E. 2nd Canadian Divn." — a slip in the original; the unit is the 3rd Canadian Division throughout (cover and diary text agree on 3rd Canadian Division).
- Split entries (diary text continuing across two pages): 1, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 14, 15, 17, 18, 20, 21 and 28 September.

### Places and map references
- Place: Bully-Grenay, France, 1–5 September; then A.14.a.5.6. (Sheet 51 B) from 6 September. The map reference changes to A.14.a.5.9. on 19 September, back to .6 on 20–21 September, then .9 from 22 September on; transcribed as written per page.
- The 6 September march: sappers and wiring party marched over Lorette Ridge; cyclists and drivers by road via Gouy Servins and Villers-au-Bois Station. Rear billets of the 210th Field Company R.E. at A.14.a.5.6. were "of an inferior quality" — "Bivies" [uncertain] and several Armstrong huts, horse standings unfinished.

### Casualties (diary text and appendix, this part)
- Killed: #541767 2/Cpl Sinker, H. — killed by shell fire in the front line, 22 September; buried 24 September in Aux Reitz Corner Cemetery (Ref Map 51 B France A.9.c.5.9.), Grave No.1, Row G.
- Died of wounds: #507708 Dvr Scarrott, J.H. — wounded by shell fire 24 September when returning with a ration wagon from Advanced Billets; died of wounds 25 September, reported 28 September from O.C. #42 Casualty Clearing Station.
- Wounded (diary text): #505953 Spr Hooley, S.F. — wounded by shell fire, evacuated, 17 September.
- Wounded (appendix only, unnamed): 1 man, 116th Bn, night of 1–2 September (Report #2, p. 37); 1 wounded, night of 10–11 September (Report #19, p. 54); 1 wounded, night of 12–13 September (Report #27, p. 62).
- Sick: #327197 Spr Lamb J.H. (10 September); #405948 Dvr. Hewitt, H. (24 September).
- Declared unfit for front-line work / transferred to Canadian Base: #502708 Spr Rawstron A. and #501052 Spr Richardson, C.H. (3 September).
- Struck off strength: #869897 Spr McHattie T. — transferred to 5th Army Troops Coy (15 September). Lieut H.D. Robertson — injured, evacuated to England, struck off from 20-8-17 (reported 14 September).
- Reinforcements/arrivals: three unnamed reinforcements from Cdn. Base (15 September); Lieut J.E. Sproule arrived 21 September vice Lieut. J.N. Alford, wounded.
- Leave: Capt. Ross to England 3 September, returned 16 September; Lieut Jones to Scotland 12 September, returned 23 September; O.C. (Major Robertson) to England 25 September; Capt. Ross then went up to Advanced Billets to live.

### Notable events
- 1st Canadian Division relieving 3rd Canadian Division (Lens-Bethune Road to Nestor trench) by 6 a.m. 6 September; 3rd Field Company C.E. relieving 9th Field Coy; 9th Field Coy relieving 210th Field Company R.E. in the Right Brigade area of the 21st English Division, Neuville-St-Vaast area.
- 52nd Battalion raid on enemy trenches near Cinnibar trench planned for 12:30 a.m. 4 September; new jumping-off trench opposite Cinnibar trench put across on the night of 1 September.
- New front-line / jumping-off trench work abandoned on instructions, 14 September; new support line and new communication trench ordered under the new policy of works, 24 September.
- 31st Division discharged gas from gas projectors at about 12:40 a.m. on 14 September into enemy lines just north of Fresnoy.
- Enemy raid on Totnes trench on the evening of 17/18 September after a fifty-minute barrage: one wounded man captured, one enemy prisoner left behind.
- 9th C.I. Bde relieved by 7th C.I. Bde, 17–18 September.
- 3rd Canadian Divisional Sports held near Berthonval Farm, 21 September.
- Two captive balloons shot down in flames north-west of the Company's billets on 27 September, observers parachuting in safety; the enemy aeroplane brought down, pilot slightly wounded and made prisoner.
- Sappers living in the trench north of Farbus moved out under protest on 12 September; the Company was ordered out of the Gun Pits advanced billets into the Brown Line in front of the railway embankment on 20 September.
