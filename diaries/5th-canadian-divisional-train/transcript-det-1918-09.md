## Diary text

Place: Sheet 51C L18 Central, STUART CAMP (1–3 Sept.); Sheet 51B H33 d 7.6 (4–20 Sept.); Sheet 51B O32 d 1.0 (21–27 Sept.); D4 a 2.6, Sheet 57C (28–30 Sept.).

### 1 September 1918

Weather - fine and windy. 2 G.S. wagons received from Ordnance to replace casualties at LE QUESNE [uncertain] Nothing further to record. G.N.C.

### 2 September 1918

Weather - fine, cool and windy. Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. in reference to general moving of artillery. G.N.C.

### 3 September 1918

Weather occasional rain. Visited all Battery wagon lines in forward area, 13th Bde & 14th Bde. in front of MONCHY. Lunched with 66th Battery. Saw many German prisoners. On way through VIS EN ARTOIS a German aeroplane came over and got two of our balloons then machine gunned the main road. Very unhealthy for a few minutes. G.N.C. One of the observers came down from balloon near convoy in charge of Lt. Ross. Lt. Paterson still indisposed. G.N.C.

### 4 September 1918

Weather fine during day. Company moved to H33 d 7.6 today. Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. and 5th M.T. Company in afternoon. All men under earth cover. Water rather scarce here G.N.C.

### 5 September 1918

Weather fair. Lorries dumped today in place of remaining loaded over night. Nothing further to record. G.N.C.

### 6 September 1918

Weather warm and occasional rain. Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. rear and made a reconnaissance of [uncertain] area with view to moving Company in near future. (Wancourt; Heninel.) G.N. Cooper Major.

### 7 September 1918

[The first two lines of this entry are heavily struck through and illegible.]

Weather fine in morning. Visited Railhead Frevin Capelle with Capt. Cole in morning visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. (rear) in late afternoon also making a further reconnaissance of Heninel area. Discovered a good place for Company if a move is necessary. Rain towards 9 P.M. but weather cleared about midnight permitting E.A. to carry out a little bombing in near vicinity.

### 8 September 1918

Weather dull and windy. Wagons moved off at 7.30 a.m. today 1/2 hour earlier than previous two mornings. Enemy shelling cross roads in near vicinity of camp. G.N.C.

### 9 September 1918

Weather fair with occasional showers. Nothing of importance transpired.

### 10 September 1918

Weather Cold and showers. Lorries dumped this afternoon wagons refilled. 3 years ago today I arrived in France for 1st time with 2nd Cdn. Divl. Train.

### 11 September 1918

Weather unsettled. Capt. Cole and myself visited the Caves of Arras with Father Macpherson & Padre 14th Bde. Visited 4th Cdn. C.C.S. in reference to discussion of Lieut Paterson. Horses being placed in trenches and shell holes for protection against bombs. G.N.C.

### 12 September 1918

Weather - dull, wet and cold. Lt. C.A. Paterson admitted 4th Cdn. C.C.S. this date sick. 17679 Brown W. admitted 13th Cdn. Fd. Ambulance this date. G.N.C.

### 13 September 1918

Weather - fair and cold. Visited O i/c C.A.S.C. Personnel in afternoon in reference to a new officer to replace Lieut Paterson. Advised that Lieut Paterson was being recalled to England for duty, as a result of my recommendation forwarded while at LE QUESNEL. Called in to see Lieut. Paterson at C.C.S. on way home to advise him of conditions. G.N.C.

### 14 September 1918

Weather - dull and cool. 13th Bde. C.F.A. rations delivered to their new location at AGNY (rear areas) Convoy in charge Lt. Love. 14th Bde. C.F.A. convoy in charge of C.S.M. Davidson Wrote H.Q. 5th C.D.A. in reference to shortage of 16 horses. G.N.C.

### 15 September 1918

Weather - fine and warm. Considerable air activity over camp in morning. One of our machines forced down by an E.A. in near vicinity of [uncertain]. Numerous machines over in night owing to moonlight. Visited 4th Cdn. C.C.S. with Col. Hanson O.C. 13th Bde. C.F.A. as [uncertain] two Major Generals from Army, these are [uncertain] General. Visited Batteries of 13th Bde. in afternoon. G.N.C.

### 16 September 1918

Weather - fine and warm. Arrows (Horse T) being placed on all wagons. also [uncertain] Rum Rags for walk. A typewriter brought into use by Company for first time. Salved in LE QUESNEL Wood in August. G.N. Cooper Major.

### 17 September 1918

Weather fine and moderately warm. Heavy Electrical storm through the night. Practically all dug outs flooded including my own. G.N.C. Lieut Love in Aubigny for day. Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. in reference to "leave" for Capt. Cole and other ranks. Canteen opened in evening G.N.C.

### 18 September 1918

Weather fine and warm. Renumbering of horses and wagons commenced today. Nothing further to note G.N.C.

### 19 September 1918

Weather unsettled. 440824 Driver Wright C.F. proceeded on leave to England. Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. General borrowed car till 5 P.M. rather inconvenient. 14th Bde. rations delivered to new wagon lines in N29.17.16.8-10. this date. G.N.C. [uncertain]

### 20 September 1918

Weather unsettled. 515035 Dvr. Theakston A.L. proceeded to England on leave. Lt. Paterson's kit forwarded. Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. with reference to moves of Batteries etc.

### 21 September 1918

Weather Rain in early morning, clearing in afternoon. Reveille 4 a.m. Company moved to O32d 1.0 this morning moving off at 7 a.m. arriving 10.15 a.m. Lorries dumped in Company lines. 13th Bde moved to WANCOURT area, 14th Bde. remained in vicinity of WANCOURT. Both Brigades moving at night to U12 b 4 d. and V7 a 9 c. H.Q. D.A.C. No 3 Sect. & T.M.B. moved night 21/22 to U10 b. No 1 Section U19 c [uncertain] No 2 Section U17 c. Wagons reported back to CHERISY on off loading. G.N.C.

### 22 September 1918

Weather - fine & windy. Men fairly well under cover. Horses being dug in. 874516 Dvr. Mackintosh P. admitted hospital yesterday (21st) Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A.

### 23 September 1918

Weather fine, windy and cool. Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. Sgt. Ball 85 O.R. proceeded to BRUGES for 17 Remounts. 16 H.D. for unit. Riding for H.Q. 5th C.D.A.

### 24 September 1918

Weather fine and cool. Visited 14th Bde H.Q. in afternoon. 3656 Dvr. Graham H.E. proceeded on leave this date to England. Nothing of importance to relate. G.N.C.

### 25 September 1918

Weather fine, windy & cool. Batteries of 14th Bde move to vicinity of REINCOURT. G.N.C. Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. to secure services of Engineer Officer to inspect dugouts in our present lines. Two found to contain packages of [uncertain] placed behind sets in wall. G.N.C. 1282264 Brown G.J. new car driver reports from 4th M.T. Coy to replace 5149 Ewing A.J. despatched to No 2 Div. M.T. Coy

### 26 September 1918

Weather fine. Visited Battery wagon lines. Horse struck by [uncertain] lorry and slightly hurt in near hind. G.N.C. 16 H.D. horses received from Remount Depot to replace [uncertain] casualties in Amiens show G.N.C.

### 27 September 1918

Weather fine. Went to Rull... [uncertain] with Capt. Cole this a.m. Remounts matched up in afternoon Col. W.A. Sm... [uncertain] D.S.O. & [uncertain] Argyll House London paid us a visit this afternoon. Made a reconnaissance of forward wagon lines in afternoon with a view to moving Company up in morning. G.N.C.

### 28 September 1918

Weather cold. Capt. E.P. Cole proceeds on leave to Paris and Marseilles. I take over Supply duties. Company moved from present location at 7.30 a.m. to D4 a 2.6 (Sheet 57 C)

[Continued on det-page-0116:]

Brigades in neighborhood of Bourlon Wood. Evacuated my riding horse to 2nd Cdn. Mobile Vet. as a result of accident a few days ago. Fine dugouts for men. Visited H.Q. Div. Arty.

### 29 September 1918

Weather fine and cool in morning. Went to Railhead in morning Pay parade held this afternoon. Batteries did not move yesterday. Visited wagon lines of Brigades at Bourlon Wood. G.N.C.

### 30 September 1918

Weather unsettled and dull. Went to FREVIN CAPELLE Railhead this a.m. No pack for us. Instructed by D.D.S. & T. 1st to draw from DUISANS went there and drew a portion of our rations. Sent on to ARRAS and drew balance. Took car to 4th M.T. Coy for new spring Lt. A.K. Reed [uncertain] reported this date for duty — vice Lieut. C.A. Paterson invalided to England sick. G.N.C.

> G.N. Cooper Major.
>
> O.C. 5th Cdn. Divl. Train Det.

## Transcriber's notes — September 1918

- All 30 days are covered. The first two lines of the 7 September entry are heavily struck through and illegible; the legible text follows. The 15 September entry contains two [uncertain] passages (a place name and a phrase about two Major Generals).
- Officers named: Major G. N. Cooper (O.C.); Capt. E. P. Cole (Supply Officer, on leave to Paris and Marseilles from 28 Sept.); Lieut. C. A. Paterson (admitted 4th Cdn. C.C.S. sick 12 Sept.; invalided to England sick, replaced 30 Sept. by Lieut. A. K. Reed [uncertain]); Lieut. T. Love; Lieut. Ross; Col. Hanson (O.C. 13th Bde. C.F.A.); Father Macpherson; Padre 14th Bde.
- Other ranks: Dvr. Wright C.F. (440824, leave to England); Dvr. Theakston A.L. (515035, leave); Dvr. Mackintosh P. (874516, admitted hospital 21 Sept.); Dvr. Graham H.E. (3656, leave); Dvr. Brown W. (17679, admitted 13th Cdn. Field Ambulance 12 Sept.); Sgt. Ball (with 85 O.R. to Bruges for 17 remounts); Brown G.J. (1282264, new car driver from 4th M.T. Coy); Ewing A.J. (5149, to No. 2 Div. M.T. Coy).
- Moves: H33 d 7.6 (4 Sept.); O32 d 1.0 near Wancourt/Cherisy (21 Sept.); D4 a 2.6 near Bourlon Wood (28 Sept.).
- Notable content: a German aeroplane shot down two British observation balloons and machine-gunned the main road at Vis-en-Artois on 3 Sept.; horses sheltered in trenches and shell holes against bombing; the company acquired its first typewriter (salvaged from Le Quesnel Wood); a heavy electrical storm flooded the dugouts on the night of 17 Sept.; 16 heavy draught horses received from the Remount Depot to replace the Amiens casualties.
