## Diary text

Place: CAGNY, France, Sheet 62D S4B85.80 [uncertain] (1–7 Aug.); Sheet 62D (8 Aug.); Sheet 66E K7B 20.50 [uncertain] (10–18 Aug.); Sheet 66E K7G 2.6 (18–21 Aug.); Sheet 62D T18 C 2.2 (22 Aug.); SALOUEL (23 Aug.); SAVY (24 Aug.); K11C 5.5, Sheet 51C (25–28 Aug.); L18 Central, Sheet 51C, Stuart Camp (29–31 Aug.).

### 1 August 1918

Reorganization of Company effective today. G.N.C. Arrived SALEUX approximately 2.30 a.m. detrained and proceeded to CAGNY by Route march, commencing 5 a.m. Fed and watered men and horses just west of Dury 6.45 to 8 a.m. Arrived CAGNY at approx. 11 a.m. Going into an orchard on Cagny - Boves Road near Cagny. Headquarters of Units situated as follows. H.Q. 5th C.D.A. Chateau No 19 Rue de la Belle-BOUTILLERIE. H.Q. 13th Bde. No 15. ditto. 14th Bde. No 11 Rue de Frederic [uncertain] - Cagny. H.Q. Dal. 1000x West of Cagny Church. 5th T.M.B. ditto. 5th C.D. Train Road Cagny - Boves near "R" of Avre River. Rations delivered to all units and wagons reported back to Company. Railhead today SALEUX. G.N.C.

[Marginal references: App. I. and App. II.]

### 2 August 1918

Weather - wet. Rations delivered to units in present location at 4 P.M. Nothing new regarding further moves. Lorries cleared Railhead and everything running smoothly. G.N.C.

### 3 August 1918

Weather - wet. Lorries dumped at 7 a.m. Refilled at 10 a.m. delivering to Units immediately. Visited 14th Bde. and Section D.A.C. with Lt. Paterson in afternoon. Nothing further to report. G.N.C.

### 4 August 1918

Weather - fine and warm. Refilled 10 a.m. 2nd [uncertain] advance parties arrive here. Lt. Col. [uncertain] Bde. [?] Lt. Col. Jarvis, A.P.M. Major Ruspin D.A.A.G. and Capt [uncertain] 5th Bde. all of 2nd Cdn. Div. lunched and dined with us to-day. No [uncertain] 2nd Div. [uncertain] near us. G.N.C. 4th Anniversary of war.

### 5 August 1918

Weather. Rain. Refilled 10.30 a.m. Lieut. Paterson goes on leave to-day 7 a.m. Nothing further to record. G.N.C.

### 6 August 1918

Weather. Rain. Refilled 10 a.m. Appointed President of a Court Martial to assemble at rest H.Q. 14th Bde. on 7th Aug. G.N.C.

### 7 August 1918

Weather. fine. - Court Martial cancelled. Visited 14th Bde H.Q. and H.Q. 5th C.D.A. in morning. Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. in reference to forthcoming moves of Division. H.Q. moved to LONGEAU. G.N.C.

### 8 August 1918

Weather, fine. Company moved from CAGNY to T2C 5.8 leaving at 7 P.M. arriving 12 P.M. Brigades in T21 and T22. Rations delivered to these locations, wagons reporting back to our new location. Supply Lorries failed to report at Railhead. Capt. Cole rectified oversight G.N.C.

### 9 August 1918

Weather fine. Company moved to U13 C 1.6 moving off 8.15 a.m. Lorries dumped here on morning 10th H.Q. in Boves [uncertain] Visited ground captured yesterday. [uncertain]

### 10 August 1918

Weather fine. Company moved off 8.30 a.m. loaded, to K7 B 3.7 arriving at about 11.30 a.m. Brigades in vicinity of K4 B. G.N.C. Bombing very severe.

[Continued on det-page-0094:]

to 142182. Driver Warwick A. on command to 55th Battery, and 2 H.D. horses killed by an Enemy Bomb. Warwick buried in BEAUFORT Church. G.N.C. Bombing severe.

### 11 August 1918

Weather fine - Several Batteries made local moves. Company in same location. Nothing further to report except E. aircraft busy. 67611 Pte. Orton J. wounded by Enemy machine gun bullet fired from a low flying E. aeroplane. Bombing in near vicinity very severe. G.N.C.

### 12 August 1918

Weather fine. Owing to bombing in near vicinity and our lines being rather exposed, reconnoitered ground a little further back. Nothing suitable. Left 4 P.M. arrived back 7 P.M. Visited Batteries in morning. Bombing bad. G.N.C.

### 13 August 1918

Weather fine. Visited Batteries in morning. Witnessed one of our planes brought down over Battery lines by 8 E.A. Moved Company across the road into thick wood. G.N.C.

### 14 August 1918

Weather fine - Lt. Col. McAlpine called on me today. Nothing to report except Maxwell motor car #75143 was condemned by S.M.T.O. and forwarded to Base. G.N.C.

### 15 August 1918

Weather bad + fine. Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. (rear) in latter part of afternoon. Nothing to report. Bombing in near area still going on. G.N.C.

### 16 August 1918

Weather fine. Visited Forward H.Q. 5th C.D.A. and 14th Bde. C.F.A. H.Q. this morning with Capt. [uncertain] of 14th Bde. Heavy firing by all guns. Barrage placed on wood nearby where enemy was assembling troops Ford Car. #55598 taken on charge from 4th M.T. Coy, replacing Maxwell car evacuated. G.N.C.

### 17 August 1918

Weather, Cool and fair. Visited Batteries of 13th Bde C.F.A. with Capt. Cole. Sun Cloud, at night; no aeroplanes over for which all ranks are very thankful. Nothing further to report. G.N.C.

### 18 August 1918

Weather, Cool and fair. Went to CAGNY to fix up billeting. Very cloudy about 8 P.M. everybody hoping it would rain to keep aeroplanes away. Cleared up about midnight - with moon very bright. About 12.30 A.M. Enemy aeroplane circles over wood and field occupied by Company, dropping at least five bombs (Spring and heavy) One big bomb hit in wood midst-Company killing 12 horses outright and wounding five men as follows. 515092 Dvr. Cox P.D. 511691 Dvr. Hunt G.J. 331238 Dvr. Whitfield C. (Car driver) 2040508 Gnr. Bannister W.O. (5th Batt) 311419 Gnr. Hollinger J. (610th Batt) [uncertain] Ford injured G.N.C.

[Continued on det-page-0096:]

1 H.D. horse on command to H.Q. 5th C.D.A.C. killed by Enemy shell fire about 7 P.M. tonight. G.N.C. Recalled all Baggage teams at present attached to Batteries to enable us to carry on. Four G.S. wagons put out of use by bomb, two of which were practically demolished. G.N.C.

### 19 August 1918

Weather - fair and a little cloudy. A year ago today received Order to proceed to France with 5th Divl. Artillery, accompanied by Lt. F.A. Walker. G.N.C. Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. (Forward) with Capt. Cole. G.N.C.

### 20 August 1918

Weather fine. Visited 5th M.T. Coy. in reference to driver for car to replace Whitfield evacuated. Beautiful night, but moon very unpopular with everybody, hot. bothered with E.A. at all. Secured first real food [uncertain] since coming into this sector.

### 21 August 1918

Weather fine and very warm. Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. with reference to forthcoming move. Orders received to move. Left present location 9 P.M. Bivouacing on side of Road at T18 C 2.2 arrived 1 a.m. Visited H.Q. [uncertain] Blankets at 3.30 a.m. 22nd very tired. Moon very bright - Brigades in U21 and U30. D.A.C. & T.M.B. less No 3 Sect & H.Q. in DOMART. G.N. Cooper Major.

[The date column on det-page-0096 shows 20, then 21 struck through, then 22; the 21 August entry appears to be the continuation of the 20 August entry, and the "22nd" in the text refers to the morning of 22 August.]

### 22 August 1918

Weather - fine and moderately warm. Refilled in this location in morning delivering to Units. Moved off at 4 P.M. for SALOUEL, empty wagons, arriving there at 10 P.M. Lt. Paterson reports back from leave tonight. Brigades in SALOUEL (13th Bde) 14th Bde. in Pont des METZ also D.A.C. + T.M.B. G.N.C.

### 23 August 1918

Weather fine and moderately warm. Refilled here in morning wagons returning and loading up and returning to their respective Units again, remaining with them, and entraining with them. H.Q. of Company entrained at SALEUX on train No 1. for AUBIGNY. Capt. Cole and myself motored to Aubigny arriving there at 9.30 P.M. We left Salouel at 6.30 P.M. dining at Doullens [uncertain].

### 24 August 1918

Company marched to SAVY. Brigades assembling at SAVY. G.N.C. Weather - fine - no delivery today, Units entrained. Wagons being collected as they detrain. G.N.C.

### 25 August 1918

Weather fine and cool. Company moved to old lines and dump in Camp near Arras. Company moved to K11C 5.5 in afternoon moving off from Bray at 2.30 P.M. Wagons reporting back to new location G.N.C.

[The 25 August entry as written is confused — "BRAY moving off at [?] 7 a.m. Delivered to Brigades in Stuart [uncertain] Camp near Arras. Company moved to K11C 5.5 in afternoon moving off from Bray at 2.30 P.M." — transcribed as read.]

### 26 August 1918

Weather cloudy and moderately cool. Refilled this location. Visited H.Q. 5th C.D.A. Brigades having wagon lines East of ARRAS. Wagon arrived back fairly late. G.N.C.

### 27 August 1918

Weather fine and moderately cool. Company in same location Batteries all in same location Nothing further to record. G.N.C.

### 28 August 1918

Weather - dull and occasional showers. Visited all Batteries and Battle H.Q.'s of 5th C.D.A. 13th & 14th Bdes. C.F.A. returning 6 P.M. Batteries fairly well forward for present location of Company. G.N.C.

### 29 August 1918

Weather moderately cool and fine. Company moved from K11 C 5.5 to L18 Central (Sheet 51C) in morning. Fairly comfortable. Visited Can. Corps H.Q. in afternoon Calling upon O i/c C.A.S.C. Personnel in connection with new driver for Car and things in general affecting reinforcements G.N.C.

### 30 August 1918

Weather - fine and moderately warm. Shelling in vicinity of Battery wagon lines. All wagons returned safe. G.N.C.

### 31 August 1918

Weather - Cool and dull. Lt. Paterson indisposed. Lt. Love in charge of convoy. 808536 A/Cpl. Drewes. C. reverts to permanent grade of driver at own request. Nothing further to record.

> G.N. Cooper Major.

### Section Officers — standing orders — det-page-0104-August_1918_p._15.jpg

1. Each Section Officer is directly responsible to the O.C. Company for the working efficiency of his Section, i.e. that his Section is properly equipped with mens equipment, vehicles, and spare parts, harness, saddlery and stores, that these and the horses are properly looked after, and that the men of his Section are efficient in footdrill, wagon drill, rifle and firing exercises, equitation, care of horses, harnessing, saddling, grooming, and laying out of horse lines.

To ensure this, he should take advantage of every opportunity possible to instruct his N.C.Os. and men in the above subjects.

2. He is responsible for the correct maintenance of discipline (this includes cleanliness of men, quarters, stables, etc of the N.C.Os. and men of his Section).

He will discourage social intercourse between the N.C.Os. and men.

He will initial all passes of N.C.Os. and men.

He will hold Section Office, when necessary, and have any N.C.O. and men of his Section, who wish to see the O.C. of the Company, in connection with matters of Routine or complaints, and any N.C.O. or men of his Section who require "telling off", paraded before him (if he can settle these matters himself he will do so). This Office will be held at such a time as to allow any matters for decision by the O.C. of the Company to come before him at his Company Orderly Room, which will in future be held at 9 a.m. conditions permitting.

He will always bear in mind that courtesy is indispensable to discipline.

3. He will be responsible for the mobility and cleanliness of all vehicles, animals, harness and other equipment under his charge.

He will see that all grooming kits in his Section are dipped in a 3 per cent solution of creosol on completion of evening stables, daily, that all harness is washed in a 10 per cent solution of the same kind once weekly, and that all stables standings, and picqueting gear is treated with the same solution and at same intervals.

He will visit the stables and quarters, and inspect the horse, wagons, harness etc., of his Section daily.

He will consult the O.C. Company on all matters pertaining to policy and receive his concurrence in these before taking any definite action.

He will keep the O.C. Company fully informed daily of all matters pertaining to his Section.

### Orderly Officer — det-page-0105-August_1918_p._16.jpg

1. The Orderly Officer of the day is directly responsible to the O.C., for the discipline of the Company during his tour of duty. He will send in a written report to the O.C., in which will be entered the names of any absentees, any complaints or irregularities which may be brought to his notice during his tour of duty.

2. He will be present at Reveille, Tattoo, and Lights Out and will receive Report from the Orderly Sergeant.

3. He will be present at all Company and Regimental duty (Guard or Picquet Mounting and Stables etc) parades, and will receive Report from the Orderly Sergeant.

4. He will visit the mens quarters and cookhouses accompanied by the Orderly Sergeant, during the morning.

5. He will visit the Guard or Picquet and Stables, accompanied by the N.C.O. in charge, once by night (after Lights Out). He will visit the Mess Room accompanied by the Orderly Sergeant during each Meal, and investigate any complaints.

6. He will not leave the Camp during his tour of duty.

### Section Sergeants — det-page-0105-August_1918_p._16.jpg (continued)

1. Each Section Sergeant is directly responsible to his Section Commander for the working efficiency and correct maintenance of discipline (this includes cleanliness of men, quarters, and stables etc) of his section.

2. He will parade his Section at Reveille and Lights Out and report any absentees, to the Orderly Sergeant.

3. He will parade before his Section Commander at Section Office any N.C.O. or man who wishes to see the O.C. Company on matters of Routine or complaints, of any men who require "telling off".

4. He will parade his section 10 minutes before "Fall In" for every parade and report to the Orderly Sergeant the names of any men who are absent, or any irregularities. If detailed for duty elsewhere, the next senior N.C.O. to take his place.

5. On calling the Roll at Reveille, he will take down the names of any men who wish passes, make these out, and submit for consideration and initialling, if recommended by Section Commanders, giving them, after they are initialled, to the Orderly Sergeant. (Section Sergeants can, for the present, have passes made out in Orderly Room.)

6. On calling the Roll at Reveille, he will take the names of any men who are reporting themselves sick and give written reports to the Orderly Sergeant.

7. He will inform the Section Commander of any men of his Section who are to be brought up on a charge and will notify the Orderly Sergeant also, if necessary.

### Orderly Sergeant — det-page-0106-August_1918_p._17.jpg

1. The Orderly Sergeant will be responsible to the Orderly Officer of the day, and to the C.S.M., that all duties are properly carried out.

2. He will take the Report from the Senior N.C.O. of each Section at Reveille, and at all parades, including stable parades) if there are any absences or irregularities, he will report to the Orderly Officer of the day and to the C.S.M.

3. He will attend Company Orderly Room and is responsible to the C.S.M. that prisoners and evidence are paraded at the proper hour and that the C.S.M. receives crime sheets properly made out.

4. He will receive from the Section Sergeants and hand to the C.S.M., all passes recommended by the Section Commanders.

5. He will warn N.C.Os. and men for Guard, Picquets, Fatigues etc., and will parade all these for inspection at the proper hour.

6. He will accompany the Orderly Officer on his tour of inspection and also on his tour of duties, respecting Guards, Picquets, Stables, etc., until Lights Out, preceding him and calling the N.C.Os. and men to attention.

7. He will furnish the C.S.M. with a list of absentees; for this purpose he will keep a list of all men legally absent i.e. on guard, in arrest, in detention, etc., so that he can give a proper report.

8. He will parade and drill defaulters at the appointed hour or employ them on fatigue if so ordered.

9. He will furnish the Commander of the Guard with a list of defaulters.

10. He will receive from each Section Sergeant at Reveille, a list of men who are reporting themselves sick and will parade these before the M.O. at the appointed hour.

11. He will visit the Cookhouse at a quarter of an hour before meals, and see that they are being prepared. He will visit the Cookhouse after tea and see that it is clean and that the regulations generally re Cookhouses are being complied with.

12. He will see that the meals for men on guard etc., are prepared and sent off at the proper time.

13. He must not on any account leave Camp during his tour of duties.

14. He will assist the C.S.M. in his duties and will, act for him in his absence.

## Transcriber's notes — August 1918

- All 31 days are covered. The 20/21 August entries are tangled: the date column shows 21 struck through, and the narrative runs from the evening of 20 August through the early hours of 22 August. The 9 August entry is partly cut off at the foot of det-page-0093; its tail continues on det-page-0094.
- Officers named: Major G. N. Cooper (O.C.); Capt. E. P. Cole (Supply Officer); Lieut. C. A. Paterson (No. 1 Section); Lieut. T. Love (No. 2 Section); Lt. F. A. Walker; Lt. Col. Jarvis; Major Ruspin (D.A.A.G.); Lt. Col. McAlpine.
- Casualties: 10 Aug — Dvr. A. Warwick (142182, on command to 55th Battery) and 2 horses killed by an enemy bomb; Warwick buried at Beaufort Church. 11 Aug — Pte. J. Orton (67611) wounded by machine-gun fire from a low-flying enemy aeroplane. Night of 18/19 Aug — an enemy bomb in the company wood killed 12 horses outright and wounded five men: Dvr. P. D. Cox (515092), Dvr. G. J. Hunt (511691), Dvr. C. Whitfield (331238, car driver), Gnr. W. O. Bannister (2040508, 5th Batt [uncertain]), Gnr. J. Hollinger (311419, 610th Batt [uncertain]); a further horse on command to H.Q. 5th C.D.A.C. was killed by shell fire on 18 Aug, and four G.S. wagons were put out of use (two practically demolished).
- Moves: the detachment detrained at Saleux and marched to Cagny on 1 Aug; moved forward through T2C 5.8 (8 Aug), U13 C 1.6 (9 Aug), K7 B 3.7 (10 Aug) during the Amiens offensive; withdrew via T18 C 2.2 (21–22 Aug) and Salouel (22–23 Aug), entrained at Saleux for Aubigny, marched to Savy (24 Aug), and returned to the Arras area — K11C 5.5 (25 Aug) and L18 Central, Stuart Camp (29 Aug).
- The 4th anniversary of the outbreak of war is noted on 4 August. The 19 August entry notes the anniversary of the order to proceed to France with the 5th Divisional Artillery.
- Appendix I is a full nominal roll of the detachment (Headquarters, No. 1 Section, No. 2 Section, Supply Section) with regimental numbers, ranks, names, details, wagon numbers and horses, plus typed standing orders for Section Officers, Orderly Officer, Section Sergeants and Orderly Sergeant.
