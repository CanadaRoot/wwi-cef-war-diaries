## Transcriber's notes — December 1917

- The diary text covers 1–31 December 1917, entirely at Fosse 10, in grouped entries: 3rd–22nd (weather only) and 26th–31st. No separate entries for 4th–12th or 14th–22nd December.
- Casualty: Driver L. Begin, No. 830041, died by suicide in his billet on 1 December 1917 (rifle, about 4.30 p.m.). He had been awarded 4 days Field Punishment No. 1 that afternoon for disobedience of Train Order No. 82 (6 November 1917) — failing to lead his horses to water. His funeral was held 2 December at Noeux-les-Mines Cemetery, attended by Capt. Cooper, Lt. Walker and men of the Company; a Court of Enquiry into his death sat the same evening in the Officers' Mess (President: Capt. G. M. Cooper; members: Lieut. C. A. Paborn and Lt. F. A. Walker).
- Lieut. F. A. Walker was granted 14 days' leave to England on 13 December, intending to arrange an immediate return to Canada; Capt. Cooper did not expect him to return.
- From 23 December the detachment drew rations through 3rd Canadian Division from Sains-Bouvigny again.
- Christmas: Capt. Cooper went to Béthune on 24 December to buy turkeys for the men's Christmas dinner. Christmas Day was cold with rain and occasional snow; the dinner was held in the Church Army Hut, kindly loaned by Mr. Hindle, the Superintendent, followed by a concert; the men were excused duty most of the day.
- The month closes with the men expecting a move now that Christmas has passed.
- People named: Capt. G. M. Cooper (O.C. Detachment); Lieut. C. A. Paborn; Lieut. F. A. Walker; Dvr. L. Begin (No. 830041, deceased); Mr. Hindle (Superintendent, Church Army Hut).
- Abbreviations as written: Divl. = Divisional; Cdn. = Canadian; Dvr. = Driver; F.P. = Field Punishment; O.C. = Officer Commanding.
