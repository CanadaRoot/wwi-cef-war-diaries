## Page 3 — Diary text, p. 1

Army Form C. 2118. — WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. (Erase heading not required.)

[Handwritten, top right:] Original. 5th Cdn. Divl. Train. Vol III Page 1.

Columns: Place | Date 1917. | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices

| Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information |
|---|---|---|---|
| FOSSE 10. | Oct. 1st to 3rd | | First two days of this month have been fine, rained through night of 2/3 and cloudy on 3rd. |
| GRAND SERVINS. | 4th | | Rode over to GRAND SERVINS in anticipation of moving Company there as our Artillery are nearly all over that way, and we are to draw through 5th Div. [uncertain] G.M.C. |
| | | | Moved Company to GRAND SERVINS. Raining very hard. No arrangements made for our reception in way of Billets. Arranged with Town Major to allow us to remain in [uncertain — lines?] taken possession of here. Railhead BARLIN. Light Railways being used. ROSIE SIDING is used by us. Very inconvenient indeed. With the wet weather, this field in which the siding was placed was very soft. The wagons were axle deep in mud. (The only thing Rosie about it was its name.) Light Railways arrived very late indeed. Endeavoured to pick additional supplies up immediately. G.M.C. |
| PETIT SERVINS. | 5th | | Moved Company to PETIT SERVINS as we were ordered out of the former place. Remains here for a few days. G.M.C. |
| GOUAY SERVINS. | 8th | | Moved Company to GOUAY SERVINS. Railhead still ROSIE SIDING and now drawing through 1st Cdn. Div.; which Division got one additional days supplies up for us. G.M.C. |
| | 10th | | Instructed by C.R.A. to take over 6 G.S. wagons and 24 of the heaviest L.D. horses from D.A.C. to make up deficiencies as brought out in my conversation with Lt. Col. [uncertain — name illegible] and G.O.C. 5th Div. Arty. G.M.C. |

[Gap: no entries recorded for 6th, 7th or 9th October. The 10th October entry continues onto p. 2.]

| | 11th | | Took over remaining 2 G.S. wagons and 8 L.D. horses. Company preparing to move back to FOSSE 10 again. G.M.C. |

| FOSSE 10 | | | Railhead SAINS-BOUVIGNY. Now drawing through 59th British Division, by light Railway from BARLIN. |
| | | | Dump — Ramps at SAINS BOUVIGNY siding. Supplies would come in through the night by light railways. Supply details has handled them on to the ramps. Wagons would load up, deliver to Units and return to Coy lines. |
| | | | Batteries all back in old lines again, and very short hauls are necessary. Conditions ideal. G.M.C. |
| FOSSE 10. | 16th | | Received copy of G.H.Q. letter 6697 (Q.A.1) in connection with my establishment. This letter recommends that 1 Officer and 32 Other Ranks be despatched immediately to France from Witley Camp England. G.M.C. |

## Page 5 — Diary text, p. 3

Army Form C. 2118. — WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. (Erase heading not required.)

[Handwritten, top right:] Original. 5th Cdn. Divl. Train. Vol III Page 3.

| Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information |
|---|---|---|---|
| FOSSE 10 France | Oct. 17th to 24th | | With the exception of the 22nd and 23rd the weather during this period was fine, the two days mentioned were dull and wet. |
| | | | Nothing of importance to record. Everything going along nicely. G.M.C. |
| | 25th | | Received orders our Railhead was to change to NOEUX-les-MINES tomorrow. |
| | | | Drew from NOEUX-les-MINES for first time today, off 11th British Division pack train. Fine Railhead from a transport point of view. |
| | | | The system we used here was as follows. Deliver to Units first thing in morning, off load wagons in time to make Railhead, to clear pack. Load forage in detail, and groceries in bulk, Established a dump in Coy lines FOSSE 10. Parked wagons till the afternoon when they would load Groceries, and then finally park on next street, ready to proceed to Units in early morning. Works very well indeed, and is a good system tactically as your wagons are loaded ready to move off at a moment's notice. G.M.C. |
| | 28th to 31st | | Everything working fine. 11th Divn S.S.O. is exceptionally nice to deal with. NOEUX-les-MINES shelled every morning practically, at hour for Railhead. Wagons late to fields for time being. No casualties up to present. G.M.C. |

## Transcriber's notes — October 1917

- The diary text covers 1–31 October 1917 in grouped entries: 1st–3rd, 17th–24th and 28th–31st. No entries for 6th, 7th, 9th, 13th–15th, 26th or 27th; 18th–21st are covered only by the grouped entry.
- The Company moved three times in the first twelve days: Fosse 10 → Grand Servins (4 Oct) → Petit Servins (5 Oct) → Gouay Servins (8 Oct) → back to Fosse 10 (12 Oct).
- "Rosie Siding" at Barlin is described as a field so soft with wet weather that wagons sank axle-deep in mud; Capt. Cooper notes "(The only thing Rosie about it was its name.)"
- Reinforcements: on 10–11 October the detachment took over 6 G.S. wagons and 24 L.D. (Light Draught) horses from the Divisional Ammunition Column, with personnel from the Trench Mortar Batteries, on the C.R.A.'s instructions to make up establishment deficiencies.
- G.H.Q. letter 6697 (Q.A.1) of 16 October recommended 1 officer and 32 other ranks be despatched at once from Witley Camp, England, to bring the detachment up to establishment.
- The railhead changed from Sains-Bouvigny (via 59th British Division) to Noeux-les-Mines (via 11th British Division) on 25 October; Noeux-les-Mines was shelled almost every morning at railhead hour.
- Casualties: none up to 31 October 1917 ("No casualties up to present").
- People named: Capt. G. M. Cooper (O.C. Detachment); Lt. Col. [uncertain — name illegible; possibly the 1st Divisional Train O.C. named as "Wadmore" on 6 September] in conversation with the G.O.C. 5th Div. Artillery.
- Places: Fosse 10, Grand Servins, Petit Servins, Gouay Servins, Barlin (Rosie Siding), Sains-Bouvigny, Noeux-les-Mines, Witley Camp (England).
- Abbreviations as written: Divl. = Divisional; Cdn. = Canadian; Arty. = Artillery; D.A.C. = Divisional Ammunition Column; T.M.B. = Trench Mortar Battery; C.R.A. = Commander Royal Artillery; G.O.C. = General Officer Commanding; G.S. = General Service; L.D. = Light Draught; G.H.Q. = General Headquarters; Coy = Company; S.S.O. = Senior Supply Officer [as written; not expanded in the diary].
