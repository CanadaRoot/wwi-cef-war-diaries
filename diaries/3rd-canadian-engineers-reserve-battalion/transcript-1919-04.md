# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Engineers Reserve Battalion — April 1919

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Engineers Reserve Battalion (3rd C.E.R.B.)
- **Month:** April 1919 — 1 April to 30 April 1919
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004925 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5008, file 705, reel T-10856, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** April 1919, p. 1 – p. 4 (image files page-0042 – page-0045)
- **April 1919 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–4): cover, diary text 1–30 April, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" not struck through on these sheets; the printed heading reads "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" with "(Erase heading not required.)" beneath). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The "Remarks and references to Appendices" column bears a handwritten initial (appears "Ah") on each row. "O.R." = other ranks; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "G.C.M." = General Court Martial; "D.S.O." = Distinguished Service Order; "M.C." = Military Cross; "C.E." = Canadian Engineers; "N.S.A." = as written (expansion not known); "C.C.C." = as written; "Kinmel Park" = the Canadian demobilisation camp at Kinmel Park, Wales; "Buxton" = the embarkation centre for men with dependents; "Bramshott" = Bramshott Camp, Hampshire. Dates are written "1-4-19" etc. Place column reads "Seaford" throughout. The Hour column is blank throughout. The diary is the demobilisation record of a reserve battalion at Seaford, Sussex: almost every entry is a party of other ranks inspected by the G.O.C. and then sent to Kinmel Park for return to Canada.

## Open questions / uncertain items (April 1919)
- 1 April: a word before "Weather fine" appears overtyped/struck through and could not be read; marked [uncertain].
- 17 April: "Party No 47, 38 O.R's for return to Canada returned to Canada" is as written — the wording is odd (the party presumably proceeded/embarked rather than "returned"), but it is transcribed as typed. "128 N.S.A. men" is read as written; the abbreviation "N.S.A." is not expanded anywhere on the page and its meaning is not known. "C.C.C. Camp" is as written.
- 22 April: "G.C.M. of Lieut. G.C. Fidler" is as written; the diary does not say what the court martial concerned. The same officer's court martial is promulgated on 1 May 1919 (see May transcript).
- 23 April: Lieut-Col. Dunbar proceeded on leave and Major Hibbert D.S.O. M.C. temporarily assumes command — Hibbert signs the April sheets as commanding officer.
- No dates are missing: every day 1–30 April 1919 has an entry. No casualties, deaths or hospital admissions are recorded in April 1919. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; the only places named are Seaford, Kinmel Park, Buxton and Bramshott.
- Names read as: Lieut. G.C. Fidler; Lieut-Col. Dunbar; Major Hibbert D.S.O. M.C. (signed "A. Hibbert").
- Return-to-Canada parties in April 1919 (as recorded): Party No 41 — 9 O.R's; No 42 — 35 inspected, 132 proceeded; No 43 — 25; No 44 — 12 inspected, 52 proceeded; No 45 — 30 inspected, 29 proceeded; No 46 — 31 proceeded; No 47 — 38; No 48 — 84; No 49 — 13 inspected. In addition 68 O.R's proceeded on indefinite leave for discharge in England (11 April) and 35 O.R's with dependents proceeded to Buxton (12 April).
