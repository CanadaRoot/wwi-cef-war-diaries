# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Engineers Reserve Battalion — December 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Engineers Reserve Battalion (3rd C.E.R.B.)
- **Month:** December 1918 (Volume No. 5) — 1 December to 31 December 1918
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004925 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5008, file 705, reel T-10856, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** December 1918, p. 1 – p. 5 (image files page-0027 – page-0031)
- **December 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–5): cover, diary text 1–31 December, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" blacked out on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Place column reads "Seaford." throughout. The "Hour" column is blank throughout. "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "O.R." = other ranks; "Lieut." = Lieutenant; "Capt." = Captain; "C.E.R.B." = Canadian Engineers Reserve Battalion; "C.E.R.D." = Canadian Engineers Reserve Depot; "C.A.M.C." = Canadian Army Medical Corps; "S.O.S." = struck off strength; "T.O.S." = taken on strength; "S.M.O." = Senior Medical Officer; "R.C." = Roman Catholic; "C. of E." = Church of England. The "Remarks and references to Appendices" column carries handwritten initials against each day's entry; they are not transcribed as names. Words split across line-ends in the typewriting ("T renches", "Occu-ational") have been joined. The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "D. D. & L., London, E.C. (29250) Wt W3200/P713 750,000 3/18 Forms/C2118/16."

Place: Seaford.

>
> **1-12-18.** — 1. Weather:-Fine. 2. Church Parade.
>
> **2-12-18.** — 1. Weather:-Dull. 2. 19 Officers On Command to Port of Embarkation pending return to Canada.
>
> **3-12-18.** — 1. Weather:-Rain.
>
> **4-12-18.** — 1. Weather:-Dull, threatening rain.
>
> **5-12-18.** — 1. Weather:-Cloudy and cold. 2. Party of 108 Other Ranks employed filling in Practice Trenches at "D" Range from 1300 to 15.30
>
> **6-12-18.** — 1. Weather:-Dull. 2. Party of 102 Other Ranks proceed to "D" Range and are employed filling in Practice Trenches.
>

> **8-12-18.** — 1. Weather:-Raining. 2. 385 Other Ranks attend Church Parade ata 0900.
>
> **9-12-18.** — 1. Weather:-Fine. 2. Parade 1000. 3. 40 Other Ranks S.O.S. to C.A.M.C. Shorncliffe.

### Page 5 — 25–31 December 1918

Place: Seaford.

> **25-12-18.** — 1. Weather:- Fine, very frosty. 2. Christmas Day. Hurrah. 3. Entertainments for men a great success. 4. Church Parade well attended, there being over 400 in attendance. 5. Visit from G.O.C. and Col. Anderson who inspected the Men's Mess and expressed satisfaction in the arrangements for the men's entertainments.
>
> **26-12-18.** — 1. Weather:- Cold, clear morning; Rain in evening.
>
> **27-12-18.** — 1. Weather:- Raining. 2. 45 Other Ranks left for Canada, Draft No. 15.
>
> **28-12-18.** — 1. Weather:- Dull, Stormy. 2. Half Holiday.
>
> **29-12-18.** — 1. Weather:- Dull, Showery. 2. G.O.C. inspected Draft No. 16 at Oval 1000H. The Battalion was congratulated on the way the draft was turned out. 3. Church Parade as usual. 4. Leave party were lectured at 1100 by O.C., the Chaplain and Medical Officer.
>
> **30-12-18.** — 1. Weather:- Cloudy, Showers. 2. New Year's Leave Party left at 0945 comprising of 289 Other Ranks. 3. Draft No. 16 for Canada left at 0940 consisting of 80 Other Ranks.
>
> **31-12-18.** — 1. Weather:- Cloudy, Showery. 2. O. C. held inspection of Draft No. 17 which has been reduced to 29 Other Ranks.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *[signature — appears to read "J.B. Dunbar"]*

## Open questions / uncertain items (December 1918)
- No dates are missing: every day 1–31 December 1918 has an entry.
- 2 Dec: the date "2-12-18" is overtyped/corrected in the Date column.
- 8 Dec: "ata 0900" is as written.
- 11 Dec: item 3 is numbered "33." as written — evidently a typist's error for "3."
- 14 Dec: the total strength of the battalion is left blank — "Total strength of Battalion................?" — the question mark is typed in the original.
- 15 Dec: "85 off 1439 other ranks" is as written ("off" presumably abbreviates officers); "Occu-ational" is split across the line-end and joined here as "Occupational".
- 19 Dec: the word before "Christmas Leave" is struck through; it appears to read "Xmas".
- 22 Dec: the church parade attendance figures are left blank (dots) for R.C., C. of E. and Other Denominations.
- 24 Dec: "43 Contacts were isolated" confirmed on close reading. The funeral party's deceased is not named, and no death is recorded in the diary.
- The Commanding Officer's signature at the foot of the December sheets appears to read "J.B. Dunbar" — recorded with that reservation; unlike November, no typed rank or title appears below the signature on this sheet.
- Names read as: Col. Anderson (25 Dec, surname only; visited with the G.O.C.). No other officers are named in December.
- Casualties in December 1918: none killed or wounded. One case of Spinal Meningitis reported in the lines on 24 Dec, with 43 contacts isolated under the Senior Medical Officer's instructions. 40 Other Ranks were struck off strength to C.A.M.C. Shorncliffe on 9 Dec.
- Movements in December 1918: 200 Other Ranks proceeded to Bexhill 11 Dec; Draft No. 15 (45 Other Ranks) left for Canada 27 Dec; Draft No. 16 (80 Other Ranks) left for Canada 30 Dec; the New Year's Leave Party (289 Other Ranks) left 30 Dec; Draft No. 17 was inspected 31 Dec, reduced to 29 Other Ranks. The battalion remained at Seaford throughout; the main work of the month was filling in the practice trenches at "D" Range (5–7 Dec) and demobilisation paperwork.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; the place is Seaford, England, throughout.
