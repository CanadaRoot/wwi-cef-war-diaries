# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Engineers Reserve Battalion — May 1919

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Engineers Reserve Battalion (3rd C.E.R.Bn.)
- **Month:** May 1919 — 1 May to 31 May 1919
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004925 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5008, file 705, reel T-10856, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** May 1919, p. 1 – p. 4 (image files page-0046 – page-0049)
- **May 1919 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–4): cover, diary text 1–31 May, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" not struck through on these sheets; the printed heading reads "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" with "(Erase heading not required.)" beneath). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed; struck-through text is shown as [struck through — appears to read "..."]. The "Remarks and references to Appendices" column bears a handwritten initial (appears "Ah") on each row. "O.R." = other ranks; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "G.C.M." = General Court Martial; "A.P.M." = Assistant Provost Marshal; "C.A.S.C." = Canadian Army Service Corps; "C.E.T.C." = Canadian Engineers Training Centre; "Oval" = the sports ground at Seaford used as the embarkation assembly point. Dates are written "1-5-19" etc. Place column reads "Seaford" throughout. The Hour column is blank throughout. "Picquet" is the diary's spelling of picket. The month is dominated by demobilisation parties for Canada and by town disturbances at Seaford in early May, with picquets called out nightly.

## Open questions / uncertain items (May 1919)
- 1 May: the row is heavily overtyped — the Place and Date columns are struck through and retyped, and the entry reads "Weather Mild. Showery. Promulgation of G.C.M. Lt. G.C. Fidler." The court martial itself was held on 22 April 1919 (see April transcript); the diary gives no detail of the charge or finding.
- 3–4 May: disturbances in Seaford town and South Camp; a picquet was called out by the A.P.M. on 3 May but not required. North Camp was quiet. No injuries, arrests or damage are recorded. These disturbances are the local echo of the wider Canadian demobilisation unrest that Lt-Gen. Turner addresses in his Special Order of the Day of 20 June 1919 (see June transcript, Appendix p. 11).
- 20 May: the Party No.56 entry has two phrases struck through ("inspected by G.O.C." and "proceeded to Kinmel Park.") — the party is recorded as proceeding to Kinmel Park the next day, 21 May. Both struck phrases are shown in the transcript as read.
- 23 May: fire at No.9 Lines, South Camp, at 0030 o'clock — the Soldiers' Club and some adjacent buildings destroyed. No casualties are recorded.
- 24 May: a word in "C.E.T.C., Sports [struck through] in forenoon" appears to read "finished" and is struck through; the C.E.T.C. Sports are recorded as finished the next day, 25 May.
- 27 May: "314 ORs" stands alone at the end of the entry as written; it is presumably the number of men who proceeded on leave, but the diary does not say so in words.
- No dates are missing: every day 1–31 May 1919 has an entry. No deaths or hospital admissions are recorded in May 1919. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; the only places named are Seaford, Kinmel Park, Buxton, Blandford, Liphook, Witley, Southampton and France.
- Names read as: Lieut. G.C. Fidler; Lieut-Col. Dunbar (signed as commanding officer); the G.O.C. is not named.
- Return-to-Canada parties in May 1919 (as recorded): Party No.51 — 49 O.R's; No.52 — 32 O.R's; No.56 — 23 O.R's; 30 O.R's inspected 26 May (party number not given); 20 O.R's proceeded to Kinmel Park 13 and 14 May (party numbers not given); 40 O.R's by Aquitania (17–18 May, with 1 officer); 6 officers to Kinmel Park (10 May); 8 officers to Witley (19 May). 50 O.R's proceeded to France for War Graves Commission work (16 May). 100 draftees to Blandford for C.A.S.C. duty (5 May), 100 draftees to General Depot (9 May), 20 draftees to Liphook (12 May). 11 O.R's discharged in the British Isles (14 May).
