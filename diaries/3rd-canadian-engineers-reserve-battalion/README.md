# 3rd Canadian Engineers Reserve Battalion — war diary

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Engineers Reserve Battalion (3rd C.E.R.B. / 3rd C.E. Res Battn), Canadian Engineers Training Centre, Seaford, England
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004925 — "War diaries - 3rd Canadian Engineers Reserve Battalion" (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5008, file 705, reel T-10856, finding aid 9-52)
- **Dates:** 6 August 1918 – 30 June 1919
- **Pages:** 60 page images (11 monthly volumes, Aug 1918 – Jun 1919), all downloaded from the LAC IIIF manifest `https://digitalmanifest.bac-lac.gc.ca/DigitalManifest/1/2004925` (`manifest.json`, `download-pages.sh`)
- **Transcripts:** 11 monthly files, `transcript-1918-08.md` … `transcript-1919-06.md`
- **Index:** 332 dated entries in `INDEX-D.md` (329 day entries + 3 June 1919 appendix entries), covering 6 Aug 1918 – 30 Jun 1919. The three June appendix entries (13, 20, 23 June) share dates with day entries — genuine duplicates, flagged [Appendix].
- **Transcribed:** 2026-10-27, Eighth D run 6 (four parallel subagents, one per period; parent run verified index coverage and spot-checked the November transcript against its page images).

## What the diary is

A typewritten Army Form C.2118 diary of a reinforcement-training battalion that never went to France as a unit. Formed at Seaford on 6 August 1918 under Major J. D. Gunn (the 3rd Canadian Engineers Reserve Battalion, Central Ontario — a sub-organization of the Canadian Engineers Training Centre), it received, trained and drafted sappers to France, then spent 1919 demobilising them home to Canada. The diary is daily weather, parades, route marches, drafts in and out, inspections, sport — and, in the background, the influenza epidemic, the Armistice, and the demobilisation unrest of 1919.

## Key findings

- **Formation:** authorised in C.E.T.C. Daily Orders No. 564 of 5 Aug 1918; Major J. D. Gunn takes temporary command 6 Aug, Capt. J. B. McPhail adjutant. First parade 7 Aug; men released from segregation divided into C, D, E, F Companies (Major R. F. E. Wood, Capt. H. E. Boswell, Lieut. W. H. Stewart, Lieut. J. H. Munro). A Company (Draft Finding) and B Company (cooks/training/B-category men) still to form. Lieut.-Col. J. B. Dunbar takes command later in 1918 and signs the diary through to its end.
- **Drafts:** the battalion's purpose was feeding the C.E. Pool — a draft of 2,183 other ranks called for 27 Aug 1918; first draft for France, 565 other ranks, left at midnight 19 Sep; Draft #2 (486 O.R.) 9 Oct; Draft #4 (126 O.R.) 8 Nov. In 1919 the flow reverses: repatriation parties Nos. 19–56, G.O.C.-inspected at Seaford, despatched to Kinmel Park or Buxton for return to Canada; the battalion itself sails 23 Jun 1919 (15 officers, 725 O.R. on S.S. Belgic from Liverpool).
- **Visitors:** Sir Robert Borden (Prime Minister of Canada) and General Sir R. E. W. Turner addressed the troops and presented medals at the 11 Aug 1918 church parade; Lieut.-General Turner inspected the troops 28 Mar 1919 and visited camp again 18 Jun 1919.
- **Influenza:** camp quarantined 10–18 Oct 1918; concerts and congregating disallowed 17 Oct; Lieut. F. L. Mitchell admitted with influenza 24 Oct. One case of spinal meningitis 24 Dec, 43 contacts isolated. Adjutant Lieut. J. C. Brown admitted with influenza 16 Feb 1919, discharged 27 Feb.
- **Armistice, 11 Nov 1918:** not mentioned at all — the entry records only "Weather:-Fair. 112 Other Ranks transferred to C.M.G.D." A Special Thanksgiving Service was held 13 Nov.
- **Christmas Day 1918:** "Christmas Day. Hurrah." — entertainments a great success, church parade over 400 in attendance, G.O.C. and Col. Anderson inspected the Men's Mess.
- **Sport (a major thread):** C.E.T.C. baseball team won the Baseball Championship in England, beating Epsom 2–0 in the final at Gilford (11 Sep 1918) and the American Navy 6–2 (28 Sep); lacrosse vs 2nd C.E.R.B.; football in the Seaford Area League; boxing in the Arena.
- **1919 unrest — what the diary does and does not say:** the March 1919 entries record nothing about any disturbance or riot (routine weather, parades, inspections and repatriation parties only). Seaford town/South Camp disturbances are recorded 3–4 May (picquet called out by the A.P.M. but not required); damage to private property by troops at night 17 Jun, with special police on duty nightly 18–22 Jun. The June appendices include Lieut.-General Turner's Special Order of the Day of 20 Jun 1919 condemning the Witley Camp rioting and the Epsom disturbance — "there can be no innocent spectators at a mutiny" — and warning there will be no general amnesty for mutinous offences.
- **Fire:** 23 May 1919, 0030 hrs — fire at No. 9 Lines, South Camp; the Soldiers' Club and adjacent buildings destroyed. No casualties recorded.
- **Closing entries:** 28 Jun 1919 — "Peace Treaty signed." 30 Jun 1919 (final entry) — "Final party 11 [uncertain] Orders and 3rd C.E.R.B. ceases to exist as a Unit in England," signed by Lieut.-Col. J. B. Dunbar.
- **Casualties:** no deaths and no wounded anywhere in the diary. Only medical items: the influenza cases above and the single spinal meningitis case.

## Uncertain / open items

- 29 `[uncertain]` occurrences across the 11 transcripts (and several [blacked out] words where the original was censored at filing). Most are single illegible words or overtyped passages; none are names that were guessed.
- 23 Aug 1918: no Place or Date entered in the original — the date is inferred from its position between 22 and 24 Aug and flagged in the transcript.
- The diary gives the O.C.'s name as "J. D. Gunn" and the guide gives "Major J. D. Gunn"; a visitor on 23 Sep 1918 is "Col. J. A. Gunn of HQ London" — transcribed as written; whether related is not stated.
- Capt. H. E. Boswell's initials vary (H. E. / H. B.) and Lieut. W. H. Stewart appears also as "W. H. Stuart" — transcribed as written, not reconciled.
- The June 1919 Belgic sailing orders give different initials for Christie (H.F./R.F.), Draper (J.J./E.J.) and Mathews/Matthews on Sheets 1 vs 2 — transcribed as written on each sheet. Train-party totals for the Belgic sailing (121 + 605 = 726) are one more than the 725 O.R. in the 23 June diary entry.
- No latitude/longitude anywhere in the diary — place is Seaford, England, throughout. The Hour column is blank throughout.
- The August cover is a "FALSE DOCKET" sheet (HQ, Department of National Defence, Canada); the false-docket file reference reads "5-M-3" [uncertain] while the monthly title pages give "5-K-3".
- The 13–19 Sep 1918 sheet is headed "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" with the second line left standing (not struck through as elsewhere).

## Access method note (reusable)

- The LAC item was found two ways, agreeing exactly: (1) a live-browser LAC Collection Search (no human-verification challenge appeared) and (2) curl to the LAC "Result" endpoint with the search form's own parameters (`ST=STAD&DataSource=Archives|FonAndCol&SearchIn_1=Title&SearchInText_1=...`) plus a Referer header — the bare `?q=` Result URL redirects to `/eng/home/nobots`, but the form-parameter version returns results. The same Title-field search also surfaced the related records: "3rd Canadian Engineers Reserve Battalion - After Orders" (2009800), two Seaford Daily Orders files (834018, 834019), "Part II Daily Orders" (835899) and an inspection report (932879) — none of them the diary.
