## Notes — May 1915
- **May is the hospital's first full working month at Wimereux**, and the diary turns into a clinical report. The month begins with 238 patients in hospital; 2,450 cases were admitted in total (minimum in hospital 107 on 3 May; maximum 342 on 11 May).
- **Gas poisoning (chlorine, from the Second Battle of Ypres):** the first gas case is received 3 May, with a detailed symptom list — intense dyspnoea and cyanosis to slight bronchitis, severe conjunctivitis, severe headache, mental dulness, and broncho-pneumonia with diarrhoea. One death from gas poisoning, primarily from the subsequent broncho-pneumonia. A further 30 gas cases are admitted by 20 May, most less serious than the first. Gas cases had already been admitted in April.
- **Boracic paste wound treatment — a 22-case trial, 4 May:** the diary analyses 22 wound cases treated with a boracic paste — 6 superficial wounds penetrating subcutaneous tissue, 8 deep penetrating wounds, 4 deep perforating wounds, 4 scalp perforating wounds — and concludes the paste helps only superficial wounds: in deep and perforating wounds it seals in infection, blocks drainage and delays healing, and in scalp wounds it causes severe pain and slow healing. The word after "Boracic" in the 4 May entry is not read with confidence (it appears to read "foots").
- **Disease:** one case of epidemic cerebro-spinal meningitis developed in the Corps and was removed to an infectious hospital; no tetanus case developed or was treated; no more than 4 cases of *B. aerogenes capsulatus* infection were observed in the month.
- **Surgery:** operations were chiefly removal of foreign bodies, free incisions and drainage; the most satisfactory after-treatment was a moist dressing of soda citrate and sodium chloride (Wright's solution).
- **Named people:** none. No personal names are given in these entries.
- No appendices are listed or included for this volume.
