## Notes — October 1915
- **The month is summarised on 31 October (pp. 4–6):** after the 1–13 October entries, the rest of the month is written up as a single month-end summary dated 31 October.
- **Dysentery, bacillary now dominant:** amoebic dysentery is becoming less and bacillary dysentery is on the increase; the cases lack the very acute symptoms of the early cases but are more severe owing to delay in reaching hospital. 90% of the patients are from troops on Lemnos, i.e. having contracted dysentery since coming here. Progress of patients is very slow. Rheumatism(?) and jaundice are common sequelae to dysentery. "Rheumatism(?)" is the writer's own mark, transcribed as written.
- **Figures:** 638 cases in hospital on 18 October; average daily state for the month 550; daily average 525 on 8 October. 18 deaths in October as compared with 4 in September, 90% due to dysentery; the death rate in the last two weeks of September was 6 times any previous two weeks, due to cases arriving in hospital in a late stage of disease. Six post mortems were held during the month.
- **Infectious and venereal cases:** a tent section was opened for venereal and infectious cases — syphilis, gonorrhoea, scarlet fever, diphtheria, chicken pox, mumps and measles, 50 cases in all. Diphtheria from the Australian Rest Camp — 17 cases developed in one week. One case of beri-beri in hospital.
- **Uncertain word:** the last word of the 8 October entry is read as "design" but the line runs off the bottom edge of the page and the next page does not continue the sentence; it is marked [uncertain].
- **Water:** from 13 October the hospital uses water from a tank brought through pipes from the condenser.
- **Accommodation:** by 1 October the hospital can accommodate 550 patients; 550 are accommodated in 12 huts planned to hold 25 patients each. All nursing sisters are comfortably accommodated in canvas huts; officers and men are still in tents. The X ray department moved into the Operating Room building. Buildings for ordnance stores and offices are not yet erected.
- **Named people:** Col Agnew, Camp Commandant (inspected the hospital 9 October); Col McKee (sailed for England on the hospital ship Aquitania 18 October); Major E. J. Williams (assumed temporary command); General Munro, who succeeded Sir Ian Hamilton (inspected troops and hospitals 27 October); Capt. Creighton (joined the unit during the month).
- **Reinforcements:** 7 men arrived by the hospital ship Delta on 9 October, with a further addition of 27 men and one officer (Capt. Creighton) during the month. Five men of the unit were invalided home to England.
- No appendices are listed or included for this volume.
