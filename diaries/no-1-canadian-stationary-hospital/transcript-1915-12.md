## Notes — December 1915
- **Trench feet (frostbite):** 800 patients were treated on 1 December following heavy rain and low temperature on the Peninsula on 27–29 November, with cases ranging from simple numbness to gangrene of toes and feet. 200 trench-feet cases were in hospital on 6 December. 2 cases of tetanus followed frostbites, one fatal; all frost cases got the serum.
- **Dysentery recedes:** dysentery cases become markedly less, as does jaundice.
- **Expansion to 1,000 beds:** preparations are complete by 15 December for 1,000 patients, using all the hutage erected on the area. Most patients were discharged to emergency tent hospitals, leaving only 350 in hospital; by 20 December patients had increased to 450, as no hospital ships had left lately for Alexandria or England.
- **"Suvla (?) Anzac":** the 15 December entry reads "No casualties from Evacuation of Suvla (?) Anzac." The "(?)" appears to be the diarist's own mark of doubt, transcribed as written.
- **Date slips:** the Date/Hour column heading on p. 2 reads "Dec 1916", and the 10 December entry carries "/16" in the Hour column — both slips for 1915, the volume covering December 1915 to January 1916.
- **Named people:** Major Charlton (proceeded to Alexandria and Cairo for purchase of supplies, 8 December); Major Munro and Capt. Foster (both to return to England, paratyphoid in each case, 25 December).
- **Year end:** 276 patients in hospital on 31 December, all progressing well; very few admissions in the previous 10 days; health of officers, nursing sisters and rank and file remarkably good; food supply much better in quantity and quality.
- No appendices are listed or included for this volume.
