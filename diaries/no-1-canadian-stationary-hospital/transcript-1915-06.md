## Notes — June 1915
- **The hospital is ordered enlarged to 400 beds (6 June)**: the Director of Medical Services (D.M.S. 17-2-0) directs the establishment to grow to 400 beds, with 4 more officers and 38 more non-commissioned officers and men; the nursing staff is reduced to 27. The reinforcements arrive 29 June — Capts. Vipond, Sutherland, Bastien and Stanners, 3 sergeants, 2 corporals and 35 men.
- **June admissions:** 635 patients admitted in the month (238 sick, 397 wounded); 16 patients remaining in hospital on 30 June; average stay 3.27 days. Cases were mostly of a light nature, needing minor operative work and nursing, and evacuable in a short time.
- **Interesting medical cases in June:** pericarditis, Vincent's angina, atropine poisoning, typhoid and paratyphoid (the counts read "1. Typhoid. 13 paratyphoid" — possibly 1 typhoid and 3 paratyphoid [uncertain]).
- **Atropine poisoning case:** following an inoculation or re-vaccination the patient developed symptoms typical of atropine poisoning; they subsided under treatment, returned the next day resembling meningitis, could not be confirmed by lumbar puncture, and did not react to treatment, with prominent meningeal symptoms and a strong hysterical element. The case was transferred (as written, "to No 1 Stationary Hosp.") under suspicion; the diagnosis received since is uncertain, with no confirmation of meningitis.
- **Named people:** Capts. Vipond, Sutherland, Bastien and Stanners (reinforcement officers, 29 June); the signing Captain, C.A.M.C. (surname [uncertain], appears to read "Bennett").
- **Casualties:** no deaths recorded in June; no individual patients are named.
- The June volume carries an A.G. Office at the Base (Canadian Section) receipt stamp dated 3 July 1915.
- No appendices are listed or included for this volume.
