## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary text in this volume is typewritten, not handwritten).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", typewritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is struck through on the printed form.
- Place names are transcribed as written (SALONICA / Salonica).
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary.

## Notes — June–August 1916
- **July and August 1916 are present.** The volume covers 3 June–31 August 1916; the July entries (1–31 July) and August entries (3–31 August) occupy pp. 61–62 of this set. There is no gap for July or August.
- **The hospital is at Salonica** throughout, and the diary is kept as weekly summaries rather than daily entries.
- **Admissions / discharges / deaths (weekly):** June — week ending 3 June: 64 admitted, 165 discharged, 1 death; week ending 10 June: 215 admitted, 138 discharged; week ending 17 June: 242 admitted, 167 discharged; week ending 24 June: 147 admitted, 114 discharged, 1 death. July — week ending 1 July: 188 admitted, 221 discharged, 281 in hospital, two deaths; week ending 8 July: 307 admitted, 212 discharged, 271 in hospital, five deaths; week ending 15 July: 184 admitted, 197 discharged, 355 in hospital, 3 deaths; week ending 22 July: 291 admitted, 251 discharged, 415 in hospital; week ending 29 July: 389 admitted, 298 discharged, 596 in hospital. August — week ending 5 August: 380 admitted, 397 discharged, 1 death, 498 in hospital; week ending 12 August: 383 admitted, 329 discharged, 2 deaths, 476 in hospital; week ending 19 August: 368 admitted, 283 discharged, 1 death, 556 in hospital; week ending 26 August: 271 admitted, 280 discharged, 3 deaths, 539 in hospital.
- **Hospital capacity** increased to 600 beds (15 July).
- **Weather:** very hot through June and July — averaging 102 degrees in the shade at noon, rising one day to 106 degrees; moderating mid-July, then temperate with occasional showers.
- **Disease:** diarrhoea among the unit's own personnel in June (11 of the 22 personnel admissions that month); malaria becomes prevalent in the unit from early August — 39 N.C.O.s and men, 2 officers and 3 sisters developed malaria during August.
- **Deaths in the unit:** Cpl. Hoare, died 29 June after four days' illness (paratyphoid); Pte Furlong, died 21 August of status lymphaticus. Hospital deaths (patients) are given only as weekly totals.
- **Named people:** Matron Charleson (invalided to England, 1 July); Nursing Sisters Smith, Cook, Bell, Johnstone, Merriman and Donkin (invalided to England, 15 June); N/Sisters Clarke, Drysdale, Kidd and Jones E. A. (in infirmary, 31 July); N/S Clarke, Drysdale, Jones and Ryan (invalided to England, 3 August); N/Sisters Brunelle, Halpenny, Kirkby, Landells, Viets and Winters (reinforcements, 4 August); N/Sisters Franklin and Moore (reinforcements, 14 August); N/Sisters Forbes and Holland (recalled to England, 16 August); Capts Smith H. S., Ramsay T. B. and Guilfoyle T. R. (taken on strength, 26 August); Pte Potter (No. 34166, transferred to the Canadian Artillery, 19 June); Cpl. Desjardins (invalided to England, 22 July); Lieut. Ainsley, Capt. Alford, Sgt Maj. McInnis (debility), Sgt Austey (dysentery), Pte [uncertain] (malaria) (invalided to England, 26 July); Ptes Ryan and Cornish (malaria, invalided to England, 29 July).
- **Uncertain readings:** the word before "cases" in the 15 June entry (a struck-through word); the word before "being very rapid" in the 29 June entry; the surname of the private invalided for malaria on 26 July.
