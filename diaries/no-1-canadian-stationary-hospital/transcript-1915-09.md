## Notes — September 1915
- **Dysentery dominates the month:** by 8 September sickness among officers, nursing sisters and men is becoming prevalent, with dysentery admissions increasing daily. The fly menace is very great, with dust and a poor food supply. During the week of 15 September, 5,000 Australians arrived from the Peninsula for a rest — many had dysentery — and the hospital was filled, with an immense influx of troops from England and the Peninsula. By 15 September the health of the unit is improving with more favourable weather, fewer flies and better camp sanitation; dysentery cases diminish, rheumatism increases.
- **The hut hospital:** erection of the hut hospital commences in the first half of September. The hospital begins moving into huts after the 15 September storm; nursing sisters move into huts on 22 September, medical officers remaining in tents. By 30 September an improvised operating room, dispensary and officers' mess are completed, trench drains and stone paths are laid out, and fitting up of the wards continues — though infectious cases are still in tents because the huts are not completed.
- **Storm of 15 September:** a heavy electric storm brings several inches of rain within an hour, flooding all the tents and overflowing the drain trenches; heavy wind, but no tents blown down.
- **Edge cut:** the last line of p. 2 (Admiral Guepratte's visit, 15 September) runs off the bottom edge of the page; the signature/initials at the bottom right are partly cut off.
- **Named people:** Surg. Gen. Bapti (inspected the hospital 1 September); Admiral Guepratte, French Mediterranean Fleet (visited the hospital 15 September).
- **Casualties:** no deaths are recorded in September (the October diary records 4 deaths in September).
- No appendices are listed or included for this volume.
