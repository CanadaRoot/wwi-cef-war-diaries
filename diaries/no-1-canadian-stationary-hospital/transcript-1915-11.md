## Notes — November 1915
- **Date as written:** the 15 November entry is dated "Nov 15-15" in the original — possibly a slip. Transcribed as written.
- **Underlined in the original:** on p. 4 the phrases "the new draft of nurses" and "acute mania" are underlined in the original.
- **Edge cuts:** the 3 November entry ("our horses have been recalled to remount depot") is cut at the right edge; the final word of the 14 November entry ("compliments") is cut at the right edge; the 21 November entry ("making regular weekly visit to hospital") is cut at the bottom edge.
- **Frostbite arrives:** cold wind, snow and rain on 27 November; very heavy conditions on the Peninsula cause hundreds of cases of frozen feet, some very bad, admitted 29 November. By 30 November there are 623 patients in hospital, 157 of them frostbite cases.
- **Dysentery recedes:** dysentery cases become much less by 8 November; rheumatism and bronchial trouble become more apparent. 42 cases of infectious diseases are in the isolation section (the average for the past month).
- **Named people:** Major E. J. Williams, C.A.M.C., Officer Commanding; Dr Scott (put in charge of the Turkish Prisoners Camp, 2 November); Lord Kitchener (inspected troops but not the hospital, 11 November); General Birdwood (visited the hospital 14 November); Major Munro (in hospital with paratyphoid, 19 November); Gen. Bapti (inspecting in Mudros, 21 November); Col Welch (regular weekly visit to the hospital); Capt. Foster (sick in hospital, 27 November).
- **Reinforcements:** 17 men received from England (15 November); 13 nursing sisters arrived on the Aquitania (19 November) and were taken on strength (24 November). Five of the hospital's huts were given up to Australian No. III for the nursing sisters (12 November).
- **Evacuations:** 194 patients evacuated to the Aquitania (27 November); the hospital ship Mauritania arrives 12 November and commences loading patients from the island.
- No appendices are listed or included for this volume.
