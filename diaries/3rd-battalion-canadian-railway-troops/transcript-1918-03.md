## Conventions
Original spelling kept. `[uncertain]` marks words not read with confidence — never guessed.

> **READABILITY NOTE — March 1918:** The March diary text is written in the same cramped, hurried hand as August 1917–February 1918. Each entry ends with a signature flourish in the margin, rendered here as [signed]; the flourish appears to read "C.M.M" throughout March, the same reading December's transcript gave. Zoom crops of all diary-text pages (pp. 6–13) were made and re-read (`zoom-mar-p6-*.jpg` … `zoom-mar-p13-*.jpg` in this folder) to verify names, numbers and place names. The map sheets (Appendix A, pp. 14–29) are described, not transcribed as text; the small place names on them are mostly illegible at this resolution and only the clearly legible ones are named. A future re-read with higher-resolution images may resolve the flagged passages.

> **CONTENTS-PAGE NOTE:** The March contents page (p. 3) lists "Diary Text 8 Pages" and "Appendix A. MAP." The diary text is pp. 6–13 (8 pages), so the contents page is exact for the diary text; the appendix map is on pp. 14–29 (no sheet count given).

## Map overview — Appendix A

Appendix A is one large multi-sheet trench map of the Béthune–Lens–Arras country, the battalion's working area in March 1918. It was filmed in four overlapping sections, numbered 3 to 6 of a six-part set; sheets 1 and 2 of the set do not appear in this appendix, and whether they were ever attached to the diary is not determined here.

- **Four distinct sheets, nine photographs.** Each sheet was photographed once, then re-photographed: p. 20 is sheet 3; pp. 21–22 are sheet 4 (p. 22 is the repeat); pp. 23–25 are sheet 5 (p. 23 is the first exposure, pp. 24–25 the repeats); pp. 26–28 are sheet 6 (p. 26 is the first exposure, pp. 27–28 the repeats). The repeat frames carry a small white "REPEAT / REPETITION" tab beside the sheet-number label, which is how they are identified; the first exposures carry only the sheet-number label. Pixel comparison of the frames confirms the groupings (p. 21 vs p. 22, p. 24 vs p. 25 and p. 27 vs p. 28 are near-identical).
- **Sheet-number labels.** Sheets 3, 4 and 5 carry a typed label "3 OF/DE", "4 OF/DE", "5 OF/DE" at the bottom-right corner. Sheet 6 carries a partly handwritten label, "6 of / de 6". The "OF/DE" wording is bilingual (English "of" / French "de"); the total of the set is not stated on sheets 3–5 [uncertain].
- **Arrangement.** The four sections overlap heavily and fit together roughly as a 2 × 2 block: sheet 3 is the north-west quarter (Béthune–Bruay), sheet 4 the north-east quarter (La Bassée–Lens), sheet 5 the south-west quarter (Hersin–Savy–Mont-St-Éloi–Arras) and sheet 6 the south-east quarter (Lens–Vimy–Arras). The middle band — Ablain-St-Nazaire, Givenchy, Souchez and Carency — appears on all four sheets, so the sections join with wide overlaps rather than clean edges.
- **Orientation and grid.** North is up throughout (Béthune at the top of sheet 3, Arras at the bottom of sheets 5 and 6). The map carries the British trench-map grid: squares numbered 1–36 in repeating blocks, each square quartered a–b–c–d, with large sheet letters (A, B, D, E, F, G, H, J, K, L, P, R, S, U, V, W) printed across the composite marking the individual 1:20,000 sheets pasted together. No title, date, scale bar or north arrow could be read on any sheet [uncertain] — the map's edges and any marginal information fall outside the photographed sections.
- **What the map shows.** Towns and mining villages (many labelled "Cité"), coal-pit heads ("Fosse"), the standard-gauge railways (solid lines), light railways and tramways (dashed lines), the La Bassée canal, and the road net. The front line is not marked as such on these sheets [uncertain] — no trench lines or "front line" labelling could be read.
- **Annotations on the map itself.** Two bold hand-drawn X marks: one near Chocques, north of Béthune (sheet 3, top), and one at Carency (sheets 3, 4, 5 and 6, on a bold dashed east–west line). Their purpose is not stated on the map [uncertain]. There are also circular ring marks around Fouquières-lès-Lens (sheets 4 and 6) and near Arras (sheets 5 and 6), purpose not determined [uncertain]. A dark oval ink blot sits on the map near grid square 28 at the top of sheets 3 and 4 (the Gorre–Essars area); whether it is ink, a censor mark or damage is not determined [uncertain].

## Appendix A — overall map coverage (summary)

- **Overall map coverage — summary:**
  - Appendix A is one large multi-sheet trench map of the battalion's working country in the Béthune–Lens–Arras area of northern France (Pas-de-Calais), filmed in four overlapping sections numbered 3 to 6 of a six-part set; sheets 1 and 2 are not in the appendix.

## Reconciliation notes — March 1918

- **Clocks put ahead (9 Mar):** all clocks were put ahead one hour at 11 pm on 9 March — the start of summer time. 9 March 1918 was a Saturday.
- **Shelling intensifies from 7 Mar:** track damaged in several places on 7 Mar; shelling east of Arras "considerably above normal" on 11 Mar and "E. + N.E. of Arras" on 12 Mar, causing numerous blowouts, all repaired immediately; heavy shelling continues 13–16 Mar with many blowouts to track. The whole area between Lens and Arras is shelled above normal on 14 Mar. No casualties are recorded on any day 1–16 March.
- **Cape to Cairo still unfinished:** the line is being ballasted on 1 Mar; on 13 Mar the unit is "again short of attached labor on Cape to Cairo Line" and "also short of ballast" — the February deadline ("must be finished by end of this month", 12 Feb) was not met.
- **New construction (4 Mar):** a line from the Cape to Cairo line, from Bray Loop to La Marais Dump, to connect with the "Bessy" line; and a line from Gorre (N.E. end of the Bessy line) to Lacouture, and from Lacouture to Loos, "connecting up with Eastern system". This follows the 22 February survey of the Gorre–Lacouture line.
- **"Bray" again:** "Bray Loop" (4 Mar) is read as written, as in January and February ("Bray Junction"); the local place is more likely Bruay, but the writer spells it "Bray" throughout.
- **"La Marais" again:** "La Marais Dump" (4 Mar) is read as written, as on 6 February ("La Marais dump").
- **Artillery Corner:** the yard at Artillery Corner is enlarged by "D" Coy on 4 Mar (all men available); additional sidings there were ordered on 1 Mar; on 10 Mar a detachment is withdrawn from elsewhere for Artillery Corner, "A" Coy continuing the work there.
- **Attached labour problems:** on 8 Mar construction is impeded because the attached labour is being changed and because of "unsatisfactory transportation arrangements for delivering attached labor to its work"; on 13 Mar the unit is again short of attached labour.
- **Sunday 3 Mar:** being Sunday, all men possible were allowed the day off and bath parades arranged; the other companies were likewise given rest and bath parades on 10 Mar.
- **La Couture line (15 Mar):** a pile driver and material for the La Couture line arrived and bridges are being located.
- **Place: Barlin throughout** — the Place column on every diary page in this part reads "Barlin" for the first entry and ditto marks thereafter, as in February.
- **The signature flourish reads "C.M.M."** — rendered [signed] here, following the February convention; its letters are not resolved.

### Reconciliation notes — March 1918, Part 2 (17–31 March)

- **The German spring offensive (21 Mar onwards):** the diary does not name the offensive, but the change is plain — up to 20 March the entries are routine (fine weather, spurs, a new yard at Savy, an inspection); from 21 March the shelling turns heavy and stays heavy. 21 March records a nine-hour continuous bombardment and over 500 feet of track to replace; 22 March records the month's first casualties; 23 March cancels all leave; 25 March has "A" Coy withdrawing two forward detachments; 26 March has men returning from the Port of Embarkation because their leave was cancelled. 21 March 1918 was the opening day of Operation Michael.
- **"Arras-Lens" / "Vimy-Arras" — the writer's paired place names:** "Arras-Lens" appears 17, 18 and 23 March (as "Arras-Lens area", "between Arras-Lens" and "Arras-Lens Jct."); "Vimy Arras & Lens" on 21 March; "Vimy-Arras area" on 22 March; "Lens & Arras" on 26 and 28 March; "Vimy & Arras Lines" on 29 March. All are read as written and flagged where the hand is cramped; the pair evidently means the Vimy–Arras–Lens front, where the battalion's forward detachments worked.
- **Cape to Cairo finished (23 Mar):** "Construction of main Cape to Cairo lines practically completed" and "Ballasting completed to Arras-Lens Jct." — the line the battalion had been building since January (B Coy's portion finished 3 February; only half a mile of ballasting remained on 28 February) is now complete.
- **Savy North Line (26 Mar):** grading begins on a new lateral line, the "Savy North Line", projected to run from Savy via Divion [uncertain], Barlin, Marles-les-Mines and Lillers, paralleling the Cape to Cairo about seven miles to the west. Two words in that sentence are struck through in the original and are illegible. Savy itself was to become a railhead (27 February; 29 March: "Railhead was being made at Savy") and a new yard there was ordered 19 March, with additional yards rushed on 28 March.
- **"Harptack" again (29 Mar):** the line from Savy to Harptack [uncertain] is to be put in shape — the same spelling as 27 February ("the line from Harptack to Savy"), still not identified.
- **Casualties in this part:** 22 March — one "A" Coy other rank wounded, one attached Labour other rank wounded, one attached Labour other rank killed by shell fire; 25 March — "Casualties" are noted when "A" Coy withdraws, but no numbers are given; 30 March — one "B" Coy other rank killed and one other rank wounded; 31 March — one other rank attached to "A" Coy wounded and afterwards died. That is two killed and four wounded in the dated entries of 17–31 March (one of the wounded afterwards died), plus the unnumbered casualties of 25 March.
- **Leave cancelled (23 Mar) and men returning (26 Mar):** all leave was cancelled on 23 March; on 26 March other ranks were "now returning from Port of Embarkation account cancellation leave".
- **Sundays:** 17 and 24 March were both Sundays, and both entries use the same formula — as many men as possible given the day off (the wording is cramped and flagged [uncertain] on both). 24 March also had bath parades arranged. 31 March was Easter Sunday, with a Headquarters Church Parade.
- **24 March entry ends mid-sentence:** "Enemy aircraft active during night bombing" — no object follows; the page ends there and 25 March begins a new entry. Transcribed as written.
- **Short of ballast (30 Mar):** ballast ran short because "all Locomotives have been required hauling troops" — the locomotives were taken up moving troops during the offensive, so no ballast trains ran.
