## Conventions
Original spelling kept. `[uncertain]` marks words not read with confidence — never guessed.

> **READABILITY NOTE — January 1918:** The January diary text is written in the same cramped, hurried hand as August–December (each entry initialled in the margin with a flourish signature [uncertain]). Most entries read clearly at full size; zoom crops of all diary-text pages (pp. 6–14) were made and re-read (`zoom-jan-p6-*.jpg` … `zoom-jan-p14-*.jpg` in this folder) to verify names, numbers and place names. ## Pages 6–7 — Diary text (January 1918, pp. 6–7)


Army Form C. 2118 — WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. Place: Barlin throughout. Each entry initialled in the margin with a flourish signature [uncertain]. Page ranges: p. 6 covers 1–3 Jan; p. 7 covers 4–6 Jan.

### 1-1-18

> Weather fine and cold. "D" Coy reported derailments due to rivets snapping on French 9 K steel portable probable reason being [÷] [uncertain] light-gauge and sudden temperature changes plus heavy loads "C" Coy working on Cape to Cairo line
>
> Lieut. J. J. Shaughnessy [uncertain] having been transferred to 5th Battn Canadian Railway Troops proceeded today.
>
> Received advice that the Military Medal had been awarded to No 279834 Spr J. W. Cooney A Coy. Spr Cooney was killed 16th Dec 1917

### 2-1-18

> Weather fine + Cold. "A" Coy who took over the Vimy system of light-railways from Canadian Corps Tramways on the 29th Dec report three breaks in track from shell fire. Very heavy enemy shelling in forward area. Rain in afternoon

### 3-1-18

> Weather fair and fine. Very heavy enemy shelling in forward area but no breaks reported. 300 chinese labour commenced work today on Cape to Cairo line under supervision of "C" Coy. Track for Cape to Cairo offloaded from Standard gauge today at FOUQUEREUIL. This track differs from other light-railways as it is composed of 45 lb steel and metre gauge sleepers though laid at 60 cm gauge. "D" Coy commenced erecting camp at Bray Junction [uncertain].

### 4-1-18

> Weather fine + Cold. A Coy reports one break in track on Vimy system from Enemy shell fire. Received instructions from A.D.L.R. that A Coy will maintain Carency and Mont Foret lines being lines East of Vimy. B. Coy detachment who are building Cape to Cairo line from Barlin North commenced laying steel at junction with Barlin System of light-railways for yard required for material

### 5-1-18

> Weather fine + Cold. A detachment of "C" Coy moved from Houchin to Hinges. "D" Coy moved their camp at Ecoivres to Bray Junction [uncertain]. Wrote ordnance requesting leather gloves for this unit, which are urgently required for tracklaying. Received instructions from A.D.L.R. to fence all coal dumps at once at the following points MAROEUIL, HERSIN, BOIS DE BRAY, ECURIE. Work was put in hand at once.

### 6-1-18

> Weather mild and rain. Thawing. A B + D Coys did not work except patrol line. C. Coy worked on Cape to Cairo line.
>
> Two NCOs of this unit received commissions and posted to this unit: Four NCOs of this unit received commissions and posted to 12th Bn C.R.T. Three NCOs of this unit received commissions and posted to 11th Bn C.R.T.

## Pages 8–9 — Diary text (January 1918, pp. 8–9)


Army Form C. 2118 — WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. Place: Barlin throughout. Each entry initialled in the margin with a flourish signature [uncertain]. Page ranges: p. 8 covers 7–10 Jan (the 10 Jan entry begins here and continues on p. 9); p. 9 covers the 10 Jan continuation and 11–13 Jan.

### 7-1-18

> Weather cold and snow in afternoon. Marqueffles [uncertain] line to dump and also Lens-Zivy [uncertain] finally turned over today for operation. D Coy finished spur for coal yard at Maroeuil. Received authority for leave to France for 30 other Ranks period 14 days

### 8-1-18

> Weather fine but with snow in afternoon. Tracks are in frozen condition. D Coy reports no work except patrols and snow shovelling. Corpl Sparrow and Corpl Copley passed the gas school as instructors. Received instructions regarding the recovery of solder from empty bully Beef tins [uncertain — trailing mark after "tins" unreadable]

### 9-1-18

> Weather cold in morning snowing in afternoon with strong wind. Owing to thaw some of the cuts on Grade are sluffy [uncertain] in especially in Blackpool cut where approx 1000 Cu yds has fallen in. The speed limit on trains over the light-railways has been reduced to a maximum of 8 miles per hour owing to the thaw.

### 10-1-18

> [Begins p. 8, continued on p. 9 — "Contd" written above the date on p. 9.]
>
> Weather fine. A Coy widening Blackpool cut. New work is contemplated in back areas and instructions received for surveys to be commenced. An Engineering party proceeded today to Savy for the commencement of the
>
> Survey of a new lateral line from the Railhead at Savy to Lillers. Notification was received of the following honours received by this unit: D.S.O. Lt-Col J.B.L. Macdonald. D.C.M. 1039115 Sergt C. H. Glisson. Four officers and two other Ranks were mentioned in despatches.

### 11-1-18

> Weather fine and cloudy. A Coy reports heavy shelling and broken track on Brunswick spur. Arranged for survey party at Savy to obtain billets and rations at CHELERS from 56th Division [XIII Corps — struck through in the original]

### 12-1-18

> Weather fine and mild. Thaw precautions now in force. Using G.S. wagons for all hauling supplies etc. One officer and 5 OR were recommended for decorations on account of gallant conduct on the night of 11th + 12th.

### 13-1-18

> Weather fine + clear slight frost. Received instructions to relay two large portions of track with heavy steel so as to allow the completion of a main line connecting with Third Army system and proceeding through First Army to STEENWERCK. A coy reports heavy enemy shelling of the Vimy detachments and several blowouts of track.

## Page 15 — Appendix 1 (January 1918, p. 15)


A typed letter, headed in handwriting "Annexure I" (underlined, top right). This is the letter the 19 January diary entry records as written to A.D.L.R. — the diary margin against that entry reads "APP 1."

> [Handwritten top right: "Annexure I" underlined]
>
> A-2-1
> Jan. 18th, 1918.
>
> From : O.C. 3rd Bn.
> Can. Rly. Troops.
>
> To : A.D.L.R. (1)
>
> STANDARDIZATION OF RULES AND PRACTICE
>
> In view of the growth and importance of light railways as a branch of the Army Services, the necessity of a uniform set of construction and maintenance-of-way rules, regulations and orders is more apparent as time goes on. At the present time the rules in force are the result of individual initiative and effort, more or less, and are not always in the line of best practice for uniform application. The principles embodied in Maintenance-of-Way Rule Books of any Standard Canadian railway, would with proper modifications to conform to Army Regulations and the requirements of light railways, provide a useful and convenient reference for those in charge of construction or track work, especially if it was got up in book form. Orders affecting this Branch of the Service could also be incorporated to advantage, with perhaps a few line cuts of necessary standards as well.
>
> These suggestions for your consideration, please.
>
> Major &
> Ch. Engr.
> for O.C. 3rd Bn. Canadian Railway Troops.

**Appendix 1 — summary:** Typed letter dated 18 January 1918 from the O.C., 3rd Bn. Canadian Railway Troops, to A.D.L.R. (1), proposing that light-railway construction and maintenance-of-way rules be standardised on the model of a standard Canadian railway's Maintenance-of-Way Rule Book, issued in book form with Army orders incorporated. Signed off, without a personal name, "Major & Ch. Engr. for O.C. 3rd Bn. Canadian Railway Troops."

## Reconciliation notes — January 1918

- **Sgt/Spr Cooney — January 1 helps, does not fully resolve:** 1 Jan (p. 6) reads "Received advice that the Military Medal had been awarded to No 279834 Spr J. W. Cooney A Coy. Spr Cooney was killed 16th Dec 1917". This agrees with the 16 Dec entry (No 279834) on the number and gives rank Spr (Sapper) and initials J. W. — but the 30 Dec entry still reads "No 22983 & [uncertain] Sgt Cooney J. D. awarded M.M." with a different rank, number and initials. The three entries are transcribed exactly as written and are NOT reconciled here.
- **"Cape to Cairo" confirmed:** January writes "Cape to Cairo line" clearly on 1, 3, 4 and 6 Jan (pp. 6–7), confirming the December "Cape to Cairo Line" reading (8 Dec) and supporting the December reconciliation note that the recurring "Capt. to Cairo" on 20, 23, 27 and 28 Dec was the same line misread in the cramped hand.
- **"Bray Junction" / "Bois de Bray":** read as written on 3 Jan ("D Coy commenced erecting camp at Bray Junction") and 5 Jan ("D Coy moved their camp at Ecoivres to Bray Junction"; coal dumps at "BOIS DE BRAY"). The local place is more likely Bruay (Bruay-la-Buissière, near Barlin), but the writer spells it "Bray" three times, so it is transcribed as written and flagged [uncertain].
- **"XIII Corps" struck through:** 11 Jan (p. 9) reads "billets and rations at CHELERS from 56th Division XIII Corps" — "XIII Corps" appears struck through in the original. Transcribed as written with the strike noted; whether the deletion is the writer's correction or a later mark is not determined here.
- **Place names:** "Marqueffles" (7 Jan) and "Lens-Zivy" (7 Jan) are read as written and flagged [uncertain] — the same spellings as December ("Marqueffles", "St. Zivy", both flagged there). "Mont Foret" (4 Jan), "Houchin" (5 Jan), "Ecoivres" (5 Jan) and "Savy" (10–11 Jan) read clearly in the zoom crops and are transcribed as written.
- **Vimy system handover — consistent with December:** 2 Jan records that "A" Coy took over the Vimy system of light railways from Canadian Corps Tramways on 29th Dec — matching the 24 Dec entry, in which the Tramways were to take over "D" Coy's lines and "A" Coy to take over lines then maintained by the Tramways.

- **Month signed by a Major Stewart, not Lt.-Col. Macdonald:** the January diary text closes on p. 14 with the signature "J. [uncertain] Stewart, Major" at the foot of the page. Lt.-Col. J. B. L. Macdonald, who signed December, proceeded on leave on 22 January, so a Major Stewart signed the month in his absence. The daily entries each end with a signature flourish in the same hand; December's transcript read that flourish as "C. M. M." [uncertain], but the January flourish does not obviously read as "C. M. M." — the two readings are NOT reconciled here. A future re-read may resolve it.
- **Appendix 1 date vs diary date:** the letter (Annexure I, p. 15) is dated Jan. 18th, 1918, but the diary entry recording it as written is dated 19 January. Transcribed as written on both; the letter was presumably dated the 18th and sent or recorded the 19th.
- **"Laurel" Junction (14, 24 Jan):** read as written and flagged [uncertain] on both occurrences. No place of that name is identified in the Barlin area; it may be a misreading of the cramped hand.
- **"Chateau fct." (24 Jan):** read as written and flagged [uncertain]; most likely "Chateau Jct." (Junction), but the abbreviation as written is "fct."
- **"Raining at A. & D. Coy H.Q." (15 Jan):** transcribed as written, although the day's weather is given as "cold and fine" — the rain may have fallen at the forward company HQs while Barlin stayed fine.
- **"enemy" interlined (16 Jan):** the word "enemy" is written between the lines, read as qualifying "drawing of fire" — "the drawing of enemy fire in forward areas by material (R.E) left near line of railway."
- **"Fosse* 7" (18, 27 Jan):** an asterisk follows "Fosse" on both occasions; no footnote or explanation is visible on either page. Transcribed as written.
- **"First Army Railhead" (23, 27 Jan):** the word "First" appears struck through on both occasions ("Administrative Commandant First Army Railhead"). Transcribed as written; the strike may be a correction to "Army Railhead."
- **"Territorial" (28 Jan):** "Requested authority … to cultivate five acres of ground in accordance with G.R.O. 3123 at Territorial" — read as written and flagged [uncertain]; the sense of "Territorial" here (a place? a camp?) is unclear.
- **"Southern Cape to Cairo" (28 Jan):** reads "Southern" — the southern section of the Cape to Cairo line, tracklaying now completed; contrast "north end" on 16 Jan, where "C" Coy began laying from Fouquereuil.
- **R.Q.M.S. Wallace:** on 16 Jan he proceeded to Audruicq "with a view to obtaining a commission"; on 26 Jan advice was received from H.Q. C.R.T. that he had received a commission and was posted to this unit. The two entries agree and are not in conflict.
- **Promotions backdated (25 Jan):** T/Capt J R McLean promoted A/Major and T/Lieut D A McColl promoted T/Capt "with effect 31st October 1917" — transcribed as written.
- **31 January strength return:** "C" Coy and one platoon of "D" Coy on construction (Cape to Cairo); the remainder of B. Coy together with A + D Coys on maintenance, "split up into 22 detachments and 67 Section gangs together with 588 OR attached labor" — transcribed as written; the arithmetic of the detachments/gangs is the diarist's own.
- **Thelus box (23 Jan):** Capt. Logan (A Coy) found a tin box near Thelus containing silver spoons and forks and securities "valued at several thousand pounds", belonging to Aimée Menard, maire of Thelus; the box was handed to the Administrative Commandant, First Army Railhead, sealed, and turned over to the French authorities. Names and values transcribed exactly as written.
- **Appendix letter sign-off:** the letter is signed only "Major & Ch. Engr. for O.C. 3rd Bn. Canadian Railway Troops" — no personal name is given. The typed "From" line reads "O.C. 3rd Bn." (the typewriter's C prints unclearly, like E).
