## What the diary is

A typewritten Army Form C. 2118 diary of an entrenching battalion — trained as infantry, used as engineering labour and as a reinforcement feeder for the 3rd Division. The daily pattern is working parties (typically 13 officers and 600 other ranks) in the trenches, then from the winter of 1916–17 water-supply work: reservoirs, pipe lines, pumping stations and light railways around Mont St Eloy, Neuville-St Vaast, Souchez and Vimy. The battalion was mobilized on 6 August 1916, marched to Havre and entrained for the front the same day, and was disbanded in September 1917, its men absorbed into the Canadian Corps Reinforcement Camp.

## Key findings

- **Mobilization (Aug 1916):** mobilized 6 Aug, marched to Havre, entrained 7.30 p.m.; arrived at P Camp (Winnipeg Camp) 2.30 a.m. 8 Aug, moved to Vancouver Camp 9 Aug. Inspected by the O.C. Second Army Group of Entrenching Battalions 10 Aug. Daily working parties of 13 officers / 600 O.R. in the E. & Q. Trenches; reinforcements despatched to 3rd Division units (2 officers + 79 O.R. on 10 Aug, 50 O.R. to the 52nd Battalion on 12 Aug, 50 O.R. to the 3rd Canadian Pioneer Battalion on 19 Aug) and received from the Canadian Base Depot (292 on 14 Aug).
- **Move to the Somme (Sep–Oct 1916):** left Vancouver Camp 7 Sep, entrained 8 Sep, reached billets 4 a.m. 9 Sep, marched to the vicinity of Albert 13 Sep. October: work on the Ancre near Albert, then a long march 14–25 Oct (Albert – Contay – La Vicogne – Candas – Bamly – Heblinghurst – Le Tirle – Acq) to camp at Acq, preparing for winter occupation.
- **Winter 1916–17 (Nov 1916 – Mar 1917):** camp at Acq, working parties at Maroeuil and Mont St Eloy; water-supply work at Bois-de-Bray reservoirs, La Targette, Chaussee-Brunehaut, Savy, Gouy-Servins and Neuville-St-Vaast. Camp shelled by a 21cm howitzer 24 Mar 1917 (1 casualty).
- **Vimy (Apr 1917):** work on the Vimy Ridge sleeper road and cable line, Mont St Eloy Road and the Vaast Road. Camp shelled at 9 p.m. on 8–9 Apr (three casualties) — the eve of the Vimy Ridge attack of 9 April.
- **Water supply, spring–summer 1917 (May–Aug 1917):** camp at Bois des Alleux from 5 May; Ecoivres pumping station, La Motte reservoirs, the Aux Ritz–Maison Blanche pipe line, and the Canadian Corps Light Railway at Neuville-St-Vaast. July 1917 was the heaviest month: 8 killed and 25 wounded, mostly 24–29 July around Lens/Hill 70. August: 4 killed, 17 wounded, plus 50 casualties reported only as numbers; camp moved to Villers-au-Bois 19 Aug, then to Ferfay/Houdain 28–30 Aug.
- **Disbandment (Sep 1917):** at midnight 15 Sep all personnel were absorbed on the strength of the Canadian Corps Reinforcement Camp; 27–29 Sep the reinforcements for the 8th, 9th and 7th Canadian Infantry Brigades were transferred to the 3rd Canadian Divisional Training Depot; 30 Sep all transport, stores and equipment were transferred to the Depot. Final entry 30 Sep 1917.
- **Casualties (whole diary):** 14 killed (1 Sep 1916, 1 Dec 1916, 8 Jul 1917, 4 Aug 1917), about 61 wounded, and about 64 further casualties reported only as a number with no killed/wounded split. **No casualty anywhere in the diary is named.** No officer is named anywhere in the diary either — the commanding officer's signature is illegible throughout.
- **Censoring:** heavy contemporary blacking-out — the whole Place column and many place names are obliterated in Aug–Sep 1916 and Apr 1917, so several camp and road names are unrecoverable.

## Uncertain / open items

- 89 `[uncertain]` occurrences across the 14 transcripts. The recurring ones: "MERLIN" (the road/railway after Neuville-St-Vaast, June 1917), "RUGBY DUMP" (reservoirs near Souchez, June 1917), "LA TARGETTE" (pipe line, June 1917), and the "216th A.F. Coy/Camp" number (June 1917) — all read as written with the reservation, none treated as certain.
- August 1916 p. 4: dates are out of sequence as typed (21, 23, 25, 26, 28, 30, then 27, 28, 29, 30, 31); 28 and 30 each appear twice. No entries are dated 20, 22 or 24 August. Transcribed exactly as typed.
- September 1916: no entries dated 11, 14 or 26 September.
- The September 1917 signature appears to read "A. B. Hopkins" but that reading is not confident enough to record as a name; the signatory is given only as "Lieut Col., Comdg 3rd Canadian Entrenching Battalion".
- No latitude/longitude anywhere in the diary. Places are camp and work-site names only.
- The diary gives the unit as "3rd Canadian Entrenching Battalion"; the LAC record title drops "Canadian" ("War diaries - 3rd Entrenching Battalion").

## Access method note (reusable)

- The LAC item was found with a two-field Title search on the Collection Search Result endpoint (`SearchIn_1=Title&SearchInText_1=War diaries&Operator_1=AND&SearchIn_2=Title&SearchInText_2=Entrenching`): a single Title search for "3rd Canadian Entrenching Battalion" missed the diary because the record title omits "Canadian", and a Title search for the full "War diaries - 3rd Canadian Entrenching Battalion" returned nothing. The two-field search returned all four entrenching battalions (2004943–2004946).
