# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group — September 1917

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group (3rd C.D.T.M. Bde. / 3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Batteries)
- **Month:** September 1917 (Volume 17)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004819 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, file 592, reel T-10809, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** September 1917, p. 1 – p. 17 (image files page-0142 – page-0158)
- **September 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–17): cover, contents, diary text 1–30 September, Appendices A–E, and five map sheets (Méricourt Road trench maps and Avion). Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The Place column reads "VILLERS AU BOIS" throughout the diary text. "C.D.T.M.B's." = Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Batteries; "C.R.A." = Commander Royal Artillery; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Trench Mortar Officer; "N.C.O." = Non-Commissioned Officer; "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "H.T.M." = Heavy Trench Mortar; "O.P." = Observation Post; "R.A." = Royal Artillery; "G." = (in references such as "G.594/12-2") as written. Map references such as "N.27.c.60.10." are trench-map references as written. Each diary sheet is initialled in the margin in ink; the initials are not read with confidence and are recorded as [initials — uncertain] where they occur. The diary is signed at the foot of the last sheet by Capt. J. T. Bennett, O.C. 3rd C.D.T.M.B's. The contents page lists "Volume 17—9" while the cover reads "VOLUME. 17" — both as written.

## Pages 4–6 — Diary text (1–30 September 1917)

### Page 4 — 1–9 September 1917

Place: VILLERS AU BOIS.

> **SEP. 1** — Owing to heavy hostile shelling for the last two days our Medium Trench Mortars have been temporarily put out of action. Heavy Trench Mortar fired 10 rounds at N. 27. [uncertain] to N.33a. S.S., suspected strong points. The results were fair. [initials — uncertain]
>
> **2** — Heavy Trench Mortar fired 11 rounds in vicinity of Railway Embankment M.27c.60.10. [uncertain] M.27c.10.75. The results were only fair. [initials — uncertain]
>
> **3** — No firing. Cleaning guns, repairing pits and refilling magazines. [initials — uncertain]
>
> **4** — Heavy Trench Mortar fired 2 rounds on Fosse 4 de Lievin, N.33.a.6.5. with excellent results obtaining one direct hit. [initials — uncertain]
>
> **5** — No firing. Usual routine work. [initials — uncertain]
>
> **6** — Heavy Trench Mortar fired 13 rounds, RED CHATEAU, N.33.d.4.7. with fair results. No direct hits, 5 rounds within 25 yards, remainder in rear some 30-40 yards. [initials — uncertain]
>
> **7** — At the request of the G.O.C. 10th, Canadian Infantry Brigade, to cease until further orders on account of the Brigade having a great many men working around the trenches doing the repairs which are badly needed, and they did not want any more casualties than were necessary. [signed in ink — [uncertain]]
>
> **8**
>
> **9** — No firing, working parties on general routine work. [initials — uncertain]

## Pages 7–8 — Appendix A: Report on new proposed emplacements, 13 September 1917

### Page 7 — Appendix A (p. 1)

> [Handwritten top left:] Appendix "A"
>
> 3rd. C.D.T.M. Bde.
>
> C.R.A. 3rd. Canadian Division.
>
> In accordance with your instructions a reconnaissance was made of our position in front of MERICOURT from MOOSE JAW ROAD to EDMONTON ROAD with a view to finding positions for the new types of Heavy and Medium Trench Mortars.
>
> I report as follows;—
>
> **Proposed emplacements.**
>
> **6" Newton T.M.**
>
> Locations
>
> T.17.b.1.1. MERICOURT ROAD Map.
>
> T.17.a.85.40. " " "
>
> T.17.a.3.8. " " "
>
> T.10.d.45.30. " " "
>
> From these positions the front can be covered with the exception of 300 yards SOUTH from T.4.d.00.88.
>
> All these emplacements should be made of a permanent type to ensure protection of Mortar and personnel. Also there is very little natural cover to conceal the flash.
>
> **9.45" H.T.M.**
>
> **(Russia Type)**
>
> Locations
>
> T.16.b.5.2. MERICOURT ROAD Map.
>
> T.17.c.15.80. " " "
>
> The first position has very good natural cover but it is off set by chalk formation which would necessarily prolong the work of excavation.
>
> The second position has not as good natural cover, but being located in clay the work could be carried on in a shorter time with less labor.
>
> Both emplacements are necessary to cover the front.
>
> NORTH WEST of the MERICOURT ROAD there are no possible Trench Mortar positions.
>
> **MATERIAL** 6" pit props, lagging, straight and curved corrugated iron, sand bags, 3"x9" timber and green camouflage. This must be available at Railhead Dump T. 16.d.1.6.
>
> **CARRYING PARTIES** It will be necessary to obtain Infantry parties from time to time to handle the material and ammunition.
>
> **ACCOMMODATION.** Dugouts are not available in the trenches, as there is not sufficient for the present garrison.

### Page 8 — Appendix A (p. 2)

> —2—
>
> **ACCOMODATION (Cont'd)** The most suitable place to build is in TEDDY GERRARD Trench in the vicinity of QUEBEC C.T.
>
> **AMMUNITION** The nearest Railhead is at T.16.d.1.6. Delivered by wagon, the Dump located at T.22b.5.7.
>
> The former Dump is preferable as it is much closer to the proposed positions.
>
> It will be advisable to build a reserve bomb store in the vicinity of Railhead Dump.
>
> **TIME REQUIRED** 50 men on 6" Mortars
>
> 50 men on each T.M.
>
> working continuously for two weeks. This does not include any carrying of material.
>
> In additional two days should be allowed for the construction of mens dugouts.
>
> **COMMUNICATION** It will be necessary for us to have authority to draw 5 miles of wire.
>
> **O.P's.** It is practically impossible to obtain a satisfactory view of the enemy front and support line or with from our forward area owing to the width of No-man's-Land at present.

>

>
> The construction of emplacements for Medium and Heavy Trench Mortars depends in a large measure upon the work they will be called upon to carry out previous to Zero hour.
>
> If it is the policy to have the T.M's cut wire and carry out trench destruction, the emplacements will have to be strongly constructed.
>
> If, on the other hand, the T.M's will not be required to fire until Zero hour, much work could be omitted on the emplacements.
>
> Could we be advised, please, the policy of work that will probably be laid down for the 3rd C.D.T.M. Bde.
>
> [Signed:] J. C. Ball

## Pages 13–17 — Maps (Méricourt Road and Avion)

Five trench-map sheets, scale 1:10,000, "SECRET — NOT TO BE TAKEN BEYOND BRIGADE H.Q." on p. 13. They show the Méricourt Road sector with proposed trench-mortar emplacements, arcs of fire and dumps marked in ink (pp. 13–14, "TRENCHES, CORRECTED TO 27-8-17"), the Vimy–Fresnoy sector (pp. 15–16), and the Avion sector (p. 17, "AVION", "7th Field Survey Co R.E. 2383", "Canadian Corps Topo. Section"). Individual trench names, grid numbers and small annotations are not transcribed here — the maps are reproduced as images in the LAC file and the emplacement positions they mark are listed in Appendix A and Appendix D above.

## Open questions / uncertain items (September 1917)
- 1 September: the target reference after "N. 27." is partly overtyped/unclear — read as "N. 27. [uncertain] to N.33a. S.S."; the second target reference on 2 September ("M.27c.60.10. [uncertain] M.27c.10.75.") is likewise partly unclear.
- The margin initials on each diary sheet are not read with confidence; the signature on 7 September is a different hand and is not read.
- 11, 16, 17, 19, 20, 21, 24, 26 and 28 September have no diary entry (blank dates).
- Appendix A: "ACCOMODATION" is spelled as written (one M in the second syllable); "The first position has very good natural cover but it is off set by chalk formation" — "off set" as written. "O.P's. ... or with from our forward area" is transcribed as written; the sentence appears garbled in the original.
- Appendix B is a faint, partly overtyped copy: the quoted paragraph (2) is largely illegible where overtyped; the words "48 [uncertain] rations" and the sentence fragment "advance." are the legible remains. The signatory is read as "J. C. Ball, Major, C.F.A., Brigade Major, R.A., 3rd Canadian Division".
- Appendix D: "Roolincourt" is spelled as written (probably Roclincourt). Battery designations in the left column are read as "X3c", "Y3c", "Z3c", "V3c" as written; the "No 1 Mortar" row for X3c and the "No 1 H.T.M." row are as laid out in the table, which is crowded and partly unclear. The two No-Man's-Land positions from 25 September (T.10b.7.2. and T.11c.3.6.) appear here as Mortars 7 and 6 (Z3c).
- Appendix E: the "6" NEWTON No. 7" row has no sap or remarks entries.
- The cover says "With appendices A - E" and the contents page lists Appendices A–E; all five are present. The diary text is 3 pages as the contents page states.
- No human casualties are recorded in September 1917. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; locations are trench-map references only.
- Names read as: Capt. J. T. Bennett (O.C., D.T.M.O.); Lieut. Cameron; Lieut. Loy; G.O.C. 10th Canadian Infantry Brigade (unnamed); Maj. J. C. Ball (Brigade Major, R.A.).
