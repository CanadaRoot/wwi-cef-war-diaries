# 3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group — war diary

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group (3rd C.D.T.M.B. / "Trench Howitzer Brigade"), 3rd Canadian Division — V3C (Heavy) Battery and X3C, Y3C, Z3C (Medium) Batteries
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004819 — "War diaries - 3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group" (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, file 592, reel T-10809, finding aid 9-52)
- **Dates:** 1 May 1916 – 15 November 1918
- **Pages:** 362 page images (31 monthly volumes, May 1916 – Nov 1918), all downloaded from the LAC IIIF manifest `https://digitalmanifest.bac-lac.gc.ca/DigitalManifest/1/2004819` (`manifest.json`, `download-pages.sh`)
- **Transcripts:** 31 monthly files, `transcript-1916-05.md` … `transcript-1918-11.md`
- **Index:** 834 dated entries in `INDEX-D.md` (merged from `index-tmg-a.md` … `index-tmg-e.md`), covering 1 May 1916 – 15 Nov 1918. Seven entries are date ranges (grouped days in the original); the rest are single dates. No duplicate dates.
- **Transcribed:** 2026-10-27, Eighth D run 5 (five parallel subagents, one per period; parent run verified index coverage and spot-checked the May 1916 cover against the page image).

## What the diary is

A handwritten Army Form C.2118 diary of the 3rd Division's trench mortar group — the heavy 9.45-inch and medium 6-inch (Newton) mortars that fired from the front line in support of the infantry. Organized near Sanctuary Wood in May 1916 under Capt. H. O. Bennett, as the Canadian Field Artillery took the trench mortars over from the infantry (P.P.C.L.I. and 49th Battalion) who had manned them until then. The diary is mostly a daily round-count: rounds fired, targets, retaliation, emplacement work, and men admitted to hospital — with long stretches of routine broken by the division's big battles.

## Key findings

- **Command:** Capt. H. O. Bennett (also signed H. O. Bennatt in secondary sources; diary spells Bennett) commanded from formation; Lieut. Bennett assumed command of the Trench Mortar Brigade vice Capt. Foster 13 July 1916. Capt. R. B. Cameron M.C. and Lieut. J. A. Loy M.C. ran the Avion/Mericourt control stations in 1918. The diarist signs as Capt., D.T.M.O. (Divisional Trench Mortar Officer), 3rd Canadian Division.
- **2–5 June 1916, Sanctuary Wood / Mount Sorrel — the group's bloodiest days:** the German attack of 2 June caught the mortar crews in their exposed positions; the crews of X3C and Z3C stood to their guns "firing till the last moment", only 4 gunners getting out of the crews of all 7 guns. 21 N.C.O.s and men reported missing on 4 June (Sgt. Aminder E. killed; several taken prisoner; others wounded). Gnr. Heaps T. alone remained unwounded of his gun crew, served the gun himself until ammunition was exhausted, then joined the infantry with a rifle. Lieut. Notman W.M. killed by shell fire 5 June.
- **Moves:** Sanctuary Wood/Ypres salient (1916) → Somme (Beaumont Hamel, Nov 1916) → Arras/Vimy sector (Mar–Apr 1917: Neuville St Vaast; 393 medium rounds fired 21 Mar) → Lens/Hill 70/Avion (mid-1917–Mar 1918) → Acheville–Arleux front (late Mar 1918, relieving 62nd Division mortars) → the Hundred Days advance (Aug–Nov 1918) → Boussu/Jemappes.
- **January 1918 (67 pages, the longest volume):** 5 pages of diary text plus Appendix 1 (daily firing reports) and Appendix 2 (target lists and Operation Orders incl. O.O. No. 1, the destructive shoot of 19 Jan). Counter-mortar work systematized: 855 rounds 6-inch and 51 heavy rounds fired 1–5 March 1918 alone.
- **March 1918 casualties:** 12 Mar — premature explosion in a 1.2-inch heavy mortar emplacement killed Cpl. J. A. Hammond (No. 114561) and 2 of his crew, wounding 5 men building a dugout nearby. Late March — Sgt. Maj. Coburn and Sgt. Head killed by one shell while working on an emplacement covering Arleux. Monthly totals for March: 1,793 rounds 6-inch and 342 rounds 9.45-inch.
- **Armistice, 11 Nov 1918:** one line — "Armistice signed all Hostilities ceased at 11:00 hours" — as HQ and wagon lines moved to Boussu. 14 Nov: orders to disband immediately; mortars and stores turned in. **Final entry 15 Nov 1918:** officers and personnel posted away; the diary ends because the unit ceased to exist.

## Uncertain / open items

- 520 `[uncertain]` occurrences across the 31 transcripts (illegible signatures — mostly the O.C.'s — place names, figures, and the repeated Remarks-column monogram).
- The diary groups days into single entries in several months (e.g. 1–8 Mar 1917; 4–6, 10–14, 16–19, 25–29 Jul 1917; the March 1918 blocks 1–5, 9–11, 13–14, 16–18, 19–24, 27–31); index entries use the first date of each block, as in the transcripts.
- Genuine gaps with no entries at all: 11–13 and 16–29 Apr 1917 (diary jumps 15 to 30 Apr); 9–27 May 1917 (apart from an illegible overwritten date entry); 9 Jun 1917. September–October 1917 contain several dates marked "No entry" in the diary itself.
- No latitude/longitude anywhere in the diary — places are trench-map references (e.g. N33a, S10d 80.80, Sheet 36C) transcribed as written.
- Cover vs. contents volume numbers disagree in several months (e.g. Jan 1918: cover "Volume 21" vs. contents "Volume 18-1"); both transcribed as written.
- 8 Mar 1918 entry is dated "8 Feb" in the original, presumably a slip for 8 March — transcribed as written, noted in the index.
- Secondary sources spell the first O.C. "Bennatt"; the diary consistently spells "Bennett" — the diary spelling is used throughout.
