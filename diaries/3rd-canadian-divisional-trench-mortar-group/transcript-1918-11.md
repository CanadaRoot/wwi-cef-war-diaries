# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group — November 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group (3rd C.D.T.M.B.), 3rd Canadian Division
- **Month:** November 1918 (Volume 31; contents page reads "Volume 18-11")
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004819 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, file 592, reel T-10809, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** November 1918, p. 1 – p. 5 (image files page-0358 – page-0362)
- **November 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–5): cover, contents, diary title sheet, diary text 1–15 November 1918 (2 pages). No appendices. The diary ends 15 November 1918, four days after the Armistice. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading of the diary sheets). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The Remarks column on the diary sheets carries a repeated short monogram entry on most lines that I could not read with confidence; it is recorded as [uncertain] in the notes for each diary page rather than in the text. "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "C.D.T.M.B." = Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Batteries; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Trench Mortar Officer; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "Bdes" = Brigades; "C.D.A.C." = Canadian Divisional Ammunition Column; "C.D.A." = Commander Divisional Artillery; "G.H.Q." = General Headquarters; "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General; "Do" = ditto. Map references are given as the Valenciennes 1/100,000 sheet, as written; no trench-map square references or latitude/longitude are given. The signature at the foot of the final diary sheet reads as initials resembling "J. O. B." (or "J. T. B.") followed by a flourish resembling a surname initial "H."; it is recorded as [signature — [uncertain]] and not used as a name. The diarist's rank is given as "Capt." and office as "D.T.M.O., 3rd Canadian Division". The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "(A7883) D. D. & L., London, E.C. Wt. W809/M1672 350,000 4/17 Sch. 88a Forms/C/2118/14".

## Open questions / uncertain items (November 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 31" with "2." written below it, while the contents page reads "Volume 18-11" — both as written; the two volume numbers disagree. The contents page's "Diary Text 2 Pages" matches the two diary sheets (pages 4–5).
- The cover's end date was corrected: "TO 30th Nov. 1918" was struck through and "15th" written above the line, so the cover reads "FROM 1st Nov. TO 15th Nov. 1918" — matching the diary's actual end on 15 November.
- Faint show-through text ("Cover for Documents") is visible vertically at the left edge of the cover; it is part of the paper/form, not diary text, and is not transcribed.
- Page 3 is a diary title sheet on an Army Form C. 2118, not a diary-text sheet: it records the unit as "Trench Mortar Batteries 3rd Canadian Division" and the original as going to "D.A.A.G. Canadian Section G.H.Q. 3rd Echelon". The "2270" and "31" at the top right are sheet/frame numbers as written.
- 1 November: the Place column reads "SOMAIN / Ref. note / VALENCIENNES 1/100,000" — "Ref. note" is read as written; its meaning is unclear. The diary text's abbreviation is read as "Map Refce VALENCIENNES 1/100,000"; the first word is written hastily and the abbreviation may be "Map Ref." as on 3 November.
- 1 November: "2 mobile 6\" Newton mortars" — "Newton" is the mortar type (the 6-inch Newton trench mortar); the diary does not say where the mortars were in action, only that the crews were attached to the 9th and 10th Brigades C.F.A. "to assist".
- The Place column spells the map-sheet word "Sheet" on 3 and 7 November and "Sh." on 8, 11 and 13 November — both as written.
- 6 November: "At WALLERS Do Do Do" is three ditto marks repeating the 5 November entry; 10 November: "Do Do Do Do" is four ditto marks repeating the 9 November entry. The ditto marks are transcribed as written.
- 11 November: the Armistice line — "Armistice signed all Hostilities ceased at 11:00 hours" — is written as a second line under the 11 November move entry, with no time given for the move itself. No further comment on the Armistice is made anywhere in the diary.
- 12 November: "Orders received from 3rd CDA" — "3rd CDA" is read as written (Commander Divisional Artillery); the same abbreviation is used on 14 November.
- 14 November: "for unit to be disbanded immediately" — "disbanded" is read as written, though the word is written without a space after it ("disbandedimmediately" in the manuscript). "All mortars, stores etc turned into Ordnance 3rd Cdn Divn" — the receiving body is given only as "Ordnance 3rd Cdn Divn".
- 15 November: "posted to the [uncertain] Brigade" — the word before "Brigade" is a short compressed word that may read "9th" (the personnel had been attached to the 9th and 10th Brigades throughout the month) but is not read with confidence, so it is marked [uncertain] and not guessed.
- The signature at the foot of page 5 is not read with confidence; the initials appear to resemble "J. O. B." (or "J. T. B.") followed by a flourish resembling a surname initial "H.", the same reading as in the January 1918 transcript, and it is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name. The Remarks column on both diary sheets carries the same repeated monogram, likewise not read with confidence.
- No human casualties are recorded in this volume, and no individual other than the signing D.T.M.O. is named. No firing, rounds fired, or enemy action is recorded in November 1918; the two mobile 6-inch Newton mortars were attached to the field artillery brigades for the whole period and were returned to the Wagon Lines on 13 November, their crews remaining with the batteries.
- How the diary ends: the final entry is 15 November 1918 — "Officers and personnel of the unit posted to the [uncertain] Brigade and 3rd CDAC." — signed by the Captain, D.T.M.O., 3rd Canadian Division. There is no closing note, summary, or sign-off beyond the signature; the unit was disbanded on 14 November and its personnel posted away on 15 November, which is why the diary stops there.
