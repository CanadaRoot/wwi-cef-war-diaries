# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group — February 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group (3rd C.D.T.M.B.), 3rd Canadian Division
- **Month:** February 1918 (Volume 22; contents page reads "Volume 18-2")
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004819 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, file 592, reel T-10809, finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** February 1918, p. 1 – p. 13 (image files page-0252 – page-0264)
- **February 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–13): cover, contents, diary text 1–28 February (5 pages), Appendix A firing reports 24–28 February. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The Remarks column on the diary sheets carries a repeated short entry on most lines that I could not read with confidence; it appears to read "A.T.B." and is recorded as such in each entry's note rather than in the text. "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "H.T.M." = Heavy Trench Mortar; "C.D.T.M.B." = Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Batteries; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Trench Mortar Officer; "O.R." = other ranks; "C.D.A." = Canadian Divisional Artillery; "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "do" = ditto. Map references (e.g. N3a 6.5, H33c 1.4) are transcribed as written; they are trench-map sheet/square references, not latitude/longitude. The signature at the foot of the diary sheets reads as initials resembling "J. O. B." followed by a surname initial resembling "H."; it is recorded as [signature — [uncertain]] and not used as a name. The firing reports are signed by a lieutenant "for D.T.M.O.", whose signature is also [uncertain]. The diarist's rank is given as "Capt." and office as "D.T.M.O., 3rd Canadian Division".

## Open questions / uncertain items (February 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 22." (with "5." written below it) but the contents page reads "Volume 18-2" — both as written. The cover also notes "with appendix 'A'" at bottom left.
- The contents page says "Diary Text 5 Pages."; the diary text in fact occupies pages 4–8 (5 pages), and Appendix A (firing reports 24–28 February) occupies pages 9–13.
- The Remarks column on the diary sheets (pp. 4–8) carries a repeated short entry on most lines that appears to read "A.T.B."; it is not transcribed in the diary text. On page 5 a large bracket in the Remarks column spans the entries for 7–17 February.
- The diary's monthly total is 519 rounds 6" Newton and 89 rounds 9.45" H.T.M. The firing-report rows sum to 513 rounds 6" (24 Feb: 130; 25 Feb: 89; 26 Feb: 57; 27 Feb: 78; 28 Feb: 141) against the diary's daily totals of 130, 99, 57, 78 and 149 — the 25 and 28 February totals disagree by 10 and 8 rounds respectively, and the monthly 6" total (519) is 6 more than the sum of the diary's own daily totals (513). The H.T.M. figures agree throughout (89). All figures are transcribed as written; the discrepancies are not resolved.
- The 28 February firing report (p. 13) dates three entries "27/1/18" and "28/1/18" although the report is headed Feb 28/18; these are transcribed as written and are presumably slips for 27/2/18 and 28/2/18.
- The 26 February firing report (p. 11) is the only February report to carry a "Cal." column and a long explanatory note: firing was reduced on 26 February at the request of the C.O.'s of the 52nd and 58th Battalions to keep the front quiet while a patrol operated around the Slag Heap at Avion.
- The 27 and 28 February reports (pp. 12–13) use a different table layout (Location / Description / Engaged by / Time / Rounds 6" / 9.45 / Remarks) from the 24–26 February reports.
- The diary records no firing by the group 1–23 February: 1–17 February it was under tactical control of the 1st Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Batteries, assisting with gun emplacements and ammunition transport at Bully Grenay; 18–22 February it rested, drilled and marched to its new front; it relieved the 2nd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Batteries on 23 February and fired its first rounds (130 × 6", 30 × 9.45") on 24 February.
- The entry for 6 February is split across pages 4 and 5 ("Party of 10 other ranks proceeded to Canadian Corps" / "Signal School for course of instruction in Signalling and Telephone work").
- No personal names appear in the February diary text or appendices, other than the [uncertain] signatures. No human casualties are recorded in February 1918.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; all locations are trench-map references. Places named: Bully Grenay (H.Q. to 22 February), La Targette Corner, S10d 8.8 (advanced headquarters from 22 February), Avion (Slag Heap), and the map reference "Can Corps Log [uncertain] CANADIAN PACIFIC 1/20.000".
