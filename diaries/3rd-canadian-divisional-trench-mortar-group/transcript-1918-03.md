## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The Remarks column on the diary sheets carries a repeated short entry on most lines that I could not read with confidence; it appears to read "A.T.B." or "100B" and is recorded as [uncertain] in each entry's note rather than in the text. "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "H.T.M." = Heavy Trench Mortar; "C.D.T.M.B." = Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Batteries; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Trench Mortar Officer; "O.R." = other ranks; "N.C.O." = non-commissioned officer; "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "C.D.A." = Canadian Divisional Artillery; "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "O.P." = observation post; "O.O." = Operation Order; "do" = ditto. Map references (e.g. N3a 6.5, H33c 1.4) are transcribed as written; they are trench-map sheet/square references, not latitude/longitude. The signature at the foot of the diary sheets and of the appendices reads as initials resembling "J. O. B." followed by a surname initial resembling "H."; it is recorded as [signature — [uncertain]] and not used as a name. The diarist's rank is given as "Capt." and office as "D.T.M.O., 3rd Canadian Division".

### Page 4 — 1 March 1918

Place: AVION–MERICOURT FRONT.

> **AVION–MERICOURT FRONT, Mar 1st** — By the 1st of the month the system of T.M. control on the Avion–Mericourt section was being systematized after the plan adopted on the Hill 70 section in January. The D.T.M.O. Headquarters was established in front of GIVENCHY at S10d 80.80. Sheet 36C. and a forward control station established on each section. Lieut S. TREMBLAY took command of the MERICOURT Control at T8a 90.40 supporting the 8th Cdn. Infantry Brigades and Capt R.P. CAMERON M.C. the AVION Control Station at T2b 20.55 supporting the 9th Cdn. Inf Bgds. Maps are attached showing the locations of Controls & Mortars on each front. As the Avion Control was established in the same dugout as the Right Battalion H.Qrs. Close liaison was possible on this front with the infantry. Lieut J.A. LOY M.C. was put in charge of signal Communications and thanks to his efforts an adequate system was established between the Mortars, O.P.'s & Control [continued on Page 5]

[Remarks column: "TRACE".]

### Page 5 — 1–7 March 1918

> **Mar 1st–5th** — stations. On the 1st 2nd 3rd 4th & 5th systematic counter-mortar work was carried out on the enemy's emplacements with ground observation a total of 855 rounds 6" ammunition and 51 heavy rounds being fired.
>
> **6th** — On the morning of the 6th a hostile gas projector attack was launched by the enemy against the 43rd Btn. This was accompanied by T.M. fire & several casualties were suffered by 43rd Bn. Thanks to their good gas discipline the T.M. personnel suffered no casualties although several of the mortars in Avion were in the centre of the gassed area. Capt Cameron M.C. retaliated heavily on the enemy mortars which were firing with excellent effect.
>
> **7.** — The 5th C.M.R. Bn. propose to carry out a raid in front of MERICOURT. In conjunction with this plan Lieut S.N. Tremblay is carrying out wire- [continued on Page 6]

### Page 7 — 12–18 March 1918

> **Mar 12** — at N33b. a premature explosion occurred in the emplacement killing No. 114561 Cpl J.A. HAMMOND one of our most valued N.C.O.'s. and 2 of his crew and wounding 5 other men who were building a dugout near the emplacement.
>
> **13 } 14 }** — Retaliation was carried out and Lieut Tremblay continued with his preparations for the 5th C.M.R. Bn. Raid.
>
> **15.** — at 5.30 a.m. (O.O. 14 attached) the 5th C.M.R. Bn. successfully accomplished their raid capturing 14 prisoners. All the Divisional Mortars co-operated in the barrage which was reported to be probably the most accurate & effective ever laid down by the 3rd C.D.A.
>
> **16 } 17 } 18 }** — Neutralizing fire & destructive shoots were carried out as usual. On the 17th an A.N.F. [uncertain] sent down by a patrol plane was responded to with good results.

### Page 8 — 19–26 March 1918

> **Mar 19 } 20 } 21st 22nd } 23rd } 24 }** — Counter mortar work was continued and several special targets engaged at the request of the Infantry with good effect.
>
> **25** — As a result of the enemy operations against the Fifth Army the front of the 62nd Division on the right of the Corps was taken over by the 2nd Canadian Division. The 3rd C.D.T.M.B's relieved the 62nd Div. T.M. Btys on the night of the 24th/25th taking over 12 - 6" and 2 - 9.45" Mortars on the ACHEVILLE–ARLEUX front. On the night of the 25th the personnel of the 2nd C.Div. T.M. Btys were placed under the 3rd C.D.T.M. Btys to man the Mortars on the new front. On the 26th this extra personnel was again withdrawn and this unit had to man the Mortars on the whole front from the LENS CANAL to ARLEUX. In view of a possible enemy attack [continued on Page 9]

[Remarks column on this page carries repeated entries that could not be read with confidence — see Transcription conventions. The date column carries a bracket spanning 19–26 March.]

### Page 9 — 27–31 March 1918 (monthly totals)

> **27 } 28 } 29 } 30 } 31 }** — the main resistance line of the Division was changed from the front line to a line about 500 yards in rear, the original front line being held with outposts which were to withdraw in event of an attack. In continuance of this policy the Mortars on the front were withdrawn and work commenced on emplacements to cover the new line. By the end of the month 6 mediums and one heavy were in action covering the new resistance line and work continuing on the remainder of the emplacements. While work was on an emplacement covering ARLEUX Sgt Maj M.M. [uncertain] Coburn and Sgt Head were killed by one shell.
>
> During the month of March a total of 1793 rounds 6" and 342 rounds 9.45" were fired in counter-mortar work, harassing fire and destructive shoots.
>

## Open questions / uncertain items (March 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 23" (with "6." written below it) but the contents page reads "Volume 18-3" — both as written. The cover also notes "with appendices 1–3." at bottom left.
- The contents page says "Diary Text 6 Pages."; the diary text occupies pages 4–9 (6 pages), Appendix 1 (O.O. No. 14 with Tables "A" and "B") occupies pages 10–12, and Appendices 2 & 3 (arcs-of-fire sketches) occupy pages 13–16.
- The diary text is narrative prose rather than the daily round-count format used in January; entries are grouped in blocks (1–5, 9–11, 13–14, 16–18, 19–24, 27–31 March) rather than one line per day.
- The date entry for the aeroplane shoot (p. 6) is written "8 Feb" although it falls in the March diary between the 7 March and 9–11 March entries; transcribed as written and presumably a slip for 8 March.
- O.O. No. 14 (p. 10) gives Zero Hour as 5 A.M. ("Zero hour will be so many minutes before or minus of that. To be communicated later"), while the diary (p. 7) records the 15 March raid as taking place at 5.30 a.m.
- The 12 March entry (pp. 6–7) names the weapon as a "1.2" Heavy Mortar" and the word before it could not be read with confidence ("During a successful [uncertain] by 1.2" Heavy Mortar on some enemy emplacements in the Cemetery at N33b"); the figure "1.2" may be a misreading of "9.2".
- The 17 March entry (p. 7) reads "an A.N.F. [uncertain] sent down by a patrol plane was responded to with good results" — the word after "A.N.F." could not be read with confidence.
- The 31 March entry (p. 9) names two men killed by one shell while working on an emplacement covering Arleux: "Sgt Maj M.M. [uncertain] Coburn and Sgt Head" — the word between "M.M." and "Coburn" could not be read with confidence; it appears to read "Bde".
- The signature at the foot of the March diary (p. 9) reads "Capt C.F.A." followed by an [uncertain] word, differing from the January and February signatures ("Capt. D.T.M.O. 3rd Canadian Division").
- The March monthly totals are 1,793 rounds 6" and 342 rounds 9.45", fired in counter-mortar work, harassing fire and destructive shoots; 855 rounds 6" and 51 heavy rounds of that total were fired 1–5 March.
- The March diary names officers for the first time: Lieut S. Tremblay (Mericourt Control, also written "Lieut S.N. Tremblay"), Capt R.P. Cameron M.C. (Avion Control), Lieut J.A. Loy M.C. (signal communications), and the diarist's signature is [uncertain] throughout. The brigade commander named in January, Capt. H. O. Bennett M.C., is not named in the March diary.
- Casualties recorded in March 1918: 12 March — premature explosion in an emplacement at N33b killed No. 114561 Cpl J.A. Hammond, 2 of his crew, and wounded 5 other men building a dugout nearby; 6 March — enemy gas projector attack on the 43rd Battalion caused "several casualties" in that battalion (no number given) but no casualties among T.M. personnel; 27–31 March — Sgt Maj M.M. [uncertain] Coburn and Sgt Head killed by one shell on an emplacement covering Arleux.
- The 25 March entry records the unit relieving the 62nd Division's T.M. Batteries on the night of 24th/25th March, taking over 12 × 6" and 2 × 9.45" mortars on the Acheville–Arleux front, in response to the enemy operations against the Fifth Army (the German spring offensive, March 1918); by 31 March the unit was manning mortars along the whole front from the Lens Canal to Arleux and had withdrawn its mortars to cover a new main resistance line about 500 yards in rear.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; all locations are trench-map references. Places named: Avion, Mericourt, Givenchy, Lens Canal, Arleux, Acheville, Ottawa Road, Souchez River, and the map sheets 36C and Ottawa Rd.
