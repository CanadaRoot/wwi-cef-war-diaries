# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group — July 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group (Trench Mortar Batteries, 3rd Canadian Division)
- **Month:** July 1918 (Volume 27)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004819 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, file 592, reel T-10809)
- **Pages:** July 1918, p. 1 – p. 10 (image files page-0312 – page-0321)
- **July 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–10): cover, contents, title sheet, diary text 1–31 July, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is not struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The year "1918" is written by hand above "July" in the Date column of each diary sheet. The Place column is entered only on the first diary sheet (1 July); it is blank on all later sheets. "M.T.M." = Medium Trench Mortar; "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "H.T.M." = Heavy Trench Mortar; "T.M.B." = Trench Mortar Battery (plural "T.M.Bs."); "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Trench Mortar Officer; "N.C.O." = Non-Commissioned Officer; "M.M." = Military Medal; "Divl." = Divisional; "Cdn." = Canadian; "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General; "G.H.Q." = General Headquarters; "M.G." = Machine Gun; "E.T.M." = as written (presumably Enemy Trench Mortar). Battery designations are transcribed as written: "X2c", "Y3c" and "V3c". "6" NEWTON" / "6 inch NEWTON" = the 6-inch Newton mortar; the diarist writes the calibre sometimes as 6" and sometimes spelled out as "6 inch", and the name sometimes "NEWTON" and sometimes "Newton". "a/DTMO" = acting Divisional Trench Mortar Officer. The "Remarks and references to Appendices" column on each diary sheet carries faint pencil initials (apparently the checking officer's initials), not read with confidence — recorded as [uncertain] initials. The printed footer on each diary sheet reads "A5834 Wt. W4973 M687 750,000 8/16 D. D. & L. Ltd. Forms/C.2118/13."

## Pages 4–10 — Diary text (1–31 July 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The "Hour" column is blank throughout.

### Page 4 — 1–5 July 1918

Place: NAILLY, R22C80.40, SHEET 51C.

> **July 1.** — all mortars in reserve positions thoroughly cleaned up and all emplacements repaired. Positions for 2 forward 6" NEWTON mortars reconnoitred (1 covering Right Group to be constructed and manned by X2c M.T.M. Battery and 1 covering Left Group to be constructed and manned by Y3c M.T.M. Battery
>
> **2.** — Work commenced by X2c and Y3c Batteries on the 2 forward mortars
>
> **3.** — Work progressing satisfactorily
>
> **4.** — Emplacements completed by both batteries and mortars placed in position ready to fire
>
> **5.** — Firing commenced by Y3c Battery. 30 rounds of 6" NEWTON being expended on selected targets in NEUVILLE VITASSE

### Page 5 — 6–10 July 1918

> **6.** — Harassing fire carried out as usual on selected points in NEUVILLE VITASSE. Expenditure 50 rounds 6 inch NEWTON
>
> **7.** — No firing. Painting all mortars and repairing emplacements
>
> **8.** — Usual programme of harassing fire carried out during the hours of darkness on various points in NEUVILLE VITASSE. Slight retaliation by the enemy with field guns for our fire.
>
> **9.** — Usual harassing fire on selected points in NEUVILLE VITASSE 80 rounds 6 inch being expended
>
> **10.** — X2c Battery expended 15 rounds on Enemy T.M's and M.G. emplacements. 40 rounds harassing fire expended by Y3c Battery

### Page 6 — 11–14 July 1918

> **11.** — Usual programme of night firing carried out by both batteries 55 rounds being expended
>
> **12.** — X2c Battery engaged Enemy Strong Point during the night with their forward mortar. Usual programme of night harassing fire on NEUVILLE VITASSE by Y3c Battery, a total of 50 rounds 6 inch Newton being being expended
>
> **13.** — 30 rounds 6 inch Newton expended by Y3c Battery on the MAZE and different points in NEUVILLE VITASSE
>
> **14.** — Enemy T.M. engaged by X2c Battery during the hours of darkness and usual night firing by Y3c Battery. Expenditure 80 rounds 6 inch Newton.

### Page 7 — 15–20 July 1918

> **15.** — 51 rounds 6" Newton expended during the night on Enemy M.G.'s T.M.'s and strong points
>
> **16.** — Usual firing during the hours of darkness Expenditure 15 rounds
>
> **17.** — X2c Battery expended 20 rounds on Enemy M.G. and posts during the night. 40 rounds expended by Y3c Battery
>
> **18.** — Harassing fire on Enemy Posts and front line by X2c Battery usual night firing by Y3c Battery. Expenditure 57 rounds
>
> **19.** — 55 rounds expended by both medium Batteries during the hours of darkness.
>
> **20.** — Suspected E.T.M. engaged by X2c Battery 15 rounds being fired.

### Page 8 — 21–25 July 1918

> **21.** — Usual programme of night firing carried out by both medium Batteries Expenditure 37 rounds
>
> **22.** — 30 rounds harassing fire by Y3c Battery on various targets in NEUVILLE VITASSE
>
> **23.** — Usual harassing fire by Y3c Battery on the MAZE during the hours of darkness Work commenced on an emplacement for a Heavy Trench Mortar
>
> **24.** — Orders received to be prepared to move on the 26th
>
> **25.** — No firing was done during the night. At the request of the Infantry 20 rounds 6" Newton were fired on enemy strong points during the night by X2c forward mortar. 9.45 H.T.M in action

### Page 9 — 25–29 July 1918

> **25.** — Y3c Battery covering Left Group and crew for 1 Heavy mortar of V3c [uncertain] Battery relieved by 1st Cdn Div. T.M.Bs.
>
> **26.** — X2c Battery covering Right Group relieved by 40th Divl. T.M.Bs. One of our most reliable N.C.Os Sgt. B. [uncertain] Mosher M.M. wounded at FICHEUX
>
> **27.** — Relief with 40th Divl. T.M.Bs. completed march commenced to SAVY BERLETTE area arrived at AGNIERES and Headquarters of D.T.M.O. established there
>
> **28.** — Complete inspection of kit held by all batteries
>
> **29.** — Physical training, Gun drill, musketry and anti Gas training carried out by all batteries

### Page 10 — 29–31 July 1918

> **29.** — Orders received to be prepared to move on the 30th
>
> **30.** — Commenced march to VI Corps area. Arrived at HUMBERCOURT U8a 10.70 Headquarters established there until further orders
>
> **31.** — Awaiting orders. Further orders received to proceed to the DURY area on 1st Aug 1918
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *[signature — [uncertain]] Capt. / a/DTMO / 3rd Cdn. Divn.*

## Open questions / uncertain items (July 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 27", but the contents page reads "Volume 18-7" — both as written; the two volume numbers disagree. A small handwritten "8." beneath "VOLUME 27" on the cover is not read with confidence.
- The contents page reads "Diary Text 7 8 Pages." as written; it is not clear whether this is "7 Pages" corrected to "8", or "78". There are 8 pages of diary text including the title sheet (pp. 3–10), and 7 sheets of dated entries (pp. 4–10).
- Page 3 (title sheet): the routing line reads "Original to D.A.A.G. Canadian Section G.H.Q. 2. 3rd Ech." — the "2." is read as written and may be "2nd". A handwritten "27" appears at the top right of the sheet, above the printed "Army Form C. 2118."
- 1 July: the parenthesis opened in "reconnoitred (1 covering Right Group" is never closed in the entry, as written. The battery designations "X2c" and "Y3c" are read as written; the "2c"/"3c" suffixes are not expanded.
- 5 July: "30 rounds of" is written in smaller script above the line over "6" NEWTON being expended"; it is transcribed inline as part of the sentence.
- 12 July: "being being expended" is transcribed with the double "being" as written.
- 25 July: the 25 July entry appears twice, on two different sheets — page 8 (firing at the request of the Infantry) and page 9 (relief of Y3c Battery by 1st Canadian Division T.M.Bs.). Both are transcribed as written.
- 25 July (page 9): the heavy mortar battery is read as "V3c" — this is its only mention in the month and the letter is read as "V" but is not certain; it is marked [uncertain].
- 26 July: "Sgt. B. Mosher M.M. wounded at FICHEUX" — the initial before "Mosher" is read as "B." but is not certain (it could be "E."); it is marked [uncertain]. This is the only casualty recorded in the month.
- 29 July: the 29 July entry also appears twice — page 9 (training) and page 10 (orders to be prepared to move on the 30th). Both are transcribed as written.
- 30 July: the map reference at Humbercourt is read as "U8a 10.70" as written.
- The signature at the foot of the July sheets is not read with confidence; it appears to begin "J.H." followed by a surname, but is recorded here as [uncertain] and not used as a name. The signature block reads "Capt. / a/DTMO / 3rd Cdn. Divn."
- The Remarks column on each diary sheet carries faint pencil initials, apparently the checking officer's initials; they are not read with confidence and are recorded as [uncertain] initials rather than transcribed.
- Names read as: Sgt. B. [uncertain] Mosher, M.M. No other personal names appear in the month.
- Places read as: NAILLY (R22C80.40, SHEET 51C); NEUVILLE VITASSE; the MAZE; FICHEUX; SAVY BERLETTE; AGNIERES; HUMBERCOURT (U8a 10.70); DURY area. No latitude/longitude coordinates are given; the only map references are "R22C80.40 SHEET 51C" (Nailly) and "U8a 10.70" (Humbercourt).
- One casualty is recorded in this month: Sgt. B. [uncertain] Mosher, M.M., wounded at Ficheux on 26 July. No deaths are recorded.
