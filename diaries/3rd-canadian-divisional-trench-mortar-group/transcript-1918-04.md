- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group (3rd Canadian Divisional T.M. Batteries), 3rd Canadian Division
- **Month:** April 1918 (1–30 April 1918)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004819 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, file 592, reel T-10809)
- **Pages:** April 1918, p. 1 – p. 15 (image files page-0281 – page-0295)
- **April 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–15): cover, contents, diary title sheet, diary text 1–30 April, appendices 1–3. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" (neither heading is struck through; the printed note reads "(Erase heading not required.)"). The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "A5834 Wt. W4973 M687 750,000 8/16 D. D. & L. Ltd. Forms/C.2118/13." `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The "Remarks and references to Appendices" column carries the word "Appy" (as written, evidently the diarist's abbreviation for "Appendix") against every diary entry; it is noted here rather than repeated entry by entry. The Place column is given on pages 4 and 6 only and is blank on pages 5 and 7–10. "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "M.T.M." = Medium Trench Mortar; "H.T.M." = Heavy Trench Mortar; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Trench Mortar Officer; "C.D." = Canadian Division; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "C.D.A." = Canadian Divisional Artillery; "R.A." = Royal Artillery; "R.H." = Royal Horse Artillery; "R.F.A." = Royal Field Artillery; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "O.P." = Observation Post; "S.O.S." = as written (S.O.S. barrage points); "G.H.Q." = General Headquarters; "A.A.G." = Assistant Adjutant General; "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General; "C.R.A." = Commander Royal Artillery; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "Bgde." = Brigade; "Bn." = Battalion; "Bdr" = Bombardier; "N.C.O." = Non-Commissioned Officer; "M.C." = Military Cross; "H.Q." / "H.Qrs." = Headquarters; "Cdn." = Canadian; "Dft." is not used in this volume. Map references are given as written (sheet, square and point, e.g. "S 30.a", "N 7 b 32.50").

## Pages 4–10 — Diary text (1–30 April 1918)

### Page 4 — 1–3 April 1918

Place: S.30.a, Sheet 36.c S.W.

> **1 Apr 1918.** — Headquarters of Right Group established at S 30.a (old HQ 10th Brigade C.F.A.) under Capt R.B. CAMERON M.C. Headquarters of Left Group withdrawn to IRISH TRENCH at S 6 b 60.10 under Lieut J.A. LOY. M.C.
>
> **2.** — Left Group Mortars were heavily engaged by enemy 5.9" + 4.1" Batteries. No injury to personnel or damage to material.
>
> **3.** — The following has been decided on as the Trench Mortar Policy on the 3rd Canadian Division front.
>
> I. Right Infantry Brigade Front:— 5 M.T.M's to be laid on S.O.S. points and manned night + day. Two of these to be laid on HUDSON TRENCH and to remain silent after registration.
>
> II. Left Infantry Brigade Front:— 2 H.T.M's + 5 M.T.M's to be laid on S.O.S. points + manned night + day.
>
> III All guns to be sited to defend main line of Resistance.
>
> IV All mortars, (except the two laid on HUDSON TRENCH (see Para i) to carry out an active firing policy subject to wishes of Battalion Commanders concerned.
>
> V In addition to above D.T.M.O. will reconnoitre and commence at once construction of positions for M.T.M's. to cover the RED LINE (6 M.T.M.'s on each Infantry Brigade Front)
>
> VI. When the positions defending the RED LINE are completed the intention is to place mortars in these positions

### Page 5 — 4–11 April 1918

> **4 Apr (continued).** — Construction of new positions as described above commenced. Active firing carried out by advanced mortars.
>
> **5.** — New organization of Medium T.M. Batteries adopted in this unit under authority War Office letter No 121/France/1630 (S.D.2) of 6/3/18. [One — struck through] Heavy T.M. Battery retained under special authority granted Canadian Corps. Under the new arrangement there will be only two Medium T.M. Batteries of six mortars each per Division. War Establishment attached. The strength of the unit as per War Establishment is now 11 Officers + 172 other ranks. Lieut J.A. Loy M.C. appointed O.C. X.3.C. Battery + Lieut S.N. TREMBLAY O.C. Y.3.C. Battery.
>
> **6th, 7th + 8th.** — Constructing new emplacements and active firing carried out
>
> **9.** — One of our most reliable N.C.O.'s while in charge of his mortar killed (Bdr J. [uncertain]).
>
> **10–11th.** — Usual construction work + firing carried out.

### Page 6 — 12–14 April 1918

Place: M.10.d.4535, Sheets 36.c S.W. and 36.c N.W.

> **12 Apr.** — Relieved on Right Sector by 4th C.D T.M. B's. 5th C.D T.M.B's. have taken over Left Sector covering the LENS-AVION front + supporting the 7th Canadian Infantry Brigade.
>
> **13.** — 3rd C.D T.M. B's. take over HILL 70 + STE EMILE fronts from 46th D.T.M. B's supporting the 8th Cdn. Inf. Bgde + 9th Cdn Inf Bgde respectively. X.3.C Battery supporting the 9th Bgde in STE EMILE SECTOR + Y.3.C. supporting 8th Bgde in HILL 70 SECTOR.
>
> Wagon lines at BULLY GRENAY — Lieut P. SANDHURST in charge.
>
> Headquarters established at M 10 d 45.35 (ST NAZAIRE 1/40,000) CITÉ STE PIERRE
>
> X.3.C. Battery H.Qrs. at N 7 b 32.50.
>
> Y.3.C. " " at M 31 b 30.15
>
> **14.** — Signal communications + mortars taken over in very bad condition. Extensive repairs required

### Page 7 — 14 April (continued)–18 April 1918

> **14 Apr (continued).** — and new positions to be built covering BLUE LINE (main line of Resistance
>
> **15th.** — LIEUT. LOY M.C. in charge of signal communications. An active firing policy was inaugurated and all available men used to construct new emplacements.
>
> Strength 10 Officers 171 other ranks.
>
> **16.** — Policy of this unit:— In order to relieve the battle front a vigorous + aggressive firing policy will be carried out in order to inflict casualties + lower morale of troops opposite this front who are apparently sent here to rest. Counter mortar work to receive first attention.
>
> **17.** — Hostile T.M.'s were subjected to neutralizing fire by day + night.
>
> **18.** — Night harassing fire carried out on enemy centres of activity + selected targets. Heavy T.M.'s engaged active hostile T.M.'s

### Page 8 — 19–22 April 1918

> **19 Apr (continued).** — counter mortar work
>
> **20.** — 6" T.M.'s were active engaging hostile T.M.'s, [and — struck through] registering targets for night firing — S.O.S., in retaliation and on targets of opportunity.
>
> **21.** — In order to more effectively deal with hostile T.M.'s. 2 O.P.'s have been established. One on HILL 70, one on HILL 65. They will be manned by day + [observers — written above the line; a word, apparently "their", struck through] duty will be to spot hostile T.M.'s. Our counter-mortar has been very effective. Hostile T.M. activity has been reduced to almost nil during hours of daylight. The only activity reported has been at night under cover of darkness + artillery fire.
>
> **22.** — 6" T.M.'s cut wire at request of infantry in preparation for raid. During the raid T.M.'s co-operated with Field Artillery in Box Barrage. Raid by 1st + 4th Can R. Bns. — Successful. Barrage reported as very effective

### Page 9 — 23–27 April 1918

> **23 Apr.** — counter mortar work continued. Enemy retaliated with 5.9" causing some damage to our pits.
>
> **24.** — Ammunition for 6" T.M.'s being scarce heavy Artillery (Left Group) are assisting in counter mortar work.
>
> **25.** — Usual harassing fire.
>
> **26.** — 6" T.M.'s engaged suspected gas projectors at N 3 e 00.50. N 3 e. 00.90 N 3 e 50.70 at request of infantry.
>
> Usual counter mortar work carried out.
>
> **27.** — During night + early morning 6" T.M.'s harassed Trench junctions, + trenches in H 32 b, H 33 c, H 26 d and at request of infantry cut wire from N 8 a 20.40 to N 8 d 25.57 in preparation for raid by 52nd Bn

### Page 10 — 27 April (continued)–30 April 1918

> **27 Apr (continued).** — 6" T.M.'s in HILL 70 Sector supported raid by 2nd Can R. Bn.
>
> **28.** — Usual harassing fire on enemy Trench mortars + trenches.
>
> **29.** — Orders received to move. Usual firing — harassing + counter-mortar work.
>
> **30.** — Relief by XVIII Corps postponed 24 hours. Our Heavy T.M.'s. carried out counter-mortar work expending 46 rounds. Medium T.M.'s usual activity.
>
> Expenditure of Ammunition for Month of April. 6" T.M. Amm = 2384 rounds. 9.45" T.M. " 4211 [uncertain]
>

## Open questions / uncertain items (April 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 24" and the diary title sheet (p. 3) is headed "1-3" and "24", but the contents page reads "Volume 16-4" — both as written; the contents page appears to be a later typed summary and disagrees with the cover on the volume number. The contents page's "Diary Text 8 Pages" matches the 8 diary sheets (title sheet plus 7 diary sheets, pp. 3–10).
- The unit is called "3rd Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group" on the cover and contents, but "3rd CANADIAN DIVISIONAL T.M. BATTERIES" on the diary title sheet (p. 3).
- The "Remarks and references to Appendices" column carries the word "Appy" (as written) against every diary entry; it is evidently the diarist's abbreviation for "Appendix".
- 1 April: the Left Group headquarters is given as "IRISH TRENCH at S 6 b 60.10" as written.
- 3 April: the Trench Mortar Policy is set out in full (paragraphs I–VI); paragraph IV cites "Para i" for the HUDSON TRENCH exception as written.
- 5 April: the word before "Heavy T.M. Battery" (apparently "One") is struck through in ink, leaving the sentence reading "Heavy T.M. Battery retained under special authority granted Canadian Corps."
- 9 April: one man killed — "One of our most reliable N.C.O.'s while in charge of his mortar killed (Bdr J. [uncertain])". The surname is not read with confidence; it appears to read "Braccion". No other casualties are recorded in the month: the 23 April enemy 5.9" retaliation caused "some damage to our pits" but no personnel injury is stated.
- 12–13 April: "5th C.D T.M.B's." and "46th D.T.M. B's" are read as written. The 13th is a separate entry (take-over of HILL 70 + STE EMILE fronts), so no day is missing: the diary covers 1–30 April continuously, with 7th + 8th and 10th + 11th combined into single entries.
- 14 April: the entry runs from the foot of page 6 onto the head of page 7 and ends unfinished — "and new positions to be built covering BLUE LINE (main line of Resistance" with no closing parenthesis and no verb.
- 15 April: the strength is given as "10 Officers 171 other ranks", one officer and one other rank fewer than the 11 officers + 172 other ranks given on 5 April.
- 20 April: the word "and" before "registering" appears struck through.
- 21 April: "observers" is written above the line over a struck-through word (apparently "their") in "They will be manned by day + [observers] duty will be to spot hostile T.M.'s."
- 22 April: "Raid by 1st + 4th Can R. Bns." is as written; the diary does not expand "Can R."
- 26 April: the suspected gas projector positions are given as "N 3 e 00.50. N 3 e. 00.90 N 3 e 50.70" as written.
- 27 April: "6" T.M.'s in HILL 70 Sector supported raid by 2nd Can R. Bn." — "2nd Can R. Bn." as written.
- 30 April: the 9.45" T.M. monthly ammunition figure is read as 4211 rounds [uncertain]; the 6" T.M. figure (2384 rounds) is clear. The diary is signed "J.T.O. Bennett, Capt, D.T.M.O., 3rd Cdn Divn"; a further mark after the name (apparently "H.") is not read with confidence.
- Appendix 1 (p. 12, printed) and the typed copy in Appendix 3 (p. 14) disagree on footnote (b): the printed table reads "Includes 2 corporals and 9 bombardiers" and the typed copy reads "Includes 2 Corporals & 2 Bombardiers" — both transcribed as written.
- Page 11 (signal communications diagram): the box at bottom left is labelled "3rd Cdn" with a further abbreviation below it that appears to read "C.L.A." [uncertain]; a small "10th" written above the box is also [uncertain].
- Page 13 (tracing of arcs of fire): the faint vertical text on the right edge is largely illegible and partly cut off, apparently reading "…which have been … …d map." [uncertain].
- Pages 14–15: text from the reverse of each sheet shows through and partly obscures the type, including mirrored fragments (e.g. "16-3-18", "M/I") on page 14.
- Page 15, first letter: the signature reads "(sgd) W. Boyce, Major, D.A.A.G., Canadian Corps." — the surname is not read with confidence and appears to read "Boyce". Second letter: the signature reads "(sgd) J. E. [uncertain], Captain, Staff Captain R.A., 3rd. Canadian Division" — the surname appears to read "Bustlake" but is not read with confidence.
