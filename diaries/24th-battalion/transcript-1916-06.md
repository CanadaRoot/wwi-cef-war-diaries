## Page 3 — Diary text (June 1916, p. 3)

Army Form C.2118. [Circled 10 at top right.] Right margin: appendix reference 1 (5 June entry).

### 1 June, 3.50 P.M. — H.28.C.5.5 [uncertain], SCOTTISH WOOD

> Captive Balloon observed drifting along in rear of enemy's lines. Being heavily shelled by enemy anti-aircraft [uncertain — the word is split across lines and the second part reads "aft."]. By direction this balloon appeared to be one of our own. When last seen was sinking slowly [over — uncertain] working parties. 7:150 [uncertain] men. supplied to 25th Cdn Bn. No casualties. — R.H.P.

### 3 June, 4.50 P.M.

> During the night 2/3 June the Bn. less 2 Companies, stood to, in anticipation of any attack developing on our front, as a result of enemy attack at HOOCE [uncertain — possibly HOOGE]. However night passed quietly. 6 19 men [uncertain — figures as written] wounded in VOORMEZEELE. — R.H.P.

### 4 June, 5.45 A.M.

> Heavy shelling of front & support lines at about 12 mn to 1:15 am night 3/4 June. Bn again standing to, but night passed quietly in our vicinity. — R.H.P.

### 5 June, 10.30 P.M.

> Bn Hqrs., A & B Coys relieved by Hqrs & 2 Coys of 13th Bn. King's L'pool Rgt. On completion of relief 24th Bn. less 2 Coys moved to rest Camp B. Reninghelst. C & D Coys relieved by 2 Coys 13th Bn. King's L'pool Rgt in VOORMEZEELE DEFENCES. On completion of relief these 2 Coys moved to rest Camp [uncertain] D at RENINGHELST. The 9th Brigade Imperials took over the 5th Brigade frontage on this night, and the 5th [Brigade — uncertain] moved into Canadian Corps reserve. — R.H.P.

### 7 June — H.5.A.5.9. [uncertain], D Camp.

> Left Camp at 7:45 pm. by lorries to YPRES. Moved up and relieved 2nd Can Bn. in trenches from MAPLE COPSE on [our — uncertain] left to ARMAGH WOOD on the right. Bn. Hqrs in VALLEY COTTAGES (ZILLEBEKE I33.C.9.6.). [The — uncertain] Battalion 4 Companies in front line and immediate supports. 3 Platoons 22nd Can Bn. in [support — uncertain] under our orders. No 5 Platoon was wiped out passing thro ZILLEBEKE. 23 casualties being sustained here. Relief completed at 12:30 am 9/6/16 [uncertain — could be 8/6/16]. — R.H.P.

### 8 June

> Heavy shelling all day. This Bn. Sector was under observation from MOUNT SORREL, making movement by day impossible. Trenches badly damaged — R.H.P.

### 9 June

> Our artillery opened an intense bombardment on enemy's lines between 1:00 am and 1:30 am. Retaliation by enemy was very prompt, but not nearly so intense as ours — R.H.P.

[Note: "10-6-16" is written in the right margin at the end of this entry.]

### 10 June

> Our guns again opened an intense bombardment on enemy, between 1:30 am and 2:30 am. Enemy again came back very quickly. — R.H.P.

### 11 June

> 1:30 to 1:50 pm our trenches were heavily shelled. Enfilade fire was brought to bear upon us in many cases, enemy shells came from our left rear, dropping on our support & front lines and intermittent bombardment of our trenches all day, with quick & effective retaliation by our guns. — R.H.P.

Form footer: "8553 Wt. W3541/1454 700,000 5/15 D.D. & L. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118."

## Page 4 — Diary text continued (June 1916, p. 4)

Army Form C.2118. Right margin: appendix reference 2 (11 June entry), 3 and 4 (14 June entry).

### 11 June — ZILLEBEKE FRONT

> Relieved at 12 mn. by 2nd Can Bn. Our patrols captured one German prisoner of 121st Regt. Wurtembergers [uncertain]. — R.H.P.

### 12 June, 5.30 A.M. — CAMP A. SHEET 28 C.6.C. [uncertain]

> Occupied Camp A, with Brigade in Corps Reserve. — R.H.P.

### 14 June, 7.00 P.M.

> Relieved in Camp A by 2nd Bn Scots Guards. Moved up to ZILLEBEKE and relieved 1st Can Bn. with 3 Companies in support line. Bn frontage from MAPLE COPSE on left to ARMAGH WOOD. Bn Hqrs in VALLEY COTTAGES. 1 Company in HALIFAX STREET [adjoining — uncertain] to 22nd Can Bn. This trench was lately occupied by enemy. Relief completed at 1:30 am 15/6/16. was carried out under heavy hostile fire. — R.H.P.

### 15 June — ZILLEBEKE FRONT

> Brought out 6 abandoned, wounded Germans and 1 unwounded German was found in a dugout. The day was comparatively quiet. — R.H.P.

### 16 June, 3 P.M. and 9 P.M.

> Persistent hostile shelling with heavy guns on our support trenches and again from 9:30 to 11:30 persistent & constant shelling with heavies etc. on our whole Sector. 11:30 to 1:00 am 17/6/16. Enemy bombarded with all calibre guns, placing a barrage of fire between Bde Hqrs. [uncertain] and our support lines, and again from 2:00 am to 3:00 am enemy again heavily bombarded old Sector. — R.H.P.

### 17 June, 9.10 A.M. and 9.30 P.M.

> For 20 minutes enemy opened up with an intense bombardment of our front and support lines. During this whole tour, owing to our trenches being under observation no movement whatever was allowed by day, and owing to enemy's persistent shelling it took every available man working during the hours of darkness to repair [uncertain] the damage to our trenches. Little or no improvement could be made. — R.H.P.

### 18 June

> In comparison, this was a quiet day but an intermittent shelling took place all day. — R.H.P.

[Note: an illegible mark stands in the Remarks column beside this entry.]

### 19 June

> Relieved by 15th Can Bn. The Bn less 2 Companies moved into close support with Bn Hqrs and 1 Company in RAILWAY DUGOUTS ZILLEBEKE Sheet I21 C 1.9. [uncertain] and 1 Company at WOODCOT HOUSE (I 20 C 5.2). 2 Companies moved to Camp B. RENINGHELST. — R.H.P.

### 20 June — RAILWAY DUGOUTS

> The Bn moved into Camp H. Reninghelst. The Brigade in Corps Reserve. — R.H.P.

### 20 June — Camp H.

> Bn attended Church Parade. Afternoon Bn sports were held. — R.H.P.

### 21 June, 11.45 A.M.

> Corps Commander visited the Camp. — R.H.P.

### 28 June, 9.00 P.M.

> 24th Cdn. Bn. moved from Camp H. and relieved the 29th Canadian Bn. in the St. Eloi trenches. With one Company in the front line occupying trenches 15-22 inclusive, One Company in support trenches, One Company in VOORMEZEELE SWITCH and One Company in VOORMEZEELE DEFENCES. Bn Hqrs at BOLLARTBEEK [uncertain] DUGOUTS. The relief was completed at 3.am. 29th June. The night of 28/29 of June passed off very quietly on our own immediate front. The 22nd Canadian Bn is on our right and the 26th Cdn Bn. on our left. One Company of 2nd Brigade Reserve Bn [uncertain] is at the disposal of the 26th Can Bn. The front line trenches were found to be badly damaged on taking over. There are no communication trenches through to the front line yet, but work is well under way. — C. F. B. [uncertain]

### 29/30 June 1916 — St. Eloi Trenches

> Day and night passed off very quietly. About 2 am. gas was very slightly noticed in our front and immediate support trenches. Lieut. C.A.B. White [uncertain] slightly wounded while on way up to front line. At about 3:30 pm today, Enemy sent over a few Rum Jars and trench mortars, our artillery soon silenced them. — R.H.P.

## Open questions / discrepancies

- The diary text covers 1–30 June but has entries for only 20 days: 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 28 and 29/30 June. There are no separate entries for 2, 6, 13, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26 or 27 June. The night of 2/3 June is covered in the 3 June entry, the relief of 5/6 June in the 5 June entry, and the battalion was in Corps Reserve at Camp H, Reninghelst, 20–28 June.
- 7 June: the relief is recorded as completed at "12:30 am 9/6/16" [uncertain — could be 8/6/16]; the move up was on the evening of 7 June, so 8 June is expected.
- 1 June: "7:150 men. supplied to 25th Cdn Bn." is as written; the "7:150" may be "7 officers, 150 men" but is not read with confidence. "When last seen was sinking slowly [over — uncertain] working parties" — the word before "working parties" is not read with confidence. "Being heavily shelled by enemy anti-aircraft" is split across two lines, the second part reading "aft."
- 3 June: "6 19 men wounded in VOORMEZEELE" is as written; the figures are not read with confidence. The enemy attack is given as "at HOOCE" [uncertain — possibly HOOGE].
- 5 June: the small word before "D" in "rest Camp [uncertain] D at RENINGHELST" is not read; it may read "to". "And the 5th [Brigade — uncertain] moved into Canadian Corps reserve" — the word after "5th" is cut off at the foot of the page.
- 7 June: the place is read "H.5.A.5.9." [uncertain] and "D Camp."; the map reference for Bn. Hqrs. is read "(ZILLEBEKE I33.C.9.6.)" [uncertain]. "3 Platoons 22nd Can Bn. in [support — uncertain] under our orders" — the word reads like "support". "No 5 Platoon was wiped out passing thro ZILLEBEKE. 23 casualties being sustained here" — the 23 casualties are those of No. 5 Platoon (of the 22nd Bn. platoons under the 24th's orders).
- 11 June: "Our patrols captured one German prisoner of 121st Regt. Wurtembergers [uncertain]" — the regiment's name is spelled as read.
- 14 June: "1 Company in HALIFAX STREET [adjoining — uncertain] to 22nd Can Bn." — the word at the start of the line ends in "ing" and reads like "adjoining". "Relief completed at 1:30 am 15/6/16. was carried out under heavy hostile fire." — the full stop after "15/6/16" is as written.
- 16 June: "placing a barrage of fire between Bde Hqrs. [uncertain] and our support lines" — the word before "Hqrs." reads like "Bde".
- 17 June: "to repair [uncertain] the damage to our trenches" — the word reads like "during".
- 19 June: the map references read "Sheet I21 C 1.9." [uncertain] and "(I 20 C 5.2)".
- 28 June: "Bn Hqrs at BOLLARTBEEK [uncertain] DUGOUTS" and "One Company of 2nd Brigade Reserve Bn [uncertain] is at the disposal of the 26th Can Bn." — neither name is read with confidence.
- 29/30 June: "gas was very slightly noticed in our front and immediate support trenches" — the word reads "noticed", confirmed by the parent run's zoom crop (zoom-jun-p5-2930.jpg). "Lieut. C.A.B. White [uncertain] slightly wounded" — the initials are as read.
- The diary entries are mostly initialled "R.H.P." [uncertain], but the 28 June entry is initialled "C. F. B." [uncertain] in a different hand — the same ambiguity as May's "C.J.B." (the middle letter could be F, J or 7). Neither writer is identified. The diary is signed at the end by Major R. O. Alexander [uncertain — initials as read], i/c. 24th Cdn. Bn. Victoria Rifles of Canada (the May diary names Maj. R. O. Alexander as acting O.C.).
- Appendix numbering: the contents list is renumbered in strictly chronological order, but the diary pages carry the older marginal references 1 (5 June), 2 (11 June), 3 and 4 (14 June), 5 and 6 (28 June). On the appendix pages, the circled numbers 10 (p. 7) and 13 (p. 8) match the contents list, while the handwritten "Appendix 2" (p. 7, struck through), the circled "2" (p. 7) and the handwritten "Appendix 3." (p. 8) are the older references. The contents list has an unnumbered line, "Instructions re taking over of line dated 13-6-16", between Appendices 13 and 14.
- The cover is noted "with appendices 1-6" although the contents list runs to Appendix 28; the note may predate the renumbering (the same discrepancy as May, where the cover said 1–6 and the contents ran to 29).
- Casualty totals for this part (1–30 June, as recorded in the diary text): wounded — "6 19 men" [uncertain] on 3 June, 23 casualties (No. 5 Platoon, 22nd Bn.) on 7 June, Lieut. C.A.B. White slightly wounded 29/30 June. No killed are recorded in this part. Enemy taken: 1 German prisoner of the 121st Regt. captured 11 June; 6 abandoned wounded Germans brought out and 1 unwounded German found in a dugout on 15 June.
- The battalion's month (this part): in the line at Scottish Wood and the Voormezeele defences 1–5 June; relieved by the 9th (British) Infantry Brigade on the nights of 5/6 and 6/7 June and withdrawn to rest camps at Reninghelst; back into the line at Zillebeke (Maple Copse–Armagh Wood) 7–11 June; relieved by the 2nd Can. Bn. on the night of 11/12 June; Camp A, Corps Reserve, 12–14 June; Zillebeke front 14–19 June; relieved by the 15th Can. Bn. 19 June; Camp H, Reninghelst, Corps Reserve, 20–28 June (church parade and battalion sports 20 June; Corps Commander visited 21 June); St. Eloi trenches 28–30 June, relieving the 29th Bn.
