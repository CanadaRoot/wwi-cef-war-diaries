## Page 3 — Diary text, 1–11 September (September 1916, p. 3)

[Army Form C. 2118 — WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. Printed instruction at left: "Instructions regarding War Diaries and Intelligence Summaries are contained in F. S. **Place: FRANCE — EPERLECQUES**

**Septr. 1** — Battalion paraded at 8 am and proceeded to Training Area, where instruction was carried out as per Syllabus attached. Battalion returned to Billets at 4.30pm.

**2** — Battalion proceeded to ranges and shot a Course of Musketry was carried out, the main purpose of which, being to accustom the men to handling the new rifle. The Battalion, less ½ D.Coy and all Regtl Units, finished shooting on this date.

**3** — ½ D.Coy, and all Regtl Units proceeded to the ranges and carried on musketry. The remainder of the Battalion carried on training as usual at Training Area. Battalion returned to Billets at 4.30pm. Battalion Church Parade at 6 pm.

**4** — Battalion paraded at 10.30am and marched out to ARQUES STATION, entraining for CONTEVILLE, as per Appendix "B" attached (O.O.No.47)

**Place: COULONVILLERS**

**5** — Battalion arrived at CONTEVILLE at 5.30am, detrainment completed at 6.20am. The BN proceeded to Billets at COULONVILLERS arriving there at 8.30am, remaining in billets for the day. Approximate distance marched 5½ miles.

**6** — The Battalion paraded at 8.30am and marched out to HALLOY LES PERNOIS, arriving there at 2.30pm remaining in bille [sic] for the night. Approximate distance marched 12 miles.

**Place: LA VICOGNE**

**7** — The Battalion paraded at 8.30am and marched out to LA VICOGNE, arriving there at 11.45am and bivouaced for the night. The Commanding Officer, Scout Officer and O.C., A.B. and C.Coys, proceeded on motor bus to the new trench area for reconnaissance, returning in the evening. Approximate distance covered by Battalion 6 miles.

**8** — The Battalion paraded at 8.15am and marched out to HERISSART, arriving there at 11am, troops being billeted in the town. The 2nd in Command, Adjutant, M.G.O., Bombing Officer and O.C., D.Coy, made a reconnaissance of the new trench area, returning at 6pm. Approximate distance covered on march by troops 6 miles.

**Place: VANDECOURT WOOD**

**9** — The Battalion paraded at 8.15am and marched to VANDECOURT WOOD ARRIVING THERE AT 11.30am. Troops were billeted in huts in the wood. Battalion distinguishing marks of Blue rectangular Square surmounted with [uncertain — overtyped/garbled words, apparently "Red Semi-Circular" / "Red Semi-circle"] were sewed on each shoulder of the men in the Battalion.

**10** — Church parade at 8.30 am for R.Cs., and 10.30am for other denominations. BN paraded at 1.30pm and marched off to ALBERT Brickfields, arriving there at 5pm. Troops bivouaced here, about ¼ mile ALBERT. A few shells fell in the vicinity of bivouac, but no damage to troops. Approximate distance covered 9 miles.

**Place: ALBERT BRICKFIELDS**

**11** — A fatigue party of 125 men paraded at 9.30am and another at the same time of 125 men, and proceeded to forward area for burying cable. A further fatigue party of 145 men were employed by the 6th Cdn.Inf.Bde. cleaning out trenches in the line.

## Page 4 — Diary text, 12–17 September (September 1916, p. 4)

[Army Form C. 2118 — WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. Diary page number "3".]

**Place: ALBERT BRICKFIELDS (continued)**

**12** — Practice in signalling positions of infantry in attack with aeroplane by means of Bengal flares was carried out. This experiment was entirely successful, the aeroplane picking up our position in a few minutes. Work parties totalling 250 men were employed burying cable in the forward area.

**13.** — A few H.E. shells fell in the vicinity of the bivouac, but no damage done. About 100 men employed cleaning trenches under the 6th Cdn.Inf.Bde. Casualties on working party 2 killed, 9 wounded.

**14** — A party of 125 men employed burying cable and 60 men cleaning trenches. The Battalion paraded at 12 midnight 14/15th, and proceeded to the CHALK PITS as Brigade Reserve attached to the 4th Cd. Inf. Bde.

**Place: MUNSTER ALLEY**

**15** — The attack by the 4th Cdn. Inf. Bde., and 6th Cdn.Inf. Bde., commenced at 6.20am. At 8.30am the Battalion, less B.Coy., received orders from the 4th Cdn. Inf.Bde., to move to MUNSTER ALLEY. The BN.Hdqrs were established with Hdqrs of the 19th BN., in MUNSTER ALLEY. B.Coy was engaged throughout the day carrying ammunition, etc., for the 4th C.I.B.. At 5.30pm, the 22nd, and 25th BNs with the 26th BN. supporting and detailed as mopping up party, attacked the village of COURCELETTE., and established and consolidated a line running outside the North and Eastern edges of the village. The 24th BN, which came under orders of the 5th Cdn. Inf. Bde before this attack commenced, supplied parties for the 5th C.I.B., which worked all night and were used for carrying bombs, ammunition, etc., under Brigade arrangements.

**16** — The 24th BN was employed all day carrying bombs, ammunition, stretchers, [uncertain — word before "signals", apparently "OS" or "CS"] signals, Bengal lights to 2 dumps which the Scout Officer selected near the Hdqrs of the 25th and 22nd BNs in COURCELETTE. In the afternoon orders were received that the 24th BN had been detailed to carry rations to the front line for the 22nd and 25th BNs, and to the close support lines for the 26th BN. In addition to carrying its own rations, and the Battalion was also ordered to relieve the 26th BN in close support so that the latter BN could move up to the front line to complete relief of the 4th Cdn. Inf. Bde. The carrying of rations for the BNs in the Brigade and the relief was successfully accomplished, BN.Hdqrs being established at 4.30am 17th inst., in a dugout at the SUGAR REFINERY.

**Place: SUGAR REFINERY**

**17** — At 12.30pm orders were received from the Brigade that the BN, less 1 Coy, were to attack the German front line, with our Right resting on the BAPAUME Road and our left connecting with the 22nd BN in the vicinity of the QUARRIES. The attack commenced at 5pm and was carried out with the BN, less 1 Coy, in extended order along the whole frontage, the lateral distance being approximately about 700 yards. The signal for the advance being one long blast on the whistle, given by the 3 Coy. Commanders. The 4th Coy (B.Coy) was placed at the disposal of the 25th BN. who were attacking from the Northern edge of the Village in a Northeasterly direction. This Coy was held in Reserve by the Officer Commanding the 25th BN and did not go into action. The result of the attack by the other 3 Coys was as follows —

## Page 7 — Appendix, 5th Cdn Inf Bde report BML 216, p. 1 (September 1916, p. 7)

[Appendix — report of the 5th Canadian Infantry Brigade on the attack on Courcelette, 15–17 September 1916. Headed: "5th Canadian Infantry Brigade. BML 216"; "2nd Canadian Division, General Staff."]

In reply to your G.340 of the 19th instant.

In compliance with orders from the Divisional Commander received shortly after 1 p.m., on the 15th instant, I pushed forward to where the 5th Brigade Units were assembled in the vicinity of the 4th Brigade Headquarters and immediately got the 4 Battalion Commanders together and gave them instructions for the attack on the town of COURCELETTE. This done, the Battalion Commanders with their officers and N.C.Os went out and made a reconnaissance of the country between that point and COURCELETTE. Time would only permit a visual reconnaissance of the terrain from the crest of the ridge due East about 1 mile from the above Headquarters.

After details of the attack had been explained to all ranks, the 3 Battalions told off to make the attack were led up by a staff officer to the assembly point on a line running due E. & W. from S.7.b.6.1. to X.1.a.6.8. [uncertain — map references]

Dispositions arranged for the attack were as follows:—
22nd Battalion on the Right: 25th Battalion on the Left:
Dividing line between Battalions – Main Street running through Village – R.24.d.8.0. to R.30.b.8.5. – landmark showing dividing line [uncertain — one word illegible] being the church steeple. The 26th Battalion following in close support and to do the mopping up in the town.
The 22nd Battalion were ordered to attack from the centre of the town to the Right, with 1 Company E. of the SUGAR Road, with orders to mop up the maze of trenches East of the Village.
The 25th Battalion attacked from the centre of the Village to the outskirts on the left.
Both Battalions had instructions to move through the town and to consolidate a line 300 yds. E. of the town and push out advanced posts.
The 26th Battalion after mopping up the town, were ordered to occupy trenches immediately on the Southern side of the ridge, where they would remain in close support to both Battalions.

At 6 p.m., the advance from their rendezvous point commenced and moved forward past the general line R.32.a. – R.9. – R.5.a.6.1. – R.35.b.3. [uncertain — map references] – at 6 p.m. in accordance with Divisional Operation Order.
The whole attack moved through heavy enemy barrage in Artillery formation from almost the moment of their starting the advance. This advance was across almost open country for a distance of 2 miles from the starting point to their objective.
The 22nd Battalion, after a sharp bayonet fight of 10 minutes, during which the bayonet was freely used, pushed through and arrived at their objective at 6.45 p.m.
The 25th Battalion, after a sharp bayonet fight of 5 minutes duration, pushed through and arrived at their objective at 6.25 p.m.
The 26th Battalion following closely had heavy fighting throughout the evening and night and even during the next day, they continued their operations clearing dugouts and cellars, This fighting all took place in the Village.

On the night of the 16th/17th, the 22nd Battalion shortened up their line and the 25th Battalion went in [uncertain — one word illegible] on their Right, from the BAPAUME ROAD to the CEMETERY, relieving one Company of the 21st Battalion of the 4th Brigade, The 24th Battalion moved up in close support to the trenches vacated by the 26th Battalion.
At 5 p.m., on the 17th, the 22nd Battalion and the 24th Battalion were ordered by the Division to take the maze of enemy trenches East of the Village. The 22nd Battalion at once took their objective, viz: trench from point 23 to point 61 (Map: COURCELETTE 1/5,000),

## Page 8 — Appendix, 5th Cdn Inf Bde report BML 216, p. 2 (September 1916, p. 8)

[Appendix continued — p. 2 of the 5th Canadian Infantry Brigade report, BML 216.]

The 24th Battalion attacked with 3 Companies from the BAPAUME Road to the Right of the 22nd Battalion: The Right Company met with heavy Machine Gun and Rifle fire and did not gain their objective. The Centre Company obtained their objective, but after severe fighting, the enemy bombed them out, working through from his main line – with the exception of a small detached post of 15 odd who held on to a section of trench immediately South of the CEMETERY where they consolidated.
The Left Company obtained their objective and passed through 50 to 75 yards beyond, pushing Lewis Guns well to the front.
In conjunction with this attack, the 25th Battalion attacked the trenches immediately North of their line by order of the Division, with Bombers and 1 Company of Infantry, with the object of gaining the high ground [uncertain — one word illegible] of their trenches and establishing detached posts. This attack failed, 2 though [uncertain — possibly "although"] attempts to move forward were frustrated, the Company suffering 65 per cent casualties.
Almost immediately after this attack, the enemy heavily bombarded the whole line of trenches and from then on, threw in many counter attacks all of which were beaten off. The 26th Battalion had to throw in their reserve Company to strengthen the flank. At the crucial moment of the heaviest counter attack which the enemy launched upon us, the relieving Company of the 4th Battalion, 1st Brigade, arrived on the spot and with their assistance, the attack was driven off with heavy losses to the enemy.

3. COUNTER ATTACKS: The 25th Battalion beat off 4 Counter Attacks on the nights of the 15th and 16th.
The 22nd Battalion beat off 7 counter attacks on the night of the 16th, 4 on the night of the 16th, and 2 on the night of the 17th. [As printed — the "night of the 16th" is repeated; likely one should read "night of the 15th".]
On the night of the 16/17th the 26th Battalion drove off several strong patrols bombing attacks in addition to the one mentioned above.
Previous to most of the counter attacks, the whole line was heavily bombarded with all classes of artillery.

## Page 11 — Appendix G, letter from Brig.-Gen. A. H. Macdonell, 21 September 1916 (September 1916, p. 11)

[Handwritten at top: circled 7]

5th Canadian Infantry Brigade.

B.G.2.

TO:-

Lieut-Colonel J.A. Gunn,
O.C., 24th Battalion (Victoria Rifles of Canada)
................................

My dear Colonel:-

I would like very much to have had the opportunity of addressing the 24th Battalion on parade, but owing to your gallant men being scattered in billets, I will not have the opportunity of doing so. Instead, therefore, I am writing this letter which I trust you will have read to all concerned.

While your Battalion was not in the actual attack itself on the town of COURCELETTE, it played a most important part in keeping their comrades supplied with every class of war material and food. In doing so, they suffered seriously in casualties but at the same time, they played a most gallant part in sacrificing themselves in order that their comrades should be fed, watered and supplied generally.

Montreal may justly feel proud of her lads — particularly towards the end of the operations, when they made such a splendid attack on the trenches East of the town.

I deeply deplore the fact that many of our comrades have fallen in action but they shall always live in our memory as having bled in a great and just cause.

Again congratulating you and your Battalion,

I remain,

Yours very sincerely,

Sgd. A. H. Macdonell, Brigadier-General.
Commanding, 5th Canadian Inf. Bde.

21-9-16.
