## Conventions
Original spelling kept. `[uncertain]` marks words not read with confidence — never guessed. The diary text (pp. 3–9) is handwritten in one hand, different from March's "C. T. R." hand; entries end with a two-letter monogram read as "P.B." [uncertain — possibly "R.B."], writer not identified. The orders and appendices (pp. 10–23) are typed, with a few handwritten additions noted in brackets. Zoom crops were made of the handwritten diary pages (pp. 3–9), of the typed orders (pp. 10–11) and of the hardest appendix passages (pp. 12, 15–18, 20–23) and re-read. The contents page lists appendices in renumbered chronological order, but the diary pages carry older appendix reference numbers in the right margin (1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8) that do not match the contents list; see Open questions. The diary pages are Army Form C.2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" ("INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through), form footer "1916. Wt. W993/856 1,000,000 4/15 J.B.C. & A. A.D.S.S./Forms/C.2118." Where the carbon copy is faint — worst on the Defence Scheme, pp. 22–23 — whole phrases are marked [uncertain]. Circled numbers at the top of several appendix pages appear to be appendix numbers; they recur across different documents (12, 20, 26), so no appendix numbers are asserted for them.

## Page 3 — Diary text (April 1916, p. 3)

Army Form C.2118. No appendix references in the right margin.

### 2 April, 10 P.M. [uncertain] — N 32 a 2-3 [uncertain], Sheet 28

> Relieved by the 25th Bn. N.F. in the H.2 and K trenches on completion of relief 24th Bn. (V.R.) moved into Rest Camp at M 5 a 5.7. Sheet 28 vicinity of RENINGHELST. P.B.

### 4 April, 8.00 P.M.

> Battalion ordered to "Stand to," received order at 10.00 P.M. to "Stand down." P.B.

### 6 April, 4.30 a.m. — M 5 a 5.7, Sheet 28

> Battalion ordered to "Stand to" P.B.

### 6 April, 5.15 a.m.

> Battalion ordered to move and rendezvous with 5th Can. Inf Bde at H 3 d 2.9. Sheet 28 P.B.

### 6 April, 5.40 a.m. [uncertain]

> Battalion moved off. P.B.

### 6 April, 7.20 a.m. — H 3 d 2-9, Sheet 28

> Battalion arrived at Camp A, after receiving an order to return to Rest Camp, which order was cancelled when the Battalion had reached OUDERDOM on its way back, when it turned about & returned to Camp A where it bivouaced for the day and night 6th & 7th. About 5 p.m. enemy shelled adjacent field causing one casualty by piece of shell hitting one of our N.C.Os. P.B.

### 15 April, 2.30 a.m.

> Enemy made a bombing attack on Craters 6 and 4, they were supported by artillery. The attack was repulsed by our garrisons. Things quietened down at 3 a.m. P.B.

### 15 April

> Enemy rifle - M.G. and Artillery very active all day P.B.

### 15 April, 10 p.m.

> Lieut V. E. Duclos and C. Ross Robertson [uncertain — initial as read] reconnoitred around Enemy's Crater No 2, approaching within 50 yards of same. They reported parapet about 6 to 8 feet high & strong, owing to bright moonlight could not approach nearer. Returned 2 a.m. P.B.

## Page 6 — Diary text continued: the craters, 15–16 April (April 1916, p. 6)

Army Form C.2118.

### 15 April, 11 p.m.

> Major J. A. Ross [uncertain] & Lieut C. G. Greenshields proceeded to reconnoitre Enemy's Crater No 3, in front of which they ran across what appears to be a crater about 80 yards long 25 yards wide & 20 feet deep. In rear of this crater appears lip of No 3 Crater or a parapet 8 to 10 feet high extending about 300 yards & probably joining No 2 crater. Huns busy working all along parapet. They returned at 2.50 a.m. P.B.
>
> Casualties for this day 1 O.R. killed 4 O.R. wounded & Capt H. D. Langstone [uncertain] slightly wounded. P.B.

### Undated entries on p. 6 (between the 15 April 11 p.m. entry and the 16 April entry)

> Enemy artillery very active all day, shelling front line & supports our artillery slow in answering but once started kept busy all day. P.B.
>
> While in this sector all garrisons are relieved every 24 hours on account of enemy heavy bombardment & great activity in rifle & M.G. fire. P.B.

### 16 April

> Enemy artillery M.G. & rifle fire active all day. At 6 a.m. commenced to lobbing [uncertain — word reads "bang", possibly "bombs into"] Craters 6 and 4 & kept it up at intervals during day. In the afternoon they fired several aerial torpedoes which landed in rear of these Craters. Support trenches & VOORMEZEELE were also heavily shelled. P.B.
