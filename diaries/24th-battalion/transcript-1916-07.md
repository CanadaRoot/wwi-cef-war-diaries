## Page 3 — Diary text (July 1916, p. 3)

Army Form C.2118. Right margin: appendix reference 1 (6 July entry).

### 1 July — St Eloi Trenches

> The day passed off very quietly with very little hostile Artillery Shelling. Machine Guns were very active throughout the night. Patrols report that enemy is holding the front line with very few men. C. F. B. No casualties.

### 2 July, 11.15 P.M. [uncertain — hour as read]

> Enemy shelled SHELLEY-LANE dropping over 100, 4.2's and 5.9's. Hostile aeroplane dropped four Bombs on Voormezeele at 10.25 a.m. Patrols report very little signs of activity in Enemy front line trenches. C. F. B.

### 3 July

> Enemy shelled Trench 21, Shelley Lane and Bus House at irregular intervals during the day, doing very little damage. or [uncertain — reads like "no"] casualties. C. F. B.

### 4 July

> Hostile Artillery was not so troublesome. Patrols report everything quiet in enemy front line during the night. C. F. B.

### 5 July

> Enemy shelled Trenches 18. and 19 for half an hour this afternoon, inflicting three casualties and damaging the trenches considerably. No working parties provided. C. F. B.

### 6 July

> 25th Can. Bn. relieved 24th Can. Bn. in Centre Sector of St. Eloi Trenches. Relief completed at 1.30 a.m. [a word struck through] 7th July, 1916. On completion of relief the Bn. moved into Brigade Reserve and distributed as follows. Bn. Headquarters "A" and "B" Companies at DICKEBUSCH. "C" & "D" Coys. Machine Gun Section, Bombing Platoon and Pioneers at SCOTTISH WOOD. Sheet 28 H.35, b, 6, 3. [uncertain — map reference as read] This last tour of eight days in the front line has been the quietest tour this Bn. has had since Christmas week. Dec. 19/15. [uncertain — date as read] Only 1 Officer and three Other Ranks wounded. C. F. B.

## Page 7 — Diary text continued (July 1916, p. 7)

Army Form C.2118. Right margin: appendix reference 9 (31 July entry). The diary is signed at the foot of this page.

### 26–27 July — Trenches M1-N2A. WYTSCHAETE

> Hostile Artillery dropped 18 High Explosives in rear of Trench M2. At 1.15 P.M. about 8. 5.9's in vicinity of VIERSTRAAT. Enemy bombed our whole front with Trench Mortars throughout the day. Our Stokes Guns were very effective in closing him up each time. C. F. B.

### 27–28 July

> Hostile Artillery fairly quiet during the day, and at night. Enemy Trench Mortars were active again today. Patrols covered our front during the night. No enemy patrols encountered. C. F. B.

### 28–29 July

> Fifteen shells only came over our trenches today. Enemy Artillery is very quiet indeed. Owing to the bright day enemy had two balloons up opposite our front, and aircraft was a little more active. Enemy does not appear to have very many anti aircraft guns opposite us. C. F. B.

### 29–30 July

> A party of Scouts and Bombers went out, under Lieut. P. Walker [uncertain — initial as read] with the purpose of cutting through the German wire and obtaining identification. The wire had been partly cut the night before, but not sufficient to get through, but enough to put the enemy on the alert. The enemy was [uncertain — reads like "to"] was watching the spot as well as having his Carriers [uncertain] well manned, so that no more wire could be cut and the scheme was finally called off. C. F. B.

### 30–31 July

> Enemy shelled front line trenches with Trench Mortar Bombs during the evening. No Casualties or damage done. Very warm weather. C. F. B.
