# Transcript — 24th Battalion (Victoria Rifles of Canada) — "December 1914" file (manifest pages 1–31)

- **Unit:** No. 24th Infantry Battalion (24th Battalion, C.E.F.; Victoria Rifles of Canada)
- **Source:** LAC Collection Search, CEF war diaries (RG 9 III-D-3) — 24th Battalion diary, Volume No. 3, 8 December 1914 to 31 December 1915. Manifest image files `1914-1915-page-0001` – `1914-1915-page-0031`.
- **Pages:** Manifest "December 1914" pp. 1–31 (31 physical pages): cover (p. 1) and the Medical Officer's handwritten diary (pp. 2–31).
- **December 1914 file — COMPLETE as far as these pages go** (31 physical pages): cover; handwritten diary 8 December 1914 – 31 December 1915, ending with the "Report on Water Carts" general remarks. Transcribed 2026-10-26.
- **Manifest order note:** the LAC manifest labels this file "December 1914", but the diary it contains runs from 8 December 1914 to 31 December 1915. Transcribed in manifest order. The diary is the battalion Medical Officer's own record — stretcher bearers, the Sanitary Squad, the Water Detail, dressing stations and the health of the battalion — not the battalion's general war diary. The typed general diary for the same period is in the "October 1914" file (manifest pages 32–40).
- October 1915 onwards in this volume — the diary continues in the next manifest files (August–December 1915), not covered here.

### Oct 30th

### Oct 31st

> Same remarks apply.
>
> No disease of a contagious nature occurred during this month nor have there been any cases of pneumonia although the men are exposed to cold and dampness. The outdoor life seems to act as a preventative. Surprisingly few cases of diarrhoea have occurred. Up to the present no cases of chilled or trench foot have been dealt with.
>
> Much difficulty has been experienced in getting the men to abstain from throwing empty tins and other refuse over the parapet to where such serve as breeding places for flies, but this matter is now under control.
>
> The number of men reporting on the morning sick parade whilst in billets is small although the admissions to the Field Ambulance were considerable. Very few of these cases, however, were evacuated. A short stay in the rest camp served to fit them again for duty.

## Page 29 — Diary text (R.E. Farms, 31 December 1915; General Remarks — Report on Water Carts)

Place: R.E. Farms.

### Dec 31st 1915

> In billets at R.E. Farms as Brigade Reserve. Usual routine followed.
>
> — General Remarks —
>
> Report on Water Carts:
>
> The type of water cart supplied for use of this Battalion has, after 3½ months experience, proved unserviceable for the following reasons:— The thin galvanized iron tank is mounted on a too rigid frame and the constant jolting the carts sustain over the hard metalled roads very soon starts the rivets and leaks are sprung.
>
> The filter chambers are attached to the sides of the tank and they, together with the necessary connections are also subject to much wear and tear due to the jolting and soldered joints soon break and put the filters out of commission.
>
> The pumps are much too small for the rapid filling of the tanks. Their power is regulated by the capacity of the filters to pass the water through into the delivery pipes and with both pumps in use some 25 minutes are required to fill the tank.
