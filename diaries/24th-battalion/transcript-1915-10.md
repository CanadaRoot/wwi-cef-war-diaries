## Pages 3–4 — Diary text (October 1915, pp. 3–4)

Army Form C.2118 — WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. Headed "24th Bn. (Victoria Rifles) Canadians". The right margin carries "C. T. Ritchie Capt." signatures against the entries, and the foot of p. 4 is signed "C. T. Ritchie Capt. Adjt. 24th Bn. Can."

### 4–5 October (night)

> On the night of October 4-5th [the "4" appears struck through], we finished our first tour of duty in the trenches. Enemy were fairly active with sniping and artillery. We were relieved by the 25th Bn. and returned to billets in Locre.

### 5–10 October

> From Oct. 5th — 10th, working parties were supplied both for day and night work to the 25th Bn. during our rest [uncertain] etc. On October 10-11th took over the trenches from the 25th Bn.

### 8 October

> Octr. 8th. On October 8th a patrol of 3 scouts comprised of No. 63305 Cpl. A. A. Ecclestone, No. 65545 Pte. A. P. Nort [uncertain] and No. 65077 Pte. F. J. Bochune [uncertain] went out from H.5 and covered the ground from there to the left of the PETIT BOIS when they came on enemy patrol of 3 men returning to their lines in front of Petit Bois, who were 25 feet away from them. On the way back from where the V.C. Road runs into the enemy's lines, our patrol examined wire entanglements in front of the Petit Bois, and they found that the average width was 30 feet with 5 feet of trip wire in front of it. This trip wire was about 10" high with an entanglement 4 high and 3 ft. in places, to which was attached tin cans. The patrol got over the wire on V.C. Road and lay against enemy's parapet and examined the sandbags which they found in very bad condition and made in some places with what appeared to be old clothing. The patrol unavoidably made some noise in examining the parapet, and were evidently heard by the enemy sentry. They could then hear whispering and calling which brought about half a dozen Germans to the scene of action. The sentry then opened fire, when two of the scouts started to make their way back to their own lines, but Pte. Nort remained behind and threw a bomb over the parapet among the Germans. He estimated that at this time there would be about a dozen in the trench. The bomb exploded with apparently good effect judging from the noise and excitement along the whole line and rapid fire was opened. Pte. Nort in turning to make his way back fell into their listening post, through which he crawled and joined the others and made his way back to our own lines without further incident.

### 13 October

> October 13th — Our artillery bombarded the enemy's lines, commencing at 2 p.m. when they did a lot of damage to their wire and trenches. We cleared the front line trenches putting the men in the [uncertain] , and as soon as the artillery bombardment was over, our men lined the front line trenches and threw over smoke bombs which completely obscured our trenches from the enemy's view, and opened up rapid fire, which kept up for about 10 minutes then everything quietened down.

### 16–17 October (night)

> On the night of the 16-17th Octr. the 24th Bn. was relieved by the 25th Bn. and our second tour of duty in the trenches, apart from the bombardment was an uneventful one.

### 16–22 October

> From 16th — 22nd Octr. working parties were supplied for work on C.T's and support trenches, both day and night.

### 22–23 October (night)

> On the night of the 22-23rd Octr. we relieved the 25th Bn. and started our third tour of duty, which was completed by the 28th [uncertain] when we were again relieved by the 25th Bn. and we moved up to billets in Locre. This tour was an exceptionally quiet one.

>

>
> 11. Officers Chargers — The T.O. will make arrangements for the Officers chargers being at the barrier at the proper hour.
>
> 12. Orderly Room — Orderly Room will be held daily in billets at 10 a.m.
>
> 13. Sick Parade — Sick parade will be held at 9 a.m. daily.
>
> 14. Hd. Qrs. — The C.O. will be available at present Bn. Hd. Qrs. until the last company of the 24th Bn. is relieved. The 2nd in Command will proceed to billets in time to open Bn. Hd. Qrs. there at 9 p.m.
