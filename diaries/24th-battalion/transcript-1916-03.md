## Conventions
Original spelling kept. `[uncertain]` marks words not read with confidence — never guessed. The diary text (pp. 3–6) is handwritten in one hand; entries end with initials read as "C. T. R." (Capt. C. T. Ritchie, Adjutant — he signs the battalion orders in full). The orders and tables (pp. 7–26) are typed, with a few handwritten additions noted in brackets. Zoom crops were made of the hardest handwriting (diary pp. 3–6) and of the poorest carbon copies (pp. 9, 15, 16, 24) and re-read. The month's appendix numbering is untidy: Appendix 26 is used three times (pp. 23, 24, 25) and the Syllabus of Training appears twice (pp. 19 and 25); see Open questions.

### 1 March — Locre

> A. and B. Companies of the 60th Bn. (Victoria Rifles of Canada) Canadians arrived under Command of Lt Col. J. A. Gascoigne and were attached to the 24th Bn (Victoria Rifles) Canadians for instruction. C. T. R.

> C. and D. Companies of the 60th Bn. V.R. Canadians arrived in LOCRE under the command of Maj. W. D. Evans. C. T. R.

### 4 March

> 24th Bn. moved from Locre and relieved the 25th CAN. BN. in trenches M1 to N2A. A. Coy garrisoning M1. M2. C Coy in M3. N1. N.Y. [uncertain] N2.A. Two companies in Bn. Reserve at [uncertain] dugouts. Bn. Hqrs at N10. C.5.7. [uncertain] Sheet 28. 1/40,000, 26th Can Bn. on our right, 21st. Can Bn. on our left. C. T. R.

### 7 March

> Snowed all day and severely cold at night. C. T. R.

### 8 March

> Two Companies of the 52nd. Can Battalion relieved two platoons of A. Coy 24th Bn. in trenches M1. M2. and two platoons of 'C' Coy in trenches M.3. N1. N2. > Three casualties. 2 slightly wounded. One severely. died later in hospital. C. T. R.

> From 6.00 P.M to 6.30 P.M. Enemy shelled our support and communication trenches with 4.1 [uncertain — unit not stated] wounding seven men. 25th Bn. relieved 24th Cdn. ## Page 5 — Diary text continued: the torpedo operation, night of 26/27 March (March 1916, p. 5)

Army Form C.2118. Place column: "N. Y. Y. a. Y. 3 / Sheet 28 / 1/40000". Right margin: appendix references 13, 14.

### 26/27 March, 11 P.M.

> Three scout patrols, of 3 scouts each, sent out into No man's land, to make a reconnaissance for the purpose of finding the three best routes to enemy's wire for setting three Bangalore Torpedoes in his wire. These patrols returned to our trenches by 2 a.m. 27th inst.
>
> One Torpedo was successfully laid in Enemy wire in front of trench K1. with fuse back to our trench, the party returned at 2.45 a.m. On the given signal at 4.15 a.m. the torpedo was successfully fired.
>
> The party taking Bangalore Torpedo out to be placed in enemy wire in front of H4 trench were seen by the enemy, who fired on them with Bombs and rifle Grenades. They were compelled to give up the attempt, so returned to our trench bringing the Torpedo with them.
>
> Two attempts were made by a third party to place a third Torpedo on enemy wire opposite J3 Right trench, but were unsuccessful owing to the enemy's activity. A third attempt was however made which kept the party out too late with the result that they were still in No man's land when our Bombardment commenced. All the party returned with the exception of No 6598 Scout W. J. Dimmer and No 41158 Scout F. Juteau. Scout Juteau was killed while making his way back towards our wire under very heavy fire. Scout Dwyer took cover in an old trench about 150 yards from the enemy wire. When [uncertain — possibly "where"] he remained until found by a scout patrol consisting of No 65077 Pte. S. J. Bethune and No 65192 Pte. E. C. Collins, who as soon as the Bombardment was over, went out in broad daylight into No man's land, and crawled up to within 15 yds of German wire, they then led Pte Dwyer safely back to our own trenches. C. T. R.

[Note: the missing scout is named "W. J. Dimmer" in one sentence and "Scout Dwyer" in the next; the two spellings are as written in the diary. See Open questions.]

## Page 6 — Diary text continued: 27–29 March (March 1916, p. 6)

Army Form C.2118. Place column: "N. Y. Y. a. Y. 3 / Sheet 28 / 1/40000". Right margin: appendix reference 15.

### 27 March, 4.15 a.m.

> An organized Bombardment in co-operation with a neighbouring operation commenced at 4.15 a.m. Immediately after the explosion of mine [uncertain — possibly "of a mine"] up on our left, the Enemy sent up one red flare which burst into two followed by three others. Exactly four minutes after, our artillery opened up the enemy artillery took [uncertain] a retaliation with heavies, trench mortars, grenades and field artillery, forming a barrage of fire directly in rear our support trenches, also heavily shelled communication trenches between our support & front line trenches. This shelling kept up until 6 a.m. when everything quietened down. Telephone communication between Bn. H.Q. and the trenches were broken at 5 a.m. and communication was only established by telephone at 7.15 a.m. Casualties 6 wounded, one Killed. The balance of the day was unusually quiet. C. T. R.

### 28 March

> Enemy very quiet all day. C. T. R.

### 29 March [uncertain — the day could read 30-3-16]

> Enemy showed unusual activity by shelling our support trenches all day at irregular intervals. C. T. R.

> [Signed] J. A. Gunn, Lt Col. O.C. 24th Bn (Victoria Rifles) Canadians.

## Page 20 — Appendix 27: Report on operations on the 27th March (March 1916, p. 20)

Typed. Headed "March 27th, 1916. 5th Canadian Infantry Brigade. Report on operations on the 27th March up to 6.30 p.m."

> Three scout patrols consisting of three men each made a full reconnaissance of the enemy's wire, and the best positions for the placing of torpedoes in enemy's wire, and the lines of approach to these positions, by 2 a.m.
>
> The patrol which reconnoitred in front of the H4 was fired on by the enemy. The parties for laying the torpedoes were organized as follows:-
>
> A patrol of three men proceeded to the front of K.1 and reconnoitred across to the enemy's wire in front of K.1 and the old trench J.3. Right and H.4. In rear of these the parties of three men each carried the torpedoes. A covering patrol consisting of three men each remained in our own wire at the places where the torpedoes were taken out. The torpedo in K.1 was placed with the fuse into enemy's wire and was ready by 3.45 a.m. It was successful discharged at 4.15 a.m. on the given signal. Two attempts were made to place the torpedo in front of J.3 Right but were unsuccessful owing to the enemy's activity. A third attempt was made which kept the party out too late with the result that they were still in No Man's Land when the operations commenced. The torpedo was successfully withdrawn and all the men returned with the exception of two Ptes. Dwyer and Juteau, the latter being wounded. It was then impossible to go out to look for these men during the enemy's bombardment. At 6.30 a.m. this morning Pte. Dwyer was brought in, unhurt, by a Scout patrol, and Pte. Juteau was discovered dead in our own wire. A report on work of this rescue patrol will be submitted later.
>
> The parties proceeding in front of H.4 were seen by the enemy and bombed, very narrowly escaping with their lives, and successfully returned with the torpedo. This torpedo was later exploded by the enemy's shell fire, while lying in our own trenches.
>
> By 4.20 a.m. the enemy retaliated on the rear trenches, shelling them heavily, in addition to sending over trench mortars and rifle grenades. This continued for about an hour and a half, a barrage of fire being put up behind S.P.13 Old and along the C.T. trenches running from H.5 to J.4. The result of this bombardment caused no serious damage to our trenches and only 6 casualties resulted. The enemy appeared to be very nervous as they continued sending up flares in day light.
>
> There has been no shelling or fire of any description since then.
>
> During the operations, the Officer Commanding was in H.5, but by 4.45 a.m. telephone communication had been cut by the enemy's fire.
>
> [Signed] W. O. Alexander [uncertain], Major, 24th Bn. (Victoria Rifles) Canadians.

Bethune and Collins. The meritorious-action account (p. 26) names the rescue party in full. See Open questions.]

## Page 26 — Appendix 28: Account of meritorious action performed by N.C.O. and men (March 1916, p. 26)

Typed. Headed "March 28th, 1916. From O.C. 24th Bn. (Victoria Rifles) Canadians. To 5th Canadian Inf. Bde."

> Sir,
>
> I have the honour to submit the following account of meritorious action performed by the undermentioned N.C.O. and men.
>
> On the morning of the 27th inst. during the operation of laying torpedoes in the enemy's wire in front of the old trench J.3 Right, owing to a chain of circumstances, 2 scouts were reported missing.
>
> At 6.30 a.m. i.e. in daylight, on the conclusion of the enemy's bombardment, No.65032 Sgt. W.C. Westwater [uncertain] with No.65427 Pte. F.J. Heckbert [uncertain], a Stretcher Bearer, proceeded out to J.3 Right with the idea of seeing whether they could find any sign of the missing men. A man was seen lying in our own wire in front of J.3. Sgt. Westwater and Pte. Heckbert proceeded down to the place and found it was one of the missing men, Pte. Juteau who was dead. They then entered J.3 over the parapet. A little later No.65077 Pte. S.J. Bethune and No.65192 Pte. E.C. Collins proceeded out to J.3 Right in advance of Sgt. Westwater and Heckbert. Ptes. Mott and Dolphin. The last four named stayed in J.3 Right while Pte. Bethune and Pte. Collins crawled along the old hedge running across No Man's Land from J.3 Right, and from thence in to an old French trench. While doing this, these two men were observed by the enemy and sniped at. They successfully got within 15 yards of the enemy's wire and found a position there which had apparently been occupied by an enemy listening patrol, as they found empty cartridge cases and used flares. Near this, in the old French trench was Pte. Dwyer the other man who had been reported missing. He was lying at the bottom of the old trench pretending [uncertain — possibly "intending"] to wait until dark before going back to our own lines, as he did not think it was possible to get back in daylight. Ptes. Bethune and Collins then returned by the way they went out bringing Pte. Dwyer with them. They rejoined Sgt. Westwater, Heckbert, Ptes. Mott and Dolphin, and the whole party successfully returned into J.3 Right, and by means of the remains of the old communication trench leading out to J.3 Right. When they regained our trenches it was nearly 8 a.m.
>
> I have the honour to be, Sir,
>
> Your obedient Servant,
>
> [Signed] W. O. Alexander [uncertain], Major For O.C. 24th Bn. (Victoria Rifles) Canadians (Absent on leave).
