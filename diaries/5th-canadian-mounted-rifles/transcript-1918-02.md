## Page 24 — Appendix A, Operation Order No. B-1 (6 February 1918), p. 1

> APPEN "A"
> Ref. Map. 36b.
> 1/40,000
> 5th. C.M.R. BATTALION. February 6th. 1918.
>
> OPERATION ORDER No. B-1.
>
> INFORMATION.
> NOTE. The object of this exercise is to ascertain (a) the best methods of forming a smoke screen to cover an attack by two Battalions and (b) what formations are suitable in conjunction with this particular form of attack.
>
> GENERAL IDEA.
>
> 1. The 3rd. Canadian Division are operating in a N.E. direction.
> 2. At the present time the 4th. and 5th. C.M.R. Battalions are in a position of assembly about P.28 Central and in close touch with the 9th. C.I. Bde on the right and the 7th. C.I. Bde on the left.
> 3. Planes report the enemy to be holding a position facing S.W. and with their centre along the S.W. edge of the woods in P.20.b.
> 4. Our patrols have been sniped at from the woods in P.20.c.
> 5. The 3rd. Canadian Division has been ordered to attack the position mentioned in para. 3 above.
> The 8th. C.I. Bde. has been allotted as its objective the S.W. edge of the wood in P.20.b. and trenches 150 yds north and south of this wood.
> (Note.) Above trenches will be marked with flags.
> The 7th. and 9th. C.I. Bdes. will attack on our flanks and conform to our movements.
>
> SPECIAL IDEA.
>
> 6. Extracts from 8th. C.I. Bde. orders states:-
>
> (a) The 4th. and 5th. C.M.R. Battalions will attack the objectives allotted to 8th. C.I. Bde. the 5th. C.M.R. Bn. on the right and the 4th. C.M.R. Bn. on the left.
> (b) The 5th. C.M.R. Bn will attack on a 225 yards frontage and with its left directed on the centre of the wood in P.20.b. The 4th. C.M.R. Bn. will attack on a 300 yards frontage and with its right directed on the centre of the wood in P.20.b.
> (c) Prior to the above attack the 4th. and 5th. C.M.R. Battalions will make good the woods in P.20.c. The dividing line between Battalions for this operation will be the centre of the practice trenches (not marked on map.)
> (d) The operations mentioned in (a) (c) will be called the 1st. Phase. The operation in (b) the 2nd. Phase.
>
> NOTES.
>
> (a) Blank ammunition will be used up to 100 yards distance from final objective. After this no blank will be fired.
> (b) The following bugle calls will be used :-
> One "G" - Cease Fire and Stand Fast.
> Two "G's" - Carry on.
> Officers Call. - Officers down to Company Commanders will report to G.O.C., at west edge of wood in P. 20.c.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–28 February 1918 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text — 20 Pages", which matches the twenty diary-text pages (pages 4–23). Appendices A–F follow on pages 24–37.
- **Officers named:** Lt-Col. G. C. Draper D.S.O. (O.C.; resumed command 18 February; signs the diary 28 February); Capt. E. Gill (Military Cross, 6 February); Capt. Atherton; Capt. Davies (chaplain, 16 February); Col. Blackburn, Assistant Chaplain General (3 February); Lieuts. MacKenzie, Atto, Barnstead, McKenzie, Curren, Dunham, Savage, Andrews, R. Andrews, R. H. Curren, Gifford, Young; Lieut. H. J. Daubney (promoted Temp. Captain, 26 February); Lieut. Christie (8th Field Company, Canadian Engineers); Sir Julian Byng (lecture on Cambrai, 26 February); Sergt. White.
- **Other ranks named:** 172374 Cpl. C. H. Pentney (Belgian Croix de Guerre, 6 February); L/c Bingham (boxing, 21 February); L/c Curtis of the R.F.C. (boxing opponent).
- **Movements:** Rest and training at Houdain 1–16 February; march Houdain → Estrée-Cauchy 17 February; → Hills Camp, Neuville St. Vaast 18 February; relief of the 19th Canadian Infantry Battalion in the front line, Mericourt sector, night 19/20 February (completed 7.45 PM, no casualties); relieved by the 2nd Canadian Mounted Rifles 25 February, moving back to Left Support with Battalion HQ at Zollern House.
- **Casualties:** None sustained by the battalion in February. The diary states explicitly (25 February) that "there was not a single casualty throughout the tour." Enemy casualties "evidently" caused 20/21 February when a patrol bombed a party of 16 Germans working through the wire ("moans being heard"); one enemy plane brought down 20 February. Enemy gas shells fell near Battalion HQ and the Culvert at 9.00 PM on 24 February.
- **Training/activity:** Gas school and respirator tests, live-ammunition platoon-in-attack and smoke-bomb practice, practice raid and retirement under smoke screen witnessed by the Divisional Commander (15 February), brigade tactical exercise with the 4th Canadian Mounted Rifles Battalion 9 February (criticised by the Division Commander), sports and boxing tournaments at Houdain (prizes: A Co 65, HQrs Co 30, C Co 28, D Co 20, B Co 3), Dumbell Concert Party at Divion, baths and transport inspection by G.O.C. 8th Canadian Infantry Brigade.
- **Front-line work 20–28 February:** Nightly patrols around Mericourt Road, Brandon Sap, Gaby Trench, Hull Road; wire examination (concertina/apron, gaps from shell fire); strong-point construction and wiring; enemy movement observed at Triangle Cutting and Cavalry Road, shells around Doris Dump. Lieuts. Gifford and Young passed through the enemy wire on 27 February and found it "very poor" and easily broken through at any point — intelligence gathered for the prospective raid to be carried out the following month.
