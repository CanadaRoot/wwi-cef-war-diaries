### 1/6/17 — Field

> Weather fair. Wind South-east. Situation overnight Normal. Enemy artillery not so active as usual. Gas shells used against VIMY and LA CHAUDIERE. During the day our artillery was active against enemy supports. Enemy artillery active against T.17.b T.7.c T.7.d and T.19.a. Some movement apparent throughout the enemy line. Our snipers obtained many hits. See disposition report attached. Marked "A"

### 2/6/17 — Field

> Weather fine. Wind S.W light. Our artillery very active against enemy trenches and wire. Enemy artillery put fifty rounds of H.E and 5.9 shells in front and support lines of our centre company. Enemy trench mortars very active against our front line. Machine Gun emplacements built of concrete seen, one at N.32.d.95.10 built on gable end of old house, a direct hit by one of our 6 inch shells made little or no impression on it. Another in old house at N.32.d.0.4. Concrete Observation Post observed at T.2.6.3.1 [uncertain] in old house. Not much movement behind enemy lines today

### 2/6/17 — continued

> Considerable activity shown by ours and enemy aeroplanes today. During last night enemy artillery heavily shelled our front line, but ceased when retaliation was called for. Trench Mortars were active, on our centre company. Lieut HAROLD and 20 other ranks with a Lewis Gun left Bombing Post at T.8.b.6.2, at 10-30 p.m. and went to approximately T.3.c.2.0 where a picquet line was formed, Patrol remained there till midnight when it returned. No casualties. At 12 midnight our artillery put down a heavy barrage on the west of LENS ARRAS ROAD. Three minutes later the enemy commenced putting up flares which appeared to be his S.O.S. signal. At 12.05 scattered shell fell in the vicinity of our left flank and from that point west. Four minutes later the shelling had extended to our right and became fairly heavy. The enemy brought his trench mortars into action also, together with rifle grenades and gas shells. ### 4/6/17 — continued

> [Weather fair — struck through] An officers patrol under Lieut HOLMES left Bombing Post at 11-30 p.m. (3rd) (T.8.a.45.90) for the purpose of inspecting wire between Railroad and AVION ROAD. Enemy were found to be occupying post at T.2.t.85.00 [uncertain] as a bombing post. Patrol returned at 2.15 A.M. Our artillery very active against enemy trenches and works, besides concentrating on several Trench Mortar emplacements. Enemy appears to be very busy defending his trenches, probably owing to our artillery activity. German stretcher bearers still carrying out wounded from LA COULETTE. Continual breaking of enemy's wire by our artillery has evidently disheartened them as they do not appear to have done any additional work. In accordance with instructions the Battalion extended the left flank and took over trenches from the 4th Battalion T.10.c.1.13 [uncertain] to T.1.c.6.4 junction of STOKES to front line, both inclusive. "A" Coy of 5th C.M.R. Battn took over this line.

### 5/6/17

> 5th. Weather fair, Wind W. Last night a large working party of 1st C.M.R. Battn was out in 'No Mans Land' digging jumping off trenches. We had covering parties put in front of them. As the working party began to file out of the trenches a Hun sentry on the railroad shouted. A white flare was sent up, then a red cluster. This brought machine gun fire to bear on the party, quickly followed by artillery, supplemented by trench mortars + Pineapples. Several casualties were caused, and whilst these were being evacuated the enemy placed a heavy barrage of heavy medium and light trench mortars 100 yards to rear of our old front line. Under this barrage two enemy bombing parties began to advance up both sides of the railroad, with a skirmishing line extended to the right. The party was about twenty strong. Our advance machine gun opened fire on the skirmishing line, breaking up its formation, and put up S.O.S signal (2.15 A.M.). Immediately the Lewis gun crew on left of right Coy (B) opened fire and put up S.O.S signal.

### 5/6/17 — continued

> The advanced Machine Gun [one word struck through] post withdrew to a picked point. The enemy were driven off by our bombing squads and Lewis Gun crews on either side of the railroad track. Our men attempted to follow the enemy up but were unable to owing to our artillery barrage coming down in front of the enemy lines. Heavy casualties were caused to the enemy. Great credit is due to Major J. S. TODD O.C. of "D" Coy on which company frontage the action took place, for his capable handling of his Company under trying conditions. Lieut HOLMES of the same company greatly distinguished himself in the attack (See recommendations for honours + awards marked [uncertain]) By 3.00 a.m. (5th) the situation had again become normal.

### 6/6/17

> 6th Weather fair. Wind west. The enemy continues to put over a number of gas shells every night but fortunately we have not had many casualties from gas. Our artillery has been very active all day firing on several enemy parties observed moving about.

### 18/6/17

> 18th Weather fine. Wind N.W. light breeze. Our artillery was active during the day ranging out shoots on definite targets in enemy trenches and rear areas. A large number of our aeroplanes were up controlling the artillery fire. Enemy artillery did not retaliate much, confining their shoots to single shots on our trenches and occasional salvos from heavy guns behind AVION on ANDROS CORNER. About 6.10 A.M. this morning three heavy explosions were heard in AVION near the railway track. Enemy artillery became more active at night, searching the LENS ARRAS RD, VIMY and VIMY RIDGE. Our aeroplanes were flying during the night.

### 19/6/17

> 19th. Weather overcast and showery, Wind Southerly. Four enemy aeroplanes flew over our lines at 3.30 A.M. and balloons were up behind AVION about 4.30 A.M. During the night the enemy sent over 6 white flare shells. Our artillery continues actively firing on enemy lines and rear country without much retaliation today.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–30 June 1917 with no date gaps. All 38 pages listed in the task were present and read. The diary text is on Army Form C. 2118, printed form pages 3–29 (27 pages), of which two pages (form pages 4 and 15) are disposition-state tables rather than diary text. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 26 pages", one fewer than the 27 form pages actually present.
- **Officers named:** Lt-Col D. C. Draper [uncertain] (O.C., signs the diary on page 30); Lt-Col McKENZIE (Officer Commanding, 25 June); Major J. S. TODD (O.C. "D" Coy); Major WILSON (O.C. "A" Coy); Capt W. ATHERTON ("A" Coy); Capt TRITSCH; Lieut R. ANDREWS (O.C. Battalion); Lieut HENDERSON (1st C.M.R. Battalion); Lieut FREEMAN; Lieut WINSLOW; Lieut R. Matthews (Adjutant, signs all four operation orders; the remarks column is initialled "R.M." throughout). No other officer is named in June.
- **Other ranks named:** Only in the casualty lists. 8 June (first tour): Ptes McKINNON E.V., McDONALD J.E., HAINES H.F., THORNE P., CARR H.A., McRAE K.W., REEDIE J., DAOUST E.E., THOMPSON G., GELDART F.B. (killed in action); Ptes WITHERSPOON D. and REEVE E. (died from gas poisoning). 25 June (second tour): Ptes BAIRD J.C., DAIGLE J., L/Cpl SELMAN C.R., Pte MEYERS N.V., Cpl HENDERSON D. McE. (killed in action); Pte COOPER F.A. (died of wounds). No other ranks are named individually.
- **Equipment:** Lewis guns (patrols and the advanced machine-gun post), Stokes guns (two in the June 1st disposition state, eight in the June 18th state; two relieved under O.O.33), rifle grenades (fired 25 June to test the enemy), "fixed rifles" installed on the front, trench mortars ("Pineapples" — enemy grenades — on 5 June), machine-gun emplacements of concrete (2 June), field kitchens, water bottles, fighting kit. Snipers claim hits on most days (8 on 3 June, 10 on 6 June, 11 on 7 June, 2 on 22 June, 2 on 23 June) and two enemy periscopes broken on 22 June.
- **Movements:** The battalion held the VIMY DEFENCES (front line) 1–7 June; was relieved in the front line on the night of 7/8 June and moved to the QUARRIES AREA (8–12 June, working parties and musketry); was relieved by the 116th Canadian Battalion on 12/13 June and marched to VILLERS AU BOIS (13–17 June, rest, church parade, musketry, platoon organisation); moved forward on 17 June to relieve the 43rd Canadian Battalion in the support line (relief complete by midnight, no casualties); held the VIMY DEFENCES 18–25 June, with "C" Coy's platoons 9 and 10 moved forward to HALIFAX TRENCH on the night of 20/21 June under 2nd C.M.R. command; was relieved on the night of 25/26 June by two companies of the 43rd and two of the 58th Battalions (relief complete 5.00 a.m. 26 June) and marched to VILLERS AU BOIS on 26 June, resting there to 30 June. A planned rest at BRUAY was ordered 28 June and cancelled 30 June.
- **Discipline:** None recorded in June 1917.
- **Casualties:** First tour (1–7 June, reported 8 June): 10 killed in action, 2 died from gas poisoning, 54 wounded, 13 gassed. Second tour (17–25 June, reported 25 June): 5 killed in action, 1 died of wounds, 32 wounded, 2 missing. The 5 June enemy bombing-party attack on the 1st C.M.R. working party caused "several casualties" to that battalion (numbers not given); the 5th C.M.R. patrol on 25 June suffered three wounded. No officer casualties are recorded.
