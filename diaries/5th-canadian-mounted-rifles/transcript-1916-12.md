### 29/12/16 — Front Line

> 4th C.M.R. Battalion on our [uncertain], 6th Battalion (9th Brigade) on our right. Whole front patrolled during the night but no enemy patrols encountered.

### 30/12/16 — Front Line

> Weather wet. Wind S.W. Situation quiet. The continuous rain rendered trenches almost uninhabitable. Dug out caved in, in many cases and trenches fell in at places, others filling with mud and water. The new men showed up well in trying conditions. Everybody hard at work trying to improve conditions. Enemy evidently in a worse plight, small parties being seen during the day, leaving the front, in full equipment, going overland to his supports. Our snipers claimed 11 hits. Enemy front patrolled at night, but found no enemy patrols.

### 31/12/16 — Front Line

> Weather misty and cold. Wind S.W. Situation normal. The hard work put in by our men during the past few days is making a little impression on the mud and water, but conditions are still very bad. Enemy put over a large number of Trench Mortars during the day, evidently in retaliation for the good work of our snipers yesterday, but inflicts no casualties. The old Year passed out uneventfully on our front.

Signed at the foot: "D. C. Draper [uncertain], Lt Colonel — O.C. 5th C.M.R. Bn."

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 December 1916 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text — 22 Pages", matching the 22 diary-text sheets (pages 3–24).
- **Front line:** The battalion began December in Brigade Support at Ecurie, relieved the 1st C.M.R. Bn in the line (R.2) on 5 December, was relieved by the 1st C.M.R. Bn on 11 December, returned to Etrun, relieved the 1st C.M.R. Bn again on 17 December, and was relieved by the 1st C.M.R. Bn on 23 December, going into Brigade Reserve; it relieved the 1st C.M.R. Bn again on 29 December and ended the year in the front line.
- **The 20 December raid:** The month's main action is the 1st C.M.R. Bn raid of 20 December (pages 17–20), which the 5th C.M.R. supported with artillery and held the line for. The 1st C.M.R. followed a heavy barrage (Lille Road to just north of the Snout, Zero 3.15 p.m.), occupied the enemy front line without being delayed by wire, and brought in about 50 unwounded prisoners and several wounded; the enemy offered very little resistance. 1st C.M.R. casualties were 6 killed and 18 other ranks wounded; the 5th C.M.R.'s own casualties while holding on were all wounded — Lieuts K. L. Duggan and W. F. Bynum, and Ptes. Bennett, McPhail, Davidson, Martin, Barnes and Jones.
- **Casualties:** 2 December: Corr. O. E. Lockhart (attached 8th Trench Mortar Battery) killed by an enemy trench mortar. 4 December: Pte. W. R. Dearden (B Coy) severely wounded (shell); he died of wounds at No. 42 Casualty Clearing Station on 6 December. 7 December: Cpl. F. P. Robinson (C Coy, fractured leg and contused eye), Pte. A. W. Tamblin (A Coy, chest and abdomen wounds — died 8 December, buried at Aubigny), Pte. G. F. Strong (B Coy, leg and eye wounds from a rifle grenade), Pte. L. Wilcock (attached 6th Canadian Trench Mortar Battery, accidentally wounded). 9 December: Pte. W. R. Ross (B Coy, slight shell wound in the neck). 11 December: Sgt. G. McNamara (C Coy) killed instantly by shrapnel at F.17 while handing over trench stores to the relieving 1st C.M.R. Bn; Ptes. Tanner, Cole and Riley (all C Coy) wounded by the same shell. The diary notes McNamara had been recommended for the V.C. for bravery at the Bluff, for which he received the D.C.M. 18 December: Sgt. C. Rafuse (seriously, fractured jaw and right leg) and Pte. C. Potter (right arm and forehead, not serious). 19 December: Pte. A. R. Neil (D Coy, battalion bomber) killed by high explosive in a dug-out; Cpl. H. R. Dayton and Pte. W. Woodrow (both battalion bombers) wounded in the shoulder; Sgt. Rafuse died of wounds (No. 42 Casualty Clearing Station, buried Aubigny). Pte. T. White (machine gun) wounded in the shoulder, not serious. 11 December: Pte. T. E. Kimball (C Coy) slight wound to the right eye.

- **Strength:** 75 other-rank reinforcements on 24 December (22 each to A, B and C Companies, 9 to D Company); 98 reinforcements on 27 December. The 29 December disposition table totals 25 officers and 659 other ranks [uncertain].
- **Notable incidents:** 8 December: cheering heard in the enemy lines, guessed to be news of the capture of Boucharet. 11 December: four German aeroplanes crossed the lines and two were brought down near Etrun; one pilot set his machine on fire and threw a bomb at a mounted policeman, breaking the horse's leg; the observer was very seriously wounded and the pilot shot in the leg by the military police. 17 December: the enemy fired 170 rounds of 77 mm and 200 heavy and light trench-mortar rounds, doing considerable damage to saps and trenches. 19–20 December: heavy artillery preparation for the 1st C.M.R. raid, with wire-cutting and a slow bombardment of selected points. 25 December (Christmas Day): "Boys made the best of the circumstances and had a good time generally." 30–31 December: continuous rain made the trenches almost uninhabitable, with dug-outs caving in and trenches filling with mud and water.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The 29 December entry on page 22 records both the receipt of operation orders to relieve the 1st C.M.R. (2) The 29 December entry on page 24 says the 4th C.M.R. Battalion is on one flank and the 6th Battalion (9th Brigade) on the right, but the first flank is not clearly legible. Rafuse is reported as died of wounds on 19 December, but the regimental number on the 19 December entry is not clearly legible, so it is not certain from the diary alone that it is the same man.
