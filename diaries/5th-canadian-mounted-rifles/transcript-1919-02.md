## Page 4 — War Diary, Sheet No. 1 (Army Form C. 2118)

Heading: WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY ("INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through).

### Feb. 1 — WATTERLOS

> BATTALION carried on with usual training.

### Feb. 2 — WATTERLOS

> SUNDAY:— A voluntary service was held in the hall near B.COY. H.Qs. at 1100 hrs. Capt. COLWELL M.C. officiating. MASS FOR ROMAN CATHOLICS was celebrated in the PARISH CHURCH, WATTRELOS at 1000 hrs. In the afternoon, officers and 300 other ranks proceeded to DOTTIGNIES to attend a performance given by the DIVL. [uncertain].

### Feb. 3 — WATTERLOS

> The weather still remains very cold. The BATTALION carried on with usual training. MUSTER PARADES were held in the afternoon.

### Feb. 4 — WATTERLOS

> Snow fell heavily during the night. The country in the morning reminded one of CANADA. Educational classes were proceeded with in all subjects.

### Feb. 5 — WATTERLOS

> Preparation for move to ENGLAND. ALL STORES were turned in.

### Feb. 6 — WATTERLOS

> Orders received that the BATTALION would proceed by march route to WILLEMS to-morrow the 7th.

### Feb. 7 — WILLEMS

> BATTALION fell in column of route on the main ROUBAIX Road at 0930 hrs. and proceeded by march route to WILLEMS, at 1200 hrs. when within five kilometres of WILLEMS, information was received that there was no train for the 1st C.M.R. Bn. whose billets we were to occupy. The BATTALION was halted and the men were given hot tea. At 1500 hrs Orders received that 12 officers and 310 other ranks would proceed on 8 days' leave to ENGLAND via CALAIS and on completion of leave, to report at BRAMSHOTT. At 1530 hrs the above party proceeded under command of Major J.S.E. TODD M.C. Later instructions were received that the BATTALION less officers and men proceeding on leave, would proceed to HEM. The BATTALION arrived in HEM at 1700 hrs. Great difficulty was experienced in securing billets, and the BATTALION was not completely housed until 1930 hrs.

## Page 5 — War Diary, Sheet No. 2 (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### Feb. 8 — WILLEMS

> The BATTALION vacated billets at HEM at 1000 hrs., and proceeded by march route to WILLEMS. Marching, again was very difficult, owing to the slippery nature of the ground. WILLEMS was reached at 1230 hrs. Instruction received the BATTALION would entrain at 1100 hrs. to-morrow for LEHAVRE, also that 9 officers and 316 other ranks would proceed on 8 days' leave at 1610 hrs. to-morrow. Preparations were made for entraining.

### Feb. 9 — WILLEMS

> The BATTALION comprising of 12 officers and 119 other ranks vacated billets in WILLEMS at 0830 hrs. and marched to BAISIEUX Station. The officers and other ranks proceeding on leave remaining at WILLEMS. Upon arrival at BAISIEUX, the remainder of the transport was turned in. Information was received that no train would be available until the afternoon. eventually at 1820 hrs. a train arrived and elements of the 2nd, 4th. and 5th C.M.R. BATTALIONS together with 8th C.I. Bde. Hqrs. quickly entrained.

### Feb. 10 — (on train)

> At 0430 hrs. the train stopped near HAZEBROUCKE in order that all ranks could procure a hot meal which was very acceptable. At 0930 hrs. at which hour we were instructed the train was due to leave the siding, it was discovered that there was no engine. Upon enquiries being made we were informed that the engine had taken the engine home. The reason for so doing was not quite clear. The quartermaster then issued rations and as the box cars were very comfortable, there being plenty of straw and a good stove in each car, no one minded the enforced wait. At 1300 hrs. a new engine arrived. Then it was discovered that the French conductor was missing, eventually he was found, and we continued our journey at 1415 hrs. The remainder of the journey was uneventful except another long wait at St. POL for five hours.

### Feb. 11 — LEHAVRE

> LEHAVRE was reached at 2000 hrs., here we were met by guides and conducted to the CANADIAN EMBARKATION CAMP, where a hot meal was provided for officers and men.

### Feb. 12 — LEHAVRE

> Day was devoted to medical inspection and baths, and nominal rolls were prepared for embarkation.

### Feb. 13 — LEHAVRE

> The morning and afternoon were devoted to preparation for embarkation. At 1930 hrs. the BATTALION comprising of 14 officers 127 other ranks fell in on No. 2 Parade Ground, and at 2000 hrs. proceeded to embarkation Jetty and embarked at 2200 hrs. At 2300 hrs we left LEHAVRE and FRANCE.

## Page 6 — War Diary, Sheet No. 3 (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### Feb. 13 — LEHAVRE (continued)

> The BATTALION arrived in FRANCE on the 24th of October 1915, and therefore served in the field THREE YEARS, THREE MONTHS AND THIRTEEN DAYS.

### Feb. 14 — ENGLAND

> The sea was smooth, and we arrived at WEYMOUTH without mishap at 0830 hrs., anchoring in the Harbour until 0900 hrs., when we moved alongside the quay. Immediately upon disembarking, hot tea and cakes was supplied all ranks and at 1000 hrs. we entrained at WEYMOUTH. A lunch was provided for both officers and men. LIPHOOK was reached at 1500 hrs., here tea and biscuits were provided after which the BATTALION marched to BRAMSHOTT CAMP. Arrangements at this Camp were most satisfactory and in twenty five minutes after arrival the men were given a good supper and had been allotted their bunks.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–28 February 1919 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text — 7 Pages"; the diary text occupies pages 4–10 (seven pages, numbered Sheet No. 1 to Sheet No. 5 plus two unnumbered continuation sheets). The contents sheet's year is typed "191." (the final digit is missing in the original).
- **Movements:** Wattrelos to Willems by march route on 7 February (diverted to Hem overnight when no train was available for the 1st C.M.R. Bn.); Hem to Willems 8 February; Willems to Baisieux Station by march 9 February, then by train (with a missing engine, a missing French conductor, and a five-hour wait at St. Pol) to Le Havre, arriving 11 February; embarked 13 February, sailed from Le Havre at 2300 hrs; arrived Weymouth 14 February, entrained to Liphook, marched to Bramshott Camp. The battalion remained at Bramshott for the rest of February on demobilization documentation.
- **Service summary:** The battalion arrived in France on 24 October 1915 and therefore served in the field three years, three months and thirteen days (entry of 13 February).
- **Leave parties:** 12 officers and 310 other ranks proceeded on 8 days' leave to England via Calais on 7 February under Maj. J.S.E. Todd, MC, to report at Bramshott on completion; a further 9 officers and 316 other ranks were to proceed on leave on 9 February. Only 12 officers and 119 other ranks (plus the leave parties) remained to entrain for Le Havre on 9 February; 14 officers and 127 other ranks embarked at Le Havre on 13 February.
- **Influenza:** Influenza was very prevalent at Bramshott Camp; 123 cases had occurred in the battalion by 18 February. 4040224 Pte. Wibaut A. died of influenza on 19 February in No. 12 Canadian General Hospital — the only death recorded in February 1919.
- **Captured howitzer:** On 24 February the battalion was notified that the 4.5-inch Howitzer No. 8416 captured by the battalion had to be destroyed, as it was impossible to remove the live round in the bore.
- **Demobilization documentation:** Daily tallies of medical boards, dental boards, D.O.S.2 forms, last leave, paid and L.P.Cs are recorded throughout the month. The D.O.S.2 other-ranks figures from 20 February onward appear to have been corrected in the original (the printed figure is overwritten, apparently to 393); the corrected figure is noted where it appears. By 28 February, 96 documents were still delayed (A.F.B. 103 — 44; A.F. B1 — 51; medals — 47; total effected 76; awaiting specialists' report — 18).
- **Investiture:** On 22 February information was received that a special investiture of all officers not yet invested, together with any other rank awarded the V.C., would be held on 26 February; Lt.-Col. Rhoades left for London on 25 February to attend the Division investiture at Buckingham Palace on 26 February.
- **Sailing:** No more personnel would be accepted for dispatch by the boat sailing 8 March after noon on 27 February.
- **Officers named:** Lt.-Col. W. Rhoades, DSO., MC (O.C.); Maj. J.S.E. Todd, MC; Capt. Colwell, MC; Capt. W.J. Cooks, MC; Capt. H.F.C. Cooks, MC; Capt. J.A. Lalanne, MC; Capt. H.S. McConnell, MC; Lt. D.C. Dixon, MC; Lt. J.W. Lewis, MC, DCM; Maj. F. [uncertain]; Pte. Wibaut A. (4040224, died of influenza 19 February).
- **Casualties:** One death from disease — 4040224 Pte. Wibaut A., influenza, died 19 February 1919 in No. 12 Canadian General Hospital. No combat casualties (post-Armistice period).
- **Anomalies:** (1) The diary uses both "WATTERLOS" (7 Feb. heading and Feb. 1–6 place column) and "WATTRELOS" (elsewhere) for the same place; both spellings are as written. (2) The 20 and 21 February medical-board figures fall (200, then 182) although the running totals otherwise rise — the figures are as written. (3) The 25 February entry is partly overwritten and its wording is uncertain in places; the names of the captains proceeding on leave are given as read. (4) Appendix "A" (Operation Order No. 192) is heavily corrected in pencil, with struck-through words; the transcription follows the visible text and does not reproduce struck-through words. (5) The 10 February entry says the engine "had taken the engine home" — as written in the original. (6) The contents sheet lists Appendix B as "C.O. 193 dated 8.2.19, 5th C.M.R. Bn. will proceed to WILLEMS this date" — as written.
