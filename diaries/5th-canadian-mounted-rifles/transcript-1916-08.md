### 1/8/16 — Infantry Barracks, Ypres

> Weather bright and warm. Day spent preparing for relief at night. Working parties returned at 4:00 A.M. Three casualties:- 444408. Pte. V. McKnight. 'B' Coy. G.S.W. in foot, near ZILLEBEKE LAKE at 3:30 A.M. 171632. Pte. P. Hughes. 'C' Coy. G.S.W. in left shoulder, left of trench 74, at 2:30 P.M. 414373. H/Cpl. R. Gould. 'A' Coy. G.S.W. in right hand, at VALLEY COTTAGES at 2:30 A.M.

> Battalion relieved by Royal Canadian Regiment. Relief commenced at 10:15 P.M. and last Company arrived at Camp ERIE at 1:30 A.M. 2nd August. No casualties.
>

## Page 4 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 1/8/16 — continued (Honours and Awards citations)

> The deeds for which the Honors and Awards were granted are detailed:- Lt.-Colonel Jenkins Colburn Draper [uncertain]:- For conspicuous gallantry in face of the enemy. He led reinforcements to exposed points and twice drove off determined hostile counter-attacks. Though himself wounded he carried his mortally wounded Commanding Officer from the firing line.
>
> Captain (Major) William Rhoades:- for his cool and unremitting collection of important information regarding the movement of the enemy, during action and his skill in having same constantly reported to his Brigade Headquarters, and for his conspicuous gallantry, in, although wounded himself, assisting in carrying the body of his Commanding Officer, who was mortally wounded, a considerable distance under heavy fire, in an endeavor to save the latter's life.

## Page 5 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 1/8/16 — continued (Honours and Awards citations)

> Captain Charles Wesley Hewson:- For the stubborn and courageous manner in which he maintained his position for 38 hours in Maple Copse under an intense bombardment, and for the cool and brave example he set his men during that time, and for his promptness and gallantry, in repelling attacks of the enemy launched against him and maintaining his position till relieved.
>
> Lieutenant George Roland Barnes:- For two reconnaissances made under very heavy fire to gain information of the disposition of the enemy's troops after their advance, and for the prompt action with his men in repelling enemy attack, and for his unflagging energy and cool example to his men during 38 hours continuous action.

## Page 6 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 1/8/16 — continued (Honours and Awards citations)

> No. 110053. Pte. J. E. Pringley:- For distinguished conduct in that he gathered together part of his Company ('A') when all his Officers had been either killed or wounded and brought them up under very heavy fire to the rest of the Company in the front line. As Company runner later on in the afternoon of 2nd June 1916, he got two messages through from Brigade Headquarters to his Battalion Commander after two other runners had failed in the attempt.
>
> No. 111195. C.S.M. George Gill:- On the afternoon of 2nd June he took up a position in a Strong Point with twenty men, and continued to hold it against repeated attacks by the enemy, and a very heavy bombardment for 33 hours, even when only two unwounded men remained, he stubbornly maintained his position until ordered to retire.
>
> No. 110254. C.S.M. Frank Hughes:- For bravery and presence of mind during the engagement. He steadied his men and with great coolness led them to their new positions under very heavy fire. He inspired the men by his splendid example

### 1/8/16 — continued (Honours and Awards citations)

> and removed all his wounded. After he was wounded he remained for 12 hours directing and controlling his men.
>
> No. 110563. Corporal Howard. B. Walton:- For conspicuous bravery and coolness throughout the engagement until wounded, and particularly for the daring manner in which he volunteered for and carried out a reconnaissance of Strong Point No. 14. with a view to finding if the same was in the hands of the enemy or not. He obtained the information required by a display of remarkable self possession under fire secured from the dead bodies of a German Officer and man, articles of great value to our Intelligence Department. His conduct throughout was marked with ability and courage.
>
> No. 110487. Sgt. Thomas East Roe:- For exceptional bravery and courage while superintending the operations at Strong Point 14. For three days he displayed great devotion to duty and when finally wounded refused to be attended to, and carried on with his work until again wounded in the head.

### 1/8/16 — continued (Honours and Awards citations)

> No. 112258. Cpl. Roy Douglas Ness:- For his coolness and bravery under heavy shell and machine gun fire for 38 hours during which time he rendered great assistance to stretcher bearers, and brought up ammunition. Although wounded he declined to leave the line, and remained with his company until it was relieved.
>
> No. 110506. Cpl. Stanley Semple:- For conspicuous bravery and devotion to duty for 3 days in Strong Point 14. When wounded through the knee he carried on his duties with great courage, and when wounded again in the wrist continued firing for some time.
>
> No. 111433. Pte. Stanton B. Reddin:- For remarkable bravery and devotion to duty on 2nd June, during very heavy artillery and machine gun fire, he volunteered to carry out his wounded comrades. He made several trips across the open to dress wounded men, and he carried one man three hundred yards to safety.

### 2/8/16 — Camp Erie

> Weather bright and warm. Day spent in resting and cleaning up until 5 p.m. when Battalion paraded and proceeded independently at 15 minute intervals to Billets near GODEWAERSVELDE. Last Company reported in at 8:50 p.m.

### 3/8/16

> Weather bright and warm. Day spent resting and cleaning up until 5 p.m. when Battalion paraded and proceeded independently at 15 minute intervals to old Billets near STEENVOORDE. Last Company reported in at 7:30 p.m.

### 4/8/16

> Weather bright and warm. Baths at STEENVOORDE allotted to Battalion for the day. Companies carried on independently with drill and Kit inspection.

### 5/8/16

> Weather bright and warm. Battalion paraded at 4:15 A.M. in full marching order with packs for a Route March which included the whole of the Brigade with Transport, Lewis Guns, and Stokes Guns. The distance covered was eight miles. Very satisfactory. March discipline of whole Brigade being excellent. Early Parade necessary to avoid the heat of the day. For the past eight days the mid-day temperature has been very high.

### 6/8/16 — Sunday

> Bright and warm. Battalion Church Parade in open, Rev: Hepburn officiating. Holy Communion celebrated after service. Lt.-Colonel J. C. Taylor 4th Bn. reports for 3 weeks instruction.

> Weather bright and warm. Battalion paraded on Battalion Alarm Post and carried on by Companies with practice of the assault in all its phases, gas helmet inspection and drill, musketry (application), bayonet fighting, instruction in bombing, sniping and wiring.

### 8/8/16

### 9/8/16 — Camp Montreal

> Weather bright and warm. Battalion paraded on Battalion Alarm Post at 4:00 a.m. and left STEENVOORDE proceeding by Route March to GODEWAERSVELDE arriving there at 9:00 A.M. ### 12/8/16 — continued

> Last night, the Engineers, protected by a screen of our Bombers and scouts exploded a small surface mine in front of and to the north of OLD CRATER, where it has been suspected that enemy have been running galleries. Engineer Officer satisfied that considerable damage was done as large baulks of timber were thrown up. Later the Engineer Officer went into damaged gallery and brought out a number of German mining tools and [uncertain] Apparatus.
>
> A raid carried out the night before last, on our right, by the 28th Battalion proved successful. The party accounted for several Germans, and brought back one prisoner (24th Pte. Eng. Div).
>

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 August 1916 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 22 Pages", which matches the 22 diary-text pages (pages 3–24). The first six diary pages (pages 3–8) are taken up entirely by the publication of the June 1916 honours and awards list and the citations for the deeds of 2 June 1916; daily entries resume on page 9 (2 August).
- **Officers named:** Lt.-Col. J. C. Draper (O.C., 5th C.M.R. Battalion; signs the diary on page 24; awarded the D.S.O.); Major W. Rhoades (Adjutant, promoted Major to date from 2 June 1916; awarded the Military Cross; signs Appendix II); Captain C. W. Hewson (O.C. 'A' Coy; Military Cross); Lieutenant G. R. Barnes (2nd i/c 'D' Coy; Military Cross); Lieutenant Stewart Basil Simpson (wounded 13 August); Captain P. H. Rooke (2nd i/c; lectured on "Discipline and tactics" 28 August); Lieutenant Arnold Vivian Miller (reported from the Base at Havre 29 August, posted to 'A' Company); Lt.-Col. J. D. Taylor (44th Canadian Infantry Battalion, attached for a three-week tour of instruction, departed for England 31 August); Captain Rev. Hepburn (chaplain, officiated at most burials); Father LaRoque (Roman Catholic chaplain); Captain Rev. Fallis (Church of England). Deceased officers reburied 28 August: Major R. C. Pilcher, Lieut. R. M. Macdonald, the late Lt.-Colonel J. H. Baker and Captain H. H. Pines.
- **Other ranks named:** The honours list and citations name C.S.M. George Gill, C.S.M. Frank Hughes, Corporal Howard B. Walton, Pte. J. E. Pringley, Sgt. Thomas East Roe, Cpl. Roy Douglas Ness, Cpl. Stanley Semple and Pte. Stanton B. Reddin (all for deeds of 2 June 1916). L/Cpl. J. Macnamara ('C' Coy) was recommended for the Victoria Cross for rescuing Sgt. Smith on 11 August and was awarded the Distinguished Conduct Medal (announced 30 August). Nearly every casualty entry names the man, with regimental number, company and wound.
- **Honours and awards (published 1 August, for 2 June 1916):** D.S.O. — Lt.-Col. J. C. Draper. Military Cross — Captain (Major) W. Rhoades, Captain C. W. Hewson, Lieutenant G. R. Barnes. D.C.M. — C.S.M. G. Gill, C.S.M. F. Hughes, Corporal H. B. Walton, Corporal J. E. Pringley. Military Medal — Sgt. T. E. Roe, Cpl. R. D. Ness, Cpl. S. Semple, Pte. S. B. Reddin. The citations describe the fighting at Maple Copse and Strong Point 14 on 2 June 1916.
- **Movements:** 1 August at Infantry Barracks, Ypres; relieved by the Royal Canadian Regiment that night and marched to Camp Erie. 2 August to billets near Godewaersvelde; 3 August to old billets near Steenvoorde. 9 August route march to Godewaersvelde, entrainment to Poperinghe, and march to Camp Montreal. 10–11 August relieved the 5th Canadian Infantry Battalion at the Bluff Tunnels and took over the Bluff and craters (trenches 29–32). 16–17 August relieved by the 4th C.M.R. Battalion and moved into Brigade Reserve (Battalion HQ and 'D' Coy at Swan Chateau). 22–23 August relieved in Brigade Reserve by the 2nd Battalion, Duke of Wellington's Own (West Ridings) and moved to Camp 'E'. 25 August route march through Poperinghe and Abeele to billets in the North Steenvoorde area, where the battalion remained in training to the end of the month.
- **Casualties:** 16 men died during the month as recorded in the August entries — killed: L/Cpl. W. R. Coles (11 Aug), Pte. H. K. Paterson (11 Aug), Pte. J. A. Keenan (12 Aug, accidentally killed by Pte. Henderson), A/Sgt. W. R. Pearson (13 Aug), Pte. J. B. Swindells (14 Aug), Pte. H. R. Cooper (15 Aug), Pte. R. Lindsay (18 Aug), L/Cpl. J. Witham (17 Aug); died of wounds: Sgt. W. R. Smith (11 Aug), Pte. E. J. Viles (15 Aug), Pte. J. Ackers (14 Aug, died 12 midnight), Pte. W. Jacobs (16 Aug, died at a Canadian Field Ambulance), Pte. G. Summerfield (17 Aug), L/Cpl. C. B. Dickson (18 Aug), Pte. R. B. Waller (20 Aug), Pte. E. Arsenault (20 Aug). About 45 other ranks and one officer (Lieut. Stewart Basil Simpson) were wounded, most by shrapnel or trench mortar in the New Crater and trenches 29–32a. Two accidental shootings are recorded: Pte. Keenan killed by Pte. Henderson (12 Aug) and Pte. Wheeler accidentally shot by Pte. Brown in the H.Q. corridor, Bluff Tunnels (18 Aug). Pte. Gervais is recorded with a self-inflicted wound (12 Aug). The dead were buried in the cemetery at Bedford House, usually by Captain Rev. Hepburn.
- **Operations:** The battalion held the Bluff and craters (trenches 29–32) 11–16 August under frequent shelling, trench-mortar fire and sniping; engineers exploded a surface mine north of the Old Crater on the night of 11–12 August and recovered German mining tools. The 28th Battalion raided successfully on the battalion's right on the night of 10–11 August, and the 29th Battalion raided on 14 August. The month ends with the battalion training for a new attack formation, and the appendices (Operation Orders E.1 and E.2, both 31 August) lay out an attack on enemy trenches X–Y (zero 2:30 p.m.) and a counter-attack (zero 3:30 p.m.) after the enemy re-occupied trenches X–Y and A–A.
