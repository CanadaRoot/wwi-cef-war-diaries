## What the diary is

The diary of a mounted-rifles battalion that fought as infantry for the whole war: training in England (Shorncliffe) and the move to France in October 1915; Mount Sorrel (June 1916, where Lt-Col. G. H. Baker was killed); the Somme — Flers-Courcelette and the Regina Trench attack (September–October 1916); Vimy Ridge (9 April 1917); Arleux/Fresnoy (May 1917); Hill 70 and Lens (August 1917); Passchendaele (30 October 1917); the March 1918 raid and the German Spring Offensive; Amiens (August 1918); the Canal du Nord and Cambrai (September–October 1918); the advance to Mons and the Armistice (November 1918); and demobilization (January–March 1919, ending with the homecoming in Sherbrooke and Montreal). O.C.s: Lt-Col. G. H. Baker (to 2 June 1916, killed at Mount Sorrel); Lt-Col. D. C. Draper D.S.O. (July 1916 – May 1918, with spells away commanding brigades); Maj./Lt-Col. W. Rhoades M.C., D.S.O. (from 24 May 1918). The diary mixes handwritten Army Form C.2118 sheets with typed sheets and long typed appendices (operation orders, raid narratives, barrage diagrams, distribution states, nominal rolls, honours lists).

## Casualties (battalion only, as recorded — key totals)

- **1915:** none recorded (training in England; France from 24 October).
- **1916:** 2–3 June (Mount Sorrel): 57 O.R. killed, 271 wounded, 50 missing; 4 officers killed, 7 wounded, 1 missing; only 325 of nearly 650 rank and file returned. 15 Sep (Somme): 11 officers and 268 O.R. casualties (the diary's own table does not add up — transcribed as written). 1 Oct (Regina Trench): 3 officers + 45 O.R. killed, 1 officer + 1 O.R. died of wounds, 6 officers + 166 O.R. wounded.
- **1917:** 9 Apr (Vimy): 17 O.R. killed, 3 died of wounds, 2 officers and 58 O.R. wounded, 5 missing, 6 shell-shocked. 30 Oct (Passchendaele): 7 officers killed, 5 wounded & missing, 10 wounded; 391 O.R. killed/wounded/missing, 14 wounded at duty (total stated 395 — arithmetic does not reconcile).
- **1918:** 15 Mar raid: Maj. T. D. Sneath + 4 O.R.s killed, 22 O.R.s wounded. 8–9 Aug (Amiens): battalion as Van Guard captured Folies, Bouchoir, cleared Arvillers, ~4,500 yards advanced, 100+ prisoners. 26 Aug (Monchy-le-Preux): 14 killed, ~15½ O.R. wounded, ~300 prisoners; Capt. W. J. Atherton killed by a short-falling British shell. 2–9 Nov (advance to Mons): 5 officers + 83 O.R. casualties.
- **1919:** one death from disease — Pte. Wibaut A. (influenza, 19 February 1919). No combat casualties.

## Honours (as recorded in the diary)

- Victoria Cross: C.S.M. John Parker Kirkpatrick (15 September 1916, killed bringing in wounded of the 4th C.M.R.); Capt. (A/Maj) George R. Pearkes (Passchendaele, 30/31 October 1917); Lt. Charles S. Rutherford (Monchy-le-Preux, 26 August 1918 — bluffed 45 Germans into surrendering at the Crucifix pillbox); Lt-Col. W. Rhoades (25 November 1918 announcement, for 26 August 1918).
- Also: D.S.O.s, Military Crosses, Distinguished Conduct Medals and Military Medals throughout, with full citations copied into the diary for the Somme, Vimy, Passchendaele and 1918 actions (see the monthly transcripts' Notes sections).
