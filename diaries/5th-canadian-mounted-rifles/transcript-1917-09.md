> Battalion paraded at 8.00 A.M. and proceeded by route march to NEUVILLE ST VAAST arriving in billets there at 5.15 P.M. ### 7/9/17 — Supports

> Weather mainly fair. Indifferent visibility. Enemy doing considerable shelling around VIMY and the CULVERT. From 8.00 A.M. to 11.00 P.M. a number of gas shells were put around "PETIT VIMY" and the "BRICKFIELDS" at (S.24.c.5.8)

### 8/9/17 — Supports

> Weather fair. In the early morning there was a very heavy mist and also in the evening, this made poor visibility during the day. Enemy practised shelling around "VIMY," "PETIT VIMY" and the "CULVERT" during the day, and a few gas shells at night around the BRICKFIELDS.

### 9/9/17 — Supports

> Weather fine, wind westerly. Heavy mist in the early morning. During the day the CULVERT received the usual enemy shelling. From 4.00 P.M. to 9.00 P.M. "VIMY" was heavily shelled with 5.9" and 8" shells mixed with gas shells.

### 12/9/17 — Front Line and Close Supports

> Weather fine, visibility good. Enemy artillery very quiet. Our gun continued to register on PEGGY TRENCH but no direct hits were made. About 4.45 P.M. an enemy dump in rear of MERICOURT was blown up causing a heavy white smoke to spread and it looks as though it may be an enemy gas shell dump.

### 13/9/17 — Front Line and Close Supports

> Weather fine and visibility fair. Enemy artillery more active than usual, guns were registered on TEDDY GERRARD and TOLEDO trenches. One direct hit made on TEDDY GERRARD. At intervals during the night a line of shrapnel was placed about 200 yards in front of TOLEDO trench. O.P. reports that the enemy was observing our lines through field glasses from T.12.a.9.4 from T.11.a.5.4

### 14/9/17 — Front Line and Close Supports

> Weather cloudy and showery. Enemy's shelling normal. At 12.45 A.M. gas was projected by the Division on our right (1st Division) just north of ACHEVILLE.

> Weather fine, visibility good. At intervals during the day the enemy shelled MT FORET QUARRIES, MERICOURT ROAD and VIMY. At 2.00 P.M. an enemy plane flying very low patrolled our lines for about an hour and was engaged by our Lewis Guns and Machine Guns without result. ### 15/9/17 — Front Line and Close Supports (continued)

> Weather fine, visibility good. Enemy's artillery very active on our flanks. At 2.00 A.M. a very heavy barrage was put down on our immediate left which lasted about 15 minutes. At 4.30 A.M. there was an intense artillery bombardment by both sides North of Lens this lasted for an hour. At about 9.30 P.M. a small enemy patrol was observed outside our wire, we immediately sent out a patrol to try and capture them but they could find no trace of them. Another enemy dump in rear of MERICOURT was exploded by our artillery at about 6.00 A.M.

### 16/9/17 — Front Line and Close Supports

> Weather fine visibility good. Enemy artillery shelled MT FORET QUARRIES with 5.9 and 8" shells during the afternoon. Division are expecting ENEMY to raid our trenches somewhere near the QUARRIE ROAD, everybody on the alert and keeping a sharp lookout. Battalion relieved by three companies of the 42nd Battalion. Relief complete at 10.40 P.M. Battalion marched into billets at NEUVILLE ST VAAST for the night. SEE Appendix ["J" struck through] I

### 17/9/17 — Neuville St Vaast

> Battalion arrived in billets at NEUVILLE ST VAAST at 2.00 A.M. Casualties for the tour were: Killed 3 other ranks, Died of Wounds 2 other ranks, Slightly wounded 5 other ranks, Gas-poisoning (slight) 13 other ranks. The raid expected by Division was made on the 16th Battalion, about 300 yards to the right of the QUARRIE ROAD. At 3.00 A.M. an enemy raiding party of one officer and 73 other ranks with two Heavy Machine Guns came up to within 300 yards of our wire under cover of a protective barrage. They then placed their Machine Guns, leaving 14 men with each gun. The remainder of the party then entered the trenches of the 16th Battalion, inflicting a few casualties on the 16th Bn. and made off with one wounded man. On its way back the party had to pass through our barrage which, no doubt [a word is struck through here] caused considerable casualties among them. One of the enemy party on its way back to its own trenches got lost and wandered towards the lines of the 16th Bn. and was taken prisoner.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–30 September 1917 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 23 Pages", but the diary text as filed runs to 13 numbered pages (diary pages (1)–(13), file pages 4–16); the remaining pages are the appendices and the renumbering letter.
- **Officers named:** Lt-Col D. C. Draper (O.C., signs the diary on 30 September); Lt J. A. Adamson (Adjutant; signs the operation orders and the 14 October renumbering letter); Capt Davis, 1st C.M.R. Bn. (officiated at church parade 1 and 30 September); Capt Stewart, 1st C.M.R. Bn. (officiated 23 September); Lieut R. Andrews (Battalion Musketry Officer); Lieut T. S. Hall (advance party, 4 September); Lieut Millar (billeting party, 17–18 September); Lieut W. Lake (Orderly Officer, 18 September); Pnr. Sgt. Brand (Bugle Band, 18 September); Brig-General Dyer, D.S.O. (presented the tug-of-war silver cup, 21 September).
- **Other ranks named:** No. 441130 Sgt Elvey, W. (awarded the Military Medal, 10 September); Pte A. B. Woodcock (Scouts; signed the forward-area diagram, page 27).
- **Equipment:** Lewis guns (a Lewis gun team hit in Canada Trench 6 September, one gun damaged); box respirators adjusted under gas shelling 5 September; gas shells used by both sides through the month; gas-helmets inspected 29 September; tanks demonstrated at Wailly 28 September; aeroplane lecture and flights 26–27 September; German machine guns demonstrated by the 8th Canadian Machine Gun Company 29 September.
- **Movements:** Bouvigny-Boyeffles to huts at Bois de Alleux (Ottawa Camp) 2 September; to billets at Neuville St Vaast 4 September; into the Support Area relieving the 2nd C.M.R. Battalion on the night of 5/6 September; into the Front Line relieving the 4th C.M.R. Battalion on the night of 11/12 September; line shortened on the night of 14/15 September; relieved by three companies of the 42nd Battalion on the night of 17/18 September; to Neuville St Vaast, then by route march to Maisnil Bouche 18 September, where the battalion remained in training to 30 September.
- **Casualties:** 6 September: a shell scored a direct hit on a Lewis gun team in Canada Trench — 2 killed, 2 badly wounded, the gun damaged. Tour totals reported 17 September: Killed 3 other ranks, Died of Wounds 2 other ranks, Slightly wounded 5 other ranks, Gas-poisoning (slight) 13 other ranks.
- **Actions:** Enemy raid on the 16th Battalion at 3.00 a.m. on 17 September — one officer and 73 other ranks with two heavy machine guns, about 300 yards to the right of the Quarrie Road; the raiders entered the 16th Battalion's trenches, took one wounded man prisoner, and one of the raiders was himself taken prisoner after getting lost on the way back. Enemy patrols approached the wire on 15 September and were driven off by Lewis guns; a low-flying enemy plane patrolled the lines on 15 September and was engaged without result. Gas was projected by the Division on the right (1st Division) just north of Acheville at 12.45 a.m. on 14 September. Vimy, Petit Vimy, the Culvert and the Brickfields were heavily shelled with 5.9" and 8" shells mixed with gas shells through 6–9 September; enemy dumps in rear of Mericourt were blown up on 12 and 15 September.
- **Discipline:** None recorded in September 1917.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The contents sheet states "Diary Text 23 Pages" against 13 numbered diary pages. (2) The appendix letters were corrected after filing: the Adjutant's letter of 14 October 1917 (page 17) says Appendix "J" should read "I" and Appendix "K" should read "J"; the diary's 16 September reference to Appendix "J" is struck through and corrected to "I", and the Appendix J heading on page 30 is written over a struck-through letter. (3) The remarks-column initials change from "Jas" (diary pages (1)–(9)) to "J.H." (diary pages (10)–(13)). (4) The 11 September entry has "CANADA TRENCH" interlined above "SUPPORTS". (5) The disposition reports (Appendices C, E and H, and parts of F and G) are faint handwritten or carbon copies; some map references and figures are illegible, and the column totals do not always reconcile exactly as read — e.g. in Appendix C the "C" Coy total reads 27 though its column figures sum to 127, and in Appendix H the officers column sums to 16 against a stated total of 17. (6) Spelling as written: "Maisnel-Bouche" in the diary Place column against "MAISNIL BOUCHE" in the orders; "Teddie Gerrard" in the orders and Appendix H against "TEDDY GERRARD" in the diary and Appendix F; "practised" and "practised" spellings as written; "VIMY" and "PETIT VIMY" in capitals in the diary text.
