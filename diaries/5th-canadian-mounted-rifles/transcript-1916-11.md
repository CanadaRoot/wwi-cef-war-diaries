## Page 3 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118)

Place column: "In the Line". Date: "Nov 1st".

### 1/11/16 — In the Line

> Early morning fine, later cloudy with showers. Night passed very quietly w/ Gun and rifle fire practically nil.
>
> Patrols
>
> (a) Patrol left Sap 30b at 8.00 P.m worked to left and then right of crater. Crater XXI was occupied by enemy. Two sentries seen some distance apart on duty on forward lip. Enemy occupy front line from A22. B.1.8 to A22 a.9.8–9.2 — they were sending up flares from here and not from their support line as previously reported. Wire very heavy between these points. Patrolling very difficult on account of shell holes, craters and trenches. Patrol returned via Sap 30b at 10.13 P.m.
>
> (b) 2nd Patrol left Sap 30b at 8 P.m. returned via Sap 30a at 10 P.m no enemy encountered, reports enemy wire to be heavy concertina & barbed string on wooden stakes

## Page 4 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

Place column: "In the Line". Date: "Nov 1st Cont.".

> (c) 3rd Patrol left Sap 32 at 1.00 am & returned 3.15 am. Worked NE to edge of crater 500. Found front very quiet. Enemy sending up flares from his front line opposite Sap 32. Patrol reports gap in our wire about 10 yards south of crater 500. Only one strand crosses this gap, which is 12' wide.
>
> (d) 4th Patrol left Sap 28 at 1.00 am and returned at 3.00 am. Patrolled lip of crater XII and along trench on South Side. Found old dug-outs, wired in front but trench was clear. Followed trench as far as A22 b 25.42. Enemy post at A22. B.23.47. Working parties busy clearing trenches which are constantly being blocked with sticks.
>
> 10 am. Enemy put over 6. H.2's in vicinity of Grand Courcelette and Sutherland 3 of which were duds.
>
> 4.25 P.m. Enemy sent over 2. shrapnel.
>
> 12.30 P.m 3 Enemy T.M's fell near Sutherland and Grand Courcelette crumping in the former Avenue. in two places (direct hits) at about 17 ft.

### 17/11/16 — In the Line

> Cold night with frost. Clear & cold this morning. Between 1.30 and 3.00 p.m. word came down from the Battalion on our left (60th Bn) that Enemy was bringing up Gas Cylinders. Companies "Stand to" nothing happened. Our STOKES active during night. Store of [uncertain] believed to have been hit in crater 21 as large flame and smoke was observed. Usual patrols. Nothing unusual reported no enemy seen or heard.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–30 November 1916 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text — 14 Pages", but the diary text in fact runs to 34 numbered sheets (pages 3–36), plus a distribution table (page 18) inserted between the 5 and 6 November entries.
- **Duplicates:** Pages 1–13 of this month were each downloaded twice as byte-identical files (0263/0264 through 0287/0288); each page is transcribed once. Page 14 (0289) onward are single files.
- **Front line:** The battalion spent the whole month in the line opposite the Regina Trench sector (craters 12, 19, 20, 21, 25, 500; Saps 28, 29, 30, 30a, 30b, 32, 33, 33a; Sutherland Avenue, Orr Avenue, Grand Courcelette, Lille Road), with two spells in Brigade Support at the Ecurie Defences (5–11 November and 29–30 November) and a rest at Etrun (18–22 November). It relieved the 1st C.M.R. Bn in the line on 11 November and again on 23 November, and was itself relieved by the 1st C.M.R. Bn on 5 November, 17 November and 29 November.
- **Honours:** Much of the month's diary (pages 5–6, 11–14, 25, 27–30) is taken up by the texts of honours awarded for the 14–15 September and 1–2 October 1916 actions, copied into the diary: Military Cross — Capt. H. Smith, Capt. T. D. Sneath, Lt. H. J. Daubney, Lt. G. R. Pearkes; Bar to Military Cross — Lt. G. R. Barnes; Military Medal — Pte. T. J. Nolan (#110431), Sgt. J. R. Harcus (#112333), Sgt. W. White (#110346), Sgt. J. Holmes (#112140), Pte. E. Schultz (#113089), Pte. A. E. Grant (#110202), Pte. R. Verhaighe (#114186), Pte. S. C. Lake (#603255); Distinguished Conduct Medal — Sgt. D. Dibblee (#110132), Cpl. J. D. Shearer (#110510). Pte. Lake's citation records him capturing three Germans with only a bomb on 1 October, one of them carrying an Iron Cross; Cpl. Shearer captured a German machine gun and turned it on its crew.
- **Casualties:** 4 November: one man of a 2nd C.M.R. working party killed and two wounded; Lt. H. E. Sellon (D Coy) wounded in the hand. 15 November: Major Barnes wounded (S.S.W. in left arm below elbow). 24 November: Pte. G. H. Maidment (D Coy) and Pte. S. O. Snow (B Coy) both with gunshot wounds in the foot — Maidment's noted as self-inflicted; one man of 4th Trench Mortar killed by enemy retaliation. 25 November: Pte. H. Isaac (D Coy) shrapnel wound to left leg, thigh and foot. No other casualties are recorded in November; most days are reported as "no casualties".

- **Notable incidents:** 2 November: repairing parties constantly unearthing skeletons of French and Germans, including four skeletons seated at a table in a dugout, apparently gas victims. 3 November: 100 Stokes rounds fired into Crater 21 after 25 Germans were seen filing in. 15–16 November: ammonal tubes placed in the enemy wire left of Crater 21 and blown at 2.30 a.m., cutting the wire completely. 17 November: warning from the 60th Battalion that the enemy was bringing up gas cylinders; companies stood to, nothing happened. 21 November: all ranks issued the new Small Box respirator at the Divisional Gas School, Ecoivres. 30 November: Corps Commander presented ribbons to decorated men of the 8th Canadian Infantry Brigade.
