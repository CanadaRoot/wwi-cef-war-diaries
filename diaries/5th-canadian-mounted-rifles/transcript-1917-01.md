### 1/1/17 — In the Line

> Weather dull and raining wind westerly. Situation normal. Men still hard at it, clearing up trenches and improving conditions. Our snipers again bagged about 3 Huns going overland, this time opposite 60th Bn's front (our left flank D). Situation normal at night on our front, and enemy appear nervous throwing up flares continuously. The Royal 22nd carried out a raid away to our right, and the enemy threw over several trench mortars into our lines but without casualties.

### 2/1/17 — In the Line

> Weather clear and bright, wind S.E. Our O.C. arranged with the 18 Pounders to conduct a shoot on the enemy front and support lines in the vicinity of the SNOUT, about 400 rounds being put over, apparently making a mess of enemy trenches. Two attached Majors — Major WILSON and Major DAVIS reported and attached to G.P.B. [uncertain] respectively.

"P.T.O." is written at the foot of the page.

### 3/1/17 — In the Line

> Weather dull, wind N.W. Patrols out at night, but nobody encountered. Enemy extremely nervous. Artillery gave the SNOUT another pounding. Capt TODD and LT EATON went into German lines and nearly succeeded in getting a prisoner, but were interrupted by snipers and had to return. Lt PEARKES in the evening took out a Lewis gun into an old German sap and fired on a enemy working party apparently clearing them up.

### 4/1/17 — In the Line

### 11/1/17 — In the Line

> Weather overcast clearing later wind S.W. Situation quiet. Enemy apparently nervous. He is not occupying his front line. Our artillery fired on a suspected O.P. at approximately [uncertain], no results observed. A patrol of [uncertain] men left Sap 29 at 8.30 pm explored Craters 19 & 19a returning by Crater 13 at 10.15 pm but no [uncertain] encountered. Officers patrol left Sap 30 at 11 p.m., worked along trench and investigated sap between Crater 29 [uncertain] and enemy's front line coming in at Sap 30 at 12.30 am. Nothing unusual noted.

### 12/1/17 — In the Line

> Weather overcast, wind N.W. Situation normal. Daylight patrol entered enemy front line right of Crater 19. They followed down line to [uncertain] Rd, then back. On return they entered enemy dug out, which was unoccupied. They found in it a shaft at the bottom going down several feet. Evidently an old mining shaft, but was disused. Patrols out at night but no enemy encountered.

## Page 8 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

Place column: "In the Line" throughout.

Remarks column: each entry is initialled "R.h."

### 13/1/17 — In the Line

> Weather foggy, wind S.W. Day normal with very little activity. At night, Officers patrols went out over No Man's Land but did not encounter any enemy patrols.

### 14/1/17 — In the Line

> Weather dull & cold, wind N.E. All preparations ready for a GAS raid to be carried out, and everybody on their toes anxious for a go at the Hun, but at the last minute arrangements had to be cancelled owing to an unfavourable wind, much to everybody's disgust.

### 15/1/17 — In the Line

> Weather dull but warmer, wind N.E. While the wind is in this direction the raid cannot be carried out, much to everybody's disappointment. A raid without GAS was contemplated, but decided against eventually as it was felt that a retaliatory bombardment might cause damage to the Gas Cylinders. Word received that Pte Main of "C" Coy who was wounded by shrapnel yesterday, died of wounds. Body conveyed to HOUZE [uncertain] and buried in the cemetery there.

"P.T.O." is written at the foot of the page.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 January 1917 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 9 Pages", which matches the nine diary-text pages (pages 3–11). Two training syllabuses follow as Appendix "AA" (pages 12–13).
- **Officers named:** Capt. Todd; Lt Eaton; Lt Pearkes; Major Wilson and Major Davis (attached majors, reported 2 January); Lts. Eaton and Thorne (left with the "storm troops" at Ariane dugouts, 21 January); Brigadier General Macdonell (visited Battalion HQ, 27 January); Sgt. Instructor Chapman (reported for duty 30 January). The diary is signed at 31 January by "R. H. [uncertain], Major, for O.C. 5th C.M.R. Battn."; the remarks column is initialled "R.h." throughout.
- **Other ranks named:** Pte Main, "C" Coy (wounded by shrapnel 14 January, died of wounds 15 January).
- **Casualties:** One death — Pte Main, "C" Coy, died of wounds 15 January (wounded by shrapnel the previous day). No other casualties are recorded in January; the reliefs of 4, 10 and 16 January were all completed without casualty. Snipers "bagged about 3 Huns" on 1 January.
- **Movements:** In the line (the Snout sector, Ecurie) 1–4 January; relieved 4 January and moved via Ecurie to Etrun the same night; in the line again 10–16 January; relieved 16 January to support at Anzin, Sunken Road and Ariane; relieved 21 January to Mont St Eloy; route marched 22 January via Acq and Savy to Villers-Brulin; moved 30 January via Mingoval to Estree-Cauchie.
- **Operations:** A 400-round 18-pounder shoot on the enemy front and support lines near the Snout (2 January); a patrol into the German lines by Capt. Todd and Lt Eaton (3 January) that nearly took a prisoner; a Lewis-gun shoot from an old German sap by Lt Pearkes (3 January); a company attack scheme practised in co-operation with aeroplanes on 8 January (the aeroplane did not arrive owing to stormy weather); a planned gas raid on 14 January cancelled at the last minute for an unfavourable wind, and a raid without gas then decided against for fear retaliatory bombardment would damage the gas cylinders (15 January); daylight and night patrols into the enemy lines around Craters 19 and 19a and Sap 29/30 (11–12 January), finding an old disused mining shaft in an enemy dug-out.
- **Training:** The month is largely a training record once out of the line — syllabuses for 4–10 January and 23 January–1 February cover squad and platoon drill, musketry, bayonet fighting, bombing, Lewis-gun, scouting and observing, and practice of the company in attack under the new platoon organisation. The diary entry for 24 January cites the syllabus as attached ("A"), while the appendix pages themselves are marked "AA".
- **Anomalies:** (1) The 10 January distribution table's row figures as read do not sum to the stated totals (officers 19, rifles 892 as read), and the remarks column gives "782 O.Rs" against the rifles total — individual figures in the table should be checked against the image. (2) The 8 January entry says the battalion "held New Year dinner" — as written. (3) The 21 January entry reads "relieving [uncertain] being complete by 3.30 p.m." (4) The second syllabus is headed "30-1-17 to 2-1-17", apparently a misprint for 2-2-17. (5) The 7.30 line of the second syllabus (24th–27th) is partly illegible. (6) The diary sheet on page 3 is headed "App AA" and its sheet number is not clearly legible.
