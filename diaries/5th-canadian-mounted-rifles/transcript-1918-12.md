### 1/12/18 — Mons

> A.M. Divine Service was held at Ecole d'Industriel. Major Burton of 1st C.M.R. giving the sermon.
>
> P.M. Recreation & Sports. Lieut Savage appointed Bn. Educational Officer

### 2/12/18 — Mons

> A.M. Battalion parade at 0930 on the "Champ de Manoeuvre", after companys carried on with training as per orders.
>
> P.M. Recreation and Sports. Headquarters Company were winners in a baseball match with "B" Company. Score:- 10-3.

### 3/12/18 — Mons

> A.M. Companys carried on independently according to syllabus. Lieut. Cocks, Adjutant of the battalion proceeded on leave. Lieut. Belding taking over his duties and Lieut. Ingersoll acting as assistant.
>
> P.M. Recreation and Sports.

### 4/12/18 — Mons

> The battalion has entered the new educational courses very strongly. At present the following courses are in full swing, and are well attended. 1- English and French Language. 2- General Course. 3- Mechanics. It is the intention to start more courses at the earliest possible moment. Lack of instructors seems the greatest difficulty.

## Page 5 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

Remarks column: each entry is initialled "J.B."

### 4/12/18 — Mons (continued)

> A.M. The men themselves are very keen. Battalion paraded at 0930 on battalion alarm post Q-20, after which the companys carried on independently with P.T., Squad Drill, Guard Mounting, Platoon & Company Drill.
>
> P.M. Recreation and Sports.

### 5/12/18 — Mons

> A.M. R.Cs of battalion paraded to a "Requiem Mass" at 1000 hours, in Cathedral. 4 officers and 200 other ranks attended. Battalion Parade at 0930 on battalion alarm post, after which Companys carried on with P.T., Squad Drill, Platoon and Company Drill.
>
> P.M. No Sports or Recreation. Companys were issued Blanco and all men "Blancoed" equipment. Orders were received from 8th C.I.B. embodying extracts from Division Operation Orders, re moving. We are now part of the 4th British Army, and must be prepared to move within a couple of days. Further orders to effect that we would move forward on 7th Saturday. Maj. Coley and Billeting Party, detailed to go forward in morning of 6th inst.

## Page 6 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 6/12/18 — Mons

> A.M. Maj. Coley and Billeting Party, of one N.C.O. per Company, and one Runner from B.H.Q. proceeded forward to "Haine St. Paul." Owing to contemplated move, Battalion Parade was cancelled, and Companys made preparation for moving.
>
> P.M. Orders received from 8th C.I.B. that 8th C.I.B. Group will move tomorrow the 7th to the "Haine St. Paul - Haine St. Pierre - Morlanwelz Area." Conference of Company Commanders with O.C. to settle details of move. O.O. drafted. Later, Wire from Brigade that proposed move is postponed, owing to Railway situation, until 11th inst. at the very earliest. Christmas cards have been received from England, 1900 in all. 1000 are being reprinted here in Mons so that they will be available for the men.

> A.M. General Course, French and English Language, Mechanics.
>
> P.M. Recreation and Sports. Headquarters Company was the winner in a soccer game with "A" Company:- Score 2-0.

> A.M. Divine Service was held in Barrack Hall at 1100 hours, Capt. Stevenson of the 4th C.M.R. officiated.
>
> P.M. Recreation and Sports.

### 9/12/18 — Mons

> A.M. Battalion parade on battalion alarm post Q-29 at 0900 hours, after which Companys carried on with P.T. Quartering-up Drill, Guard Mounting, and Platoon and Company Drill. Educational Classes were also carried out.
>
> P.M. Sports and Recreation. Twelve officers of the battalion proceeded to "Brussels" to attend 3rd Divisional Ball by train. Notification received that Lieut. Cocks has been promoted to Acting Captain, also that Lieut. Adamson has been awarded the Military Cross.

### 10/12/18 — Mons

> A.M. No battalion parade. Operation order received from Brigade that we will move tomorrow. Operation order issued and Company made preparation to move.

### 10/12/18 — Mons (continued)

> P.M. Lieut E. J. Moir [uncertain] reported to the battalion for duty, and was posted to "C" Coy.

### 11/12/18 — Mons

> A.M. Battalion parade on "Champ de Manoeuvre" at 0900 hours, previous to proceeding by march route to "Haine St. Paul". The weather was poor, sky overcast, and there were frequent showers of rain. The surrounding country abounded with mines. Battalion halted for lunch at 1150 until 1300 hours. Arrived at "Haine St. Paul" P.M. at 1600 hours, distance covered 20 kilometres. Everyone was tired and certainly appreciated the billets, which were good. Whilst on the march we "Marched Past" the G.O.C. Division who commented very favourably upon the marching and appearance of the entire Brigade. Instructions received from 8th C.I.B. that march would be continued tomorrow the 12th inst. to a new area, the battalion proceeding to Pont-a-Celles.- Operation order issued accordingly.

### 12/12/18 — Mons

> A.M. Battalion proceeded in column of route on main road in Haine-St-Paul at 0840 hours, and started on route for destination. The weather still continues very bad, raining most of the time. The route led through villages which had been previously decorated wreaths and miniature triumphal arches of tree branches and flowers of all descriptions.

### 12/12/18 — Haine St. Paul (continued)

> P.M. Lunched from 1150 to 1300 hours. Arrived in "Pont-A-Celles" at 1400 hours. Billets good.

### 13/12/18 — Pont-A-Celles

> A.M. Companys paraded independently at 0900 hours for rifle inspection. The M.O. inspected the mens feet throughout the day.
>
> P.M. The men enjoyed an afternoon of rest and recreation.

### 14/12/18 — Pont-A-Celles

> A.M. Companys carried on independently with P.T., Smartening up Drill, Rifle Inspection. Shortages in Equipment were also checked up. Billeting party under Lieuts. Savage and Poley [uncertain] proceeded forward to new area. Instructions received from 8th C.I.B. that march would be continued on 15th inst., this battalion to occupy village of "LOUPOIGNE". Operation order issued.

### 15/12/18 — Pont-A-Celles

> A.M. 0745- Proceeded by march route to Loupoigne. Weather a bit improved, no rain but the roads quite muddy. No halt for lunch. Total distance covered 11½ kilometres. Arrived 1130 hours. Billets good. En route we passed the monument erected in memory of QUATRE BRAS June 1815.
>
> P.M. Later operation order from 8th C.I.B. that group would move forward into a new area, this battalion to occupy LASNE-CHAPELE and vicinity. O.O. issued.

## Page 10 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

Remarks column: each entry is initialled "J.B."

### 16/12/18 — Loupoigne

> A.M. Battalion paraded 0845 hours, and proceeded by march route to new area. Weather very bad, raining almost the entire time. Battalion was reviewed en route by the 4th Army Commander General RAWLINSON, who was well pleased with the appearance, and marching of the battalion.
>
> P.M. Arrived in area at 1200 hours. Billets are very poor. Battalion is very scattered. Intention is to procure a new area, for although nothing has been officially received we are all expecting to remain here over Christmas.

### 17/12/18 — Lasne-Chapele

> A.M. Companys paraded independently, and carried on with muster parades, rifle and foot inspection Capt. B. M. BECKWITH and Lieut. L. J. YOUNG M.C. returned from leave.
>
> P.M. Recreation and Sports.

### 18/12/18 — Lasne-Chapele

> A.M. Companys carried on independently as follows:- P.T. and B.T. Smartening up Drill, and Platoon Drill. Men's feet inspected by M.O. during the day.
>
> P.M. Recreation and sports. The area occupied by the battalion is very close to the site of the battle of WATERLOO, a place of great historical interest.

### 19/12/18 — Lasne-Chapele

> A.M. Companys carried on independently with P.T. B.F. and a short route march to places of historical interest near MONT ST. JEAN. The men were paid today.
>
> P.M. Sports and recreation.

### 20/12/18 — Lasne-Chapele

> A.M. Companys held short period of P.T., followed by route march.
>
> P.M. Sports and recreation. Advice received from Brigade that we would be able to re-billet part of battalion in new area MARANSART-PLANCENOIT. Billeting party sent forward accordingly.

### 21/12/18 — (no place given)

> A.M. H.Q. and "A" Coy. moved to MARANSART "B" Coy. to PLANCENOIT. The billets are somewhat better than the ones vacated albeit somewhat crowded. "C" and "D" Coys. carried on with P.T. and a short Route March. The area now occupied by the battalion is a narrow strip 5½ or 6 Kilometres long. The arrangement is highly unsatisfactory from every standpoint. We are all hoping for a change into a better area.

### 22/12/18 — (no place given)

> A.M. Divine Service was held on the square at MARANSART at 1000 hours Capt. CALDWELL of the 2nd C.M.R. officiated.
>
> P.M. Wet weather cancelled all sports arrangements. The companys were bathed during the afternoon.

### 23/12/18 — Maransart

> A.M. Companys carried on independently with ½ hours Physical Training, ½ hours Smartening up Drill, and 1½ hours Route March.
>
> P.M. The remaining Companys "B" and "C", were bathed. A cable was received from the associated Regt. [uncertain] Sherbrooke P.Q. extending greetings to the battalion and expressing a wish to present colors to the unit. The C.O. has cabled acceptance of the offer. Lieut. D.C. Dixon M.C. proceeded on leave to the United Kingdom.

### 24/12/18 — Maransart

> A.M. Battalion paraded on battalion alarm post- MARANSART at 1030 hours. Companys carried on independently before and after battalion parade with ½ hours Physical Training, ½ hours Smartening up Drill, 2 hours Route March. Great preparations are being made by entire battalion for a record Christmas. Halls have been procured by the various companys, and arrangements for a real turkey dinner with the "fixings" have been completed. An allotment of 3 francs per man has been made for the purpose of providing a good Christmas dinner.

### 25/12/18 — Maransart

> A.M. A voluntary service was held in the Hall at MARANSART at which Capt. CALDWELL of the 2nd C.M.R. officiated.

## Page 13 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

Remarks column: each entry is initialled "J.B."

### 25/12/18 — Maransart (continued)

> Final preparations for mens dinner tonight were completed.
>
> P.M. C.O. visited all Companys whilst at their dinner. He, as well as all the officers and men are of the opinion that this has been the best Christmas which the battalion has spent in this country, which is quite fitting since it will no doubt be the last which we shall enjoy together. One Company, "A" has the distinction of dining in a cafe' which is just along side the "Lion of Waterloo" a monument erected on the site of the famous Battle of Waterloo. Later, information received from Brigade that the 8th C.I.B. group would move into 5th Army Area, on the 27th instant. As a result the battalion officers dinner will be held tomorrow night 26th December. About 300 individual Christmas parcels and 2000 cigarettes have been received and distributed.

### 26/12/18 — Maransart

> A.M. Companys carried on independently, parading in heavy marching order, checking shortage and making preparations for move. Information received from 8th C.I.B. that move on 27th is postponed to 28th.

> P.M. Battalion officers dinner held at H.Q.'s officers mess "MARANSART" at 1800 hours. The dinner was a great success. The Brigade Commander and most of his staff attended.

### 27/12/18 — Maransart

> A.M. Companys carried on independently with ½ hours Physical Training, ½ hours Smartening up Drill, and 1½ hours Route March.
>
> P.M. Sports and Recreation. Information received from 8th C.I.B. that 3rd Canadian Division would march to 5th Army area prior to demobilization. > Companys spent the day in making preparations for move tomorrow. Billeting party under Maj. Coley went forward.

### 29/12/18 — Maransart

> Battalion paraded at 0840 hours according to operation order, and march commenced to new area. (Town of RHODE ST. GENESE) Arrived at destination at 1230 hours. Billets poor. People here are mostly Flemish and not very cordial in their reception of the troops. Later, Operation Order from 8th C.I.B. that move would be continued tomorrow. Operation order issued accordingly to companys.

### 30/12/18 — Rhode St. Genese

> Battalion parade at 0830 hours and march commenced to new area "Lennick St. Quentin. En route the Adjutant Capt. H.F. Cocks joined the battalion from leave. Arrived at destination at 1430 hours very tired. Total distance 20 kilometres. Billets very crowded and accommodation poor. Information from 8th C.I.B. that move would be continued tomorrow. Later, Move [uncertain] for tomorrow postponed till Jan. 1st, 1919.

### 31/12/18 — Lennick St. Quentin

> A complete history of the Battalion Colours has been written up. (See Appendix A attached.) The men spent the day resting. Feet were inspected by M.O. during the day. Tentative instructions re- demobilization were received from 8th C.I.B. We hope from them to be in Canada within the next few months. A fitting close with which to close the year.

## Page 32 — Appendix "A" — The Colours of the 5th C.M.R. Battalion

> APPENDIX "A".
>
> The Colours of the 5th C.M.R. Battalion.
>
> Unlike quite a number of Units raised in CANADA for service overseas, this Battalion until the cessation of hostilities had no authorized Battalion Colours.
>
> This was due mostly to the fact that this Unit neither existed before the war, nor, during the short period of training in Canada was it affiliated with any of the permanent force, which had traditions and customs symbolized by Regimental Colours.
>
> However, since those days of training and preparation this Unit has undergone the trial by fire and has built up an "Esprit de Corps" at the cost of many precious lives. This spirit, and the memory of those who have laid down their lives for it, are embodied in our Regimental Colours as they exist today.
>
> The deep bluish purple of the upper rectangle of the body of the flag is one of the colours of "Bishop's College School" of LENNOXVILLE, Quebec, and is in memory of the large percentage of the original members of the Battalion who came from this School and district.
>
> Likewise, the lower red rectangle is in memory of the men from "McGILL COLLEGE," of MONTREAL, who constituted an even larger percentage of the originals of this Battalion.
>
> Between the two solid colours is a strip of orange and black, which are the composite colours of various schools and districts in the MARITIME PROVINCES. These Provinces furnished the men for one of our original companies and the colours - orange and black - are in memory of them.
>
> In the centre are the Brigade and Divisional Colours which to the initiated may be read at a glance.
>
> The Divisional Colour, a small rectangle of French grey, is the symbol of the 3rd Canadian Division, of which we are a part. Previous to the capture of VIMY RIDGE, the Divisional Colour was black but since then it has been the French grey. Whether this is due to the fact that this Division did very good work in the capture of the town and ridge is not definitely known.
>
> The smaller red square superimposed on the grey is the Colour throughout the entire 8th Canadian Infantry Brigade, of which we are part. The patch is square thus showing at a glance the number of the Battalion in the Brigade, i.e. 4th or last.
>
> In the lower right hand corner is an exact reproduction of the Battalion badge on a larger scale. The maple leaf is the emblem of CANADA, the HOMELAND. Upon the maple leaf is the number "Five" (5) which was the original number of the Battalion in the Canadian Expeditionary Force. Due to the re-organization of that Army in 1916, into infantry, the original order of the numbers were lost, but we still retain our old number. Within the curve of the "Five" is a small beaver, the emblem of the Province of Quebec, the place where the Battalion was recruited, mobilized and trained for service overseas.
>
> To the initiated, the flag is an opened book to be read quite easily. But to those who do not know, this short explanation is made, in the hope that they may also realize, in part, just what the members of the Battalion know, feel and think of their Regimental Colours.

- **Equipment:** Companies were issued Blanco on 5 December and "Blancoed" their equipment. Lewis gun limbers and field kitchens feature in every operation order. No weapons, vehicles or animals are otherwise itemised in December.
- **Movements:** The battalion began December at Mons. The planned move on 7 December to the Haine St. Paul–Haine St. Pierre–Morlanwelz area was postponed by wire from Brigade (railway situation) to 11 December at the earliest. Route marches: 11 December Mons to Haine St. Paul (20 km, arrived 1600 hrs); 12 December Haine St. Paul to Pont-a-Celles (arrived 1400 hrs); 15 December Pont-a-Celles to Loupoigne (11½ km, arrived 1130 hrs); 16 December Loupoigne to Lasne-Chapelle (arrived 1200 hrs); 21 December H.Q. and "A" Coy. to Maransart, "B" Coy. to Plancenoit; 29 December Maransart area to Rhode St. Genese (arrived 1230 hrs); 30 December Rhode St. Genese to Lennick St. Quentin (20 km, arrived 1430 hrs). The 31 December move to Ophasselt (Operation Order 187) was postponed by memorandum B.36 to 1 January 1919. The battalion was told on 27 December that the 3rd Canadian Division would march to the 5th Army area prior to demobilization, with entrainment to commence at Baisieux on 6 January 1919.
- **Discipline:** None recorded in December 1918. Every operation order draws attention to 4th Army "NOTES ON MARCH DISCIPLINE" and requires billets to be left clean and sanitary, with a certificate from the Town Commandant, Town Mairie, Bourgemestre or Town Major as applicable.
- **Casualties:** None recorded in December 1918.
