### 1 June 1916 — Trenches

> Battalion paraded last night, entrained 9.40 at C.11 8.19, detraining 40 minutes later at ASYLUM. Companies proceeded independently from ASYLUM (H.12.d. [uncertain]), "C" Company leading, followed by "A" Coy and Staff, then "B" & "D" Companies. Journey in made across fields, very quiet, only two shells coming in while Battalion passed through YPRES. Machine gun & rifle fire very incessant through MAPLECOPSE which has been shelled during last 10 days. Relief completed at 1.35 A.M; no casualties. Directly after completion of relief No 1111805 Pte Dobson of "A" was hit by rifle bullet in left fore arm whilst lying in dugout at MAPLECOPSE (I.23.b.9.3). Brigadier-General V. A. Williams O.C. Brigade, visited Battalion H.Q. at 2.15 p.m. with A.D.C Captain H. Fraser on his way to front line trenches. Morning broke bright and clear. Enemy shelled N.E. corner of MAPLECOPSE (I.25.b.9.3) and S.W. of SANCTUARY WOOD (I.24.b) with shrapnel and high explosive. Battalion disposed as follows. "A" Coy & Battalion H.Q in MAPLECOPSE, "C" Coy in S.Ps. 13.14. 16 & 17 (one platoon in each) "D" Coy ZILLEBEKE DUGOUTS (I.21.a.6.), "C" Coy. 2 platoons ZILLEBEKE DUGOUTS and 2 platoons TRANSPORT FARM (I.21.a.7.).

### 2 June 1916 — Trenches (continued)

> About 6.00 p.m. Captain Roscoe, O.C. "B" Coy which had been at ZILLEBEKE BUND (I.21.a.6) brought his Company up through the open, with splendid leadership into MAPLECOPSE and was ordered to firmly establish a line on the East end of the Copse, from the communication trench on the South side to S.P.16 on the North East. This order was carried out with signal ability and the line maintained. A detachment of the 42nd Battalion came up about this time and occupied a position in support of the P.P.C.L.I.'s to the North East of the Copse. At about 7.00 p.m. Major Draper 2nd i/c of the Battalion was ordered to take a force of 20 men to reinforce S.P.16. This order was immediately carried out and C.S.M. Gill left in charge of the Strong Point, which he gallantly held until his force was practically wiped out. The position of affairs did not change markedly until about 8.15 p.m. when enemy began to appear in the vicinity of OBSERVATORY RIDGE, coming apparently from the direction of RUOKIN HOUSE (I.27.b. [uncertain]) and about 8.30 p.m. the enemy's bombardment increased in intensity, particularly along DURHAM AVENUE and South Side of MAPLECOPSE. (I.23.b.9.2) The enemy used Trench Mortars, Bombs, and guns of all calibres, enfilading the trench from behind with shrapnel.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–30 June 1916 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text — 18 Pages", which matches the eighteen diary-text pages (file pages 3–20). The contents sheet also lists an appendix, "Position of battalions on night 1st/2nd June, 1916 to 9 A.M. 9th June, 1916", which is not among the 21 pages in this range — no map or appendix page is present.
- **Place column:** "Trenches" 1–4 June; "STEENVOORDE" from 5 June to the end of the month. The Remarks and references to Appendices column is blank on most pages; pages 10–20 carry a check mark against most entries.
- **The battle of Mount Sorrel, 2–3 June 1916:** The battalion went into brigade support at MAPLECOPSE (Maple Copse) on the night of 31 May/1 June. At about 8.30 a.m. on 2 June the enemy opened a very heavy bombardment of the front line and Maple Copse, after springing a mine in front of the 4th C.M.R. Battalion's line. The 4th C.M.R. sent the message "Our wires are cut, please have Corps Artillery turned on. General Mercer is here and wants it. General Williams has been hit" (signed J. A. Symonds, Capt. & Adjt., 4th C.M.R.). Runners sent to the front line were killed or wounded; all telephone communication was broken by about 1.30 p.m. The battalion held the east side of Maple Copse and the communication trench on its south side through the day and night, repelling several enemy attacks on the east and south-east sides. The commanding officer, Lt.-Colonel J. H. Baker, was hit and died shortly after 9.40 p.m. on 2 June; Captain C. J. Pitts, O.C. "A" Company, was mortally wounded carrying out the order to reinforce S.P.14. The 2nd C.M.R. Battalion under Major Allen tried to dig in between Maple Copse and Observatory Ridge Road during the night of 2/3 June and withdrew; the 14th Battalion came up at 2.30 a.m. on 3 June. The 9th Brigade relieved the battalion, which came out of the line in the early hours of 4 June.
- **Casualties (as stated 4 June):** Other ranks — killed 57, wounded 271, missing 50. Officers — 4 killed, 7 wounded, 1 missing. Officers killed: Lt.-Colonel J. H. Baker, Captain C. J. Pitts, Lieutenant P. D. Ottey, Lieutenant T. L. Darling. Officers wounded: Major D. E. Draper, Captain W. Rhodes, Captain B. W. Roscoe, Lieut. W. H. Rankin, Lieut. H. G. Rogers, Lieut. A. H. Lee, Lieut. J. Adam. Missing: Lieut. J. Docker. Only 325 rank and file returned out of nearly 650, and only 6 of the 18 officers who went into the line remained in the line (12 officers remained in total, 4 of them non-combatants and 2 returned from leave). Lt.-Colonel Baker was buried with full military honours on 4 June in the new Military Cemetery at Poperinghe (Lot 2. G1), his cross inscribed "Killed in action"; the Chaplain of the 3rd Canadian Division, Rev. Major A. W. Woods, officiated. Pte Dobson (No 1111805, "A" Company) was hit by a rifle bullet in the left forearm on 1 June, the battalion's first casualty of the month.
- **Movements:** 1 June — entrained and detrained at ASYLUM, marched across fields through YPRES to MAPLECOPSE in brigade support. 4 June — remnants back at rest camp. 5 June — moved back to the outskirts of STEENVOORDE, in farmhouse billets, where the battalion remained training for the rest of the month.
- **Reinforcements:** 6 June — 13 other ranks and 2 officers. 9 June — 408 other ranks in all (from the 23rd, 53rd, 12th, 58th and 70th Reserve Battalions). 10 June — 71 other ranks. 11 June — 55 other ranks from Infantry Pool Havre. 14 June — 4 officers (Lieuts. Harold Gordon Young, William Taylor Wilson, William Irving Heart, William Joseph Atherton). 18 June — 12 officers from England. 20 June — 2 more officers (Captains Smith H. A. and Macdonald J. A.).
