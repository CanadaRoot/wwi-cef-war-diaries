### 1/7/16 (Saturday) — Steenvoorde

> Weather bright and warm. Companies carried on with practice of the attack in all its phases, and lectures, in morning. Afternoon devoted to Brigade sports at Y M C A recreation grounds in STEENVOORDE, the 5th C. M. R. Battalion carry off more than their share of the events. British and French offensive begins

### 2/7/16 (Sunday) — Steenvoorde

> Weather bright and warm. Church parade at Hq Company billet. Captain Reverend McGreer officiated. Good news continues of the offensive. Progress very satisfactory. British take 1500 prisoners, French 3500 and more coming in. Eleven Officers and 489 other ranks - Captain P. W. Roscoe in command - left billets at 7 p.m. for an instructional tour in trenches of four days, to be attached to 9th Canadian Infantry Brigade at YPRES. This detachment made up of reinforcements, with enough of the old men, to give the necessary stiffening.

Signed at foot of page: "P. H. O."

### 20/7/16 — Trenches

> Wind N.W. Enemy quiet overnight, becoming active during the morning with trench mortars and rifle grenades, causing casualties, and damaging trenches 49 and 51. We retaliated with No. 1 & No. 2 Stokes guns. Work progressing with sap at Trench 50, and damaged trenches repaired. Sanitation improved and trenches squared for trench map. Enemy aeroplanes fairly active over our lines during afternoon. Casualties:- No. 141747 Pte Brown. J. 'C' Coy. Killed. No. 141844 Pte Shaver E. J. 'C' Coy. Killed. both by Trench Mortars. No. 445589 Pte Rector G. 'D' Coy g.s.w head. Killed. No. 603254 Pte Murphy M. R. 'A' Coy. g.s.w face, wounded, No. 123578. Pte. Fryer. A, g.s.w head wounded, No. 110288. Pte Kelly. J. J. 'A' Coy. s.i.w. while cleaning rifle (left hand), and No. 408955 Pte Webster. J. S. 'B' Coy. g.s.w. right eye wounded. Eight other ranks shell shocked, but sent back to transport line.

### 21/7/16 — Trenches

> Wind, North, weather clear and mild, situation quiet overnight. Work progressing with cleaning up trenches, executing repairs. Parados sand bagged at damaged places. Four dug outs completed, and four others commenced. Parapet repaired for about thirty yards in Winnipeg Street. St Peters Street deepened two feet.

Signed at foot of page: "P. H. O."

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 July 1916 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 10 Pages", which matches the ten diary-text pages (pages 3–12).
- **Officers named:** Lt.-Col. Dennis Colborne Draper (promoted and given command of the battalion 12 July; signs the diary 31 July); Lt.-Col. George Harry Baker (former commanding officer, killed in action 2 June 1916); Capt. P. W. Roscoe (second in command; led the instructional detachment to the trenches 2 July; pall bearer at Capt. Pineo's funeral); Capt. Henry Hoyt Pineo (O.C. 'B' Company, killed 21 July, buried 24 July at Poperinghe); Capt. Reverend McGreer (officiated at the 2 July church parade); Lt. Herbert Courtney Fisher (reported for duty 17 July from the 40th Reserve Battalion, posted to 'B' Company); Lt.-Col. Lamb (Intelligence Officer, 3rd Canadian Division, lectured 11 July); Lt. Gen. the Hon. Sir Julian Byng (Corps Commander, inspected the battalion 10 July); Capts. R. C. Cleveland, A. S. Robinson, H. Macdonald and A. Trickey, and Lieut. R. B. Mathers (pall bearers at Capt. Pineo's funeral). The initials "P. H. O." appear at the foot of every diary page and in the remarks column; the officer is not otherwise identified in July.
- **Other ranks named:** Pte A. Moseley, No. 110378 ('A' Coy, selected 14 July to attend the National Review in Paris for France's Day); Cpl Fower R., Staff Signaller (killed 21 July); and the men named in the casualty lists below.
- **Movements:** Steenvoorde (1–15 July, training and brigade sports) → Camp Erie (16–19 July; map reference R.11.C.5.3) → front-line trenches near Ypres and Zillebeke (19–23 July, relieving the Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry) → Zillebeke Bund (23–27 July) → Infantry Barracks, Ypres (27–31 July). Strength at the end of the month: 10 officers, 510 men.
- **Casualties:** Killed or died of wounds (11 — 1 officer, 10 other ranks): 20 July — Pte J. Brown (No. 141747), Pte E. J. Shaver (No. 141844), Pte G. Rector (No. 445589), all by trench mortar; 21 July — Capt. Henry Hoyt Pineo, Pte J. K. Atkins (No. 110379), Pte W. Brook (No. 135459), Cpl Fower R. (No. 111490), Pte J. Donaldson (No. 136225), all by trench mortar; 22 July — Pte E. R. Jones (No. 171629, died of wounds from a rifle grenade); 23 July — Pte J. S. Alcorn (No. 111003, trench mortar); 30 July — Pte T. V. McDonald (No. 110404, killed by shell fire in the courtyard of the Infantry Barracks, Ypres). Wounded (13): 20 July — Pte M. R. Murphy (No. 603254, face), Pte A. Fryer (No. 123578, head), Pte J. J. Kelly (No. 110288, self-inflicted wound while cleaning his rifle, left hand), Pte J. S. Webster (No. 408955, right eye), plus eight other ranks shell-shocked but sent back to the transport line; 21 July — Pte A. Lynott (No. 110334, right foot), Pte H. P. McCann (No. 123734, left arm), Pte J. Hurley (No. 110670, left wrist); 22 July — Pte Eldie J (No. 171621), Pte R. Oliver (No. 120206), Pte A. Darby (No. 120626), Pte W. J. Hill (No. 111231), all by rifle grenade, and Pte J. Mitchell (No. 124476, left foot); 30 July — Pte C. Robson (No. 603131, shell fire).
- **Battles and action:** The diary opens with the British and French offensive that began 1 July 1916 (the Somme), reporting British prisoners taken as 1,500 and French as 3,500 on 2 July. The battalion's own fighting in July is trench warfare in the Ypres salient: it went into the front line on the night of 19–20 July (trenches 47–55, Winnipeg Street, Stafford Street, Davidson Street, St Peters Street, X Trench, Old Halifax), endured trench-mortar and rifle-grenade fire 20–23 July, sent a patrol into No Man's Land on the night of 22 July (a dead German's identification marks were brought in), and occupied an enemy mine crater on 23 July.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The first diary sheet is numbered "10" at the top right as well as "1" in a circle; the ten diary sheets are numbered 1–10 in circles, and the cover calls the month "Volume 10". (2) The word "have" is inserted above the line in the 22 July entry ("Our snipers claim to have enemy snipers well in hand"). (3) The word "Killed" is inserted above the line for Pte McDonald on 30 July. (4) The trench reference for 'D' Coy on 19 July reads "Trenches 47-48-48A-49-50 [uncertain] Canada" — the characters between "50" and "Canada" are unclear, possibly "& 50A". (5) The 26 July entry gives Capt. Pineo's initials as "H. H." while the casualty entry of 21 July names him in full as "Henry Hoyt Pineo". (6) Pte Kelly's wound on 20 July is recorded as "s.i.w." (self-inflicted wound) while cleaning his rifle. (7) The spelling "St Peters Street" appears without an apostrophe; "practise" is not used — the diary writes "practice" throughout. (8) The 28 July strength is 10 officers and 495 men, rising to 510 men on 29–31 July with no explanation given in the diary.
