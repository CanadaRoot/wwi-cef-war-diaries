### 1/7/18 — Front Line, Mercatel Section

> Weather Fine. The 5th C.M.R Battalion relieved the 22nd French Canadian Battalion in the Front Line Right Sub Section, Mercatel Sector, Relief being Completed by 2.10 A.M. Enemy very quiet. There was very little artillery, trench mortar or machine gun activity during the day.
>
> Three Patrols left our line at 11-00 p.m. to Cover Battalion Frontage. The Patrol under LIEUT WELDON consisting of 19 Other Ranks patrolled from S.6.b.05.60 to S.6.b.10.20. Patrol under LIEUT SAVAGE and 17 other ranks patrolled from S.6.b.10.20. to S.12.a.20.70. Patrol under LIEUT SMITH Patrolled from S.12.a.20.70. to S.12.a.70.00. Companies Carried on with the work of Constructing funk holes and trench maintenance in their respective areas.

### 2/7/18 — Front Line, Mercatel Section

> Weather Fine Our Patrols Returned 2.30 AM. Patrol under LIEUT SAVAGE observed enemy patrol at S.12.a.90.90 but the enemy apparently observed our patrol and Retired. Our wiring Parties erected new defences at S.12.a.70.00 – S.12.a.50.40. There was practically no artillery activity during the day.
>
> The usual Patrols were sent out during the night

### 3/7/18 — Front Line, Mercatel Section

> Weather Fine Patrols Returned about 2.00 AM. There was nothing to Report. At 1.00 AM a Raid was Carried out by the 96th + 97th Imperial Brigades with Artillery Support.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 July 1918 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 17 Pages", which matches the seventeen Army Form C. 2118 diary-text pages (form pages (1)–(17), diary pages 4–20). Pages 1–3 are the cover, contents sheet and inner title page; pages 21–39 are appendices.
- **Officers named:** Lieut Weldon, Lieut Savage and Lieut Smith (patrol commanders, 1 July); Lt.-Col. D. C. Draper D.S.O. (promoted to Temporary Brigadier General, 11 July; inspecting the battalion 16 July); Major General Mewburn (Minister of Militia & Defence) and Colonel Ballantyne (Minister of Marine & Fisheries), who inspected the battalion 16 July; Major MacFarlane, 4th Cdn. Mounted Rifles Battalion, and Captain Clarke, 1st Cdn. Mounted Rifles Battalion (tracer-ammunition demonstration, 21 July); Lieut. H. B. Walton, DCM (in charge of guides, Operation Orders 128–131); Lieut. H. V. Atto, MC (advance party, Operation Order 131); Lieut. C. V. [uncertain] Carter (advance party, Operation Order 129); Lieut. G. R. Foley (advance party, Operation Order 133). The diary is signed on page 20 by a Major, Commanding 5th C.M.R. Bn., whose surname is not legible. The Adjutant's signature on the typed July orders appears to read "H. Cooke".
- **Other ranks named:** None named in July 1918.
- **Casualties:** None recorded in July 1918. The only casualty statement is "No Casualties" on the 14 July relief.
- **Movements:** 1 July — relieved the 22nd French Canadian Battalion in the front line, right sub-section, Mercatel Sector (relief completed 2.10 A.M.). 4–6 July — inter-company reliefs in the line. 8 July — relieved in the line by the 1st C.M.R. Battalion (relief completed 11.50 P.M.) and moved to the Support Area (Brickfields / Intermediate line, S.32.D). 14 July — relieved by the Royal Canadian Regiment (relief completed 11.45 P.M.) and moved back to billets in Bellacourt in Divisional Reserve. 23 July — route march via Wailly and Thainville to Y Huts near Etrun (taking over huts vacated by the 3rd Canadian Infantry Battalion); the battalion was then with the Canadian Corps in G.H.Q. Reserve at 12 hours' notice. 25 July — marched to Bois de Bray. 30 July — route march via Tusans and Warlus to Saulty (arriving 6.00 P.M.). 31 July — route march to Mondicourt Station, entrained 5.50 A.M. (transport entraining at Doullens), reached Prozeul at 1.00 P.M., then marched to Bois de Bacquel and camped there for the night.
- **Strengths:** Disposition Report 1 July 1918: Total Officers 30, O.R. 684. Disposition Report 5 July 1918: Total 31 Officers, 653 O.Rs. The two reports show the companies rotated after the 5–6 July internal relief.
- **Patrols and enemy activity:** Nightly patrols covered the whole battalion frontage 1–8 July; enemy patrols were observed 2 July (S.12.a.90.90) and 4 July (S.12.b.60.65). A Special Battle Patrol working with the 2nd C.M.R. Battalion found two belts of wire blocking the "Sunken Road" objective 7 July, and on 8 July found the enemy post deserted after being fired on by a machine gun from about S.6.b.30.60. A raid was carried out by the 96th + 97th Imperial Brigades on 3 July. Gas was projected by the guards on the right flank on 11 July, with an exceptionally heavy artillery barrage. Enemy shelling of the Brickfields/Brickpikes area was reported 12 July (5.9s and 4.1s). A hostile aeroplane flew low over the lines on 10 July and was driven off.
- **Working parties:** 300 other ranks on a new trench and wiring 9 July; a continuous party of 40 men on dugouts in Elm Lane; a special wiring party of 4 officers + 100 other ranks 10 July; 400 other ranks on working parties 12 July; 12 officers + 514 O.Rs under the 4th Army Troops 26 July; 14 officers + 575 other ranks on new trenches between Ecurie and Neuville St. Vaast 28 July.
- **Training and recreation:** Dominion Day sports at Tinques 1 July (three battalion entries, defeated in the finals); 8th Canadian Infantry Brigade Group mounted sports 18 July (battalion won a first prize — wrestling and tug of war on horseback, plus places at tent pegging); gas helmet drill, platoon drill and manning the Purple Line in skeleton 17–19 July; tracer-ammunition demonstration near Dainville 21 July; Lewis gun tactical training 26–27 July; range practice at Bray 27 and 29 July; baseball — "D" Company beat Headquarters Company 8–6 for the battalion championship 20 July, and the other ranks beat the officers 21 July.
- **Defence Scheme (8 July):** The battalion held the Right sub-section, Right Mercatel Section, 3rd Canadian Division, between a southern (divisional) boundary at S.12.a.80.00 and a northern boundary at S.6.a.05.60; 3rd Guards Brigade on the right, a 3rd Canadian Division battalion on the left. Front line through S.12.a., S.6.c., S.5.d. and S.6.a.; Sunken Road used as a living trench; Mercatel Switch as the main resistance (reserve) line. Artillery cover for S.O.S.: 10 4.5-inch howitzers and 25 18-pounders; 3 light trench mortars; machine-gun cover by the 2nd Canadian Divisional Machine Gun Battalion. S.O.S. signal: No. 35 rifle grenade, green over green over green. R.A.P. at S.11.a.55.05, relay post S.11.d.90.00, A.D.S. M.31.b.80.00.
