### 1/2/17 — ESTREE-CAUCHIE

> Weather cold — wind NE. Battalion paraded for inspection by C.O. (Major Blandis [uncertain]) carrying on with syllabus later. (Marked A)

### 2/2/17 — ESTREE-CAUCHIE

> Weather cold — wind NE. Battalion carried on with training according to Syllabus. Lt. Andrews and 100 other ranks reported to the Officer i/c Special Dumps for the purpose of storing large quantities of artillery ammunition at CAMBLIGNEUL.

### 3/2/17 — ESTREE-CAUCHIE

> Weather cold — wind NE. Battalion paraded at 10 am and route marched to MT ST ELOY taking up billets there preparatory to going into the line.

### 4/2/17 — MT ST ELOY

> Weather cold — wind changing to W. Battalion supplied a working party of 3 officers 8 N.C.O.s and 100 men for work on burying cable. Balance of Battalion carried on independently with route marches.

### 5/2/17 — MT ST ELOY

> Weather cold — wind back again to N.E. Battalion paraded and marched by platoons to the front line to relieve the 4th C.M.R. Bn. Relief completed by 2.30 pm without casualties.

### 5/2/17 (cont.) — In the Line

> Battalion finds that enemy has been allowed to do a lot of work on his lines since last tour.

### 6/2/17 — In the Line

> Wind NE — weather cold — Situation quiet — In afternoon Lt. LINDSAY accidentally wounded in thigh. — Lt TODD, Brigade Bombing Officer was testing a bomb in the front line. He threw (over the parapet) a bomb into a shell hole, but further wall of shell hole broken allowing the bomb to fall into SUTHERLAND AVE where Lt LINDSAY was passing. No. 832179 Pte Price W. A. Coy, accidentally wounded in right hand. States that he was asleep in dugout — piece of live coal fell out of brazier on to ammunition, exploding it and causing his wound.

### 7/2/17 — In the Line

> Weather cold — wind N. NE. Situation normal. Our defensive patrols out at night, but no enemy encountered. About 5.45 pm premature explosion occurred in a Stokes Mortar emplacement destroying gun emplacement and killing Capt. JUICE [uncertain] (1st C.M.R.) who was at the gun.

### 9/2/17 (cont.) — In the Line

> showed no signs of life during the night. During the morning and afternoon, Major Rhoades Acting O.C. arranged with the artillery to fire 100 to 150 rounds on to enemy's front line — and support lines in the vicinity of the SNOUT, with the object of damaging some new work which has been observed. The shoot was entirely successful, and caused considerable damage.

### 10/2/17 — In the Line

> Weather cold — wind N.W. — Situation normal. One man killed and two wounded today.
>
> 709898. Pte Hanson H.W. "B" Coy. Killed at 3.12 Sap by sniper.
> 832599. Pte Leach. O.A. "C" Coy. GSW in face near crater 25.

### 11/2/17 — In the Line

> Weather cold — wind N.E. Situation normal. We are to be relieved tomorrow by the 31st (ALBERTA) Battalion. Parties of officers visited the line to get acquainted with the area.

> Wind N.E. weather cold again. Battalion resting with exception of 25 men per Coy (100 in all) who formed a working party burying cable.
>
> The Battalion moved off at 11.30 am from Billets and route marched to HOUDAIN by way of CAMBLAIN L'ABBAYE — ESTREE-CAUCHIE — REBREVE. Arrived at HOUDAIN about 5 pm. Road very congested with troops but everything went splendidly. The men marched well and looked well after so long a spell of front line work. Divisional Commander saw the men en route.

The diary's own tour summary (12 February) gives the whole tour's casualties from 23 October 1916 to 12 February 1917 as 8 other ranks killed and 46 wounded, and 5 officers wounded, none killed — the February deaths and woundings above are part of that total.
- **Movements:** Estree-Cauchie 1–3 February; Mont St Eloy 3–5 February; in the line (Ecurie sector, the Snout) 5–12 February; relieved 12 February by the 31st (Alberta) Battalion, back to Mont St Eloy; route marched 13 February via Camblain l'Abbaye, Estree-Cauchie and Rebreuve to Houdain; 14 February via Divion and Cauchy-le-Tour to Raimbert, where the battalion remained training to the end of the month.
- **Operations:** A 100–150 round artillery shoot on the enemy front and support lines near the Snout (9 February) after Brigade warned of a possible enemy raid that night — the enemy showed no signs of life. Defensive patrols nightly; no enemy encountered. A premature Stokes mortar explosion (7 February) destroyed the gun emplacement and killed Capt. Juice and Sgt. Lewis. A gas-raid warning was not followed by an attack.
- **Training and honours:** After leaving the line the battalion trained on the new platoon organisation — squad drill, bombing, sniping, Lewis gunnery, bayonet fighting and attack practice in practice trenches — inspected by the G.O.C. 3rd Canadian Division and the Divisional Commander (20 and 24 February), the latter praising the transport turnout in a letter from Brig. Gen. Elmsley. On 25 February the Corps Commander presented decorations at Burbure: D.S.O. to Major B. W. Roscoe, M.M. to Lieut. T. W. Martin, Sgt. R. B. Rogers and Sgt. D. W. Graig.
