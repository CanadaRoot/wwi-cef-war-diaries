## Page 3 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118)

Heading: WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through. The page is numbered "4" at the top right.

Place column: "Meteren" throughout.

### Jan 1/16

> The first day of the New Year and the first day of the new Infantry formation. Companies begin training under Infantry Instructors. Name of Regiment changed from 5th Canadian Mounted Rifles to 5th Battalion (C.M.R.) The 2nd Brigade C.M.R. to which we were originally attached has become defunct, a new Brigade called the 8th Canadian Infantry Brigade (C.M.R.) taking the place of the 1st and 2nd C.M.R. Brigades. Infantry establishment [uncertain] in force, the whole personnel being changed, the scheme now being 1st, 2nd, 4th, and 5th Battalions all under the regime of the 8th Canadian Infantry Brigade (C.M.R.) The old 3rd C.M.R. and 6th C.M.R. are now absorbed into other Battalions. This 5th Battalion, to bring it toward the new establishment has absorbed the Headquarters Staff, A, and C Squadrons of the late 6th C.M.R. Regiment, together with the Sub-ordinate Staff of the 2nd Brigade C.M.R. The new Brigadier, Brigadier G. S. Williams, gave orders for the new Battalions comprising his Brigade, to assemble for inspection on the morning of 3rd January 1916.

### 2nd (Sunday)

> Companies continuing new Infantry instruction.

> Weather very bright and clear. Strong wind blowing. Morning, scheme of "Attack and Defence" carried out by the Battalion, before the General Officer Commanding. Afternoon, Company drill, and lectures.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 January 1916 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 8 Pages", which matches the eight diary-text pages (pages 3–10).
- **Officers named:** Brigadier G. S. Williams (new commander of the 8th Canadian Infantry Brigade (C.M.R.)); Lieut. B. B. Morrell, 2nd in command (struck off strength, invalided, 5 January); Captain A. C. Charles (Machine Gun Officer of the late 2nd C.M.R. Brigade, taken on strength 7 January); Captain J. Knot, Roman Catholic Chaplain (posted to the Battalion, 4 January); Major A. C. Draper, O.C. "A" Company (takes over as 2nd in command, 11 January); Major R. F. Stockwell; Captain J. R. Macleod; Lieutenant C. Chown; Lieutenant Todd; Lieutenant Gill [uncertain]; Lieutenant Oly [uncertain]; Lieutenant Harton; Lieutenant Green (Machine Gun Section); Lieutenant G. V. Hannah; Lieut. W. Campbell (transferred to 3rd Canadian Division Signal Company, 27 January); Lieutenant L. H. Wright (transferred to 6th Field Artillery, 27 January); Captain J. J. Pitts (posted to "B" Company as 2nd in command, 27 January); General Mercer, Divisional Commander (inspected transport, 28 January). The diary is signed "For O.C. 5th Bn (C.M.R.)" by a Major whose name is not clearly legible [uncertain].
- **Other ranks named:** Pte W. A. Lewis No 112205, "A" Company (death in hospital reported, 31 January). Reinforcements: a draft of twelve men to "C" Company (4 January), a draft of four men to "A" Company (5 January), and eight reinforcements taken on strength (29 January).
- **Movements:** The battalion remained in billets at Meteren for the whole month, training as infantry; on 31 January it moved to 3rd Divisional Reserve at [uncertain] Farm, relieving the 14th Battalion, with the Machine Gun Section going into the trenches that evening.
- **Casualties:** One death: Pte W. A. Lewis No 112205, "A" Company, died in hospital (31 January). Three men returned from the gas demonstration at No 1 Grenade School suffering from the effects of gas (18 January).
- **Action:** No front-line action in January — the month is entirely infantry conversion training at Meteren: company and battalion drill, musketry, bayonet exercises, attack-and-defence schemes (20–21 and 25–26 January), a gas demonstration (18 January), and a "Gas Alert" on 23 January. Small parties were sent for 24-hour spells in the trenches from 27 January.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The unit is renamed on 1 January from 5th Regiment, Canadian Mounted Rifles to 5th Battalion (C.M.R.), 3rd Canadian Division, absorbing the Headquarters Staff and "A" and "C" Squadrons of the late 6th C.M.R. Regiment and the subordinate staff of the 2nd Brigade C.M.R.; the 3rd and 6th C.M.R. are absorbed into other battalions. (2) The 1 January entry is written in a different, more formal hand than the rest of the month and reads as a summary of the reorganisation rather than a daily log. (3) The diary heading on page 3 has "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through; on later pages the heading is struck through wholesale. (4) The 27 January entry is very compressed and several officer names and transfer details are not clearly legible. (5) The Corps Commander inspected the battalion on 5 January, the General Officer Commanding on 4 January and the Brigadier on 3 January — three inspections in three days as the new infantry formation was stood up.
