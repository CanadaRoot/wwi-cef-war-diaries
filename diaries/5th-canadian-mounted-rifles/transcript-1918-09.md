Heading: WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. The Place column is filled in only for 1 September ("GOUVES"); it is blank for the 2nd and 3rd.

### 1/9/18 — GOUVES

> Sunday. Church Parade was held at 11.00 A.M. on the Battalion Alarm Post. Capt. Stewart officiated. In the afternoon the Battalion had much needed baths. Capt. T. D. Johnston and Lieut. INCE reported for duty.

### 2/9/18

> The Battalion paraded at 8.30 A.M. From 9.00 to 12.30 Companies carried on independently, with P.T. & B.F., consisting of drill, & Organization. At 5.00 A.M. Orders were received to be ready to move at four hours notice, and at 10.00 A.M. orders were received that the battalion would be ready to move at one hours notice. At 3.30 P.M. the order was received that the battalion would proceed to ARRAS by March Route, and at 5.00 P.M. the Battalion moved off, arriving in ARRAS at 7.30 P.M. On the road we passed thousands of Hun prisoners, and heard the wildest rumors regarding a great advance by our 1st and 4th Divisions. Later, information was received that we had broken through the DROCOURT LINE, obtained 4500 prisoners, and that our patrols were pushing on.

### 3/9/18

> The summary of operations for Aug 26–28 was forwarded to Brigade. Our casualties for these operations were: — Killed, one Officer 10 O.Rs. Died of Wounds, 1 O.R. Wounded 8 Officers 277 Other Ranks. Wounded at Duty 2 Officers 2 Other Ranks.

### 3/9/18 (continued)

> Lieut THORNE returned from the 3rd Cdn Div. Wing and was replaced there by Lieut LINDSAY. Lieut DUNHAM returned from 1st Army Course.

### 4/9/18

> Orders were received that tomorrow we would move to VIS-EN-ARTOIS. The Companies carried on during the morning with reorganization. The following was submitted for Honors & Awards for operations of Aug 26–28: — 1 V.C. 3 Bars to M.C. 5 M.C. 11 D.C.M. 20 M.M.

### 5/9/18

> The news from the Armies on our right & left is very good, and especially that from our own Corps which is reported to have reached the Western bank of the Canal du NORD & to have taken 7000 prisoners since Sept 1st. At 3.00 P.M. Companies moved off independently, assembling in PELVES ROAD at 5.45 P.M. The Battalion arrived in O.29 (South of VIS EN ARTOIS) at 8.00 P.M. & found very little accommodation, both officers & men having to "dig in".

### 6/9/18

> Division moved here & there is a great congestion of troops, which can be well observed by the enemy. Expect he will shell the place before long. We are still pushing on the South & North. Lieut. ANDREWS reported for duty.

### 7/9/18

> Company inspection took place in the morning. At 4.00 p.m. the enemy started shelling the Camp with two 5.9's. There is a large tunnel here, capable of holding two Battalions, and all ranks found shelter in it. Bn. H.Q. were also moved into it. Lieut. INGERSOLL reported for duty. Lieut. J. A. ADAMSON is appointed Bn. Staff Officer, vice Lieut. L. ATTO (wounded). CASUALTY: — Pte. LANCASTER was wounded in the leg by H.E. Shell.

### 8/9/18

> Division evacuated the tunnel and we moved in. There is ample accommodation for the whole Battalion, and, considering that we are at least 60 feet under ground, the air is very good. Corporal RICHARDSON, B. Coy. was killed by shell fire & was buried by Capt. STEWART.

A faint interlineation above the line reads "in VIS EN ARTOIS CEMETERY" [uncertain — it may be show-through from the reverse of the sheet].

### 9/9/18

> A.M. A working party of 2 Officers & 100 O.Rs. was sent to Brigade to build a Range. The remainder of the Battalion were allotted baths in VIS EN ARTOIS. P.M. Each Company cleaned up its own area & entrance to the tunnel, which looks very clean. Cpl. RICHARDSON, Scout Sect. died of wounds in No. 8 Field Ambulance. He was buried by Capt. Stewart in a cemetery near VIS EN ARTOIS. Pte. SIMS was wounded & evacuated. Lieut. POLEY returned from a course. The O.C. went to Brigade for dinner.

### 10/9/18

> The O.C. Lieut. Colonel M. RHODES, D.S.O., M.C., proceeded on leave to England, and Major J.S.E. TODD, M.C., took over command of the Battalion. A working party of 2 officers and 100 O.Rs. was detailed to report at Division. A salvage party of 1 Officer & 20 O.Rs. per Coy. cleaned up Square O.20. A Warning Order was received that we would relieve the R.C.R. in right support, and at 2.00 p.m. Major TODD, with a party from each Coy., went forward to reconnoitre. Major General LIPSETT, the Divisional Commander, visited the Battalion at 4.00 p.m. to say farewell, prior to his leaving to take over command of the 4th Imperial Division. All Officers & N.C.Os. were paraded to meet him. He expressed his regret at leaving the Division & congratulated & thanked the Battalion for the loyal support we had given him & the splendid work we had done whilst he had been our Divisional Commander. Owing to Lieut. Col. RHODES, D.S.O., M.C., being on leave, and Major TODD, M.C. away reconnoitring, Brig. General Draper suitably responded on behalf of the Battalion.

### 11/9/18

> Preparations were made for moving out at 6.30 p.m. Coys. moved off. Relief was completed at 9.00 p.m. The R.C.R. moved back to area vacated by 1st C.M.R. Bn. ### 11/9/18 (continued)

> Strength of Battalion 35 Officers 1455 Other Ranks.

### 12/9/18

> The enemy continues to shell all around us, but does not show good marksmanship. At 6.00 p.m. we moved Battalion H.Q. to P.28. C.20.30. This area is not so heavily shelled, but accommodation is very limited. Lieut. MacKENZIE reported back from leave. Lieuts. RIX and MASSIAH reported for duty.

### 13/9/18

> Information was received that the 1st C.M.R. Bn. would make good the Outpost Resistance Line, at 12.30 a.m. the 14/9/18. We were to place one Company (A. Coy.) at the disposal of the 1st C.M.R. Bn. At 9.00 p.m. Major TODD, Lieut. JOHNSON, & Lieut. INCE moved forward & established Advance H.Q. at P.36. B.35.95.

### 14/9/18

> There was very little retaliation to the 1st C.M.R. Bn. attack. Information was received that they had attained their objective. The enemy continued to shell the ARRAS-CAMBRAI ROAD. At 4.00 P.M. Bn. H.Q. closed at P.28. C.28.9 & reopened at Adv. H.Q. P.36. B.35.95. A new scheme received necessitates a change in our defence plan. The Americans have made a very successful debut in the south, which augurs well.

### 15/9/18 — P.36.b.70.90

> Orders were received that at dawn we were to send a reconnoitring party to forward areas, to arrange about relieving 1st C.M.R. Bn. in the line. At 1.00 p.m. the enemy commenced to shell our H.Q. which was in a gun pit, with a roof about two feet thick. Major TODD, Lieut. INCE & LIEUT. COOKS, vacated this & moved out into the road. The enemy continued to shell the locality until he obtained a direct hit. The M.O., Padre, & Lieut. Cooks who had taken refuge in an old bluff, being badly shaken up & gassed, the Padre, Capt. NICHOLSON, being evacuated. Orders were received for the relief of the 1st C.M.R. Bn., which commenced at 9.00 p.m. At 12.00 midnight the Adjutant 1st C.M.R. Bn. asked permission to go forward with his platoon and investigate. This was granted & at 4.10 a.m. he reported that the situation was normal.

### 16/9/18 (continued)

> Capt. CRAIG, 8th G.I.B. reported for Liaison. At 10.30 a.m. Major TODD returned having made preparation to retake crater, from which we had been driven out, but on consultation with Brigade it was decided to hold an outpost line immediately west of the crater. From this place command of the Canal could be obtained. Lieut. INGERSOLL, Assistant Staff Officer reported that at 9.00 p.m. the night previous he had made a reconnaissance of the crater & had found it unoccupied. He could not find any trace of our missing men, (18 men are reported missing) and it is believed that they are either wounded or killed. Harassing fire was employed on the Canal area all day. At 5.00 p.m. D. Coy. commenced to relieve A. Coy., & we drew in our line [uncertain] with further instructions from Brigade. One of our 6" Howitzer guns fired short killing 2 & wounded 8 of our men.

### 18/9/18

> Relief was complete at 1.00 a.m. & we had also taken up the new outpost line in accordance with instructions from Brigade. The night passed quietly, the enemy appearing to have withdrawn his guns further north. A Warning Order was received that we would be relieved the night of 19/20 by two Imperial Battalions, i.e. The London Scottish and the 1st London Regiment. Many aerial combats took place during the day.

### 18/9/18 (continued)

> At dusk, B. Coy. who were in Right Support relieved the left Coy. of the 2nd Bn. in the line. A Coy. 1st Cdn. Bn. moved into right support, & came under command of the C.O. 5th C.M.R. Bn. ### 19/9/18

At 11.35 p.m. on completion of relief the Battalion moved to bivouac & funk holes near CHERISY. Casualties for tour: — 1 Officer wounded; 1 do. gassed; 4 O.Rs. killed; 24 do wounded; 15 do missing; 1 O.R. missing, believed prisoner; 2 do do do wounded.

### 25/9/18

> The Battalion paraded to receive Gen. [uncertain] at WARLUS. Lieut.-Col. M. Rhodes, D.S.O., M.C. returned from leave & took over command of the Battalion. Preparations were made for moving.

### 26/9/18

> The Battalion paraded on Battalion Alarm Post at 11.30 a.m. & marched to WANQUENTIN. (The transport moved by road to new area.) They entrained there for CROISILLES. On reaching BOYELLES it was found that 200 yards of track had been torn up. The Battalion detrained, marched two kilometres & then entrained again, arriving in CROISILLES at 10.30 p.m. & immediately detraining. They then marched to the new area in U.23.D. After about a kilometre march the enemy opened fire on the road with his long range guns, and obtained a direct hit on the transport, which was in front of us. Two men were killed and eight wounded, and two horses killed. The rain started & the road became very slippery & made marching difficult, especially near BULLECOURT where the ground was covered with shell holes. The Battalion reached the new area (near REINCOURT) at 5.45. U.23.D.70.00. 2.00 a.m.

### 27/9/18

> 5.45 a.m. The 1st & 3rd Cdn. Divisions attacked in conjunction with troops on right & left.

### 29/9/18 (continued)

> The 8th C.I.B., in conjunction with the 9th C.I.B. on right & 7th C.I.B. on left, attacked. 1st & 2nd C.M.R. attacked. 4th C.M.R. support. 5th C.M.R. Reserve. Zero at 8.00 A.M. Battalion reached assembly area without sustaining any casualties. The enemy is apparently fighting very hard in front of CAMBRAI. Later in the afternoon the situation improved, the 11th Bn. & the 1st C.M.R. capturing the OLLE. English Regiments are also pushing up on the south of CAMBRAI. Apparently we will not move tonight. Rations & Field Kitchens were brought up & the men dug into funk holes & made themselves as comfortable as possible.

### 30/9/18

> The day opened up very cold & overcast. The attack continued on our left at 6.00 a.m. The 8th C.I.B. will not attack this morning, but the 1st & 2nd C.M.R. will keep in touch with the 7th C.I.B. We remain in reserve. At midnight the 24 hour clock system will be adopted (Continental time). Brigade informed us that Bulgaria had surrendered unconditionally. This will doubtless have a great bearing on the war, and this may turn out to be the greatest day of the War.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Diary text 1–30 September 1918 (pp. 1–16, with no entry dated 17 September); operation orders, a sketch map and an honours and awards list (pp. 17–32). No pages missing from the 32-page September sequence.
- **Pages 1–16 ([uncertain] flags in this batch: 4):**
  - **Coverage:** Entries run 1–30 September 1918, except that there is no entry dated 17 September — the diary jumps from 16 September (continued on page 10) to 18 September; the date column on page 10 confirms "16" followed by "18". The contents sheet states "Diary Text 13 Pages", which matches the thirteen diary-text pages (pages 4–16).
  - **Officers named:** Lt-Col M. Rhodes, D.S.O., M.C. (O.C.; on leave to England 10–25 September; signs the diary on page 16); Major J. S. E. Todd, M.C. (took command 10 September); Capt. T. D. Johnston; Lieut. Ince; Lieut. Thorne; Lieut. Lindsay; Lieut. Dunham; Lieut. Andrews; Lieut. Ingersoll (Assistant Staff Officer); Lieut. J. A. Adamson (Bn. Staff Officer); Lieut. L. Atto (wounded); Capt. Stewart; Capt. Nicholson (Padre, gassed and evacuated 15 September); Lieut. Poley; Lieut. MacKenzie; Lieuts. Rix and Massiah; Lieut. Johnson (13 September — possibly the same officer as Capt. T. D. Johnston of 1 September); Lieut. Ganong (O.C. A Coy.); Capt. Craig (8th G.I.B., liaison); Lieut. B. Long; Lieut. Dibble; Lieut. Logie; Lieut. Cooks; Major General Lipsett (Divisional Commander, farewelled 10 September on leaving to command the 4th Imperial Division); Brig. General Draper. The M.O. is mentioned (15 September) but not named.
  - **Other ranks named:** Pte. Lancaster (wounded in the leg, 7 September); Cpl. Richardson, B. Coy. (killed by shell fire, 8 September); Cpl. Richardson, Scout Sect. (died of wounds in No. 8 Field Ambulance, 9 September); Pte. Sims (wounded and evacuated, 9 September).
  - **Equipment:** Two 5.9-inch guns shelling the camp 7 September; a large tunnel at Vis-en-Artois holding two battalions, 60 feet underground; a 6-inch howitzer of the battalion's own artillery firing short on 16 September; Lewis Gun carts and field kitchens moving with the battalion 28 September.
  - **Movements:** Gouves (1 September) → Arras by march route (2 September) → O.29 south of Vis-en-Artois (5 September), into the tunnel there (7–8 September) → Bn. H.Q. P.28.C.20.30 (12 September) → Advance H.Q. P.36.B.35.95 (13 September) → relieved by the London Scottish and 1st London Regiment, to bivouac near Cherisy (19 September) → Croisilles, Wanquentin, Simencourt (20 September) → Warlus (25 September) → Wanquentin, Croisilles, Boyelles, U.23.D near Reincourt (26 September) → Inchy Cross, H.Q. D.4.d.4.5 (27 September) → west of Bourlon Woods, E.11b (28 September) → assembly area R4.F.9.C.70.30 (29 September), in reserve before Cambrai (29–30 September).
  - **Discipline:** None recorded in September 1918.
  - **Casualties:** Operations of 26–28 August, reported 3 September: killed 1 officer and 10 O.Rs.; died of wounds 1 O.R.; wounded 8 officers and 277 O.Rs.; wounded at duty 2 officers and 2 O.Rs. September: 7 September, Pte. Lancaster wounded; 8 September, Cpl. Richardson (B. Coy.) killed; 9 September, Cpl. Richardson (Scout Sect.) died of wounds, Pte. Sims wounded; 11 September, 4 men wounded and 1 horse killed and 2 horses wounded in shelling on the move; 15 September, the Padre Capt. Nicholson gassed and evacuated, the M.O. and Lieut. Cooks shaken up and gassed; 16 September, 18 men missing (believed wounded or killed) after the Crater fighting, and 2 men killed and 8 wounded by the battalion's own 6-inch howitzer firing short; tour casualties to 19 September: 1 officer wounded, 1 do. gassed, 4 O.Rs. killed, 24 do. wounded, 15 do. missing, 1 O.R. missing believed prisoner, 2 do do do wounded; 26 September, 2 men killed and 8 wounded and 2 horses killed by a direct hit on the transport.
  - **Anomalies:** (1) No entry for 17 September; entries jump from 16 to 18 September. (2) The 13 September entry says the 1st C.M.R. Bn. would make good the Outpost Resistance Line "at 12.30 a.m. the 14/9/18". (3) Two men named Richardson are recorded: Cpl. Richardson, B. Coy., killed by shell fire 8 September and buried by Capt. Stewart; and Cpl. Richardson, Scout Sect., who died of wounds in No. 8 Field Ambulance 9 September and was buried by Capt. (5) The cover gives the volume as "VOLUME 36" (handwritten), while the contents sheet gives "Volume 18 – 9". (6) The 16 September entry breaks off mid-sentence at the foot of page 9 ("At 10.00 p.m. [uncertain]") and resumes on page 10 with Capt. Craig reporting for liaison. (7) The 26 September entry ends with "2.00 a.m." after the map reference U.23.D.70.00; it is not clear whether this is the time the battalion reached the new area. (8) The place names "BOURLOON WOOD" (27 September) and "BOURLON WOODS" (28 September) are as written.
