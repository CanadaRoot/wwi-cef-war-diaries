## Page 3 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118)

Heading: WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. The page is numbered "27" at the top right.

Place column: "Hill 63" throughout.

### Dec 1/15

> Enemy quiet during the night. Day broke cold with rain. Heavy bombardment of German lines by our artillery at noon, which was reported as effective. [uncertain] Work in trenches consisted of repairing parapets, constructing a drying shed, and draining. Some enemy shells dropped near Regimental H.Q. at Hill 63, without any damage to men of 5th C.M.R., but one man of 4th C.M.R. was killed and four injured. Orders received for relief of Regiment. Movement was carried out, the disposition being as follows: Headquarters Staff and A Sqdn at Grande Munque Farm, B Sqdn. at [uncertain] and C Sqdn at Hill 63. Regimental Grenade Company at English Farm.

### 2

> Conference of Officers in the morning. [uncertain] twelve days in trenches [uncertain] over by O.C. and various points discussed. Squadrons supplied working parties in morning and afternoon. Weather misty.

### 3

> Squadrons supplied working parties as on previous day. Weather wet and misty.

### 4

> Squadrons supplied working parties as before. Weather again wet.

### 5 (Sunday)

> Heavy firing of our artillery during night. Morning broke bright and sunny. Communion service held at Grande Munque Farm.

### Dec 22/15 (contd)

> The undermentioned report has been received from Lieutenant E. Chown, O.C. Regimental Bombers:- About 1 a.m. on the night of Dec 19/20 an advanced listening post of the 4th C.M.R. was bombed by a German patrol. German was killed. The men being nervous after the incident, Lieutenants Mar[uncertain] H.H. C.M.R. asked for a bomber to accompany [uncertain] the outlying listening post. No 110033 Pte J. R. Beckett one of the 5th C.M.R. grenadiers who were on duty with the 4th C.M.R. said he would go. Men were also called for to bring in the body of the dead man. There was no response, until Beckett came forward saying he would look for him. At the listening post the two men of the 4th C.M.R. waited while Beckett went out 40 yards in front to the advanced listening post to look for the dead man. After a search of half an hour Beckett found the body and carried it in to the listening post. From there only did the two men of the 4th C.M.R. help bring in the body through the wire and over the parapet.

### Dec 30/15

> Weather bright and chilly. Squadrons carried on independently.

### 31

> Weather bright and slightly colder. At 10 a.m. all the Officers of the 1st and 2nd C.M.R. Brigades met the Corps Commander, Lt-General Alderson, at Ploegsteert, and were informed that it had been deemed advisable for the 1st and 2nd C.M.R. Brigades to adopt infantry formations, their present formation being entirely unsuitable to the work required on this front. In accordance with this the 1st and 2nd C.M.R. Brigades are to be brought up to the strength of two Infantry Battalions. The present 3rd Regiment C.M.R. being divided and allotted to the 1st and 2nd C.M.R. Regiments, and forming the 1st and 2nd Battalions (C.M.R.) The 6th Regiment C.M.R. being used in like manner to re-inforce the 4th and 5th Regiments C.M.R., which are to form respectively the 4th and 5th Battalions (C.M.R.) the whole to form respectively the 8th Infantry Brigade (C.M.R.) under the Command of Brigadier General T. A. S. Williams. The following valedictory address from Colonel C. A. Smart speaks for itself:- To Officers, N.C.O.'s and of the 2nd Brigade C.M.R. It having been deemed advisable by the G.O.C. Canadian Army Corps to convert the two Mounted Rifle Brigades into Infantry
>
> P.T.O.

### Dec 31/15 (continued)

> formations, orders have now been issued incorporating the 2nd Brigade Canadian Mounted Rifles in the new 8th Brigade which will be known as the 8th Mounted Rifles Brigade, and will be commanded by Brigadier General T. A. S. Williams. In transferring my Brigade to the larger formation I do so with a great [uncertain] pride in its efficiency and discipline, but having assured Brigadier General Williams that he will receive from all ranks the same loyal and hearty devotion to duty that has been accorded to me since I took command of the 2nd Brigade nearly a year ago. I part from you with feelings of deepest regret but in the exigencies of military life on active service, such changes frequently occur, and we all must subordinate our own personal views and wishes to that of our superior officers, and remember the first duty of a soldier is to obey. Only those who have learned to obey are fit to command. "I shall look back on the time I have served with you as the happiest and proudest time of my military career, and nothing will give me greater pleasure than to hear later on from my old friend Brigadier General Williams, that my recommendation of you in every way justified. H. C. A. Smart, [uncertain] and men, serve your new leader and the Officers [uncertain] over you with true soldierly devotion, set yourselves diligently to acquire the new drill that is laid down for your instruction and in all ways play the game.
>
> P.T.O.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 December 1915 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text pages 11", which matches the eleven diary-text pages (pages 3–13).
- **Officers named:** Canon Scott of St Matthews, Quebec City (communion service, 5 December); Canon Almond of Trinity Church, Montreal (divine service, 19 December); Brigadier General Mercer, O.C. Corps Troops (present at divine service, 19 December); Colonel C. A. Smart, Brigadier, 2nd Brigade C.M.R. (Christmas address, 25 December; valedictory address, 31 December); Colonel J. R. Baker, O.C. (Christmas dinner, 25 December); Lt-General Alderson, Corps Commander (announced the conversion to infantry, 31 December); Brigadier General T. A. S. Williams (to command the new 8th Mounted Rifles Brigade); Lieutenant E. Chown, O.C. Regimental Bombers (report on the night of 19/20 December); Lieutenant Cotton (absent sick from the officers' dinner, 24 December); Major [uncertain] (also absent sick, 24 December); Major Eaton, R.D.R. (to supervise infantry training); Captain McGillivray (instructor); Lieutenant Logan, R.D.R. (instructor). The Adjutant signs page 13, name not clearly legible [uncertain].
- **Other ranks named:** Pte J. R. Beckett No 110033 (recovered a dead German's body from in front of an advanced listening post on the night of 19/20 December); Pte O'Heile (tried by Field General Court Martial for sleeping in a listening post, 15 December); Arm. Sgt. Smith (reported at Bailleul for a two-month course, 14 December). Parade state on 14 December: 26 officers and 529 other ranks (not including 1 officer and 30 other ranks in hospital).
- **Movements:** The regiment was relieved from Hill 63 on 1 December and dispersed to Grande Munque Farm, [uncertain] and Hill 63; moved to Aldershot Huts on 5 December; moved back to billets near Meteren on 9 December; shifted billets again on 11 December; Regimental Headquarters moved to fresh billets south of Meteren on 21 December.
- **Casualties:** One man of the 4th C.M.R. was killed and four injured by shells near Regimental Headquarters at Hill 63 on 1 December (no 5th C.M.R. casualties). No 5th C.M.R. battle casualties are recorded in December. A German patrol bombed an advanced listening post of the 4th C.M.R. on the night of 19/20 December, killing one German.
- **Action:** A gas attack on part of the line north of Meteren in the early hours of 19 December, traces of gas faintly noticed by the regiment. Trench repair working parties at Aldershot Huts (6–7 December). Visits to the Kemmel defences, Toronto area, on 17–18 and 20 December.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The 31 December entry is the most important in the volume: the 1st and 2nd C.M.R. Brigades are to convert to infantry, forming the 8th Infantry Brigade (C.M.R.) under Brigadier General T. A. S. Williams, with the 5th Regiment C.M.R. becoming the 5th Battalion (C.M.R.); Colonel C. A. Smart's full valedictory address is copied into the diary across pages 11–13. (2) The diary's unit heading changes across the volumes: "5th Regt. C. M. R., 2nd Canadian Division" (Volume I), "5th Regiment, Canadian Mounted Rifles" (Volume II), "5th C. M. R. Regiment, Canadian Corps" (Volume III). (3) The entry for 27 December ends mid-sentence with the word "Three" [uncertain]. (4) The Christmas Day entry records the men being relieved of all but absolutely necessary duties, communion services at Bailleul, and a Christmas meal for every man.
