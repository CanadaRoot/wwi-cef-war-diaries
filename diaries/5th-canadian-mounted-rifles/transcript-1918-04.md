### 1 April — Cellars Camp

> Weather fine: A.M. and P.M. the Battn. carried on with "Practice Contact with enemy Patrols in Co's — Practice of Platoon in Counter Attack in semi-open warfare — Musketry, Fire Orders — Fire Control — Care of Arms in Trenches — Platoon Organization and Post Repeater Drill the Battn. being done when marching to and from training area."
>
> About 4:30 P.M. an enemy aeroplane came over and attacked our Balloons, four of which were brought down. Our A. A. Guns and Machine Guns opened fire on him but apparently with no effect. None of our planes were visible in the air at the time. Orders received from 8th C. I. Bde. saying that our transport would move to BERTHOUVAL FARM area. F'd move to be completed by noon 2/4/18.

### 8 April — Front Line (continued)

> Weather dark and showery, visibility poor. Enemy Artillery more active than usual around the forward Area 3 direct hits were made in HUDSON TRENCH. A very heavy fog came down about 7.00 P.M. Warning order received stating the 5th Cdn. Mounted Rifles Battalion would be relieved by the 2nd Cdn. Mounted Rifles Batt'n on the night of the 10/11th April 1918. Defensive patrols covered our frontage throughout the night.

### 9 April — Front Line

> Weather dark with a very heavy fog all the morning which lifted about noon but visibility was very poor. Enemy Artillery quiet until 4.00 P.M. when he opened up with various calibres along Vancouver Road and Communication Trenches T.28.a & b. and repeated at intervals until 6.00 P.M. There were a considerable number of Gas Shells especially along VANCOUVER ROAD.

### 9 April — Front Line (continued)

> At 6.30 P.M. his Guns opened up again and specially concentrated on the BEEHIVE defences T.27. VANCOUVER Dump Vicinity T.28.C and all the Trenches leading back from Canada between B.45.10.90 to B.4.60.50. The rest of the Area not being subjected to such a severe bombardment. A large number of Gas Shells were thrown at us during the bombardment. About 9.30 P.M. everything became normal again and our men commenced to clean out the trenches as they were pretty well knocked about. Our Communications were cut with "A" "B" and "D" Companies all this time, as many as 28 breaks being found in our line. Our Casualties during this bombardment were very light, One Killed, two wounded one gassed and one Concussion, this latter being buried very badly. At 10.00 P.M. an enemy patrol worked down ANTELOPE ALLEY toward our block at T.28.d.50.10.

### 11 April — Support Line

> Weather Fine Visibility Good. At 2.00 A.M. Warning Order was received from the 8th C.I.B. that the 5th C.M.R. Battalion were to be relieved that night by the 102nd Cdn. Inf. Batt'n and after relief would move back to CELLARS CAMP, NEUVILLE ST. VAAST. At 6.00 A.M. Orders received and we issued operation Orders accordingly. At 2.00 P.M. we received word that the 87th Cdn. Inf. Battalion would relieve us instead of the 102nd Battalion. Considering that the night was very dark we had a good relief, with the exception of "B" Company, which had one Platoon relieved which went off without reporting. Enemy Artillery of all calibres mixed with a number of Gas Shells were very active throughout relief. Relief was not completed until 1.15 A.M. the 12th Inst.

### 23 April — Front Line

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Diary entries run 1–30 April 1918 with no date gaps. Note the diary text skips 4 April as a dated entry: the 3 April entry ends on page 6 and the next dated entry is 5 April; 4 April has no separate entry (the battalion was in Cellars Camp that day). The contents sheet states "Diary Text 30 Pages", which matches the 30 diary-text pages (pages 4–33). Appendix A holds Operation Orders Nos. 105–114 (pages 34–44); there is no Operation Order No. 111 in the appendix — the contents list likewise jumps from C.C.110 to C.C.112.
- **Officers named:** Lt J. Adamson (A/Adjutant; signs the operation orders); Lt A. W. Logie (guides officer, O.O. 107 and 112); Lt Lyle (guides, O.O. 108); Lt Walton and Lt Carter (copy list, O.O. 108); Capt J. E. Wilson, Lt L. J. Young and Lt A. E. Gillis (Military Cross, 18 March raid); Capt Stewart, 1st Cdn. Mounted Rifles Battalion (chaplain, officiated 28 April); [uncertain] Nicholson, R.C. Chaplain of the unit (Mass, 28 April); Lt Atto (intelligence officer); the G.O.C. 3rd Canadian Division (visits 6 and 27 April, unnamed). The diary is signed on 30 April by "[uncertain] Major, A/O.C. 5 C.M.R. Bn".
- **Other ranks named:** Military Medal recipients for the 18 March 1918 raid (8 April): Sgt W. Mann (709956), Pte J. Cusack (110117), Sgt C. H. Simpson (415344), Pte L. Shaw (748147), Sgt T. Louis (409813), Cpl M. Fleiser (433874), L/Cpl W. Anderson (111601), Pte F. R. Dewhurst (110103), Pte W. B. Stenson (709728), and Pte J. [uncertain] Worthy (attached 8th Cdn. Inf. Bde.). Distinguished Conduct Medal: Sgt A. Maron (415964) and Sgt J. Hunter (408569). Pte J. Dessureault (243073, "B" Company) was shot dead 19 April by a private of the 52nd Cdn. Inf. Battalion who mistook him for a German.
- **Movements:** 1–4 April training at Cellars Camp (Neuville St. Vaast area); night 5/6 April relieved the 58th Canadian Infantry Battalion in the right front line, Acheville sector; 7 April extended left flank in Montreal Trench; night 10/11 April relieved by 2nd C.M.R. Battalion into support; night 11/12 April relieved in support by the 87th (diary text) / 22nd Cdn. Inf. Battalion (O.O. 108) and back to Cellars Camp; 12–13 April entrained at Territorial Siding for the Hill 70 sector, relieving the 5th Leicester Regiment in support (relief complete 5.00 a.m. 13 April); 18/19 April moved into the Hill 70 front line, relieving 1st C.M.R. Battalion; night 24/25 April relieved by 1st C.M.R. Battalion into divisional reserve at Les Brebis; 30 April relieved at Les Brebis by the 7th Northamptons (72 Brigade, 24th Division, Imperial) and marched to Gouy Servins, arriving 10.00 p.m., with orders to march to the Dival area on 1 May.
- **Casualties:** 9 April bombardment: 1 killed, 2 wounded, 1 gassed, 1 concussion (buried). 19 April: Pte J. Dessureault shot dead (friendly fire). 21 April: 2 killed, 6 wounded. Tour totals to 25 April: 2 other ranks killed in action, 2 died of wounds, 10 wounded. No casualties on the reliefs of 10/11, 11/12 or 12/13 April.
- **Battles/actions:** Night patrols and sniping throughout the Acheville and Hill 70 tours; enemy raid attempt repulsed on Saskatchewan Road 9 April; the 11th Canadian Infantry Brigade raid on the right flank captured 10 prisoners (10 April); the 1st and 4th C.M.R. Battalions' raid on the night of 21/22 April captured 5 prisoners and a machine gun (1st C.M.R. 3 prisoners, 4th C.M.R. 2 prisoners and the machine gun; 4th C.M.R. lost 3 men presumed prisoners). German spring-offensive context: on 18 April a warning of an expected attack from Givenchy to Robecq north of the La Bassée Canal put the battalion on stand-to at 4.30 a.m.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The contents sheet (page 2) dates C.C.105 to the relief on the night of 3rd/4th April, but both the diary text and O.O. 105 place the relief on the night of 5th/6th April. (2) The 11 April diary text says the battalion was to be relieved by the 102nd Cdn. Inf. Battalion, then that the 87th Cdn. Inf. Battalion relieved it instead; O.O. 108 (11 April) names the 22nd Cdn. Inf. Battalion as the relieving unit. (3) The cover reads "VOLUME 31" with a "27" written beneath it, apparently a correction. (4) Pages 39–40 (O.O. 109) are faint and partly illegible. (5) The 4 April date has no diary entry. (6) The 16 April entry says the enemy put "about 40" around Battalion H.Q. without naming what was put (shells implied).
