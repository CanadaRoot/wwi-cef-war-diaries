### 1 May 1917 — Quarries Line

> Weather warm and bright. Left VILLERS AU BOIS at 3.30 P.M. and moved up to QUARRIES AREA with Battalion H.Q. in old dugout at S.27.A.3.5. Companies in EMPIRE SHELTERS. Arrived at 6.00 P.M. (See G.O. No 22)
>
> Working Parties today: 125 men and full complement of Officers each from "A" "B" "C" and "D" Coy's, to work on roads forward of NEUVILLE ST. VAAST. Parties returned at 7.00 P.M. without Casualties.
>
> 10.00 P.M. Heavy Barrage fire South and South East of here lasting until 10.00 P.M.

### 2 May 1917 — Quarries Line

> Weather warm and bright. Heavy artillery fire throughout night and becoming intense between 4.30 a.m. and 5.30 a.m. Working parties on roads today, returned without Casualties.
>
> Tomorrow morning at 4.30 a.m. the 2nd and 1st Divisions and the 3rd BRITISH DIVISION (XIII Corps) are attacking. Also 3rd Division — less V.19.2. — FRESNOY — C.1.8 and D. — OPPY. [uncertain] 1st and 2nd C.M.R. Bns. to be in Corps Reserve, 4th and 5th C.M.R. Bns to carry on with working parties.
>
> Heavy barrage, apparently, similar to last night.

No place or date column entry on this page; it continues the 5 May entry with a message from the Corps Commander.

> Following from Corps Commander.
>
> The brilliant operations during the last month, culminating in the capture of ARLEUX and FRESNOY, seem to give me the opportunity of expressing to all ranks the pride I feel in commanding the CANADIAN CORPS.
>
> "Since the 9th April when the offensive against VIMY RIDGE began, till the morning of May 3rd. when FRESNOY was captured and consolidated it has been one series of successes only, obtained by troops whose courage, discipline and initiative stand pre-eminent. NINE villages have passed into our hands. EIGHT GERMAN DIVISIONS have been met and defeated. Over 5000 prisoners have been captured and [uncertain] some 64 Guns and Howitzers, 106 Trench Mortars and 176 Machine Guns are now the trophies of the Canadians.
>
> The training undergone during the winter has borne
>
> P.T.O.

### 7 May 1917 — Vimy Defences (continued)

> Our barrage caught his rear waves and broke them up. A thin front line reached the slopes in front of left Battn. but was wiped out before it reached the line. Our planes numerous and very active. ### 11 May 1917 (continued)

> 5.30 P.M. O.C. our left Coy. reported that the Coy. of the 85th Bn. on our immediate left had withdrawn his entire command into the support and communication trenches, leaving our left flank "in the air". In view of the situation thus created, the O.C. ordered "C" Coy. (Major PEARKE) to establish a defensive flank on the left and had the artillery groups stand to. Brigade was at once notified and the O.C. 85th Bn. advised and asked for an explanation. The position was eventually restored at 6.00 P.M., the 85th re-occupying their front line and support trenches. The movement caused by dispersion, most of which could be seen clearly observed from enemy lines brought on heavy enemy shelling during which our front line and communication trenches received several direct hits — 2 men wounded, 1 killed. ## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 May 1917 with no date gaps. The diary text occupies 16 sheets (printed diary pages 3–18, file pages 4–19), including the Corps Commander's message (diary pages 6–7) and two disposition reports (6 May and 28 May, the latter as Appendix "I"). All nine appendices A–I listed on the contents sheet are present. No pages in the range are missing; no duplicate pages.
- **Officers named:** Lt.-Col. Sam Sharpe (O.C. 116th Cdn. Bn., relieved 5 May); Lt.-Gen. J. Byng (Commanding Cdn. Corps; his 3 May message is copied into the diary); Lt.-Col. Jas. Kirkcaldy (O.C. 78th Bn., Brandon, Man.); Brig.-Gen. J. H. McBrien (12th C.I.B.); Major McLeod (42nd Cdn. Bn., 7th C.I.B.); Major Pearke (O.C. "C" Coy, 5th C.M.R. Bn.; author of the 11 May report on the 85th Bn. withdrawal); Major Rowlands (85th Bn.); Brig.-Gen. Odlum (11th C.I.B.); Major J. R. Wilson (Reserve Company, late 60th Cdn. Inf. Bn.); Major Walker (Camp Commandant, Quarries area); Lieut. E. W. Hall ("B" Coy, missing off patrol 11/12 May); Lieut. Atto (advance party, 27 May); Lieut. Lalarme (A/Adjutant; signs Operation Orders No. 27 of 19 and 26 May — the Christian-name initial is not clearly legible); an unnamed Major, Adjutant, signs Operation Orders Nos. 22, 24, 25 and 26; the diary itself is signed by the O.C., Lieut.-Colonel, 5th C.M.R. Battalion, whose signature is not clearly legible.
- **Other ranks named:** None named in May 1917. A Reserve Company "lately absorbed from the 60th Canadian Infantry Battalion" is noted in Operation Orders Nos. 22 and 27.
- **Equipment:** Lewis guns (3 per company in the 6 May disposition report; Lewis guns in trench at regular intervals, 28 May); Stokes gun detachments and bombers under O.C. H.Q. Details for the 10/11 May move; 17th Tunnelling Coy. building dug-outs and an M.G. emplacement in Chaudiere Trench; G.S. limbers, Officers' Mess Cart and Medical Cart in the transport arrangements; steel helmets and 24 hours' rations for moves; pack horses bringing rations to Sunken Road S.29.B.8.8.
- **Movements:** 1 May — from Villers-au-Bois billets to Empire Shelters, Quarries Area (Bn. H.Q. at S.27.A.3.5). 5/6 May — relieved the 116th Cdn. Bn. in the Vimy defences (Bn. H.Q. S.18.D.6.2). 10/11 May — inter-battalion relief and re-adjustment of the 3rd/4th Cdn. Division boundary; the battalion took the Left Front (H.Q. T.15.A.2.7). 11/12 May — relieved by the 42nd Cdn. Bn., moved to Winnipeg Camp, Quarries Area, then 12 May back to Suburban Huts, Villers-au-Bois. 15–19 May — training at Villers-au-Bois. 20 May — relieved the 42nd Cdn. Bn. in Winnipeg Camp, Quarries Area. 27/28 May — relieved the 58th Cdn. Bn. in the Support Line (under 9th C.I.B. until night 28th).
- **Discipline:** The 11 May incident in which two platoons and 3 Lewis guns of the 85th Cdn. Bn. withdrew from the front-line trench on the battalion's immediate left without notice, leaving the flank "in the air"; Major Pearke's report (5.40 p.m.) and the battalion's defensive measures (temporary wire block, bombing section, Lewis guns standing to, patrol to the Lens–Arras Road) are copied in full. The position was restored by 6.00 p.m. No courts-martial or punishments are recorded.
- **Casualties:** 3 May — 1 casualty (working party). 5 May — 1 prisoner of war and 3 wounded during the relief. 6 May — 6 men wounded returning from a working party. 9 May — 2 men died of gas poisoning (one "from effect of Gas" during shelling, one in the working party) and 2 others wounded. 10 May — 1 casualty (working party, night 9/10 May); 3 men wounded on completion of the 10/11 May relief. 11 May — 2 men wounded, 1 killed during the shelling that followed the 85th Bn. withdrawal; Lieut. E. W. Hall missing off patrol. 24 May — 9 casualties; 25 May — 7 casualties; 26 May — 2 casualties; 28 May — 1 casualty; 30 May — 3 casualties; 31 May — 1 casualty (at duty). Total as stated in the diary: 2 killed (1 by shelling 11 May; Lieut. Hall missing), 2 died of gas, 1 prisoner of war, and about 40 wounded/casualties on working parties — the diary does not always distinguish killed from wounded in its daily casualty totals.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The contents sheet states "Diary Text 15 Pages", but 16 diary-text sheets (printed pages 3–18) are present. (2) The contents sheet entry for Appendix "B" appears to read "11th Cdn. Bn.", while Operation Order No. 24 itself (Appendix "B") and the diary name the 116th Cdn. Bn. (3) Appendices "G" and "H" are both headed "Operation Order No. 27" (dated 19 May and 26 May respectively). (4) Operation Order No. 26, para. 4, repeats the code-phrase clause twice ("to this Headquarters by the code phrase 'RATIONS ARRIVED AT.....'") — the repetition is in the original. (5) The 1 May entry gives a barrage beginning and ending at 10.00 p.m.; the 8 May entry gives two times for the same shelling ("9.00 P.M. at 2.5 P.M."); both are as written. (6) The 5 May entry ends mid-sentence ("without notifying") and its continuation (the Corps Commander's message) occupies diary pages 6–7; the 5 May relief narrative resumes on diary page 9. (7) The disposition-report tables (6 May and 28 May) have several strength-column headings and figures that are not clearly legible; the 6 May totals (25 officers, 824 O.Rs) do not reconcile exactly with the column figures as read. (8) Spelling in the original is preserved, including "Baloons" (7 May), "reconnoitre", "FRESNOY", "ACHEVILLE-FRESNOY", "Cons." for the 72nd (10 May), and "GOVY SERVING" (Appendix "A", para. 7). (9) The diary is handwritten (Army Form C. 2118) except for the typed contents sheet and typed appendices; the remarks-column initials on the diary pages and the signatures on the orders and maps are mostly not clearly legible. (10) The 11 May entry records the 72nd Bn. being relieved by the 85th Bn. and, in the same entry, the 85th Bn. withdrawing from the line — the two statements are as written.
