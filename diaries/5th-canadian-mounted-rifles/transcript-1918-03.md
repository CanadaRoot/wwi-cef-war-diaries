> Place: ZOLLERN HOUSE and FRONT LINE — Date 1918 March 1st
>
> Weather Fine. A Patrol consisting of MAJOR WILSON and three Other Ranks from "B" Company, LIEUT GILLIS and eight Other Ranks from "D" Company, eight Other Ranks from Battalion Headquarters, LIEUT GIFFORD from "C" Company and one Other Rank from 2nd Canadian Mounted Rifles Battalion making a total of twenty three all ranks left our lines at 7.30 PM and worked along about 100 yards left of HULL ROAD up to about 100 yards from enemy wire where a covering was left. Then small party of all ranks (7 in all) worked their way to wire finding gap about 75 yards left of HULL ROAD, here the wire was carefully examined and found it poor, working their way further through, a party of about 12 of the Enemy were observed to leave their trench, after waiting till they got close, our party decided to withdraw to covering party where they remained for some time, finally returning

> OBJECTIVES. All objectives were gained and very light casualties were sustained in the Raided Area. The Raiding Troops met with feeble opposition About 35 Germans in all are known to have been killed. Apparently the area Raided was held by a very small garrison.
>
> PREPARATION AND ORGANIZATION. The preparation and organization for this Raid was carried out with extreme degree of thoroughness. Immediately the Raiding Scheme was accepted, volunteers were asked for, both Officers and O.Rs. Selection was then made by the Officer Commanding, and complete Raiding Party was assembled and kept together, encouraged to offer any detail required to be treated. Arrangements were made for the Raiders to be taken back to comfortable billets near Tape Trenches, which were an exact replica of the area to be raided. Raiders were thus made absolutely familiar with this area. Each component part of the Raiding Party was allotted a definite objective, according to the weapons with which they were armed. Practices were made as realistic as possible, using Live Ammunition Flares, Smoke Bombs, etc. Every member of the Raiding Party were encouraged to offer suggestions as to any part of the programme, all of which were most carefully considered. This system resulted in obtaining many useful suggestions and also served to stimulate interest and added to the keenness of the men taking part.
>
> RECONNAISSANCE. A Thorough reconnaissance was made of what is thought to be the most feasible part of the enemy lines opposite our Battalion Frontage to Raid. This reconnaissance revealed the fact that the enemy's wire at this point was weak and gaps were found into the Outpost Line, which was an important factor in determining the area to be Raided. The reconnaissance also established location of M.G. about 50 yards to the left of these gaps, this in addition to meeting patrol of about 15 men, which established the fact that the enemy's Outpost Line was manned. A Listening Post, which was kept with this area in order to note any change in the attitude of the enemy or strengthening of his defences.

- **The raid of 15 March 1918 (Mericourt sector):** Zero hour 5.30 a.m. Raiding force 5 officers and 151 other ranks (Raid H.Q. 2/23, No.1 Party 1/45, No.2 Party 1/39, No.3 Party 1/41) under Maj. T. D. Sneath M.C., across some 900 yards of No Man's Land. All objectives gained; about 35 Germans known killed; 19 prisoners captured from the 23rd R.I.R., including 1 Sergeant-Major; one captured machine gun (Pte. Elliott). Casualties: killed 1 officer (Maj. Sneath) + 4 O.Rs.; wounded 22 O.Rs. (the casualty table's wounded total does not reconcile with its printed columns — the Left Covering Party figure is overwritten/illegible); wounded at duty 4 O.Rs.; missing nil; stated as 13% of all ranks engaged. Maj. Sneath was killed by shell-fire at Raid H.Q. (T.11.a.18.95) at 5.45 a.m.; Capt. J. E. Wilson assumed command. Maj. Sneath, Lieut. K. I. Somerville and the 4 other ranks were buried with full military honours at Thelus Military Cemetery on 17 March. A 4th C.M.R. patrol clash on the night of 14/15 March (several wounded, one man missing, two dead Germans brought in) delayed the covering parties' departure by about 15 minutes.
- **Movements:** Relieved the 2nd C.M.R. in the front line, Mericourt Sector, night 3/4 March (completed 9.00 PM 3 March, no casualties); relieved in turn by the 2nd C.M.R. night 10/11 March, back to Left Support, HQ at Zollern House; relieved the 2nd C.M.R. again night 17/18 March; relieved by the 38th Canadian Infantry Battalion (diary) / 28th Canadian Infantry Battalion (Order 92) night 20/21 March, to Cellars Camp, Neuville St. Vaast; "Stand To" 26 March, cancelled 8.00 AM the same day; 100 reinforcements received 27 March (stated as from the "10th Mounted Canadian Battalion"); ordered forward 28 March (German Spring Offensive period): St Andrews Post / Ashford Post, then Ashford Post and Junction Redoubt, then overnight 28–29 March to the Green Line and Point du Jour Redoubt under the 56th Imperial Division / 167th Imperial Brigade, holding Point du Jour Redoubt to Railway Cutting; relieved by the 78th Canadian Infantry Battalion, relief completed 1.30 AM 30 March, back to Cellars Camp. Trench strength 28 March: 28 officers, 646 other ranks.
- **Casualties:** Raid (15 March): 1 officer + 4 O.Rs. killed, 22 O.Rs. wounded, 4 O.Rs. slightly wounded at duty. Tour casualties to 30 March: 2 Other Ranks killed and 9 Other Ranks wounded. Only 2 casualties on 28 March (one serious). No casualties on the 3 March or 10 March reliefs; the 6 March patrols explicitly suffered no casualties despite gas and phosphorus shelling.
- **Enemy activity:** Nightly patrols throughout; gas and phosphorus shells and flame projectors on the night of 5/6 March; repeated "hammering of iron" / pounding on metal and auto horns heard in the enemy line (6–7 March), later attributed to new construction near a house at T.5.C.00.45, demolished by 6-inch howitzers on 10 March.
- **Reinforcements:** 98 other ranks (French Canadians) joined 5 March; 100 other ranks joined 27 March.
- **Places:** Zollern House, Hull Road, Mericourt Road, Mericourt Sector, Villers-au-Bois, the Brickfields/Brick Piles, Toledo Trench, Teddy Gerrard, Thelus, Cellars Camp / Neuville St. Vaast, St Andrews Post, Ashford Post, Ridge Post, RoclinCourt, Plank Road, Lens–Arras Road, Junction Redoubt, Green Line, Point du Jour Redoubt, Railway Crossing/Cutting, Maroeuil (reference map), Chaudiere Spur, Gertie and Hayter Trenches, Victoria Dump, Zivy Siding.
