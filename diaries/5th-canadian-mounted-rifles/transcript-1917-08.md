### 21/8/17 — FRONT LINE

> [uncertain] attack launched plenty of [uncertain]. Heavy barrage. Enemy sending up large number of flares of all descriptions but this retaliation not heavy. Major J.S.E. TODD accidentally shot in the foot through no fault of his own. Spasmodic shelling throughout the day. Considerable enemy movement in vicinity of HURRY ROAD and Bois du HUIT. H.26.c. At 11-20 p.m. enemy threw over fifteen to twenty gas shells close to Battalion Hdqrts. Masks were quickly adjusted and only one man was slightly affected.

### 22/8/17 — FRONT LINE

> Situation during night was quiet little machine gun or artillery fire. Our snipers claim some hits. Considerable enemy movement observed. At 8-30 a.m. three enemy aeroplanes flying very low fired into our trenches and repeated this performance later during the morning. During the day enemy planes were very active flying low and appeared to be directing artillery fire which was fairly heavy on our communication trenches and support area, the planes continued to patrol our lines throughout the day Battalion Headquarters receiving special attention.

### 22/8/17 — FRONT LINE, continued

> At about 10.00 a.m. an enemy Pineapple Thrower (small trench mortar) which had been captured together with a considerable quantity of ammunition, was put into operation against the enemy's front line system of trenches on our immediate left flank by C.S.M. STEPHENSON of "D" Coy. This action furnished considerable amusement to the men of the company, as it was found easy to keep at least six bombs in the air at one time. About three hundred rounds in all were fired and the sound of explosion in the enemy's line clearly indicated good effect. This minor operation caused a good deal of comment by all ranks of the company and the Pineapple Thrower was finally christened "C.S.M. STEPHENSON'S Whiz-Bang Battery." The constant alertness of our snipers frequently firing on enemy movement caused him to move through openings quickly and apparently in a highly nervous state.

### 22/8/17 — FRONT LINE, continued

> Only one of our planes was seen during the whole of this time and no action occurred. At 5-15 p.m. very heavy explosions occurred in Loos at one of our trench mortar dumps. At 6-20 p.m. large explosions occurred in enemy lines at approximately 88° TB from OP, H.29.d.5.9 possibly from a similar cause. French attack in region of VERDUN over 11,000 prisoners so far reported. Italians on ISONZO front carried whole of Austrian first line between [uncertain] and upper ISONZO (30 miles) prisoners captured exceeding 10,000.

### 23/8/17 — FRONT LINE

> Weather fine and warm our guns very active during day and night. At 3.00 a.m. enemy started to shell our trenches heavily, in retaliation our guns put down a very heavy barrage for about one hour. Between 2.00 p.m. and 4.00 p.m. our Artillery did considerable registering and occasional shots were conducted over front line trenches principally in the vicinity of H.26.c.3.2 and H.25.d.4.4 and Bois-du-HUIT. Considerable movement heard in enemy front line at H.26.c.7.6 and H.26.c.2.9 enemy appears to be working on his new trench in this area.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 August 1917. There is no entry dated 3 August — the date column on diary page 1 jumps from 2nd to 4th. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 13 Pages", which matches the thirteen diary-text pages (pages 4–17), numbered (1) to (13) with page 11 numbered (7A). The month's appendices are A–I, plus two sketch maps and three distribution/disposition states.
- **Officers named:** Lt-Col D.C. Draper, D.S.O. (Commanding Officer; returns from leave 2 August and signs the diary 31 August); Major W. Rhoades, M.C. (Second in Command); Major T.D. Sneath, M.C. (promoted Temporary Major 27 August); Major J.S.E. Todd, M.C. (accidentally shot in the foot 21 August); Major H.L. Duggan; Major B.B. Morrill; Capt J.R. McLeod; Capt K.C. Campbell; Capt E.A. Robinson; Lieutenants H.G. Porter, J.J. Harold, J.R. Gray, H.F.C. Cooks, L.H. Millar, G.W. Carter, H.L. Atto, H.R. Gifford and T.S. Hall (all present at the 8 August dinner); Lieut W.J. Atherton, Lieut L.C. Eaton and Lieut M.O. Johnson (promoted Acting Captain 13 August, and Temporary Captain 27 August); Lieut O.W. Loggie (explored the enemy mine tunnel 25 August); Lieut-Col Johnston (2nd C.M.R., at the 17 August manoeuvre); Major Pearkes (Advance Guard commander, 17 August manoeuvre); Capt Johnson (advance party, Les Bresbis); Lieut Millar (guides, Brigade Headquarters); Lieut Barnes, G.R. and Lieut Duggan, H.L. (promoted Temporary Captain 27 August); Lieut (A/Major) Pearkes, G.R. (promoted Temporary Captain 27 August).
- **Other ranks named:** C.S.M. Stephenson of "D" Coy (fired the captured German "Pineapple Thrower", christened "C.S.M. Stephenson's Whiz-Bang Battery", 22 August); Pte Molyneaux of C Company (won the six-mile inter-battalion road race, 14 August); Sgt Loggie (escorted the packs lorry, 15 August); S/M James (rear party, Raimbert).
- **Casualties:** For the tour ending 27 August the diary totals: Killed 11 other ranks; Died of wounds 3 other ranks; Wounded 3 officers and 25 other ranks. Major J.S.E. Todd was accidentally shot in the foot on 21 August "through no fault of his own". One man was slightly affected by gas shells on the night of 21 August. Several civilian casualties were caused by scattered shells falling in Les Bresbis on 19 August (number not given).
- **Movements:** Raimbert 1–15 August (training, sports, rifle ranges); march to Gouy Servins 15 August; brigade tactical exercise (detached force) 17 August around Chateau-de-la-Haie, Bois de la Haie, Carency Ridge, Souchez Ridge and Mintopart; motor move to Les Bresbis 18 August; support line 19/20 August relieving the 14th Canadian Infantry Battalion; front line 20/21 August relieving the 3rd Canadian Infantry Battalion; close support 24/25 August, relieved by the 4th C.M.R.; relieved in support 25/26 August by the 1st Buffs, 1st K.S.L.I. and 2nd Yorks and Lancs; Les Bresbis 27 August; Bouvigny-Bouffelles 27–31 August.
- **Battles and operations:** 16 August — the 1st and 2nd Canadian Divisions attacked and captured the north-east outskirts of Lens. 17 August — the 8th Canadian Infantry Brigade detached-force manoeuvre: the "enemy" holding Chateau de la Haie turned out to be a billeted battalion wearing steel helmets; the final objective east of Mintopart was secured at 5.40 p.m. after an attack held up by strong opposition; 65 prisoners were taken in the exercise (11 Canadian Light Horse, 10 cyclists, 5 Machine Gun Brigade, 39 of the 133rd Battalion). 21–25 August — front-line tour in the Lens/Loos sector: gas shells, enemy aircraft flying low to direct artillery fire, a captured German Granatenwerfer ("Pineapple Thrower") turned on the enemy by C.S.M. Stephenson (about 300 rounds), about 150 German grenades on 25 August, and an enemy mine tunnel discovered at H.25.d.4.4 and explored by Lieut Loggie (galleries, charges, listening apparatus; all leads cut).
- **Sport:** The battalion won the Brigade Cup at the brigade sports at Ferfay on 12 August (54 points; 1st C.M.R. second with 35, 2nd C.M.R. third with 28, 4th C.M.R. last with 14); football beat the 2nd C.M.R. 4–3 and the 1st C.M.R. 2–0; baseball beat the Brigade team 13 runs to nil; tug-of-war beat the 4th C.M.R. "by eight runs to one". Platoon No. 10 won the Dumbbell Badge competition (160 marks) on 30 August.
- **Anomalies:** (1) No entry is dated 3 August; the date column jumps from 2nd to 4th. (2) The 20 August entry says "The 4th C.M.R. Battn relieving the 5th C.M.R. Battn" while the 5th relieved the 3rd in the front line — the 4th took over the 5th's support positions. (3) The contents sheet lists Appendix B as "C.O. No.1 dated 16.8.17" while the order itself is headed "Operation Order No. 11". (4) Appendix E numbers two paragraphs "4." (Guides, then Transport). (5) The 12 August entry has a word struck through after "among other". (6) The 21 August entry opens mid-sentence as written, and the 25 August entry breaks off mid-sentence at the foot of diary page 9 and continues on page 10. (7) The 22 August Isonzo sentence appears to be missing a place name between "between" and "and upper ISONZO". (8) The bearing in the 22 August entry is read as "88° TB from OP, H.29.d.5.9". (9) "HURDEL Trench" and "H.32.v2 Trench" in Appendix G, and "AULAIN ST. NAZAIRE" in the contents sheet versus "ABLAIN ST. NAZAIRE" in Appendix B itself, are transcribed as written. (10) Distribution-state strengths: State I (20/8/17) 23 officers / 652 other ranks, 675 all ranks; Report II (21/8/17) 23 officers / 654 other ranks; Report III (25-8-17) 20 officers / 539 other ranks.
