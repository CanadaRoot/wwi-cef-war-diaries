## Page 16 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

> gained, approximately another 600 yards of enemy trench and by 4.00 am we had cleared the Germans out of REGINA trench as far as R.23.A.0.6 our original [uncertain] objective. The trench here was deep and I considered it a suitable place to put in a block. Our casualties had been very heavy and it would have been unwise to push on any farther. 18 unwounded German prisoners had been taken and passed back and very heavy casualties inflicted on the enemy. Many had been caught by our machine gun fire as they retired on their support line. As no reinforcements were arriving, I set out myself to obtain them. 10 men were sent up HESSIAN trench and also the two platoons of the 2nd C.M.R. They attacked in overwhelming numbers and also endeavoured to outflank on our right. Our men held on with great gallantry until two of our machine guns had been put out of action and the supply of bombs exhausted. At 7 am the position became untenable and our men retiring in good order bringing the majority of wounded out. The reinforcements arrived a few minutes

> minutes too late to retake the trench which had been so stubbornly held. Took immediate steps to hold HESSIAN trench and by 8.35 am the situation was again normal.
>
> For the remainder of the day the front and support lines were subjected to heavy intermittent shelling. Every effort was made to bring in as many wounded as possible. At about 4.30 p.m a furious bombing attack started to the right of our sector. S.O.S signals were sent up but we could not tell whether the attack was being made by the enemy or the Battalion on our right. The bombing and rifle fire seemed to be spreading towards our front and I asked for a heavy barrage from our guns on the enemy front line and support. At the same time a small attack was made by the enemy on a sap in the neighbourhood of R.23.A.7.1. We had no men in this sap at the time. At about 10 p.m we were relieved by the Royal Canadian Regiment.
>
> All ranks displayed the greatest gallantry and devotion to duty. They were severely tried fighting continuously, often practically hand to hand for 24 hours against superior numbers. The artillery support was unfailing, prompt and accurate. The good work of the detachment of 1st & 2nd [uncertain] Batt. [uncertain] enabled the Battalion to secure the gains and hold out so long.
>
> Casualties 3 Officers 45 O.R. Killed. 1 Officer 1 O.R. died of wounds, 6 Officers 166 O.R. wounded.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 October 1916 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text — 32 Pages", matching the 32 diary-text pages (pages 3–34), plus Appendix A (page 35).
- **The 1 October attack on Regina Trench:** The diary opens with the full operation order for the 1 October attack (pages 3–7), then a continuous narrative of the day (pages 8–17). "A" Coy took its objective (Regina Trench, R.22.B.5.4 to R.22.B.4.3) and held about 100 yards of it through the night against counter-attacks, with both flanks in the air after the 4th C.M.R. Bn on the left and the 2nd Bn on the right were held up; "B" Coy was stopped by uncut wire on the right and fell back with heavy losses. A night bombing advance by 1st C.M.R. Bn (Capt. Bradbrook, under Lieut Perkes) cleared Regina Trench to R.23.A.0.6 by 4.00 am on 2 October, but a German counter-attack at about 7 am retook it; the battalion then held Hessian Trench. The battalion was relieved by the Royal Canadian Regiment at about 10 p.m. on 1 October and moved back to Albert on 2 October.
- **Casualties:** 1 October (as stated on page 17): 3 officers and 45 other ranks killed; 1 officer and 1 other rank died of wounds; 6 officers and 166 other ranks wounded. Lieut Campbell killed and Lieut Daubney wounded in the attack; Lieut Chauvin last seen leading "B" Coy forward (page 14); Capt. Beckwith wounded in Hessian Trench. 10 October: a 5.9 shell struck the Headquarters dug-out mouth at about 1.30 am, killing 2 men, wounding 3 and burying Col. Gascoigne (O.C. 60th Battalion) and others; the same day's shelling (5.55–6.20 p.m.) killed 2 and wounded 4; Lieut Gray wounded in the left leg by shrapnel. 14 October: Lieut James Gill MacLachlan killed by shell fire just as the relief was reported; buried at the cemetery near Pozières, Rev. Labonte officiating.
