### 1 — NORTH STEENVOORDE

> Weather cloudy. Morning- Battalion in practice of the attack and wood fighting. Corps Commander demonstrated with a company of 2nd C.M.R. Battalion how he wished Company attack to be carried out. O.C. and Company Commanders attended. Afternoon- Battalion in attack practised.

### 2

> Weather fine and warm. Morning- Battalion route march, in full marching order with packs. Fifty men of A Company and fifty men of B Coy attended ranges for musketry application. Afternoon- Brigade Sports. Our men pulled off a good share of the events.

### 3 (Sunday)

> Weather. Warm and showery. Church parades in morning. Captain Father Labonte officiated at Roman Catholic parade, and Captain Kephurn at Church of England parade. Afternoon- Company Musketry Parades.

### 4

> Weather, warm and showery. Companys carried on with bayonet fighting, musketry, practice of attack at Corps School during morning and afternoon.

### 5

> Weather, raining all day. Companys carried on as yesterday with addition of wiring instruction.

### 6

> Weather showery in morning clearing later. Companys carried on with practice of the attack. Afternoon- Orders received for Battalion to move with Division to the SOMME to take part in the offensive. (Program of move attached)

## Page 7 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued, diary p. 5

### 12 (continued)

> a Strong Point in the enemy lines situated on rising ground between the forks of the POZIERES and LA BOISELLE ROADS. Very deep dugouts going down 50 to 75 feet and in one case over 100 feet. Although only two miles away from the enemy front line the roads are filled with traffic of every kind, and all movement is in the open. Guns are not concealed, and guns and ammunition dumps are in every conceivable spot. Our airmen have absolute supremacy in the air thus making observation difficult for the enemy. An enemy plane came over our lines today but was immediately beaten down in flames by our airmen. Even enemy observations balloons dare not stay up very long as many of them have been fired by our airmen. Owing to the rolling nature of the ground, the vantage points being mostly in our hands, observation balloons of the enemy can only obtain local information.
>
> Twenty reinforcements arrived today.
>
> Battalion is to go into the line tomorrow evening and relieve 4th C.M.R Battalion in the Right Sub-Sector. The 10th C.M.R Battalion relieve the 2nd C.M.R Battalion in the Left Sub-Sector (MOUQUET FARM) at the same time.

## Page 8 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued, diary p. 6

### 12 (continued)

> We are to attack - in conjunction with a larger movement south of us, on the morning of 15th - our objective being the enemy line in front of our Sub-Sector and the 4th C.M.Rs Objective being MOUQUET FARM - which is very strong, with very deep dugouts. The Australians captured the FARM a week or so ago but failed to hold it, chiefly, it is believed on account of not "mopping up" thoroughly. Their two waves went over all right but the enemy emerged immediately, and turned machine guns on them in their rear.

### 15

> Preparatory to the attack the scouts patrolled No Mans Land along our entire front reporting that the enemy occupied their front line trenches, but had no saps out in front, and had no advanced Bombing Posts. Also that the ground was suitable for attack and there were no obstacles.
>
> At ZERO (6.20 am) an intense and effective barrage was opened, 50 yards short, of enemy front line trench.
>
> At 0.1. Barrage lifted on to German front line trench.
>
> At 0.4. Barrage lifted 100 yards and at 0.4 lifted another 100 yards till further orders.
>
> Previous to this, at about 2.00 AM, the first wave of the attacking force took up a position about 30 yards in front of our front line, and dug themselves in from view. The second wave also dug in from view on our front lines. Immediately our artillery opened up, the first wave moved forward followed one minute later by the second wave. The Barrage was followed very closely, the first wave suffering a few casualties from our own fire, the Bombing Officer LIEUT: William WILLISON being Killed.

## Page 15 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued, diary p. 13

### 15 (continued)

> one minute later by the second wave. The Barrage was followed very closely, the first wave suffering a few casualties from our own fire, the Bombing Officer LIEUT: William WILLISON being Killed.
>
> The men moved steadily forward at the walk. Immediately the barrage lifted off the enemy trench, the first wave went in and had the situation well in hand by the time the second wave arrived. All the Germans in the trench were either killed or wounded. Mopping up parties immediately proceeded to Bomb the dugouts inflicting many casualties, amongst whom were two Officers. The Bombing sections bombed down the communication trenches. Considerable opposition on the part of the enemys bombers was met with in the left communication trench, our first bombing section of the left company being practically wiped out. The second section which had been held in readiness for such a contingency, immediately continued the attack and very quickly overcame the enemys bombers, all of whom were killed. Enemy reinforcements who were on their way up retired in disorder. Our Bombers went on and put in a "Block" at R.28.d.4.4½ [uncertain]. Infantry posts were established to continue our line to this Block.

> For his consistent and skilful handling of his Company in attack on the morning 15th September, 1916. - **Movements:** Training at North Steenvoorde 1–6 September; entrained at Bayinghove 7 September and detrained at Candas 8 September; route march via Fienvillers, Bernaville and Ribeaucourt to Houdencourt (8 September), Montrelet (10 September), Vadencourt (11 September); route march via Warloy, Senlis and Bouzincourt to the Albert area and La Boisselle (12 September); into the front line at Cemetery/Front Line (14 September); relieved by the 9th Lancashire Fusiliers and back to the Brickfields, Albert (16–17 September); Senlis (18 September), Warloy (19 September), Bouzincourt (21 September, in Corps Reserve); route march via Brickfields to Tara Hill (27 September); Chalk Pits (28 September); back into the front line on the night of 30 September to attack Regina Trench the following day.
- **The attack of 15 September 1916:** Zero 6.20 a.m. "B" and "D" Companies attacked in two waves from R.34.b.9.7 to R.28.d.4.2 (reference map Ferme du Mouquet 1/5000), with "C" Coy consolidating and "A" Coy holding the old front line. About 500 yards of German trench were captured and consolidated by 11 a.m.; 20 prisoners were passed back; approximately 250 of the enemy were accounted for; three enemy machine guns were captured (two used against the enemy). The 4th C.M.R. Battalion attacked from the new trench at 6.30 p.m. the same day towards Mouquet Farm. The battalion was relieved by the 9th Lancashire Fusiliers on the night of 16–17 September.
- **Casualties:** 15 September attack, as tabulated on Page 20 — Officers: 3 killed, 4 wounded, 1 shell shock, total 11; Other ranks: 48 killed, 143 wounded, 4 died of wounds, 19 shell shock, 24 missing, total 268. In addition: 2 wounded on 13 September; 5 other ranks wounded and Capt Newson seriously wounded during the relief of 14 September; Lt Winslow and 12 men wounded on working parties on 25 September; 1 killed and 4 wounded in the relief of 28 September. The battalion's strength on marching out on 7 September was 37 officers and 890 other ranks; the 30 September distribution table totals 534 all ranks (510 rifles).
- **Other actions and events:** 26 September operations in support of the 1st and 2nd Canadian Divisions (Corps message quoted in full on Page 30: 14th Corps 1,000 yards east of Morval, troops entering Gueudecourt and in Combles, 50 officers and 1,500 other ranks prisoners, 40 machine guns). Working parties throughout 23–25 September on roads around Albert (Stone Siding, Corbie–Albert Road, Ovillers–Courcelette Road, Red Cross Flag on the Poziers Road). A battalion smoking concert on 22 September, the 800 litres of beer paid for from Mr C. B. Gordon of Montreal's $200 gift. Col Draper's dinner for the officers on 27 September in the Church Army hut.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The 15 September casualty table does not add up as written: the officers' rows sum to 8, not the stated total of 11, and the other ranks' rows sum to 238, not the stated 268; the figures are transcribed exactly as written. (2) The 7 September parade state likewise does not reconcile: the company breakdown sums to 34 officers and 837 other ranks against the stated 37 officers and 890 other ranks. (3) The 28 September distribution table does reconcile (547 rifles, 568 total). The 30 September table's Total column sums to the stated 534, but the per-row totals are computed on inconsistent bases (some count rifles plus Lewis guns only, others include signallers and stretcher-bearers). (4) Page 29 (26 September entry) has a large tear across the lower half of the sheet, obscuring the middle of the entry. (5) The diary-text sheets carry two paginations: a handwritten sequence 1–34 and a printed sequence 2–35; Appendix A is printed p. 36 and Appendix B pp. 37–45. (6) The sentence about the O.P. is split across Pages 13–14, with the word "officer" appearing at the foot of Page 13 and again at the head of Page 14. (7) In Appendix B, Pte Elmer James (123024) is given no surname, and the surname Colpitt is spelled "COLPITTS" in the Pickles citation but "COLPITT" in Colpitt's own entry. (8) The spelling "Companys" (for Companies) is as written throughout. (9) The 25 September orders for the Regina Trench operation (Pages 25–29) are copied in full, including the objectives for the 1st and 2nd Canadian Divisions, Lewis-gun post locations and divisional headquarters — the battalion itself was in Corps Reserve for that operation and took no part in the 26 September attack.
