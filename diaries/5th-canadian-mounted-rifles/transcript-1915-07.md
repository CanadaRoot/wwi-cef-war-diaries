> Beginning of two days tactical scheme. March off at 9 a.m. full marching order. Less blankets & rubber sheets which are carried on transports. 1 Field Kitchen borrowed from 4th Cavt. Scheme of attack & defence. 5th Regt to hold line running roughly from Lyminge to Roman Road through Rhodes Minnis. 4th & 6th Regts endeavour to break through above line towards Shorncliffe. 1st day ground allotted & bivouacs located. Outposts placed at dark remain in position all night

### 14

> Second day Tactical scheme. Advance of 4th & 6th Regts begins at 9 a.m. Result of days operations being that 4th Regt & 6th failed to break through [uncertain] line. This being due largely to proper retirement of 5th Regt & to absence of any sign of initiative, & delay on part of attackers, in their not pressing attacks upon tactically weak points, on 5th front, which owing to fog & [uncertain] conditions might easily have been penetrated by an aggressive assault.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** The 16 pages LAC labels "July 1915" are in fact the whole of Volume I of the diary, covering **29 July to 31 October 1915** with no date gaps: 29–31 July, all of August, all of September and 1–31 October. The contents sheet confirms "Diary Text pages 14", which matches the fourteen diary-text pages (pages 3–16). There are no August, September or October 1915 pages elsewhere in the LAC manifest for this file — the diary genuinely jumps from the end of Volume I (31 October 1915) to November 1915. The next volume begins with November 1915.
- **Officers named:** Capt Clifford, A.P.M., 1st Canadian Division (lecture on discipline, 11 August); Lt Gibson (lectures, 23–24 September); Lieut Harton (detailed for a course in general instruction, 21 August); Lieut W. J. [uncertain] (struck off strength to the Machine Gun School, 19 October); Lieut L. [uncertain] Green (appointed Regimental Machine Gun Officer, 19 October); Major General Steele (inspected the Brigade, 20 October); Lt General E. A. H. Alderson C.B., Corps Commander (inspected the regiment, 29 October); Rt. Hon. Mr Bonar Law, Colonial Secretary (inspected Canadian troops, 4 August); Captain [uncertain] (in charge of the transport trains to France, 22 October). The Adjutant signs page 16, name not clearly legible [uncertain].
- **Other ranks named:** Pte C. Lambert (District Court Martial, 112 days' detention for striking a superior officer, 26 August–1 September); Pte A. McCarthy [uncertain] No 110396 (Court of Enquiry, 16 September); Ptes N. O'Brien No 110431 and O. [uncertain] No 110396 (discharged medically unfit, 17 September); Pte Findlay No 110[uncertain] (transferred to [uncertain] Pay Office, 20 September); Q.M.S. [uncertain] No 110077, "B" Squadron (discharged to take a commission in the 11th Reserve Battalion, York & Lancaster Regiment, 21 September); Pte T. D. Anderson No 110011 (Court of Enquiry, 26 September); Pte J. D. Anderson No 110011, "A" Squadron (declared a deserter and struck off strength, 28 September); [uncertain] H. A. Lawley No 110231, "B" Squadron (sent to Casino Hospital, Boulogne, 25 October).
- **Movements:** The regiment arrived at Dibgate Camp, Shorncliffe, Kent, from Plymouth via London on 29 July 1915; moved camp from Dibgate to Caesars Camp, Shorncliffe, on 2 October; transport trains left Caesars Camp for France via Southampton on 22 October (9 officers and 122 men); the remainder sailed from Folkestone on 24 October, disembarked at Boulogne and marched to St Martins Camp; entrained at Boulogne on 26 October and arrived at Hazebrouck at 12.45 p.m., going into billets around Hazebrouck.
- **Casualties:** No battle casualties in this volume — the regiment was still in England. One civilian child died after a motor accident near the Cherry Garden Avenue gate to Caesars Camp on 18 October (a woman was also injured); the driver was blamed for excessive speed and loss of control. Pte T. L. [uncertain] was injured in a motor cycle accident on 10 September.
- **Discipline:** Pte C. Lambert, 112 days' detention for striking a superior officer and using threatening language (1 September); two men declared deserters (Ptes J. D. Anderson, 28 September, and W. B. Cameron, 7 October); courts of enquiry into illegal absence and kit deficiencies recur through August–October.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The LAC manifest labels all 16 pages "July 1915", but the pages contain the whole of Volume I, 29 July–31 October 1915 — the diary does not jump; the manifest label is misleading. (2) The date column on page 6 is headed with a month that reads like "Aug/15" although the entries are 1–10 September. (3) The entry for 23 September has a line crossed through. (4) The diary is entirely a training record in England until 24 October — no front-line action; the regiment only reaches France on 24–26 October. (5) German air raids / threatened Zeppelin raids forced lights-out at 7 p.m. on 13–15 October. (6) The handwriting on pages 3–16 is a single hurried hand; many proper names, especially farm names around Hazebrouck on pages 14–15, are not clearly legible and are marked [uncertain].
