Transcription conventions: original spelling, wording, dates and places are preserved. Anything illegible or uncertain is marked [uncertain]. The diary is typed (Army Form C. 2118); "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" in the form heading is struck through on the diary pages.

Note on the newspaper clippings (pages 10–34): the diary's contents sheet lists them as "News paper clipping of home coming of 5th C.M.R. Bn. in March, 1919." They are full pages and cuttings from the Sherbrooke Daily Record (19 and 22 March 1919) and the Montreal Daily Star (19 March 1919), several of them duplicated in the file. The battalion homecoming articles are transcribed as far as the small newsprint is legibly readable at the scan resolution; passages that cannot be read reliably are marked [uncertain] and are not guessed. Headlines, captions and the identifiable contents of the other (non-battalion) pages are summarised in the notes for each page; their small-type body text and advertisements are not reproduced verbatim.

### 6th — Bramshott

> PRESENTATION OF COLOURS. The Bn. paraded at 1130 hours. At twelve noon, Sir George Perley arrived accompanied by Mrs. Perley was met by the Divl. Commander, Major-Gen. LOOMIS, CMG. ## Page 6 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 7th — Bramshott (continued)

> 1000 hours Capt. BECKWITH, Lieut. DIBBLEE, DCM. Lieut. LOGGIE, MC., DCM. and 87 other ranks whose homes are in the Maritime Provinces paraded in front of Bn. Orderly Room and proceeded to "A" Wing.

### 8th — Bramshott

> The last Part II Orders were issued. All the officers and other ranks were struck off strength and the Bn. ceased to exist as a Unit in ENGLAND. The Bn. paraded in rear of Bn. Orderly Room at 0630 hours. Before moving off the Officer Commanding addressed the Bn.
>
> The Bn. moved off and entrained at LIPHOOK STATION at 0900 hours. LIVERPOOL was reached at 1910 hours. Arrangements for embarking were good and by 2000 hours, the men had been given tea and sandwiches and were aboard the "CARMANIA." The Divl. Commander Major-Gen. LOOMIS, CMG. DSO. and the Brigade Commander, Brig.-Gen. DRAPER, CMG., DSO. wished the officers and men farewell as they embarked.

### At Sea 9th

> A lovely day. We are in the Irish Sea, which is smooth. At 0930 hours Boat Parade. All ranks were shown the particular part of the ship they would parade in case of necessity. There are four Battalions aboard: P.P.C.L.I., 49th Bn. 4th C.M.R. and ourselves. In addition, we have the 9th Field Ambulance and small detachments from other Units of the Division.

## Notes

- The newspaper clippings in this file (pages 10–34) come from two papers: the Sherbrooke Daily Record of Saturday, March 22, 1919 (pages 10–17) and Wednesday, March 19, 1919 (pages 18–25), and the Montreal Daily Star of Wednesday, March 19, 1919 (pages 26–34). Several pages are duplicated in the file as differently cropped second scans: pages 18–19 (Record front page), 20–21 (Record page Two), 22–23 (Record want-advertisement page), 24–25 (Record page Ten), 30–31 and 34 (Star page Five), 32–33 (Star page Six).
- The Star's front-page article (page 27) says the battalion embarked "ONE sunny July day in 1917"; this is as printed, but the diary itself (page 8) records the battalion setting foot on Canadian soil again "after an absence of three years and eight months", i.e. it sailed in July 1915, and Appendix "A" (page 9) gives the battalion's original strength as 24 officers and 625 other ranks. The "1917" in the Star is therefore evidently a misprint.
- The Star's front-page article (page 27) says "twenty-six officers and 661 men" marched from Place Viger station and that "Of those 687 officers and men, but seventy-three can recall the sunlight on the river as they set forth to war"; Appendix "A" (page 9) gives strength on demobilization as 55 officers and 714 other ranks, of whom 13 officers and 87 other ranks were originals. The figures differ because the Star counts only those who marched in Montreal.
- The battalion's colours, presented at Bramshott on 6 March (page 5), were, according to the Star (page 29), to be placed "in all probability in the City Hall at Sherbrooke".
- The diary text ends on 17 March 1919 (page 8); demobilization was completed in Montreal on 18 March, and the homecoming was reported in the papers of 19 and 22 March.
- The small-type body text of several newspaper pages is only partly legible at the scan resolution; passages that could not be read reliably are marked [uncertain] and were not guessed.
