### 1st April 1917 — Woodman Camp

> Morning bright and clear. "B Coy. details returned from "forward" at 2.00 A.M. Afternoon dull and stormy with occasionally snow flurries. Usual artillery activity — NEUVILLE ST. VAAST receiving its share of Hun attention.
>
> HONORS AND REWARDS.
>
> 111472 Sgt. W. A. STEPHENSON.
> 111296 L/Cpl. C. McARTHUR.
> 124702 Pte. H. B. DONNELLY } MILITARY MEDAL D.R.O. 1652. 31/3/17.

### 2nd

> Cold night with frost. Morning dull and threatening. Afternoon rain sleet and heavy snow. Capt. McLEOD and Batman Cpl. ARNOLD left for ENGLAND on Senior Officers Course — 2½ months. More than usual artillery activity. Lieuts. GIFFORD (wounded 15-9-16) and LINDSAY (sick) reported back.

## Page 4 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 3rd April 1917

> Cold and blustery night. Morning bright and clear. Our artillery keeping up steady, continuous bombardment. Lieut. MATHEWS reported back from LEWIS Gun Course. Work continued on BIVVIES and constructing brush paths. Mud knee deep.

### 4th

> Wet night. Weather dull with occasional rain. Companies carried on independently with Arm Inspection, Musketry, Platoon and Coy drill and Bayonet fighting, etc. At 1.00 Noon Officers and N.C.O's attended a demonstration by the 172nd TUNNELLING Coy. in blowing a Tunnell trench (a number of which will be "blown" from our present front line into NO MAN'S LAND just prior to our attack on Vimy — preparations for which are being pushed with all possible dispatch. The weather conditions are abominable.
>

## Page 5 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 4th April 1917 (contd.)

> bombardment, day and night, of enemy's defences, strong points, dugouts, etc. The whole country around here is full of men and guns. Calibre of all Calibres up to 15 inch. 52nd Canadian Infantry Battalion pulled off a raid last night, securing one prisoner and inflicting a number of casualties. 30 Reinforcements (25 of which were old 5th C.M.R. men returned from Hospital on account of sickness and wounds) arrived today. Equipment Complete. Lieut. LINDSAY to Hospital. Enemy retaliation today practically nil.
>
> 8.00 P.M. Very heavy artillery fire, lasting half an hour to the East and North of here. No information as yet.

### 5th

> Artillery active throughout the night which was showery. Today is fine and bright with considerable ground haze making poor visibility. Lieut. T. S. HALL returned from "course" and proceeded to Brigade as Brigade Bombing Officer.

"P.T.O." at foot of page.

### 6th

> GOOD FRIDAY. Night showery, morning fine and bright. Afternoon showery, developing into a steady rain which lasted nearly all night of 7th. LIEUT. FARNSWORTH detailed to take charge of surplus kits, Baggage, etc. in CORPS SUB AREA. Heavy artillery bombardment developing at times into drum fire. The enemy retaliates on our front line, support and on MOUNT ST. ELOY, etc.

## Page 8 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 6th April 1917 (contd.)

> Y/Z night postponed 24 hours.

### 7th

> Morning fine but misty, clearing and bright sunshine. Our artillery has been pounding away all day. Enemy retaliation weak, but his aeroplanes very active. This RED PLANE makes rings around ours. One of our Balloons was brought down in flames this evening, its observers escaping by parachute. Preparations about complete for our move "forward" to-morrow night, preparatory to the attack on Vimy, which will probably take place early in the morning of the 9th.
>
> 11th Cdn. Inf. Bde. (3rd Div. 9th Bde.) relieved the 43rd Cdn. Inf. Bn. in DUMBELL CAMP (adjoining WOODMAN CAMP). 43rd Bn. going forward. "C" Company returned from PYLONES to WOODMAN CAMP tonight. Strength of Companies going forward (See appendix) Lieut. JONES, J.A. returned from Hospital. Two Battalion Runners sent up line yesterday, did not return. 1st C.M.R. Bn. have just wired that 2 have been killed, one wounded, and one shell-shock.

"P.T.O." at foot of page.

## Page 11 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 9th April 1917 (contd.)

> 2.30 A.M. "A" Company entered GOODMAN TUNNELL followed by "D" Company, and halted with head at BAY ST. 4th C.M.R. Bn. in Tunnel ahead.
>
> 4.30 A.M. "A" and "D" Coy's moved forward in Tunnel again and halted with head at P.S. line, "B" and "C" Coy's filing into Tunnel. Q H.Q. Details in PYLONES dugouts and shelters. Bn. H.Q. established in dugout at ROSS ST. at 5.27. D.6.4. with Brigade Advanced H.Q.
>
> 5.10 A.M. Capt. JOHNSON sick — Command of "B" Coy. assumed by Lieut. M. O. JOHNSON. Everything now ready for the attack. Guns which have been firing continuously throughout the night are now silent.
>
> 5.30 A.M. ZERO HOUR. Intense artillery bombardment — one continuous roar. The ground trembles and this is mingled with the roar of the guns. The whistling and screeching of the shell-filled air. 60 guns are covering their own advance, forming a "rolling barrage". Smoke and debris thrown up by the bursting shells give the appearance of a solid wall.

"P.T.O." at foot of page.

## Page 12 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 9th April 1917 (contd.)

> 5.50 A.M. "A" and "D" Companies commenced filing out of TUNNEL via ALBANY AVE entrance (as the P.S. LINE entrance had just been blown in) and took up a position in our old front and support trenches, "A" Coy in area of 1st C.M.R. Bn. and "D" Coy. in area of 4th C.M.R. Bn. (Captain JOHNSON having received somewhat assumed Command of "B" Company.)
>
> As "A" Coy. moved forward toward SWISCHEN STELLUNG they came under the fire of a party of about 40 HUNS who had evidently been hiding in dug-outs in the CRATER LINE whilst our barrage was on, and been overlooked by the 1st C.M.R. Battalion as they moved forward. These were immediately engaged and after a number had been killed, the remainder — about 23 — surrendered and were taken prisoners. "A" Coy. continued on and reached SWISCHEN STELLUNG at 7.00 A.M., coming under the orders of the O.C. 1st C.M.R. Bn. — The SWISCHEN was at this time very crowded — men of the 22nd, 24th and 26th Battns (22nd Cdn. Div) having lost direction and moved in. "A" Coy.

"P.T.O." at foot of page.

## Page 13 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 9th April 1917 (contd.)

> 9.00 A.M. Lieut. ATTO and six (6) Scouts followed the attacking troops of the 8th Cdn. Inf. Bde., locating them all and returned about 12.00 Noon. He gave the location of the attacking troops from personal observation, and indicated the best routes for "C" Coy. to follow in the event of their going forward to the attack.
>
> Four Battalion runners were then sent out with orders to get in touch with the H.Q. of "A" "B" and "D" Coys. 5th C.M.R. Bn. — details advance H.Q. of the 1st, 2nd and 4th C.M.R. Bns. This duty was performed very satisfactorily.
>
> At about 1.00 P.M. the O.C. 5th C.M.R. Bn. visited the advance H.Q. of the 1st, 2nd and 4th C.M.R. Bns. and the forward area where "A" "B" and "D" Coys. were in support.

### 9th April 1917 (contd.)

> was ordered by Lt.-Col. ANDROS of 1st C.M.R. Bn. to "dig in" about 200 yards in front of the SWISCHEN. This they did, under a heavy machine gun fire from the enemy which caused a number of casualties.

> 10.30 P.M. The Intelligence Officer (Capt. BRISCOE) killed outside entrance to the H.Q.

"P.T.O." at foot of page.

>
> 1.25 P.M. O.C. "A" Coy. (CAPT. ATHERTON) reports that under orders from O.C. 1st C.M.R. Bn. he has moved forward and re-inforced "B" Coy. 1st C.M.R. >
> 3.00 P.M. "B" Coy. ordered forward to clear up situation at STRONG POINTS on 1st C.M.R. front.
>
> 4.00 P.M. O.C. "B" and "D" Coys. each report about 25 casualties, enemy having shelled them heavily for an hour.
>
> 5.00 P.M. Capt. T. D. JOHNSON went out sick — Lieut. M. O. JOHNSON assumed command of "B" Company.

>
> 7.00 P.M. Majors ROSCOE and SNEATH reported from the rear.

### 10th

> Intermittent shelling throughout the night, which was very cold, with rain, sleet and snow. Stretcher bearers doing their work well, under great difficulties.
>
> 10.00 A.M. C.C.H.A. to bombard and cut wire on BLOATER, FLICKER and FLIRT Trenches. Bombardment to cease at 4.00 P.M. when 1st, 2nd and 4th C.M.R. ## Page 17 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 10th April 1917 (contd.)

> 2.00 P.M. Battalion H.Q. moved forward and established in SPANDAU HAUS (in PRINZ ARNOLF TUNNEL) with 1st C.M.R. Bn.
>
> 7.00 P.M. Patrols of 4th and 2nd C.M.R. Bns. report VIMY defences strongly held — patrols suffered considerable casualties from Rifle and Machine gun fire. Patrol of 1st C.M.R. Considerable shelling throughout the day which was mostly wet with high winds. Our artillery moving forward.
>
> 11.00 P.M. Orders received that the 116th Bn. (Lt.-Col. SHARPE, M.P.) is to relieve portions of the 9th Cdn. Inf. Bde. in the line tomorrow afternoon, 11-4-17, and that the 5th C.M.R. Bn., less one Company ("A" Coy. which is to be relieved with 1st C.M.R. Bn.) would relieve the troops of the 1st, 2nd and 4th C.M.R. Battalions in the SWISCHEN STELLUNG. H.Q. of 5th C.M.R. Bn. and 116th Bn. to be established together in SPANDAU HAUS, 60th Cdn. Inf. Bn. (Lt.-Col. GASCOIGNE) to be in support.

- **Movements:** 8–9 April — battalion moved from Woodman Camp via Berthonval Farm and Chassery Trench to Pylones and through Goodman Tunnel to the attack on Vimy Ridge; zero hour 5.30 A.M., 9 April. "A" Coy. (attached to 1st C.M.R. Bn.) reached the Zwischen Stellung at 7.00 A.M., 9 April; "B" and "D" Coys. moved up to the Zwischen Stellung the same morning; "C" Coy. and H.Q. into Goodman Tunnel. 11–12 April — relieved by the 52nd Cdn. Inf. Bn. and back in billets at Woodman Camp. 17 April — moved to the Zwischen Stellung, relieving the 43rd Cdn. Inf. Bn. 18–19 April — relieved the 52nd Cdn. Inf. Bn. in the Outpost Line (Bois de la Chaudiere / Vimy Station area, frontage about 4,000 yards). 21–22 April — relieved in the Outpost Line by the 4th C.M.R. Bn.; Battalion H.Q. to Quarries Line. 24 April — relieved by the 58th Cdn. Bn. and back to billets at Villers-au-Bois. 30 April — 11 officers and 225 other ranks of the 60th Cdn. Inf. Battn. (being broken up) taken on strength.
- **Strength:** Parade state 8 April (eve of Vimy): 27 officers, 1,004 other ranks (23 officers and 860 other ranks of the battalion, plus attached carrying, stretcher-bearer and engineer parties). Muster parade 27 April: 34 officers, 958 other ranks.
- **Honours and gallantry:** Military Medals to Sgt. W. A. Stephenson, L/Cpl. C. McArthur and Pte. H. B. Donnelly (1 April). A/Serjt. Demetlin's action on 9 April (took over an 8th M.G. Coy gun, bombed dugouts, captured 2 officers and 25 other ranks while wounded). Sergt. Critchlow's rescue of 12 boxes of S.A.A. under fire on 20 April. G.O.C. 3rd Cdn. Div. commended the battalion's patrols of the night of 21/22 April as "a most valuable reconnaissance".
