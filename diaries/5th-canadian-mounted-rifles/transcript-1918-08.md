### 1/8/18 — Bois de Bacouel

> Weather fine. Battalion remained in open all day, all ranks enjoying the camping out very much. Orders were received that the Battalion would move by march route to RUMAISNIL and NAMPS AU MONTS. Battalion moved off at 7.00 p.m., 'A' Coy. and Headquarters being billeted in latter place and 'B' 'C' and 'D' Coys. in the former.

### 2/8/18 — Namps au Monts and Rumaisnil

> Heavy Rain. Orders were received at 2.00 A.M. that Battalion would witness Tank Demonstration, two Coys. participating, at Bois De Bacouel, also that Battalion would vacate present Billets at 9.30 P.M. 2-8-18 and March to Bois De Hebecourt and bivouac there. At 12 noon Battalion with Lewis Gun Limbers proceeded to Demonstration, at 4.50 P.M. orders were received that Battalion would be Billeted in PLACHY BUYON. All Details proceeded direct to PLACHY BUYON at 5 P.M. Field Kitchens were sent direct to Battalion at Bois de BACOUEL.

### 2/8/18 — Plachy Buyon (continued)

> Tea was served to Men at 6 P.M. — No more Rations or Tea. Battalion Paraded at 8.45 P.M. Moved off at 9 P.M. Marched to PLACHY BUYON. In spite of the Rain and no Rations the spirits of the Men are splendid, upon arrival at PLACHY BUYON, Rations were in and Men were given a good supper which they appreciated.

### 3/8/18 — Plachy Buyon

> Weather Fine. Transport arrived in from the North at 4.15 A.M. Horses and Men were in good condition in spite of the long March. The Convoy only moved at night and were parked in Woods and etc, during the day. All traffic proceeding North moved by day. Battalion carried on with checking up equipment. Company Commanders Conference at 12.45 P.M. intimated that we must be prepared for any eventuality. Battalion moved off at 9.30 P.M. in the following order, 'H.Q's, 'C', 'D', 'B', 'A', Transport, 400 yds, distance between Coys.

### 4/8/18 — Bois de Boves

> Weather Fine — BOIS de BOVES was reached at 2.45 P.M. The March was rather hard on the Men owing to the congestion on the road. The Men quickly went to sleep, although it was in the open and no covering available. The Men rested all day, at 9.45 P.M., the Battalion continued on their March less Transport, Q.M. Stores, Band and Details which were left in the woods and reached ST. NICHOLAS area at 12.15 A.M. relieving part of 45th AUSTRALIAN BATTALION, the 12th AUSTRALIAN BRIGADE.

The remarks column for the 4 August entry reads "APPEN 2".

### 5/8/18 — St. Nicholas Area

> Weather Fine — ST NICHOLAS area we are more comfortable than at the previous stopping places. A good night's rest was obtained by all Ranks. The O.C. proceeded to Brigade for Conference at 10 A.M. The overcast sky enabled the Preparation for the forthcoming Offensive to progress with great vigour. Every thing is being carried out with great secrecy, everyone feels that it is going to be a great success.

## Page 7 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 6/8/18 — St. Nicholas Area

> Rainy Weather to-day;— Though the weather has changed to-day, Preparations are being carried on vigorously for the Offensive. There seems to be no end to the Traffic, the Guns that are getting in position, makes us confident of the great victory that awaits us.

### 7/8/18 — St. Nicholas Area

> Fine Weather — Operations orders for the advance received. It is apparently going to be on a big scale but the principle feature will be, the absence of a Preliminary bombardment. The O.C. attended a Conference held at Bde. H.Qs. T.16. A.9.0 (GENTILES WOOD) at 12 noon. In the afternoon Extra Rations were issued making total for each man 48 hrs. in addition to Iron Rations. Also an extra water bottle, with [uncertain] bombs, stretchers and etc, to be issued. The O.C. returned at 5 P.M. Conference of Coy. Commanders at 5.40 P.M. Operations orders issued at 8 P.M.

### 16/8/18 — K. [uncertain] Central t / L. Quesnel Wood

> The Battalion was relieved by the 1st Can. Bn. & moved back to Le Quesnel Wood. Bn. H.Q. was relieved by the 1st C.I. Bde (Brig.-Gen. [uncertain] Griesbach). Upon arrival at Le Quesnel Wood the Battalion immediately dug themselves in for protection against tanks, etc.

### 17/8/18 — Le Quesnel Wood

> D. Coy was moved to trench system immediately east of Le Quesnel Woods. There was nothing unusual happened. The 4th C.I. Bde. and the French have had heavy fighting but have been successful in pushing forward. One riding horse was evacuated. Lieut. SAVAGE proceeded on 10 days French leave.

### 18/8/18 — Le Quesnel Wood

> Sunday. In the morning the Battalion was allotted baths in Le Quesnel. 2.30 p.m. Church Parade. The 9th Field Ambulance & T.M's also attended this Parade. Capt. Coldwell officiated. Packs and stores were brought up by lorry. The men will appreciate getting their packs as the nights are getting cold & their greatcoats will be welcome.

### 19/8/18 — Le Quesnel Bois

> 1.20 a.m. Enemy bombing planes were over & dropped bombs. Four riding horses were killed and one riding horse was wounded and evacuated. None of the Personnel was hit. 11 a.m. At 11.00 a.m. orders were received that we would entrain tonight at ST. NICHOLAS Sta.

### 19/8/18 — Le Quesnel Bois (continued)

> BOYES with 7th C.I. Bde. The remainder of the Brigade will move tomorrow night 20/21, by bus. ### 20/8/18 — Boyes / Bouquemaison / Grouches

> At 1.30 A.M. Battalion arrived about 1 Kilo of BOYES (a 14 mile march) and, owing to the fact that the 7th C.I. Bde. had not yet entrained (although they were supposed to do so at midnight), bivouacked in a field, where tea was soon served. At 5 a.m. orders were received that Battalion would entrain immediately. Tea was again served & the Battalion entrained at 6.00 a.m. The arrangements for entraining were poor & in future when [uncertain] are carried other arrangements must be made for unloading. The Battalion detrained at BOUQUEMAISON at 3.00 p.m. and marched to GROUCHES arriving at 6.00 p.m. and going into billets. The Machine Gun Bn. is here, but are moving off at 8.00 p.m. We wonder what our next move will be as we are now under orders of the 7th C.I. Bde. who are billeted at DE SOUICH.

### Aug. 9th, 1918 (Ctd.)

> 1.30 p.m. Message received from 8th. Cdn. Inf. Bde. that the French were in K.14.b. & c. and were being held up by large number of enemy machine Guns in Wood in K.15.a. & c.

>
> 2.10 p.m. Four (4) Tanks of the 5th. Tank Battalion under the Command of Capt. DAWSON reported.
>
> 2.20 p.m. Enemy Machine gun fire was very intense, and Lieut. H. L. ATTO, M.C., Battalion Scout Officer, was severely wounded in the neck, and a number of men were also hit.
>
> 2.50 p.m. Attack launched. Two (2) Tanks had been detailed to accompany the LEFT Company of the Battalion. >

> 4.20 p.m. Our advance had reached ARVILLERS-FOLIES Road, and Beet-Root Factory at K.22. Central & Southern outskirts of FOLIES in our possession. Battalion, were ordered to form a defensive flank along the AMIENS-ROYE Road from K.21.b. to BOUCHOIR. A. Company of 1st. C.M.R. Battalion was later moved up by the G.O.C., 8th. C.I. ### Aug. 9th, 1918 (Ctd.)

> 5.20 p.m. Our line at this time, ran as follows :— K.24.c.8.9. — K.30.a.9.6. & K.30.a.0.0. — Southern outskirts of BOUCHOIR & Cemetery K.23.c.2.2. — thence North-west along AMIENS-ROYE Road to K.22.a.1.8. Battalion H.Qs., had moved to K.22.b.92.55.

One Tank was detailed to assist in the operation. This was fully accomplished and ARVILLERS reported cleared of the enemy at 5.45 p.m. and the French so advised.
>
> 5.40 p.m. A considerable number of enemy vehicles were noticed retiring South-eastwards from Southern outskirts of ARVILLERS. This was pointed out to a Squadron of Imperial Cavalry, who had just [uncertain — word struck through] moved up in close proximity to our H.Qs., and we suggested that they could, with very little difficulty, make a good capture, but they were either unable or unwilling to seize the opportunity. A small detachment of C.L.H. consisting of Lieut. STEWART, a Sergeant and 2 men, who had reported to the O.C. 5th. C.M.R. Bn. for Scouting purposes, volunteered to try and cut the convoy off. The party galloped forward and succeeded in turning back the head of the column (first 3 wagons) and had returned with same about 300 yards, when some Huns who had been concealed in a dugout in the vicinity, opened fire on them with machine guns. The Officer and Sergeant, first shooting the lead horses, managed to get back. One of the men was wounded and the other remained out to guard the prisoners and his wounded comrade. As the French were now beginning to move on ARVILLERS and our own situation was well in hand, a platoon of A. Coy. under Lieut. W. H. A. SHORT and One Tank were ordered to round up this enemy convoy, and bring in the two C.L.H. Men and their prisoners. Subsequently this party had to be recalled before accomplishing their object, on account of the French putting down a rolling barrage on ARVILLERS. This barrage also necessitated the recall of the Platoon and Tank which were mopping up ARVILLERS. Lieut. G. W. CARTER who had been previously sent out with 2 signallers, to reconnoitre ARVILLERS, returned at this time with eight prisoners.
>

>
> 7.00 p.m. Our situation remained substantially unchanged. The French on our right came up, occupied ARVILLERS, and establish an Out Post line 500 yards in front.
>
> 8.00 p.m. No material change. Considerable enemy movement could be observed on BEAU[uncertain]-ARVILLERS Road, and also in Squares L.31. — L.25. — K.36. Enemy Machine Guns very active especially on our SUPPORT positions, and a good deal of shelling on south-eastern outskirts of BOUCHOIR.

He was a considerable distance ahead of his party when he observed a large party of Huns (30 to 40) near the Crucifix, who were standing outside the Pill Box fully armed. Lieut. Rutherford waved his revolver at the Huns, motioning to them to come forward. They did not move but waved for Lieut. After a little discussion amongst themselves, one of the party threw down his rifle and took off his equipment. It remained in the St. Nicholas area preparing for the offensive until 7 August, when it moved forward to assembly positions west of the Avre River.   - **Battles:** The Battle of Amiens, 8 August 1918: the 2nd C.M.R. advanced on Les-Quesnoy at 4.20 a.m., captured it after hard fighting, and the 1st C.M.R. pushed through to the north to the old British front line of 1916; the 96th Infantry Brigade, 32nd Imperial Division, passed through the 2nd C.M.R. The 5th C.M.R. itself was in Corps reserve on 8 August and consolidated a defence line. On 10–11 August the 7th and 9th Canadian Infantry Brigades passed through the 32nd Imperial Division. Parvillers was captured by the 7th C.I. Bde. on the night of 14/15 August and Damery by the 9th C.I. Bde. on the morning of 15 August; an enemy counter-attack that afternoon was repulsed except at Damery Wood.
