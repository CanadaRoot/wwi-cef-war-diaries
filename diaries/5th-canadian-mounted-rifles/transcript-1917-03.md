### 1st Mar. 1917 — Cauchy-a-La-Tour

> Weather dull and cold. Companies and H.Q. details carried on independently with new Platoon Organization, bombing, bayonet fighting and shooting and machine gun firing.

### 2nd

> Weather dull and raining. Companies worked as yesterday.

### 3rd

> Weather clearer but cold. Companies worked as yesterday.

### 4th

> Church parades in morning. Recreation in afternoon.

### 5th

> D.C. returned from sick leave. Weather bright and cold. Snow overnight.

### 6th

> Companies carried on: one Coy at Tape Trenches, one at Practice Trenches, two on new Platoon Organization.

### 7th

> Weather clear and cold. Companies carried on as yesterday. 87 reinforcements reported from the 3rd Canadian Entrenching Battalions.

### 8th

> Weather clear and cold. Companies carried on as yesterday.

### 9th

> Weather raining. Companies carried on as yesterday.

"P.T.O." at foot of page.

## Page 4 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 10th Mar. 1917

> Weather clear and mild. Battalion proceeded as a working party to the Corps School at H 5 d. 9. 7 Map 36 B. to fill trenches. Army Commander sent congratulatory message with reference to turn out of transport on 6th Inst. at which we were represented.

### 11th

> Weather clear and mild. Church Parade in morning. Recreation in afternoon.

### 12th

> Weather clear—milder. Companies carried on with Platoon and Company Drill and practice of the attack. Special classes for reinforcements and rifle grenadiers.

### 13th

> Weather clear and mild in morning, turning to rain later. Companies carried on as yesterday.

### 14th

> Weather clear and mild. Companies carried on with practice of the attack at Tape Trenches.

### 15th

> Weather clear and mild. Companies carried on independently in morning. Afternoon, Battalion took part in a Brigade attack practice at Tape Trenches.

"P.T.O." at foot of page.

## Page 5 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 16th Mar. 1917

> Weather clear and mild. Companies carried on with practice of the attack at Tape Trenches. Reinforcements instructed in bombing and musketry at the ranges.

### 17th

> Weather fine but cold. Companies carried on with Platoon drill in morning. Afternoon given up to kit inspection and preparation for move tomorrow (See Appendix "A")

### 18th

> Weather dull and cold. Battalion paraded at Battalion Alarm Post at 8.45 A.M., clearing billets by 9.15 A.M. and route marched to Houdain via Cauchy-A-La-Tour and Divion arriving about 12.30 P.M. Billets fair but not to be compared with billets at Raimbert.

### 19th — Houdain

> Weather dull and cold. Battalion paraded at 12.45 P.M and marched to practice trenches just N.W. of Houdain. Foot inspection held in morning by Medical Officer.

### 20th

> Weather cloudy with some snow flurries. Companies carried on independently with practice of the attack and general smartening up.

"P.T.O." at foot of page.

## Page 6 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 21st Mar. 1917

> Weather cloudy. Companies carried on as yesterday and with wiring instruction.

### 22nd

> Weather cold with snow flurries. Companies carried on as yesterday.

### 23rd

> Weather cold with snow in early morning. Orders received overnight for the Battalion to proceed to Villers-au-Bois. Battalion paraded at 10 A.M. and route marched to Villers-a-Bois via Rebreuve-Cauchin Legal—Estree Cauchie and Camblain L'Abbey, arriving about 4 P.M. Battalion bivouacked in woods for the night at X.26.C (Sheet 36 B) known as Forester or Woodman Camp. (See Appendix "B")

### 24th

> Weather cold but sunny. Battalion resting up and putting Camp in order. Considerable artillery activity throughout the night, mostly our heavies. Orders received for 425 other ranks and 11 officers to move forward tonight. (See Appendix "C").

"P.T.O." at foot of page.

## Page 7 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 24th Mar. 1917 (contd.)

> To move forward tonight. (See Appendix "C"). An additional party of 1 Officer and 100 other ranks reported to Ammunition Dump, nr. St. Eloy and worked all night loading ammunition lorries. Another party, strength 1 officer 100 other ranks, detailed nightly for burying Cable.

### 25

> Sunday. Night cold with frost. Morning fine and clear. All available men at work fixing up Camp, sandbagging ends and sides of bivouacs, etc. Church Service held at 2.30 P.M. followed by Holy Communion, Capt. the Rev. J. Stewart Chaplain to the 2nd C.M.R. officiating. Between 4 and 5 P.M. the enemy threw 30 5.9's Southern part of this locality. One man reported slightly wounded "A" Coy. working party.

### 26

> Rained hard most of the day. Lieut. Miller and 39 other ranks proceeded to Neuville St. Vaast at 4 A.M. to work on new Brigade Aid Post. All available men in Camp working on improvements to same.

"P.T.O." at foot of page.

## Page 8 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 26th Mar. 1917 (contd.)

> Aid Post. All available men in Camp working on improvements to same.

### 27

> Weather cool with occasional snow flurries. Considerable artillery activity throughout last night and today, our "heavies" pre-dominating. Neuville St. Vaast was heavily shelled in the afternoon with 5.9s. Enemy obtained a direct hit with a 5.9 [uncertain] on dug-out in which were 14 men of "B" Coy. of ours and 16 men of the Pioneers and C.E.'s, killing or wounding all its occupants. Our Casualties were, 6 men killed, 2 died of wounds and 6 wounded.

### 28.

> Weather overcast with occasional showers. Heavy artillery bombardment throughout the night, and continuing intermittently all day. Burial of 9 N.C.O's and men at 9.00 A.M. this morning in the Cemetery, Ecoivres, Captain the Rev. Duncan, and our R.C. Chaplain Father Lowry officiating. All available officers N.C.Os and men in Camp attended.

## Page 9 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), continued

### 29 Mar. 1917

> Continuous rain, and Camp in consequence a sea of mud. The shelters, however, are waterproof, and with the ends and sides built in with sand bags and a good supply of dry straw for the interiors all ranks are fairly comfortable. In view of the pending operations, work has been carried on continuously. The 9th Can. Inf. Bde. are relieving the 8th C.I.B. on the night of March 31st/April 1st, "C" Company 5th C.M.R. Battalion remaining forward to continue work. (See APPENDIX "D".) Artillery activity has been above normal in spite of weather conditions.

### 30

> Weather threatening with occasional rain in the morning clearing and bright in the afternoon. Usual artillery activity and shelling of Neuville St Vaast and forward areas.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 March 1917 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 9 Pages", but the diary text itself occupies pages 3–10 (8 pages); pages 11–15 are Appendices A–E.
- **Officers named:** Lt.-Col. D. C. Draper (Commanding Officer; signs the diary on page 10). Appendix E lists the full nominal roll of officers as at 31 March 1917 (see page 15 above). In the diary text: Capt. the Rev. J. Stewart, Chaplain to the 2nd C.M.R. (officiated 25 March); Capt. the Rev. Duncan (officiated at the burial 28 March); Lieut. Miller (39 other ranks to Neuville St. Vaast, 26 March); Lieut. R. Andrews (billeting party, Appendices A and B); Lieut. H. L. Atto (billeting party, Appendix D).
- **Other ranks named:** None named in March; casualties are given by number only.
- **Casualties:** 25 March — one man of "A" Coy. slightly wounded by enemy shelling (30 5.9-inch shells). 27 March — enemy shell made a direct hit on a dug-out at Neuville St. Vaast holding 14 men of "B" Coy., 16 Pioneers and C.E. men: of the battalion's men, 6 killed, 2 died of wounds and 6 wounded. 28 March — burial of 9 N.C.O.s and men at Ecoivres Cemetery. 31 March — one man badly shocked (not killed) by a thunder-bolt striking just north of the camp.
- **Movements:** 18 March — battalion marched from the Raimbert area (Cauchy-a-La-Tour) to billets at Houdain via Divion. 23 March — marched from Houdain to Villers-au-Bois (Forester/Woodman Camp, X.26.C, Sheet 36 B) via Rebreuve, Estree Cauchie and Camblain L'Abbey. 24 March — working parties moved forward at night: "A" Coy. (4 officers, 175 other ranks) to Goodman Tunnel; "B" Coy. (4 officers, 150 other ranks) to Pylones and Quarries; "C" Coy. (2 platoons, up to 100 all ranks) to Neuville St. Vaast; plus a party of 1 officer and 100 other ranks to the ammunition dump near St. Eloy and a party of 1 officer and 100 other ranks burying cable nightly. 31 March/1 April — 8th Canadian Infantry Brigade to be relieved in the line by the 9th Canadian Infantry Brigade; "C" Company to remain forward in the Quarries Line under 9th C.I.B. orders; the rest of the battalion remains at Woodman Camp.
- **Strength:** 87 reinforcements reported from the 3rd Canadian Entrenching Battalions on 7 March. Effective strength as on 31 March 1917: 32 officers, 1,045 other ranks.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The burial on 28 March is of 9 N.C.O.s and men, but the diary's own casualty figures for 27 March give 6 killed and 2 died of wounds (8 men) — the ninth man buried is not accounted for in the diary text. (2) The camp is called "Forester Camp" in Appendix B and in the 23 March diary entry ("known as Forester or Woodman Camp"), and is renamed "Woodman Camp" on 31 March; Appendix C calls the forward accommodation "GOODMAN TUNNEL". (3) The contents sheet's Appendix A line reads "O.O. 14 dated ?" with the date not clearly legible; the order itself (page 11) is undated in its heading. (4) Appendix E's entry for Lt. Knowlton reads "G.H.Q. Base. June 16" — the date appears to be a later addition and is marked [uncertain].
- **[uncertain] flags used:** 6
