### 15th — MAISNIL-BOUCHE

> Weather fine. The Battalion paraded on the Battalion Alarm Post at 10.30 A.M. less "C" Coy, and marched to SAVY where they entrained at 2.30 P.M. for CAESTRE. A hot meal was served at the station previous to leaving. The train journey consumed about seven hours. "C" Coy entrained at 10.30 P.M. and came under the orders of O.C. 1st Cdn. Field Ambulance and arrived at CAESTRE at 4.30 A.M., 16.10.17. The train journey was uneventful and was enlivened by selections of the band. The Battalion were allotted billets in St. SYLVESTRE-CAPPEL area and were very comfortably accommodated. See Appen: [A]

### 25th — WIELTZE AREA

> Weather fine. Battalion moved off at 10.00 A.M. and proceeded to CALIFORNIA TRENCH Reserve Area and arrived in billets at 1.00 P.M. During the afternoon "D" Coy was shelled and 3 O.Rs wounded (APPEN.)

### 26th — RESERVE-AREA

> Rain. Battalion spent in preparations for the attack. Battalion moved off at 5.00 P.M. and proceeded to Intermediate Area, "CARNARVON KEEP." The night was spent in pumping water out of dug-outs in order to make them habitable. (APPEN.)

- **Movements:** Maisnil-Bouche (1–15 October, training: musketry, taped-trench attacks, brigade attack, concert party, baseball, rifle meet, Corps Commander's inspection 8 October, General Horne's farewell inspection 11 October). Entrained at Savy 15 October for Caestre, billets at St. Sylvestre-Cappel (16–22 October). Marched to Caestre station 22 October, entrained for Ypres, marched to Wieltje camp (22–25 October; bombed by enemy aircraft 22–24 October, two bandsmen wounded 24 October). Moved to California Trench reserve area 25 October, Carnarvon Keep (intermediate area) 26 October, Cluster House support area 27 October, front line at Kron Prinz Farm 28 October (relieving 1st C.M.R. Bn). Attacked 30 October (Passchendaele offensive, zero hour 5.50 a.m.), relieved by 2nd C.M.R. Bn 1.35 a.m. 31 October, withdrew to Capricorn Keep.
- **Casualties:** 25 October — 3 O.Rs wounded ("D" Coy shelled). 27 October — 1 O.R. killed, 3 wounded. 28 October — R.S.M. Melville seriously wounded, 4 O.Rs killed, 12 wounded. 30 October (Passchendaele attack) — officers: 7 killed (including two company commanders and two medical officers), 5 wounded & missing, 10 wounded; other ranks: 391 killed, wounded and missing, 14 wounded at duty, total 395 (see anomalies). Named killed 30 October: Capt Maron (O.C. "D" Coy), Capt Ireland (M.O.), Lieut Rogers; Capt McGuish (replacement M.O.) wounded and later died. Battalion estimated about 350 casualties by midday 30 October and 75 per cent casualties by 1.35 p.m. Trench strength at zero hour 30 October: 25 officers, 535 other ranks.
- **Battle — 30 October 1917 (Passchendaele):** Zero hour 5.50 a.m. "A" and "C" Coys first wave, "B" and "D" second wave; objective the BLUE line; intermediate objective reached but the 30-minute barrage halt let the enemy re-organise. "C" Coy under Major Prarkes took and held Vapour Farm and Source Farm with both flanks in the air, beating off counter-attacks; Vanity Farm was taken then evacuated under machine-gun fire from Vine Cotty. The 49th Battalion swung away to the right, leaving "A" Coy's flank in the air; the Artists Rifles (left) advanced only a few hundred yards. 51 unwounded German prisoners captured at Source Farm; 34 taken by "C" Coy alone; one prisoner of the 10th Bavarians captured 29 October. The artillery barrage was judged "generally faulty and unsatisfactory" — many casualties from our own guns; the machine-gun barrage "very good". Two horses killed and four evacuated wounded at X Camp, 31 October.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The diary says "C" Coy came under the orders of the O.C. 1st Cdn. Field Ambulance on 15 October, but Operation Order No. 54 (Appendix A) says the O.C. 9th Cdn. Field Ambulance. (2) The diary (29 October) says the second patrol was sent out by "B" Coy under Lieut Miller, but Appendix F is the report of a patrol by "A" Coy under Lieut. Millar, signed by Major K. L. Dugan, O.C. "A" Coy. (3) The administrative arrangements for Operation Order No. 55 give the battalion parading at 4.15 a.m. and entraining at Caestre at 4.30 a.m. on 22 October, but the diary records the march at 4.15 p.m. and entrainment at 7.00 p.m. (4) Casualty arithmetic on page 43: other ranks 391 killed, wounded and missing plus 14 wounded at duty does not equal the stated total of 395. (5) The contents sheet lists Appendix A as "C.O.54 dated 14.10.17" and Appendix B as "C.O.55 dated 21.10.17", but Operation Order No. 55 is dated 20 October 1917 and its administrative arrangements 21 October. (6) The disposition state on page 23 totals 636 all ranks; the figures' column alignment is uncertain in places. (7) Page 33 is the reverse of the page 32 sheet (show-through, not separate content). (8) The spelling "PRARKES" for Major G. R. Prarkes (O.C. "C" Coy) is as written in the typed appendices; the signature is not clearly legible.
