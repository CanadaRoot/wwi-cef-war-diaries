### Apr 1/16 — CHATEAU BELGE

> Weather bright and clear. Wind south easterly. Day spent in cleaning up ready for move into front line. In accordance with Brigade Orders the Battalion moved off at 6.45 p.m. to relieve the 4th C.M.R. Battalion. Route taken KRUISSTRAAT - YPRES - MENIN GATE - HELL FIRE CORNER - GORDON HOUSE - YEOMANRY POST. Disposition "C" Coy Right Sub Sector (B4 and R7), "B" Coy. Left Sub Sector (B.8. and C.1 Appendix) "A" Coy. In support (C.1.R and B.8.3 Right Support) (R.3.2 Left Support) "D" Coy in Rest Camp "A". Relief completed by 11.45 p.m. No casualties.

### 2 — TRENCHES

> Weather fine and clear. Wind very light N.N.E. Some activity by our artillery during day, fire being directed on enemy front line. Sniping quiet during night, on front line, but active on communication trenches. Bomb post on left of B2 reported movement by enemy in GAP in early morning. Two red flares were sent up from about J.13. at 3 a.m and 3.15 a.m. Our patrol of 4 men investigated movements and reported enemy working on his wire in front of GAP. They were dispersed by our machine gun fire from APPENDIX at 8.15 a.m. Patrol of one N.C.O and four men left APPENDIX at 1 p.m. and worked to sight covering front of C2, B8 and GAP as far as C4. They report nothing seen in NO MANS LAND and everything quiet. No enemy patrol out. Enemy very nervous using many flares, and bombs which all fell short. All bath mats taken up on GOOROOK ROAD and trench cleaned and drained from
>
> P.T.O.

### Apr 2/16 (contd)

> LOVERS WALK to WARRINGTON AVE. Bombing Post at left of B4 reclaimed, sand bagged and ready for bath mats. Dugouts cleaned and disinfected in B4 and C1 and B8. Bath mats taken up and trenches cleaned up.

### 3

> Weather clear. Wind mild N.W. Temperature warm. Rifle fire active during night. Machine gun fire active especially on MAPLE COPSE and R7. in evening about 7 p.m. At 9.15 a.m. enemy artillery put six shells (3.3) into rear of our front line, and 3 H.E. shells to left of MAPLE COPSE at 12.45 p.m. and at 4.45 p.m. enemy threw 9 shrapnel shells between MAPLE COPSE and ZOUVE WOOD from S.E. At 4.45 a.m. enemy plane high over our lines, others seen at 9.30 a.m. and 5.40 p.m. Our Trench mortars fired 7 rounds at 5.15 p.m. in vicinity of BIRDCAGE getting two direct hits on same, and one breaking over. Officers patrol left APPENDIX at 10.30 p.m. going up Sap to enemy trench on left of C.1. and returning by right of B.8. at 1.50 p.m. Another left APPENDIX at 2.30 a.m. working to left and returning by enemy post at J.13.c.2.8 which was bombed. Enemy alert and nervous.

### 4

> Weather overcast and colder. Wind northwest by north. Rifle fire inactive. Snipers active on communication trenches. Our snipers report having killed five enemy snipers yesterday. Enemy machine guns active, evidently trying to find our ration parties.

### Apr 4/16 (contd)

> Our artillery very active. Battery of 18 pounders registered on sap at J.19.a.6½.8 and did some damage to snipers posts in it. Enemy artillery at 9.15 a.m. threw 8-3.3 shells near YEOMANRY POST. At 11.30 a.m. five shrapnel shells in ZOUVE WOOD. At 1 p.m. more shrapnel at YEOMANRY POST. From 2.40 p.m. till 5.05 p.m. enemy fired 33 5.9 H.E. shells around vicinity of CHARING CROSS and communication trenches to N and N.E. from there. Eleven of these were dead. At 4.30 p.m. enemy fired on our front line putting over 25-5.9 H.E. shells. Fire directed first at B.8. Enemy aeroplanes active over lines, evidently taking photographs. Our Trench Mortars in conjunction with Artillery fired 22 rounds at sap and shaft in front of APPENDIX doing a lot of damage, practically destroying it. Many grenades thrown by enemy. Our rifle grenade battery fired intermittently at sap at J.19.a.6½.8. Our grenadiers also very active on BIRDCAGE. One grenadier put one grenade through rifle opening in BIRDCAGE. Patrol of two under one N.C.O worked in front of B.4. and investigated sap at J.19.a.6½.8 which they reported [uncertain — word crossed out] was occupied by two men. Rifle grenades thrown. Patrol of four worked from APPENDIX to B.4. Nothing to report. Officers patrol, patrolled sap from APPENDIX to investigate damage done by Trench mortars. Enemy very alert, and machine gun observed in this part of line for first time. Wire not broken, but sap and snipers posts apparently destroyed.

### 5

### 10

> Weather dull and showery. Wind N.E. Companies carried on independently. A Supper was held in the evening by the Sergeants Mess, in the Y. M. C. A hut at the Rest Camp "A". Colonel G. C. All lights had to be extinguished, as it was reported that enemy aeroplanes were over the camp. No damage was done although three bombs were known to be dropped.

### Apr 16/16 (Sunday) — TRENCHES

> Weather bright and sunny. Wind west by north west. Warm. Between 11 a.m and 11.30 a.m enemy artillery shelled area between road in front of RAILWAY DUGOUTS, and railroad, using fourteen 5.9 H.E. and seven 4.1 shells. No damage, or casualties. Again between 5 and 6 p.m the same area was shelled with six 3.3 H.E. No damage, no casualties. From one to four p.m there was great activity among our own aeroplanes, apparently trying to locate enemy batteries. Between 4 and 6 p.m an enemy biplane, coming from an east, north easterly direction hovered over RAILWAY DUGOUTS (Bn H.Q.) and southerly outskirts of YPRES, apparently trying to locate our batteries, and observing damage from enemy artillery. At times the aeroplane was not more than 500 yards above Head quarters. Eventually the aeroplane went away in a direction East, South east. "D" & "C" Companies working parties acted as carrying parties, from R.E. Dump to support trenches. Work done on DAVIDSON STREET, cleaning up debris caused by enemy shells in early morning. Parapets and parados repaired, and [uncertain — word crossed out] Stokes gun emplacement rebuilt. Ninety feet of front line trench heightened 3 feet. Revetting work done at ST PETER STREET and CRAB CRAWL. RAILWAY DUGOUTS, cleaned and repaired. BN. H.Q. Officers Mess Dug out, strengthened on top with two layers of filled sand bags. "B" Company working party, acted as carrying party from ZILLEBEKE to R.E. dump at MAPLE COPSE.

### 23 (EASTER SUNDAY)

### Apr 24/16 — TRENCHES

> Wind light S.W. Weather bright and clear. Overnight enemy artillery quiet. Our trench mortar battery registered on German new front line opposite ours. Damage not known, but sniper was quieted. ### 26

- **Movements:** The battalion went into the front line in the Ypres sector (Sanctuary Wood / Maple Copse / Railway Dugouts) on the night of 1/2 April, relieving the 4th C.M.R. Battalion. Relieved by the 42nd Battalion, 7th Canadian Infantry Brigade, on the night of 5/6 April and by train to Camp "A". Back into the line on the night of 13/14 April, relieving the 60th Canadian Battalion (Railway Dugouts and Maple Copse). Relieved the 4th C.M.R. Battalion again on 21 April, taking over trenches 52–57. Relieved by the 42nd Canadian Infantry Battalion on the night of 29/30 April and back to Camp "A" via Observatory Ridge, Zillebeke, Transport Farm, Shrapnel Corner, Kruisstraat and Asylum.
- **Action:** A month of trench warfare in the Ypres salient, dominated by working parties — digging, revetting, draining, laying bath mats and sandbags at Romers Lane, Sanctuary Wood, Maple Copse, Railway Dugouts and the Strong Points — under regular shelling of Battalion Headquarters, much of it from 5.9 H.E. and 3.3 shrapnel. Enemy aeroplanes were over the lines almost daily, and enemy observation balloons were up on 17 and 22 April. The battalion's snipers claimed five enemy snipers killed on 4 April and broke four enemy sniperscopes on 5 April. On 26 April an intense bombardment, preceded by a mine explosion, fell on the brigade to the battalion's right; the battalion stood to, expecting a gas attack at dawn. Easter Sunday (23 April) brought communion services at Maple Copse and the Border Dugouts. On 10 April a Sergeants Mess supper in the Y.M.C.A. hut was interrupted by an enemy bomb dropped 100 yards away.
