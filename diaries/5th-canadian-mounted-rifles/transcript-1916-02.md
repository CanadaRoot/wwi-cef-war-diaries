### Feb 1/16

> After resting during the morning the Battalion mobilised, and proceeded by Companies independently to the Trenches. The relief was completed 7.55 p.m. punctually. The Battalion being disposed as follows:- "B" Company trenches 136, 137 and 138. "D" Company trenches 139, 140 and 141. "C" Company in Stinking Farm, and "A" Company with one Platoon "B" Company in [uncertain] (T.7.C.6.?) Headquarters in [uncertain] Walk. 10th Can. Inf. Bn on our right & 1st Can. M.R. on our left.

### 10.00 a.m.

> No 110573 Private J. T. Wood wounded by gunshot in right shoulder, and No 110650 Pte W. Bradley shrapnel wound in right forearm. Both of the Machine Gun Section.

> Weather bright and mild during day, very dark at night.

### 2

> Situation generally quiet during night. Occasional rifle shots. Wind light S.E. later wind S.W. Officers patrolled out overnight, where [uncertain] patrolled to distance 200 yards out. No enemy patrols seen or heard. No working parties heard.

### 11.15 a.m.

> No 7746 Pte A. J. [uncertain] Killed by sniper, at Trench 136, bay 9 in morning.

### 2.00 p.m.

> No 7792 Pte J. Jennings, Regiment [uncertain] wounded in right shoulder by shrapnel. Both of "A" Company.

> Enemy generally quiet with occasional sniping. Enemy fired 3 "whiz bangs" to left and in front of Trench 141, and a rifle grenade struck front of parapet. Artillery quiet. Wind light S.E.

### Feb 17/16 (contd)

> At 6.45 p.m. it was reported that letters were being signalled apparently by flash lamp from enemy's lines about U.2.C.7.7 and answered by two signals from behind our own lines, thought to be between GABION FARM (U.1.D.9.1) and STINKING FARM (U.7.A.7.7.) Another patrol of 1 Officer and 4 men covered the front from 2 a.m. to 3.45 a.m. Bright moonlight impossible for patrols to penetrate far without being detected.

### Feb 25/16

> Morning dull and cold. Ground still frozen hard. 10.00 a.m. Brigade formed up in field at X.17.C.6.5 and was inspected by Lieut-General A. H. Alderson. who complimented all ranks on the work done during their tour in the trenches. The following copies of communications received from the O.C. 1st CAN. DIV. and the O.C. 8th CAN. INF. BDE. were read out to all ranks before the battalion was dismissed.
>
> I
>
> 1st Can Division February 24th 1916.
>
> My Dear Mercer, I am enclosing herewith a letter from Col. Marshall, O.C. 3rd Brigade, in which he records his appreciation of the extraordinarily good work done by your 8th Brigade during the period which they relieved the 3rd Brigade of this Division. I have not yet visited those trenches since my return from leave, but from my staff - from the 8th Brigade - and from the work reports, I learn that General Williams and his men have worked wonders.

- **Movements:** The battalion moved from Kortepyp into the front line trenches 136–141 (Messines sector) on 1 February, was relieved by the 4th Battalion on 7 February and went into Divisional Reserve at Kortepyp; returned to the front line on 13 February, was relieved by the 4th Battalion on 19 February and went into Brigade Reserve at Red Lodge; relieved by the 14th Canadian Infantry Battalion on 20 February and moved back to billets near Meteren.
- **Casualties:** Killed in action: Pte A. J. [uncertain] No 7746 (2 February), Pte S. R. Morehouse No 110363 (3 February), Pte John O. Y. Kitteredge No 110301 (6 February) — the diary notes Kitteredge was "the third man killed since taking over the line". Wounded: Ptes J. T. Wood and W. Bradley (1 February), Pte J. Jennings (2 February), Pte E. J. Harrison (3 February), Pte A. R. Grant (10 February), Pte George Laing (20 February).
- **Action:** The battalion's first sustained front-line tour: trenches 136–141 in the Messines sector, with constant sniping, shelling, patrols and working parties. A German aeroplane dropped bombs on trenches 136 and 139 (7 February). A detailed Special Intelligence Report on enemy strong points (the barrier on the Messines–Wulverghem road, "Snipers House", and a new enemy front line under construction) was forwarded to 8th Canadian Infantry Brigade on 17 February. The 10th Battalion's bombing attack from trench 135 on 5 February is recorded. A Zeppelin was heard overhead on the night of 19/20 February. Letters were reportedly signalled by flash lamp between the enemy lines and a point behind the battalion's own lines on 17 February; search parties found nothing.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The diary adopts a new, more structured format in February, with separate headed sub-sections (PATROLS, WORKING PARTIES, ENEMY ACTIVITY, WORKS, LEAVE) and much more detail than earlier months. (2) The 25 February entry copies in full two letters of appreciation — from Major-General A. W. Currie (1st Canadian Division) and Lt.-Col. W. R. Marshall (3rd Brigade) — praising the 8th Brigade's rebuilding of the front line. (3) The burial note on 6 February is confused, naming Pte S. R. Morehouse twice with different dates. (4) A German flamethrower ("Flammenwerfer") demonstration was held for 350 all ranks on 24 February. (5) The place column on pages 4–6 reads "Trench [uncertain]" rather than a place name.
