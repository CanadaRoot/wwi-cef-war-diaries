## Page 4 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), sheet (1)

### 1st July — VILLERS-AU-BOIS

> Weather fine. Morning devoted to cleaning up billets preparatory to move. Battalion fell in on Battalion Alarm Post at 2.00 pm moved off at 2.30 p.m. and marched to "SEAFORTH CAMP" "Bois de BERTHONVAL" arriving there at 4.30 PM and relieved the 75th Canadian Inf. Battalion. (Appen: A)

### 2nd July — SEAFORTH CAMP

> Weather fine. Morning devoted to practice of the attack in all its phases Afternoon: Platoon and Company drill.

### 3rd July — SEAFORTH CAMP

> Weather fine. Battalion carried on as on the 2nd.

### 4th July — SEAFORTH CAMP

> Weather fine. Battalion carried on as on previous day

### 5th July — SEAFORTH CAMP

> Weather fine. Working party consisting of 5 Officers and 110 Other Ranks under the command of Lieut EATON proceeded to forward area to prepare and clean dugouts etc for occupation by "A" + "B" Coys. Party returned at 3.30 P.M. Remainder of Battn carried on with drill, etc.

### 5th July (continued) — SEAFORTH CAMP

> A + B Coys fell in at 6.30 PM and proceeded to forward area. "A" Coy occupying "BENO TRENCH" from S.16.b.0.4 to S.16.b.2.0 and BASIN TRENCH from S.16.a.6.1 to S.16.a.9.3 (Map Reference 36c S.W. Edition 9a) Strength 5 Officers and 170 Other Ranks. "B" Coy taking over dugouts in "CIMBAI" [uncertain] and "CZAR" Trenches S.1a.d.9.5 [uncertain] to S.17.a.4.2. Strength 3 Officers + 170 Other Ranks. These two companies came under the command of the 7th Can: Inf Brigade to be utilized for working parties.

### 6th July — SEAFORTH CAMP

> Remainder of Battalion carried on with practice of attack, drill etc.

### 7th July — SEAFORTH CAMP

> Weather continues fine. "C." "D." + Headqtrs Coy carried on as previous day.

### 8th July — SEAFORTH CAMP

> Church Parade cancelled on account of rain.

### 9th July — SEAFORTH CAMP

> "C" Coy supplied working party of 3 Officers and 125 other ranks to "Corps LIGHT RAILWAY"

### 10th July — SEAFORTH CAMP

> Weather fine. "D" Coy supplied working party of 3 Officers and 125 other ranks for work on Corps Light Railway, party worked from 8 AM to 2.00 PM. "C" "D" + Hdqtrs Coys fell in at 5.00 PM and moved off at 5.30 PM. "A" + "B" Coys moved forward from "BENO" and "CIMBAI" Trenches at 10.15 PM

## Page 6 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), sheet (3), continued

Place column: [uncertain] for 10th July (a single letter, apparently "R"); "RESERVE" for 11th and 12th July. Remarks column: entries initialled "H.C." [uncertain].

### 10th July (continued)

> The Battalion relieving the R.C.Rs in reserve. Relief completed at 11.30 PM. See disposition state attached. (Appendix B)

### 11th July — RESERVE

> Weather fair. Situation normal. Our aircraft active against enemy's rear area. During the day ten enemy planes crossed our lines and were engaged by our machine guns. Working party supplied for work on [uncertain] RESISTANCE LINE of 9 officers and 450 other ranks. One officer and 120 other ranks worked on building machine gun emplacements, repairing roads, + carrying parties

### 12th July — RESERVE

> Weather fine. Ours and enemy artillery active, field + heavies firing throughout the day. Considerable aerial activity many enemy planes were engaged and one enemy plane fell near AVION TRENCH another was driven back and apparently forced to land well behind the German lines. Working parties were supplied as on the 11th.

### 13th July — RESERVE

> Weather fine, Wind light S.W. Ours and enemy artillery very active throughout the day. Enemy shelled LENS-ARRAS ROAD and Bois-de-la-CHAUDIERE. Battalion furnished the same working parties as previous day.

### 14th July — RESERVE

> Weather fine. Wind S.W. fresh. Our artillery shelled FOSSE 14, (N 33.a.7.3) and destroyed it. A large number of very heavy explosions occurred in LENS between 5.30 AM and 6.30 AM. Working parties provided as on 13th.

### 15th July — RESERVE

> Weather cleared [uncertain] at 7. AM. Heavy rains. Wind S.W. Aircraft very active our planes worked well over enemy lines. Enemy artillery active on our front and support lines. Working parties as on 14th.

### 16th July — RESERVE

> Wind S.W. At 1.00 AM gas was successfully projected by "B" Special Coy R.E. on our Brigade frontage. Our artillery co-operating. Enemy retaliation very light. At 4.20 AM, several explosions were observed

### 16th July (continued)

> in LENS and again at 5.15 AM and 5.50 AM. One enemy plane brought down behind our lines in flames at 8.00 AM. Working parties supplied as on previous days.

### 17th July — RESERVE

> Weather fine wind West. Our artillery very active heavily shelling LENS and trenches North of AVION. Explosions observed in vicinity of Railway Embankment East of Avion. At 4.55 PM twelve enemy planes were engaged by seven of ours, after some exchange of shots they dispersed. Working parties as on previous days. Officer commanding Lieut Col: D.C. DRAPER, returned to transport lines to proceed on leave and Major W RHOADES assumed command of the Battalion.

### 18th July — RESERVE

> Weather fair, light wind S.E. Further large explosions observed in LENS Artillery on both sides very active, considerable counter battery work. Working parties as on previous days

### 18th July — RESERVE

> Relief of Battalion by 116th Can: Inf: Battalion commenced at 11-30 P.M.

### 19th July — RESERVE

> Relief completed by 12-30 AM and Battalion moved to camp situated immediately North of Bois-de-BERTHONVAL. Dirty weather prevailed during relief and march out. Battalion paraded at 2-00 PM and marched St LAWRENCE CAMP, CHATEAU-de-la-HAIE at 4-30 PM. (Appen: C) Total casualties for tour as follows. Killed 2, Wounded 14,

### 20th July — St. LAWRENCE CAMP

> Weather improved. Day spent in general cleaning and smartening up

### 21st July — St. LAWRENCE CAMP

> Baths were allotted to Battalion. Companies carried on independently with Platoon and Company drill. A concert was given to the Battalion by the concert party of the 16 Can: Inf: Battn, which was very much appreciated, and unanimously voted as the best delivered to this Battalion.

### 22nd July — ST. LAWRENCE CAMP

> Weather fine. 4th and 5th C.M.R Battalions paraded together for Church Service at 9.00 am. Battalion sports were held in the afternoon all events were very keenly contested and very promising material for the coming Divisional Sports brought to light

### 23rd July — ST. LAWRENCE CAMP

> Weather fine. 9.00 am Inspection by platoon + Company Commanders Companies carried on with Platoon and Company drill, musketry and fire orders, etc. All Officers and N.C.O's witnessed a demonstration of a trench to [uncertain] attack emerging into open order attack, to illustrate the principles governing the tactical handling of Platoons both in close and open order action, provided by 3rd Can: Divisional School Special Platoon. Major J.E. TODD awarded "Military Cross" Major K.L. DURRANT returned and assumed command of "A" Company

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 July 1917 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 9 Pages"; the diary text is nine sheets counting the disposition state (sheets (1)–(8) plus the final 29–31 July sheet, which carries the diary's own pagination number 12). All 21 source images were present and read; no pages are missing or duplicated.
- **Officers named:** Lieut. Col. D. C. Draper (Officer Commanding; returned to the transport lines to proceed on leave, 17 July); Major W. Rhoades (assumed command of the Battalion, 17 July; signed the diary 31 July "for O.C."); Lieut. Eaton (commanded the working party to the forward area, 5 July); Major J. E. Todd (awarded the Military Cross, 23 July); Major K. L. Durrant (returned and assumed command of "A" Company, 23 July); Lieut. Winslow, H.Q. Coy. (advance party, Appendix A; guides, Appendix C); Lieut. Atto (advance party, Appendices B and C); Lieut. R. Andrews (advance party, Appendix D). The Adjutant (Lieutenant, A/Adjutant) signs all four appendices, but his signature is illegible.
- **Other ranks named:** None named in July 1917. The R.S.M. is mentioned in the orders (unnamed); a Sergeant in Appendix D para 10 is named only by an illegible surname.
- **Equipment:** Lewis guns (Appendix D para 9); machine gun emplacements built on 11 July; Fosse 14 shelled and destroyed by the battalion's artillery, 14 July; gas projected by "B" Special Company, Royal Engineers, on the brigade frontage, 16 July; steel helmets, water bottles and haversacks for the 25 July march; men's packs conveyed to Raimbert by motor lorry (Appendix D para 10); field kitchens and officers' mess baggage in Appendix A para 6(b) (words partly illegible).
- **Movements:** 1 July — Villers-au-Bois to Seaforth Camp, Bois de Berthonval, relieving the 75th Canadian Infantry Battalion. 5 July — "A" and "B" Companies forward to Beno, Basin, Cimbai and Czar trenches. 10–11 July — the battalion relieved the Royal Canadian Regiment in reserve. 18–19 July — relieved by the 116th Canadian Infantry Battalion and marched via Souve Valley to St Lawrence Camp, Chateau-de-la-Haie. 25 July — marched to rest billets at Raimbert.
- **Discipline:** None recorded in July 1917.
- **Casualties:** Total for the tour (10–19 July) as recorded 19 July: Killed 2, Wounded 14. No names are given, and no other casualties are recorded in the month.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The contents sheet does not match the orders themselves: it describes Appendix B as "C.O.37 dated 8.7.17 ... night 9th/10th July", but Operation Order No. 37 (8-7-17) places the 5th C.M.R. relief on the night of 10/11th July; it describes Appendix C as "C.O.38 dated 15.7.17", but the order is Operation Order No. 39 dated 18th July 1917; and Appendix D as "C.O.38 dated 23.7.17", while the order itself reads No. 38 (flagged [uncertain]) dated 23rd July 1917. (2) Diary pagination: the diary sheets are circled (1)–(8); the final sheet (29–31 July) bears no circled number but is numbered 12 in the diary's own pagination, and the numbers 5, 10 and 11 appear at the head of the summary column on sheets (3), (7) and (8). Counting the disposition state, the diary text is 9 pages, as the contents sheet states. (3) Disposition state (10 July): the stated totals do not all match the column sums — officers sum to 25 against a stated total of 26, and bombers sum to 136 against a stated total of 139; the other columns reconcile. Bayonets reconcile (382) only if "A" Coy is read as 106 (96 with 10 written below). "D" Coy officers are written as 5, though the stated total of 26 implies 6. (4) The 15 July entry reads "Weather cleared at 7. AM. Heavy rains." — the two statements sit oddly together; "cleared" is flagged [uncertain]. (5) The 19 July entry ends "Total casualties for tour as follows. Killed 2, Wounded 14," with a trailing comma and no continuation. (6) The trench name in the 5 July and 10 July entries and in Appendix B is read as "CIMBAI"; the final letter is not fully clear and the name may be "CIMBAL". (7) Appendix C para 3 gives the 19 July parade time as "12.30 a.m." (flagged [uncertain]), while the diary entry for 19 July says the battalion paraded at 2.00 PM. (8) The Place column for the 10 July entry is a single letter, apparently "R". (9) The right edge of the disposition state sheet is torn, and a further column at the right is partly illegible. (10) The words "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" are struck through on diary sheets (4), (6) and (7) and left standing on the other sheets. (11) Appendix C para 7 (transport arrangements) is largely illegible in the copy; several words are flagged [uncertain]. (12) Appendix B para 3 names a second trench at the junction with BENO which is illegible; the diary's 5 July entry names BASIN TRENCH in the same area. (13) The remarks-column initials read as "H.C." throughout, but are faint and are flagged [uncertain] on each page.
