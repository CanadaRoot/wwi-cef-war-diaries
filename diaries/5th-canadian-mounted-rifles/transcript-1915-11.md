Place column: "Hazebrouck" throughout.

### Nov 1/15

> Corps Commander's instructions were, to carry out Regimental practice of the attack, assembly and holding of positions taken. This was done. Orders received to vacate present Billets on the morrow, and move to Aldershot Huts, Belgium (Reference Map Belgium and France, 1/5 series, Sheet 28.) (T.19.6.d.). Weather fair.

### 2

> Regiment vacated Billets, and cleared Regt H.Q. 2 at 11.45 a.m. complete. Carrying out the following schedule punctually:- Arrived junction of road leading from [uncertain] to the Messines Road, east of Brigade H.Q. 2 at 11.50 a.m. and rear of column clear by 12 noon. Route taken was through Bailleul via Grande Place, Lille Road, to junction of Lille and Neuve Eglise Road. Aldershot Huts being reached by 3 p.m. Two short halts were made en route, one of 5 minutes duration, and one of 20 minutes. Halt for lunch and feeding horses. Accommodation for the regiment was as follows:- 26 Huts, capacity 20 men each, with 5 bell tents, and 2 Officers Huts. The whole Camp a sea of mud, but considering the long march over muddy roads in heavy rain, the men were in splendid shape, and quickly adapted themselves to conditions

### Nov 3/15

> The morning was devoted to Muster Parade, fatigues, and settling down generally. Orders having been received, that the Units of the Brigade be attached for Instruction as follows, Brigade Staff to 2nd Infantry Brigade Staff, Headquarters (T.22. B.2.2.) 4th C. M. R. to 1st Canadian Infantry Brigade, (N.33.d.3.2) 5th C. M. R. to 2nd Canadian Infantry Brigade, Headquarters Petit Pont Farm (T.22. B.2.2) 6th C. M. R. to 3rd Canadian Infantry Brigade, Headquarters, Petit [uncertain] Farm (T.23.d.8.9). "A" Squadron paraded at 3 p.m. and proceeded to the trenches, Guides being furnished by the 5th Battalion, and the 7th Battalion. 1st and 2nd Troops and Squadron H.Q. 2. reporting to the 5th Bn., 3rd and 4th Troops to the 7th Bn. "B" Squadron reporting at the Bombing School. "C" Squadron furnishing guards and fatigues around Camp, and at 6 p.m. furnishing 100 men for working party. The working party were taken by Guides to a point directly in rear of Trenches Nos 132-3-14 where work was carried on from 8.30 p.m. until 1 a.m. Nov 4. under direction of Lt Macleod No 1 Co. C.E.

### Nov 3/15 (contd)

> Work consisted of preparing new Trenches for winter use about 50 yds in rear of present front line trench. The use of star shells by the enemy was continuous, and their snipers were very active during the whole time party were working. There were no casualties. Party returned to Camp 3.45 a.m. November 4th. Distance from camp to trenches approximately 6 miles.

### 4

> Raining most of the day. Morning spent in fatigue work, inspections etc. "B" Squadron furnished working party of 100 men, which continued work on the winter trenches, they were continually sniped at during the night but suffered no casualties. Commanding Officer and Adjutant spent the day in the trenches with the 5th Bn. Weather dull and heavy.

### 5

> Morning. "C" Sqdn Bombing School, "B" Sqdn, resting and cleaning up generally. Afternoon. "B" Sqdn Bombing School "C" Sqdn. [uncertain] at 3.30 p.m. and proceeded to the trenches to relieve "A", the latter reaching Camp at 9 p.m. reporting All Well, No Casualties, [uncertain] although under fire of both rifles and shell

### Nov 5/15 (contd)

> Machine Gun Section, 2 Gun teams, accompanied "C" Squadron to the trenches, one gun team being attached to the 5th Bn., and one to the 7th Bn. Lt. [uncertain] M.9.0 going in with them.

### 6

> C.O. and Adjutant spent the day in the trenches with 7th Bn. "B" Sqdn went to Bombing School all day. "A" Squadron Bombing School in the afternoon. Weather fine

### 7 (Sunday)

> Roman Catholics proceeded at 10.30 a.m. to Bulford Camp where service was held in the Y.M.C.A. tent by Reverend Father Workman. Afternoon, report received that No 110614 Cpl R. V. [uncertain] and No 110342 Pte K. Hill both of 3rd Troop, "C" Sqdn had been wounded. Following details were afterwards learned:- They were in trench No 132 repairing parapet when at about 2.30 p.m. the enemy opened fire with high explosive shrapnel. There not being sufficient room in the [uncertain] for the 7th men and our own, our men were instructed to keep close to the parapet. Cpl [uncertain] whilst leaning against the parapet was struck by a piece of shrapnel in the right leg above the ankle making a nasty flesh wound. Pte Hill was running down the trench when a shell exploded at the entrance of a communication trench he was passing and he received 3 pieces in the back, and appears to be in a serious condition.

### Nov 10/15

> In accordance with orders, Regiment complete in every detail, were clear of their billets, Aldershot Huts by 11.15 a.m., reached Grande Place, Bailleul at 1.15 p.m. and proceeded direct to its former billets. No casualties en route. Weather generally fine, with a few scattered showers.

### Nov 17/15

> Route march by squadrons commencing at 6.30 a.m. Rendezvous took place at Regimental Alarm Post opposite Regimental Headquarters, where the Officers of the Regiment played a game of Association football against the Officers of the 4th C.M.R. Arrangements were made whereby all Officers took part in the game at some time or other. Game was entered into with an enjoyable spirit of fun by Officers and men. Many humorous efforts were pulled off, [uncertain] being adopted by many of the players, and the game was [uncertain] without incident. The 5th C.M.R. eventually proved winners by 2 goals to 1 goal. ### 18

> Squadrons carried on independently, with special attention being given to Bomb throwing and bayonet fighting. Weather clear & frosty.

### 19

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–30 November 1915 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 12 pages", which matches the twelve diary-text pages (pages 3–14).
- **Officers named:** Lt Macleod, No 1 Company, Canadian Engineers (directed the working party, 3–4 November); Lt Chown (bombing parties, 6–9 November; Regimental Grenade Company, 16 November); Lt Green (Machine Gun Section, 5 and 16 November); Lt [uncertain] (with the machine gun teams, 5 November); Captain [uncertain] ("B" Squadron troop commander, 9 November); Reverend Father Workman (Roman Catholic service, 7 November); Corporal Hodge, 4th C.M.R. (officiated at the burial, 29 November). The Adjutant signs page 14, name not clearly legible [uncertain].
- **Other ranks named:** Cpl R. V. [uncertain] No 110614, "C" Squadron (wounded in the right leg, 7 November); Pte K. Hill No 110342, 3rd Troop, "C" Squadron (seriously wounded in the back by shrapnel, 7 November); Pte W. Bray No 110657 (tunic and pants cut by shell, slightly bruised, 7 November); Pte J. Childs No 110891 (clothing cut by shell, uninjured, 7 November); No 110311 [uncertain] (slightly wounded in the head by a premature grenade explosion, 7 November); Cpl Macleod and Cpl Martin (returned from Grenade School, 7 November); Pte Roper J. [uncertain] No 110030 (accidentally wounded in the foot at Ash House, 24 November); Pte R. Freke No 110462, "C" Squadron (wounded in the foot on Messines Road, 26 November); Lance-Corporal [uncertain] No 110150, "B" Squadron (killed in action, 28 November — the regiment's first death, per the diary).
- **Movements:** The regiment left its Hazebrouck billets on 2 November and marched to Aldershot Huts via Bailleul; squadrons were attached for instruction to the 5th and 7th Battalions in the trenches (Nos 130–132, Hill 63 sector) from 3 November; returned to Hazebrouck billets on 10 November; moved on 22 November into the 1st Canadian Divisional area, occupying Hill 63, Grand [uncertain] Farm, English Farm and Stable Fort, and taking over trenches 130 and 131.
- **Casualties:** The regiment's first fatal casualty: Lance-Corporal [uncertain] No 110150, "B" Squadron, killed in action 28 November and buried 29 November (grave T.18.d.3.8). Wounded: Cpl R. V. [uncertain] and Pte K. Hill (7 November, shrapnel in trench 132); Pte Roper J. [uncertain] (accidental foot wound, 24 November); Pte R. Freke (foot wound, 26 November). Minor injuries: Ptes W. Bray and J. Childs (7 November); No 110311 [uncertain] (grenade, 7 November).
- **Action:** First front-line experience: working parties preparing winter trenches under continuous star-shell and sniper fire (3–4 November); bombing parties against enemy wire and listening posts on the nights of 6–9 November, including one enemy sentry killed; heavy bombardment of enemy trenches 24 November; a German aeroplane reported brought down 27 November; La Basse Farm shelled to the ground after snipers were suspected there (27 November).
- **Anomalies:** (1) The 9 November entry is unusually long and runs across pages 7–8, with the place column on page 8 carrying the note "Nov 9th (contd)". (2) The handwriting is a single hurried hand and deteriorates in places — the long 9 November entry and the 7 November casualty passage have many words that are not clearly legible. (3) Page 9 is numbered "21a" rather than "22", and the following page is numbered "22". (4) The diary reports the first death as "1st death reported" on 28 November, although two men were seriously wounded on 7 November.
