### 1st Jan — MAZINGARBE

> AM Weather Fine. The Battalion carried on with Physical Training and Smartening up drill, also preparations were made for moving into the line.
>
> P.M. Recreation

### 2nd Jan — MAZINGARBE

> AM At 2.00 AM, word was received that the Battalion would relieve the 1st C.M.R. Battalion in the line on the night of the 2nd/3rd January 1918.
>
> AM The Battalion paraded during the morning for the Commanding Officer's inspection, after which, final preparations for move were carried out
>
> P.M. The Battalion moved off from MAZINGARBE at 2-30 P.M. and proceeded to the line, taking over from the 1st C.M.R. Battalion, the relief being completed by 8-05 P.M. without mishap or casualties.

### Night of 2nd/3rd Jan — FRONT LINE AND CLOSE SUPPORTS

The first entry on this page has no date or hour entered in the Date and Hour columns; it appears to record the night of 2nd/3rd January, before the 3rd January morning entry.

> Enemy Artillery seemed to be registering on Communication Trenches and around Battalion Headquarters during the evening, also a few Whizbangs were dropped in Gravel Pit from the direction of SALLAUMINES. Under cover of barrage the Enemy raided the 2nd C.M.R. Battalion and took one prisoner.

### 3rd Jan — FRONT LINE AND CLOSE SUPPORTS

> AM Weather Fine, Visibility good. Enemy very quiet, Artillery Activity increased, HYTHE ALLEY was registered and two direct hits were made.
>
> PM At N.28.80.98. an Enemy working party was dispersed. We carried on with wiring from 5-30 P.M. until 10-00 P.M. when it became too light to work owing to the bright moon.

### 4th Jan — FRONT LINE AND CLOSE SUPPORTS

> AM Weather Fine. Enemy quite active on our Right Company during the night. Our Right Posts and NUNS ALLEY were heavily shelled about 8-00 P.M.

### 23rd Jan — BULLY GRENAY

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 January 1918 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 18 Pages", which matches the eighteen diary-text sheets (Army Form C. 2118, numbered (1)–(18), pages 4–21). The diary is handwritten throughout; the cover, contents sheet and appendices are typed, with handwritten additions. Pages 30 and 35 are the reverses of the Appendix E and Appendix F sheets respectively and carry only show-through text plus a handwritten "War Diary" note; they are not transcribed as separate content.
- **Officers named:** Lt-Col. D. C. Draper D.S.O. (Officer Commanding; returned to the battalion 26 January after two months commanding the 9th Canadian Infantry Brigade, and proceeded on leave 31 January); Major J. D. Snead M.C. (took over command 31 January and signs the diary as Major for O.C. 5th C.M.R. Bn.); Capt. (A/Major) C. R. Pearkes M.C. (awarded the Victoria Cross, announced 28 January, for Passchendaele 30/31 October 1917); Capt. E. Gill (commanded the 14/15 January raid); Lieut. R. H. McKenzie (patrol 4 January); Lieut. Atto [uncertain] (patrol 7 January); Lieut. H. G. Porter (awarded the Military Cross, announced 11 January); Brigadier General Emsley and the Divisional Commander (unnamed), who inspected the battalion on the march 23 January; Major Harboard [uncertain] and Lieut. J. A. Adamson (advance party to Houdain, 22 January); Lieut. Barnstead [uncertain] (guides, 21 January); Lieut. Carter (advance party to Bully, 20 January); Capt. Beckett (8th Canadian Infantry Brigade). The Adjutant signs the operation orders as Lieut., A/Adjutant; his signature appears to read "Jas Lawson" [uncertain]. The Medical Officer, Transport Officer and Quartermaster are referred to by appointment only.
- **Other ranks named:** 114218 L/Sgt. Mead J. W. (awarded the Distinguished Conduct Medal, announced 11 January); 114398 Sergeant D. G. Ward and Private S. Maynard 139139 (both awarded the Military Medal for the raid of 14/15 January 1918).
- **Equipment:** Lewis guns in use throughout (firing 5 January; transport by limber to Bully 21/22 January). 18-pounder guns, 4.5-inch howitzers, heavy artillery and Stokes mortars supported the 14/15 January raid under a "Box Barrage". Enemy gas shells were fired at Gravel Pit on 7 January and at the rear areas on 15 January. Steel helmets, gas helmets (drill 31 January), water bottles and haversack luncheons feature in the 23 January march orders. The battalion used the light railway to South Dump for Lewis guns and baggage (2 January) and laid overland routes between company headquarters and front-line posts (17 January).
- **Movements:** 1–2 January at Mazingarbe; 2 January the battalion moved into the line at Hill 70, relieving the 1st Canadian Mounted Rifles (relief complete 8.05 p.m., no casualties). 8/9 January relieved by the 1st Canadian Mounted Rifles and moved back to support (Harts Crater, Quarries, Harrison Crater, O.G.1, Brewery Loop, Tosh Alley/Loos). 14/15 January relieved the 1st Canadian Mounted Rifles in the front line again (relief complete 6.55 p.m.). Inter-company reliefs were carried out on the nights of 5th/6th, 16th/17th, 17th/18th and 19th/20th January. 21/22 January relieved by the 8th Canadian Infantry Battalion and marched to billets at Bully-Grenay (relief complete 9.10 p.m., no casualties). 23 January marched Bully-Grenay to Houdain via Petit-Sains, Hersin and Barlin, arriving 2.45 p.m.; the battalion remained at Houdain for the rest of the month, training, cleaning equipment and parading.
- **Discipline:** None recorded in January 1918.
- **Casualties:** Raid of 14/15 January: one other rank wounded (own casualties); two prisoners taken (one wounded, one unwounded); one enemy light machine gun captured; eight Germans known to be killed. 17 January: one man slightly wounded by an enemy sniper. No casualties are recorded for any of the reliefs or the march. The enemy raid on the 2nd Canadian Mounted Rifles on the night of 2nd/3rd January took one prisoner from that battalion.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The contents sheet's dates do not always match the filed appendices: it gives the Appendix B disposition report as "5.1.18" but the report itself is dated 9.1.18; it gives the Appendix E disposition report as "on 15.1.18" but the report is dated 16.1.18; and its Appendix F description (C.C. 83, 18.1.18) is the order actually filed as Operation Order No. 83 dated 17-1-18. The contents sheet also leaves the dates of C.C. 80 and C.C. 85 blank/illegible. (2) The diary's appendix cross-references are not always exact: the 20 January entry says "See Appendix F" for the 19th/20th inter-company relief, which was ordered by Operation Order No. 84 in Appendix G. (3) The 14 January entry records "Relief completed by 6.55 P.M." under the AM heading. (4) The 15 January PM entry reads "had nothing to report and enemy was encountered", which appears contradictory; it is transcribed as written. (5) The 17 January entry's word "bathmat" is as written; the sense is unclear. (6) Operation Order No. 80 (Appendix D) states the raiding party as two officers and twenty-six other ranks, but the parties listed in paragraph 5 add to fewer men. (7) The disposition-report strength tables do not fully reconcile: on page 23 the totals row adds to 427 against the stated 430; on page 25 the totals row's Bombers and Rifle Grenadiers figures (38 and 37) do not match the column figures (56 and 57); on page 36 the totals row adds to 434 but the legible row figures do not sum to it, notably the Scouts column (the "C" Coy figure of 24 alone exceeds the totals-row figure of 14), and the "B" Coy sub-row figures are hard to read. The 16.1.18 report (page 32) is the only one whose columns and totals reconcile exactly (22 officers, 431 all ranks). (8) The strength sub-column headings in the disposition reports (Officers, Bombers, Bayonet men, Rifle Grenadiers, Lewis Gunners, Scouts, S.Brs, Signallers, Runners) are written vertically in small script and are read as written. (9) The remarks-column initials change during the month: "Jaa" [uncertain] on diary pages (1)–(10), then a different circled monogram from diary page (11) onward. (10) The spelling "wored" (Operation Order No. 78, paragraph 8) and "through out" (10 January) are as written in the original. (11) The diary spells the trench "NOGGIN TRENCH" clearly on 17 January and in the disposition reports; the 16 January spelling is less clear but is read the same. (12) The place "NOCEIN" (6 January) is as written; the intended place name is not clear.
