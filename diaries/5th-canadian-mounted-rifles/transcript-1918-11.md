### 1st November 1918

> ZERO HOUR for the attack and capture of VALENCIENNES was at 0515.
>
> At 0900 instructions were received from Brigade that the Battalion would be ready to move at 0930, and participate in the above operation. Subsequently orders were received that we would not move at 0930, but would "STAND TO" ready to move at "one Hours notice". Later in the day this was cancelled and the Battalion was ordered to "STAND DOWN" for the night.
>
> HONOURS and AWARDS
>
> 110347 Cpl (A/Sgt) MACKENZIE, G. A. MILITARY MEDAL.

### 11th November 1918

> Telegram received from Brigade stating that Armistice had been signed, and would take effect at 1100.
>
> At 1100 hostilities ceased.
>
> Information received from Brigade that the 3rd Cdn Division captured MONS at 0600 this morning.
>
> In the afternoon the Commanding Officer and all available Officers proceeded to MONS to witness the official entry of the Corps Commander. It proved to be a most impressive sight.

### 12th November 1918

> The morning was devoted to Cleaning and Smartening up.
>
> At 1300 orders were received for the Battalion to proceed to MONS as soon as possible.
>
> Battalion moved off at 1400 and arrived at MONS

### 12th November 1918 (continued)

> at 1830. The inhabitants of the city extended a very hearty welcome to the Battalion.

### 13th November 1918

> The Commanding Officer attended a conference at 3rd. Cdn. Div. H.Qs. at 1000, after which he proceeded to Brigade conference at 1400.

> On the evening of the 1st. November, the 5th. C.M.R. Battalion was in Brigade Support, the 4th., 2nd. and 1st., C.M.R. Battalions holding the line of the CANAL immediately NORTH of VALENCIENNES.
>
> November, 2nd. 1918.
>
> 0900. Orders received for the 5th. C.M.R. Battalion to be prepared to pass through the 4th. C.M.R. Battalion, who were holding the CANAL Line just NORTH of VALENCIENNES, and co-operate with the 4th. Canadian Division, who were then clearing the EASTERN outskirts of the City. Two strong patrols were immediately detailed to report to the RIGHT and CENTRE Companies, 4th. C.M.R. Battalion, respectively, and get in touch with the situation on their front.
>
> 1100. Battalion Headquarters established at W.19.b.7.7. in conjunction with 4th. C.M.R. Battalion Headquarters.
>
> 1900 Orders countermanded. Patrols were withdrawn and Battalion H.Qs moved to W.20.c.7.4.
>
> November, 3rd. 1918.
>
> 0800. Orders received from 4th. Cdn. Inf. Brigade, that the 5th. C.M.R. Battalion would relieve the 4th. C.M.R. Battalion in the Line on night 3rd/4th. November, 1918. Operation Order issued accordingly, and Advance Parties sent forward.
>
> 1800. Relief cancelled and the Battalion was ordered to be prepared to move through 4th. C.M.R. Battalion and establish connection with 7th. Canadian Division on the RIGHT. Information at this time, was to the effect that the enemy was retiring.
>
> 1900. In view of the above, all Companies 5th. C.M.R. Battalion, were moved forward to RAISEMES, and Battle H.Qs. was established at W.19.b.3.3.
>
> 1840. Telephone message from Brigade Major (later confirmed by wire) that the 72nd. Canadian Infantry Battalion had occupied ONNAING and that the 5th. C.M.R. Battalion would immediately push forward through ONNAING and secure VICQ.
>
> 1930. Instructions were issued accordingly for "A" Coy. to push forward as far as ONNAING, swing NORTH, and in conjunction with "C" Coy., secure VICQ. "B" Coy. to follow in close support, "D" Coy. being held in Reserve, Battalion Headquarters moved to W.23.d.7.7.

## Page 17 — Appendix I, conclusion — General, Casualties, Captures

> GENERAL.
>
> During the period November 2nd/9th, the Battalion marched and fought over approximately 16 (sixteen) kilometres. The ground for the most part being marshy and flat, and peculiarly adapted to the defensive machine gun tactics of the enemy.
>
> The men were practically without cover, during the whole of this time, and their cheerfulness, endurance, and the vigor with which they pushed their advance, was deserving of the highest credit. There were many instances of individual acts of gallantry. The list of Honours, is being submitted separately.
>
> Continuous telephone communication was maintained with the advancing Companies, and in some cases with the forward patrols, enabling important information to be transmitted to Brigade in the shortest possible time.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–30 November 1918 with no date gaps. 1 November is a short entry (Valenciennes zero hour 0515; the battalion stood to but did not move); 2–9 November are covered only by "SEE APPENDIX I" in the diary text, with the full operations summary in Appendix I (pages 11–17); 10 November is the march from Crespin to Boussu (Operation Order No. 180, Appendix II); 11 November is the Armistice; 12–30 November are post-war occupation, training and ceremonial entries at Mons. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 7 Pages", which matches the seven diary-text pages (pages 4–10).
- **Officers named:** Lt-Col. Rutherford, M.C., M.M. (Victoria Cross, notified 25 November; the commanding officer's signature on pages 10 and 17 is not clearly legible and is marked [uncertain]); Major Todd (inspected the battalion 22 November; returned from leave 14 November); Capt. Caldwell (officiated at Divine Service 24 November); Capt. Beckwith (proceeded on leave 25 November); Capt. Lewis (patrol to Thivencelle, 8 November, Appendix I); Lieut. G. R. Foley, M.C. (Advance Party, Operation Order 180; also Billeting Officer, 18 November — spelled "POLEY" in the diary text and "FOLEY" in the order); Lieut. Ingham [uncertain] (Advance Party, Operation Order 180); Lieut. J. J. Harold (patrol, 5 November, Appendix I); Lieut. W. O. Barnstead (patrol at St. Ayert, 7 November, Appendix I); Lieut. C. G. Dunham (foot-bridge patrol across the Condé Canal, 8 November, Appendix I); Lieut. Massiah (patrol to Condé, 9 November, Appendix I); Lieut. Dixon (Thanksgiving Service, 17 November); Lieut. Belding (returned from leave 14 November); Lieut. N. W. M. Morrison (returned from leave 19 November); Brig.-Genl. D. C. Draper, D.S.O. (Brigade Commander, spent the night of 8th/9th November with "D" Coy., Appendix I); Major-General Loomis (Divisional Commander, inspected the Brigade 30 November). Wounded officers in the 2nd–9th November operations (Appendix I casualties): Lieut. A. W. Loggie, M.C., Lieut. A. E. Gillis, M.C., Lieut. F. I. Andrew (wounded); Lieut. M. H. Rix (wounded at duty); Lieut. H. H. Bluethner (gassed). The Adjutant's signature on Operation Order 180 is not clearly legible and is marked [uncertain].
- **Other ranks named:** 110347 Cpl (A/Sgt) G. A. MacKenzie — Military Medal (1 November). No other rank is named; the battalion band is mentioned in the march order (Operation Order 180) and a detail of two officers and forty other ranks lined the streets for the King of the Belgians on 27 November.
- **Equipment:** Lewis Guns loaded on L.C. Limbers (Operation Order 180); two batteries (K. & M.) of "A" Coy., 3rd Battalion, Canadian Machine Gun Corps, reported for duty 3 November (Appendix I); one captured 77mm field gun with carriage (Appendix I captures). Enemy machine guns, trench mortars, snipers and aeroplanes opposed the advance 4–5 November; the bridge at Thivencelle was found mined on 8 November and the wires cut by the patrol.
- **Movements:** 1 November in Brigade Support north of Valenciennes; 2–9 November the advance through Raismes, Onnaing, Vicq, Quarouble, Crespin, Hensies, St. Ayert, Thivencelle and Pommeroeul (Appendix I); 10 November march from Crespin via Lacroix–Thulin–Hainin to Boussu; 12 November move to Mons, arriving 1830; the battalion remained at Mons for the rest of the month (training, inspections, Divine Service, recreation, the King of the Belgians' visit 27 November, and the Brigade inspection by Major-General Loomis on 30 November). The Canadian Corps was to form part of the Army of Occupation and proceed to Germany on 17 November — this move was then countermanded (16 November) and the battalion stayed at Mons.
- **Discipline:** None recorded in November 1918.
- **Casualties:** 2nd–9th November operations (Appendix I, "Casualties"): killed in action — 9 other ranks; wounded — 3 officers (Loggie, Gillis, Andrew) and 69 other ranks; wounded at duty — Lieut. Rix and 3 other ranks; gassed — Lieut. Bluethner and 1 other rank; missing believed killed — 1 other rank. Total casualties: 5 officers and 83 other ranks (88 all ranks). Captures: 8 prisoners and 1 field gun (77mm) with carriage. No casualties are recorded in the diary text itself for 1 November or 10–30 November.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The diary text for 2–9 November is a single cross-reference ("SEE APPENDIX I"); the fighting is recorded only in the typed Appendix I summary. (2) Several time entries in Appendix I are out of chronological order as typed: 3 November has 1900 followed by 1840, then 1930; 4 November runs 0900, 0950, then 0800, 0840, 0850, 0845; 7 November runs 0130, then 0110, then 0240. (3) Appendix I, page 3, entry 1110 on 5 November refers to instructions "at 0930 5th. November" — a time earlier than the receipt time as typed. (4) Appendix I, page 6, says "For disposition at conclusion of Battalion operations, see Appendix 2", but the contents sheet lists the disposition map (night 7th/8th November) under Appendix 1, and Appendix 2 as printed contains only Operation Order No. 180; the unlabelled sketch map on page 20 may be the "conclusion" disposition, but it carries no title or date. (5) The Billeting/Advance Party officer is spelled "POLEY" in the diary text (18 November) and "FOLEY" (Lieut. G. R. Foley, M.C.) in Operation Order 180. (6) The contents sheet entry for Appendix 2 reads "O.C. 180", while the document itself is headed "Operation Order No. 180". (7) The Place column is blank on every diary-text page. (8) The Remarks column on pages 4–10 carries a repeated mark on every entry (a circled cross-like mark, apparently an initialled mark) that is not clearly legible; it is noted here once rather than repeated after every entry. (9) Page 3 is the reverse of a War Diary form sheet used as a title page; the form heading shows through in reverse. (10) The spelling "Smartening" (for smartening up) and "St AYERT" (for St-Aybert) are as written in the original.
