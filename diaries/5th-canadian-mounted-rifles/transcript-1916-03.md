## Page 3 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118)

Heading: WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through. The page is numbered "65" at the top right.

Place column: "METEREN" throughout.

### Mar 1/16

> Weather bright and milder. Wind S.E. Morning. Platoon drill. Practice of Company in the attack, particular attention being paid to work of Section Commanders, and Artillery formations. Feet inspection. Afternoon. Battalion bathing parade. Eight other ranks [uncertain] for one weeks course in machine gun work. Twenty five others detailed for instruction at Grenade School.

### 2

> Weather clear in morning, turning to rain in afternoon. Wind moderate S.E. Morning Practice in Artillery formations by Companies. Fire discipline. Fire control. Special attention paid to work of section commanders.

### 2.30 p.m.

> At 2.30 p.m. orders received for Battalion to mobilise at Alarm Post, to stand to, in readiness to move off. Order obeyed immediately, the whole Battalion parading fully equipped with Pack Horses and Field Kitchens, in [uncertain] readiness to move off. This order came through owing to counter attacks by Germans being very severe. At 4.30 p.m. the situation having again become normal, companies returned to Billets.

### 3

> Weather dull and heavy, colder. Rain and snow flurries in afternoon. Morning Company in attack, particular attention paid to N.C.O.s in leadership. Charger [uncertain] loading. Grenade throwing.

### Mar 3/16 (contd)

> Afternoon. Concert held at 2 p.m. in Bailleul, the Battalion being given the opportunity to attend.

### 4

> Weather cold. Rain and snow at intervals throughout the day. Morning Inspection of rifles. Bayonet fighting. Charger loading. Company Commanders lectures to N.C.O.s. Afternoon. Recreation, football etc. Confessions at Meteren Church 5 to 6 p.m. for R.C.'s.

### 5 (Sunday)

> Weather cold. Squally. Snow flurries in morning. Morning. Church parade. Roman Catholics paraded for Mass at Meteren Church. Holy Communion at 11 a.m. for Church of England. Afternoon. General cleaning up.

### 6

> Weather cold. Snow flurry in morning clearing in afternoon. Morning. Inspection of rifles. Bayonet fighting. Charger loading. Company Commanders lectures to N.C.O.s. Afternoon. Company Route march.

### 7

> Weather cold and damp. Snow flurries all day. Morning. Inspection of rifles, and as for the previous day. Afternoon. Company Route march.

### Mar 23/16

> Enemy artillery opened fire occasionally during day, dropping shrapnel and high explosive about three quarters of a mile from H.Q.

### 24

> Heavy snowfall during night. Weather cold. North easterly wind. At about 7.45 last night two red flares were seen south of YPRES one directly above the other. At 9.45 p.m. two green flares seen at the same spot. Enemy using powerful searchlight all night, north of YPRES. Message received reporting Zeppelin travelling near YPRES. The Battalion moved off from BELGIAN CHATEAU at 7 p.m. in the following order "A" Coy, "B" Coy, "C" Coy in Battalion Reserve, to relieve the 4th C.M.R. Battalion in the front line. Route taken KRUISSTRAAT - YPRES - MENIN GATE - HELL FIRE CORNER - GORDON HOUSE - YEOMANRY POST. Positions taken up "A" Coy. Trench B4 Right Sub Sector, and R.7 in Support. "B" Coy B.6 - C.1. a - and Appendix, Left Sub Sector. "C" Coy in Q.1.R and R.8.S and R.S.2 in Support. Battalion H.Q. at ARTILLERY PLACE. Relief completed by 11 p.m. No casualties.

### 25

> Wind north west, weather fine and clear. Situation quiet overnight. Snipers on both sides very active during morning. Sniping activity from enemy BIRDCAGE No 111396 Pte. J. W. [uncertain] died of wounds early this morning.

### Mar 25/16 (contd)

> At 9.50 a.m. 4 enemy aeroplanes appeared directly over our lines from the north. Our aircraft and aircraft guns drove them back, but not before enemy planes dropped several pieces of paper intended for us, but wind carried them away. Enemy artillery sent over about 40 small calibre shells (3.3) and a few H.E. (5.9) into SANCTUARY WOOD in morning. In afternoon enemy artillery very active shelling SANCTUARY WOOD and MAPLE COPSE with 5.9 H.E. These came over in fours, at an interval of five minutes, apparently from the bank of HILL 60. At 5.30 p.m. same batteries sent over 10 5.9 H.E. which all landed in vicinity of LOVERS LANE and WARRINGTON AVE.
>
> CASUALTIES No 110349 Pte a. a. [uncertain] Machine gun section wounded in right hand. No 415331 Pte. A. S. [uncertain] "A" Coy, wounded in back. No 110097 Pte Clarkson R "A" Coy, wounded in left thigh. Captain A. C. Charles hit in face by piece of shrapnel. No 110200 Pte Graham S. [uncertain] section wounded in light thigh. No 110285 Sergeant H. T. Kay Bombing section, wounded in both thighs.

### 26

> Wind, mild south east. Weather overcast, with showers. Enemy nervous overnight. Our Bombers, bombed the BIRDCAGE continually during the night. Patrol of three men out during night from the APPENDIX. They reported enemy very nervous.

### Mar 26/16 (contd)

> Our artillery quiet during morning. Enemy artillery also quiet in morning, but opened up at 1.30 p.m. and continued until 5.30 p.m., doing considerable damage to our dug outs at junction of LOVERS WALK and WARRINGTON AVE. Between 5.15 p.m. and 6.25 p.m. our batteries put 72, 18 pound shells in BIRDCAGE, partly demolishing it.
>
> Casualties. No 111327 Pte Mills. H. J. Killed by fragment of H.E. shell hitting him head.

### 27

> Wind mild south west. Weather overcast and cloudy and cold. Enemy very nervous all night. Bombing patrol went out to investigate reports mining sap in enemy line at J.13.c.2.8. but after approaching to within 10 yards, were stopped by exceptionally strong wire. A charge was placed under a sand bag and wire blocks about 15 yards from enemy trench. At the time the charge was exploded about 50 bombs were thrown. Our party returned safely. It was found as a result of the expedition that the enemy were not mining, but a sap had been run out about 15 yards, giving enemy great advantage. Our and enemy artillery active with H.E. shells all day.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 March 1916 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text 13 Pages", which matches the thirteen diary-text pages (pages 3–15).
- **Officers named:** Lieutenant Cotton (advance party to Camp "F", 19 March); Lieut. Hewson (advance party to Belgian Chateau, 20 March); Captain A. C. Charles (hit in the face by a piece of shrapnel, 25 March); Reverend Captain Fallis (Holy Communion, 12 March); Lieut. J. [uncertain] ("A" Company, reinforcement, 11 March); Lieut. J. G. Rogers; Lieut. D. W. [uncertain] ("B" Company); Lieut. J. R. Macdonald ("B" Company); Lieut. H. G. Rogers; Lieut. J. J. Fraser (Machine Gun Section). The diary is signed by Lt.-Col. C. W. Baker, O.C. 5th C.M.R. Battalion.
- **Other ranks named:** Pte J. W. [uncertain] No 111396 (died of wounds, 25 March); Pte A. A. [uncertain] No 110349, Machine Gun Section (wounded in the right hand, 25 March); Pte A. S. [uncertain] No 415331, "A" Company (wounded in the back, 25 March); Pte R. Clarkson No 110097, "A" Company (wounded in the left thigh, 25 March); Pte S. Graham No 110200 (wounded in the [uncertain] thigh, 25 March); Sergeant H. T. Kay No 110285, Bombing Section (wounded in both thighs, 25 March); Pte H. J. Mills No 111327 (killed by a fragment of H.E. shell, 26 March).
- **Movements:** The battalion remained at Meteren until 19 March, then marched via Bailleul and Locre to Camp "F", relieving the 2nd Leinster Regiment; on the night of 20/21 March it relieved the 8th Battalion, Royal West Kent Regiment, at Belgian Chateau; on 24 March it moved into the front line in the Ypres sector (Sanctuary Wood / Hill 60 area, via Menin Gate and Hell Fire Corner), relieving the 4th C.M.R. Battalion; relieved by the 4th C.M.R. Battalion on 28 March and back at Chateau Belge.
- **Casualties:** Killed: Pte H. J. Mills No 111327 (26 March). Died of wounds: Pte J. W. [uncertain] No 111396 (25 March). Wounded 25 March: Ptes A. A. [uncertain], A. S. [uncertain], R. Clarkson, S. Graham, Sergeant H. T. Kay, and Captain A. C. Charles.
- **Action:** First tour in the Ypres salient: heavy shelling of Sanctuary Wood and Maple Copse (25 March), the battalion's bombers bombing the enemy "Birdcage" strong point night after night, 72 eighteen-pounder shells put into the Birdcage on 26 March, and a bombing patrol's demolition of an enemy sap on 27 March. A Zeppelin was reported near Ypres on 24 March, and enemy aeroplanes dropped propaganda leaflets over the battalion's lines on 25 March.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The 7 March entry copies in full the Brigade Headquarters order for the "Kemmel Defences" — the battalion's assigned sector (the Toronto area) in the event of a serious attack, with distribution, route, supplies, refilling points and field ambulance stations. (2) The diary still writes "Squadrons" once on 18 March, a leftover from the mounted-rifle days. (3) On 2 March the battalion was mobilised at its alarm post in full marching order in response to severe German counter-attacks elsewhere, then stood down at 4.30 p.m. (4) The whole battalion was inoculated against typhoid and enteric fever on 8–9 March. (5) The 25 March casualty list is written as a separate headed "CASUALTIES" paragraph at the foot of the page.
