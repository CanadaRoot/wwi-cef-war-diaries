# War Diary — 9th Canadian Light Trench Mortar Battery

## Source
- **Unit:** 9th Canadian Light Trench Mortar Battery (formed at Meteren, March 1916, as C-9-1 and C-9-2, 9th Canadian Infantry Brigade; reorganised and consolidated 20 June 1916 as the 9th Canadian Trench Mortar Battery; amalgamated and designated 9th Light Trench Mortar Battery 9 September 1916)
- **Archival reference:** RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, folder 596; microfilm reel T-10810, file part 9
- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2005971 — "War diaries - 9th Brigade, Trench Mortar Battery = Journal de guerre - 9e Brigade, Batterie de mortiers de tranchée", 1916/03/15–1916/09/30
  - Record: https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=2005971
- **Page images:** 47 in the LAC viewer (March pp. 1–8; June pp. 1–4; July pp. 1–11; August pp. 1–11; September pp. 1–13). No April or May 1916 pages exist in the manifest — the diary jumps from the March–June synopsis (Volume 1A) to 20 June. Fetched from LAC via the IIIF manifest `https://digitalmanifest.bac-lac.gc.ca/DigitalManifest/1/2005971`; page images saved alongside this file (`page-NNNN-<Month>_1916_p._N.jpg`, `manifest.json`).
- **Commanding officers:** Capt. H. J. Horan (52nd Battalion), O.C. until 20 June 1916; Capt. John E. Ryerson, O.C. from 20 June 1916 — killed by a sniper on **19 September 1916** about 10:00 AM while reconnoitring the ground for the 43rd and 58th Battalions' attack, and buried that evening near the Albert–Pozières road (diary, 19 September 1916). Secondary sources that give 9 September are wrong; the diary is the primary record. Lieut. R. MacDonald took command after Ryerson's death and signed the September diary as O.C.
- **Note on the record title:** LAC titles this record "War diaries - 9th Brigade, Trench Mortar Battery" — the battery's designation 20 June–9 September 1916. There is no separate LAC record titled for the "9th Canadian Light Trench Mortar Battery". The unit's 1917 desk diary is a different record (RG9-III-D-1, vol. 4686, folder 35-12, Item ID 5744881) and is not digitised.

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the synoptical March–June diary is typewritten, including its typos; the June–September daily entries are handwritten).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; places are recorded as written, with the trench-map sheet references (e.g. Sh. 28, S.P. 16) as the location detail of the time.

## Page 4 — Diary text, page two (March 1916, p. 4)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" (the "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading struck through).

> **March 15th 1916** — Officers, N.C.O's and men [struck through in original: one word] from 43rd, 52nd, 58th and 60th Battalions met at Meteren for Trench Mortar Course – to be known as Batteries C-9-1 and C-9-2, having full complement as called for. Capt. Horan of 52nd in addition to being officer of left half No.1 Battery was Brigade Officer of the Trench Mortars, responsible for organising and work. The Unit was not officially formed for some time. It is regrettable that Capt. Horan was not aware of the necessity of rendering a diary – which results in only being able to give a synopsis of work done, as follows :-
>
> **Mar. 15 to 26th.** — For a week the Batteries went through a course; then joined brigade, proceeding with it to a point near Poperinghe.
>
> **Mar. 27 to Apr. 12th** — Battery C-9-1 with two officers proceeded to right sector of Brigade frontage, relieving 7th Bde. T. M.B. taking over two 3.7's of that brigade. Up to 7th May we were without Stokes guns. About 4th Apr. C-9-2 Bty. relieved in same sector. During this period, owing to misunderstanding on part of other brigades, we only handled half of frontage – from now on handling whole Brigade frontage.
>
> **Apr. 12/13th.** — Relieved by 8th Brigade. During this period there was nothing of importance to report; Ypres salient being quiet; but more registering and shooting should have been done. The usual trouble, however, was experienced more or less throughout the tour, viz- the open antagonism of the Trench and Battalion Commanders to the mortars and their personnel, in fact, the whole period covered by this synopsis was taken up in a struggle to educate these commanders re usefulness of mortars. The attached copy of report submitted to 9th Brigade speaks for itself and shows that in addition to this opposition there was the want of cooperation on the part of the Light Trench Mortars of the other brigades and the Divisional Mortars (mediums).
>
> **Apr. 19/20th to 6/7th May.** — This period was divided into two 8-day reliefs; C-9-1 going in 20-28 and C-9-2 to end of tour, one officer and gun crew with extra man going to Hooge cellars and crater and another officer and crew going to Sanctuary Wood. In this way each officer and crew had just 4 days in the front line – the O.C. after 4 days at Hooge having his headquarters in Zillebeke Village. Re firing. During this tour the remarks in last apply here. We had in Hooge a 4" R.M.L. and two 3.7s. The condition of trenches in Hooge and Sanctuary Wood aggravated the difficulty of getting much firing done, some of the Batt'n Commanders considering it foolish to draw fire where the trenches were not in fit condition to stand punishment. During this tour towards end an attack took place on Sanctuary Wood when the 3.7 under Lt. Mc.Donald did good work.

## Page 5 — Diary text, page three (March 1916, p. 5)

> **May 14th to June 1st 1916** — During this period the Bateries wnet into the Right Sector and Sanctuary Wood. Dispositions were as follows. 1. Right sector and crew. 2. Sanctuary Wood, officer and crew. 3. Maple Copse Sgt. Major [uncertain] gun crew in charge 4 Stokes ready at all times to go up. 4 Zillebeke Village Gun crew and extra men in reserve. 5 The other Battery for first 8 days back in Rest Camp relieving about 22nd May. The guns in use were Stokes; 2 - 4" R.M.L and 3- 3.7s . The remarks re difficulty in firing applied this trip and in addition we had to contend with orders re keeping Stokes 1000 yds back of front line except when firing. It certainly made prompt retaliation and satisfactory registering almost impossible. At end of month 3 officers went to Stokes Gun Course. The Batteries were relieved June 1st and as the German attack took place June 2-3rd, both batteries stood for several days ready for action
>
> **June 5-15th.** — In evening orders received to proceed with 4 Stokes guns to Zillebeke Bund. These were carried; 4 gun crews; 2 officers. At the Bund we awaited for orders for two days when two guns and ammunition were taken up to S.P. 16, near Maple Copse in anticipation of an attack. Nothing of moment occurred. On the 11th June, Mr. Ryerson was ordered up with two crews as a relief. On the way up he received instructions covering our attack on 10th, 11th June. More ammunition was brought up but as it poured for over 24 hours before the attack; owing to the fact there was no shelter for the ammunition and owing to the condition of the fields it was well night impracticable to do any firing. On 13th June relief went up to Dormy House - the S.P.16 having become untenable. On 15th June we received orders to retire to Divisional Rest with the Brigade.
>
> **June 16-18th.** — In "rest" doing regular work, drill, etc. Between trips drilling was proceeded with covering physical drill; route marches, gun practise, lectures, bathing parades, etc. While too little was accomplished by my batteries owing to a certain extent to our all being new to the work and to circumstances referred to - a great deal of good work was done in the line of educating commanders, but a minimum of results will never be reached until all LIGHTS of the three brigades are under central of a Divisional Officer - as in the case of the mediums. This would result in more satisfactory arrangements as regards the requisitioning for ammunition; building dugouts; emplacements and so on. The handling of these and other matters by each brigade separately, sadly militated against successful results.
>
> All of which is respectfully submitted.
>
> [signed] *H. J. Horan, Capt.* [signature]

> Sirs;
>
> General Progress report. Recommendations. Gun positions. Results Ap 19/May 7th.
>
> I attach two separate memoranda (1) Locations of Guns and Dugouts and (2) resume of results of last "tour".
>
> This report is made for the good of the service. You should know what we have accomplished re assisting Commanders by firing ..by educating them re merits of Mortars and so on. By judicious handling all are now looking upon Trench Mortar people as their friends in need.
>
> The 9th Brigade have more than done its share of work outside the firing. A dugout was built in Sanctuary Wood left. Three were built in right sector. A Bombproof ammunition also built there. And we were ready to go ahead with the Hooge Crater Dugout had cooperation between Brigades been more in evidence. In addition we have done sand bag work for the other Brigades back here. We also built parapets for firing Mortars from and a Grenade parapet.
>
> More telling results would obtained if the three Brigades and the Divisional Mediums were to co operate more instead of carrying on mereas separate units..or entities. The officers should get together; discuss matters froms broad standpoint and play into each others' hands.
>
> Here are some pertinent points. So far as 9th Brigade is concerned, Battalion & Company Commanders meet us more than half way. What are results so far as other Brigades are concerned &.
>
> The placing of guns; requisitioning for ammunition; building of Stores and dugouts and the firing should be done more with the knowledge of all concerned. Following shows what I mean.
>
> So far as officers of C/9/ 1 & 2 are concerned I insist that they keep in touch with Commanders as soon as these come into trenches; advising as to location of guns ( including others than those of 9th ); discussing a more or less definite plan of action... answering any questions re guns and pointing out that we are at the service of the Commanders. I am trying to get blueprints of locations of all guns ( no use having plans covering each units ) to be handed Commanders.

> While the "Lights" have to get permission of Commanders re firing, the Divisional (Medium) people can fire without. A Commander forbids us to fire and presently hears a 60 pounder fired. He is nonplussed.
>
> To give concrete instances of mixups. A Batt. Commander (49th) rang me up early one day demanding that I discontinue firing as he had already had 17 casualties through Mortars. He wanted the Divisional people, altho I believe they had not fired. Shortly another Commander (58th) [uncertain] asked me to go up to see him and asked me why we werent doing anything: I pointed out that in the attack a short time before we had done good work. He assured me his officers had said not. Later we found they referred to the Mediums who had been silent.
>
> Major Chalmer spoke of advisability of embodying in daily letter some data enlightening Commanders re Mortars. If the Divisional authorities would follow this up, too, it would remove troubles.
>
> The requisitioning for ammunition independently by each Brigade is of course necessary but each should keep the other posted as to what is ordered &c : otherwise present conditions will continue; namely Batteries arrive [uncertain] in Trenches to find perhaps very little ammunition on hand and non- ordered. Taking over of Stores should include any in reserve and not only that near guns.
>
> This applies to Trench Stores to a certain extent. Waders should be passed from one Brigade to another. The 7th did this with us but the incoming Brigade did not want to accept mine.
>
> The matter of convenient points ( such as Zelebeke or half way House ) to store reserves of ammunition came up. This should be gone into by the three Brigades..otherwise we will have separate Reserve Stores for each Brigade. Only by accident I found we had some at Island Cottage.
>
> The taking in of our own guns is nearer settlement but there's still too much of the idea " If it suits me I'll take over what guns of yours I need"
>

## Page 11 — Diary text (June 1916, p. 3)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" (heading struck through). Handwritten; headed "9th Canadian Trench Mortar Battery."

> Place: Belgium, Sh. 27. H. 13. [uncertain] | Date: 1916, June 20th
>
> **20 June** — C/9/1 and C/9/2 Trench Mortar Batteries reorganized and consolidated - Capt H J Horan returning to his Unit 52nd Can. Battn. and Lieut J. E. Ryerson taking command. Battery to be known in future as 9th Canadian Trench Mortar Battery. [Remarks: 3rd Can Divn R.O. 641 [uncertain]; initials J.E.R. [uncertain]]
>
> **21** — A.M. Training - Physical Drill, Bayonet Fighting - Gun Drill. P.M. [struck through in original: one word] 9th Brigade held Sports day.
>
> **22** — Training - Lieut R. R. Macdonald [uncertain] left on leave
>
> **23** — Training - 4 men returned to their battalions on a/c defects of eyesight, inefficiency etc [uncertain] men obtained in their place.
>
> **24** — Practise shoot with live Stokes ammunition. 4 Stokes guns used at range of 200/250 yards. Wind rather strong making good shooting difficult. Gun Captains making their own allowance for wind, range and making their own corrections. Endeavoured to get a proportion of air bursts. Found difficult to find what variation from clinometer reading (as regards cutting fuse) was necessary to obtain satisfactory air bursts. At 200 yards reading one second should be deducted from clinometer reading.
>
> **25 (Sunday)** — No parades
>
> **26** — A.M. Route march - [struck through in original: one word] Heavy marching order. - P.M. 9th Brigade Horse show, Mule races, jumping, riding + driving competitions.
>
> **27** — Training.
>
> **28** — Made preparations to go to Trenches - Inspection equipment, Gas Helmets etc. Lieut. J.E. Ryerson went to Ypres Ramparts to 7th Can. Inf Bde HQs. and thence to Half way House to make arrangement to [uncertain] and gather information re relief of 7th Can. T.M Battery
>
> **29** — In Trenches, 6.30 P.M. 3 officers and 25 O.R. marched to Half way House - 4 Guns + Equipment by Transport. Took over positions, dug outs etc. held by 7th Canadian Trench Mortar Battery - as follows:- 1 Officer, 1 Crew with 1 Gun in S.P.16 / 1 Gun + Crew in R.S.8 - 2 Guns, 1 Officer and 10-O.R. in Half way House. Canadian Corps Trench [uncertain] assumed responsibility as regards Stokes of front then held by 9th Can Inf Bde. 60 - 74. [Remarks: Trench Map. ZILLEBEKE Sh. 28. N.W 1/10.000]
