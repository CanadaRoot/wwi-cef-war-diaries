## Page 39 — Diary text (September 1916, p. 5)

> Place: FRANCE | Date: 1916, September 6–7
>
> **6 September (contd)** — 2.00 PM Rapid fire test - competition between two gun crews in rapid fire. One crew fired 34 rounds in 57 seconds and the other 33 rounds in 59 seconds. Range 350 yds Red cartridge. [Remarks: 9th Bde. Operation Order No. 31; MAP HAZEBROUCK 5A 1/100.000]
>
> **7 September** — 2.00 AM Reveille. 3.00 AM Breakfast. 4.00 AM Parade. In preparation for move to new area. 9th Brigade operation order No. 31. 4.30 AM Left billet pushing 7 push wagons with mortars, stores, cooking dixies etc. - 3 mortars and several boxes of stores etc. had to be left behind owing to absolute inadequacy of push wagons as transport. They are very poorly constructed for this work - Stokes boxes will not fit in and when loaded are very difficult to balance - no drag ropes are provided. One G.S. wagon would be far more useful. Why should men of Light trench mortar batteries do the work that is done in other units by horses and mules? It was a tedious march as the loads kept slipping - kits fell off - the wheels would strike a stone cobble and would wrench the handles of the wagon out of the hands of the men - the whole load would then upset on the road. The hills about Cassel especially were very hard pulling and the men soon became fatigued even with only about one half of what the load they would have had if everything had been brought. 9.30 AM Arrived at BAVINCHOVE - wagons + loads were loaded on motor lorries and the men boarded other lorries - 25 men per lorry. 9.15 [uncertain] Lorries started - went to Railway Station at ARQUES.

## End of the diary

The LAC record (Item ID 2005971) ends 30 September 1916. The battery was redesignated the 9th Light Trench Mortar Battery on 9 September 1916 (per secondary sources), but the diary continues to use "9th Canadian Trench Mortar Battery" throughout September. The 1917 desk diary (RG9-III-D-1, vol. 4686, folder 35-12, Item ID 5744881) is a separate record and is not digitised.
