## Page 15 — Diary text (July 1916, p. 3)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" (heading struck through). Handwritten; headed "Original", "JULY 1916", "9th CANADIAN TRENCH MORTAR BATTERY".

> Place: BELGIUM, S.E. of YPRES | Date: 1916, July 1
>
> **1 July** — Battery still in trenches as on 30 JUNE. DAY. No firing or work done. NIGHT 1 Gun crew from [1/2?] HALF-WAY HOUSE relieved crew at S.P.16. Lieuts READE & RYERSON made reconnaissance of HILL 57 and vicinity I 24 B 2 3 for position to build defensive emplacement. Suitable location found. Crews worked improving emplacements and cover. No firing done. [Remarks: Trench Map ZILLEBEKE Sh. 28 (part) 1/10.000]
>
> **2 July** — DAY No firing or work done. NIGHT Took over 33 rds. Ammu 3" Stokes left in trench 71 [uncertain] by 7th Can TMB. Lieut. RYERSON with 1 Corpl [uncertain] + 1 man made reconnaissance of NEW BOND St. ROSLYN RD, REGENT St to the CULVERT and Trenches 6 B to 74 (I 17 b-d - I 18 A-c) This district unsuitable for STOKES Mortars. Enemy trenches out of range for offensive operations and ground in front of our trenches very low, wet and badly cut up by shell fire that it is most unlikely that enemy would attack here so that defensive positions hardly necessary. Considerable work would be required to build defensive positions. Whole of this ground under observation by day. No firing done. Crews employed as on 2nd [as written]
>
> **3 July** — DAY No firing or work done. Instructions received from Bde Major 9th Can Inf Bde to put 1 STOKES mortar at CULVERT and one in HILL ST digging them in temporarily and commencing to build proper emplacements. 10.00 PM Lt. McKENZIE left HALF-WAY HOUSE with Crew of 1 NCO. 3 men, 1-3" STOKES Mortar, and carrying party of 1 NCO 15 men with 51 rounds STOKES ammunition, proceeding to CULVERT. [uncertain] Mortar set up in temporary open emplacement. Ammunition put under cover. Mortar taken into men's shelter at daybreak. 10.00 PM Lt READE with crew of 1 NCO 3 men left S.P.16 with 1 STOKES mortar and proceeded to HILL St where mortar was set up in temporary open emplacement, position at S.P.16 abandoned, crew camping some 20 yds to Hill St during the night.
>
> **4 July** — DAY No firing done. Site [uncertain] selected in HILL St [uncertain] for defensive emplacement. NIGHT Defensive emplacement in HILL St commenced. Lt MacKENZIE made further reconnaissance of vicinity of CULVERT. [uncertain] Officer [uncertain] refused to allow any work to be done building emplacement for STOKES mortar for fear of indicating to enemy that position was occupied. Casualties - 1 O.R. wounded by 77 cm [as written] shell which made direct hit on men's dug-out at HALFWAY HOUSE. Little damage done.

## Page 16 — Diary text (July 1916, p. 4)

> Place: BELGIUM, S.E. of YPRES | Date: 1916, July 5–8
>
> **5 July** — DAY No firing or work done. NIGHT Work on HILL St emplacement proceeded with. Work commenced building emplacement in Culvert - large hole made by shell in centre of [uncertain] MENIN ROAD (breaking one of the Culverts) used for the purpose. Part of culvert on either side of hole intact and provides cover for crew and ammunition. Crew at Culvert relieved by crew from HALFWAY HOUSE. No firing done. Crew at R S 8 employed improving position there. [Remarks: TRENCH MAP ZILLEBEKE Sheet 28 (part) 1/10.000]

> **6 July** — DAY No firing done. Crew at HILL St employed selecting logs etc of trees in SANCTUARY WOOD which would be suitable for use in construction of emplacement. NIGHT Work proceeded with. Officers at CULVERT and HALFWAY HOUSE interchanged. No firing done.
>
> **7 July** — DAY Same as yesterday. NIGHT Work proceeded with. do HILL St do do do [ditto marks]
>
> **8 July** — 5.30 PM Lieut. RYERSON received verbal instructions from B.Gen. Hill, Brigadier 9th Can. Inf Bde. (duly confirmed by 9th Can Inf Bde operation order 25) regarding operations to be carried out on night 8/9 July. STOKES Mortars in HILL St and CULVERT were to be used, although neither of the emplacements were complete. A temporary splinter proof cover was hurriedly erected over HILL St emplacement, a hole being left just large enough for discharge of shell. 7.00 PM Lieut. READE was given charge of HILL St gun. 44 rounds [uncertain] were prepared at HALFWAY HOUSE and carried by reserve men there to gun position, all the available red or long range cartridges being used. 9.00 PM Lt. RYERSON left HALFWAY HOUSE for CULVERT to give detailed instructions to Lt. MacDONALD who was put in charge of operations there. A carrying party was arranged to carry ammunition from HALFWAY HOUSE to CULVERT as supply was low - 45 rounds carried. 11.45 PM STOKES Mortar in HILL St (I 24 B 2 1/2 1 1/2) [uncertain] opened fire on enemy bombing post in our old front line trench #61 and from J 19. A. 0. 2 1/2 to J 19 A. 7. 2. Range 360 to 380 yards

## Page 17 — Diary text (July 1916, p. 5)

> Place: [blank] | Date: 1916, July 8/9–10
>
> **8/9 July (contd)** — 44 rounds were fired - firing ceased 12.00 AM promptly. Bombing officer of 58th Battn was in trench at junction of our old front line + Darlington [uncertain] where he was able to observe our fire. He reported that fire was well directed and placed, searching bombing post + vicinity and enfilading trench #61 (old). Bombing post unoccupied on arrival of Bombing party of 58th Battn. It was later reported that ground in this vicinity was badly cut up. 11.45 PM STOKES Mortar in Culvert opened fire on our old bombing post No. 8 (Hooge) and trench on either side of it, mortar being traversed gradually. 12.00 AM Fire was directed on trench at head of FORRESTER LANE. 12.00 [as written] Ceased fire. 40 rounds in all fired. Bombing officer of 52nd Battn. reported old bombing post #8 badly broken up and scarcely recognizable, and was unoccupied on his arrival. Results of setting up guns at night by compass from bearings obtained from maps more satisfactory than anticipated. No casualties sustained. The [uncertain] splinter proof cover was most valuable.

## Page 20 — Diary text (July 1916, p. 8)

> Place: FRANCE | Date: 1916, July 22–24
>
> **22 July** — 6.00 AM Registering on trenches in preparation for tactical scheme. 9.30 AM / 12.00 [uncertain] Tactical scheme. 4 mortars were dug in, in bush of wood K34 D. On a signal all commenced rapid fire for one minute on system on model trenches - then lifted and barraged support + communication trenches. 60 rounds in all were fired. Results satisfactory - especially as a demonstration to officers of Bde in tactical use of Stokes mortars, rapidity and effect of fire. Casualties nil. 2.30 [uncertain] Model defensive emplacement completed.
>
> **23 July** — 9.00 AM / 12.00 [uncertain] Men attached given [uncertain] practise in firing live ammunition. Lieut. MacDONALD and 3 Corporals returned from Trench Mortar School TERDEGHEM. Model emplacement tested out to see what traverse could be obtained. Emplacement is 6'x6' with floor 8' below surface of ground - an aperture 2'x3' being left in roof for discharge of shell. This allowed traverse sufficient to bombard 100 yds of trench supposing the emplacement to be 200 yds behind front line. = On examination of craters it was found that total traverse of Mortar at 300 yards is LESS than that at 200 yds. whereas various Trench Mortar Schools including 2nd Army school at TERDEGHEM teach that traverse at 300 yards is 50% greater than at 200 yards.
>
> **24 July** — 9.00 AM Parade to STEENVOORDE for pay. 10.30 AM Lecture on MAP READING by Lieut. READE. 2.00 PM Further experiments carried out re traverse of Mortar using "Dud" bombs. Confirmed about [uncertain] statement but results were puzzling as traverse at 200 yds was greatest - 300 yds next and that at 250 yards least of all. Further experiments will have to be carried out before [uncertain] results can be considered as correct.

## Page 23 — Diary text (July 1916, p. 11)

> Place: BELGIUM | Date: 1916, July 29/30–31
>
> **29/30 July (contd)** — NIGHT (contd) 3 Mortars in Mount Sorrel fired 56 rounds on front line + bombing post opposite trench 47 and front line opposite trench 50 - Object - retaliation. Results - Good. Casualties nil.
>
> **30 July** — Day. Work commenced on "mining sets" dug-outs for gun crews. Also improving emplacement taken over in damaged condition.
>
> **30/31 July** — NIGHT 1.30 AM Mortar in Crab Crawl fired 15 rounds as retaliation to Rifle grenade fire. Enemy ceased fire promptly + were not heard from for balance of night. Mortars in Mount Sorrel fired 45 rounds in retaliation to enemy TM fire. Results - Good - enemy ceased fire. In early evening 1 round was fired from repaired emplacement for purpose of registration and enemy immediately fired several rounds Shrapnel and two H.E. The latter appeared to be short and enemy fired one green flare + his fire ceased. {Reserve men used each night as ration party and to carry ammunition.
>
> **31 July** — DAY Quiet. Work on dug-out continued.
>
> **General** — 43rd Battn report Mortar in Crab Crawl of value to them in silencing enemy Rifle grenade fire. Also that direct hit was obtained on enemy bombing post and that on more than one occasion men in our bombing post heard commotion in enemy trench following our fire. This is a distinct change of attitude of this battalion toward our trench mortars. 58th Battn. report Mortars in Mount Sorrel very useful in silencing Enemy TM fire - especially as retaliation can be obtained immediately wherever and whenever wanted which is more difficult with Artillery. Stokes Mortars especially useful in this sector as lines are so close artillery fire cannot be brought to bear on enemy front line.
>
> [signed] *John E. Ryerson, Capt., O.C. 9th Canadian Trench Mortar Battery.*
