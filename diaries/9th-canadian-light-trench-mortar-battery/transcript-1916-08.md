## Page 26 — Diary text (August 1916, p. 3)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" (heading struck through). Handwritten; headed "Month of August 1916", "9th Canadian Trench Mortar Battery."

> Place: BELGIUM | Date: 1916, August 1–3
>
> **1 August** — In trenches as on 31st July 1916. Mortars in Mount Sorrel fired 45 rounds on enemy front line and suspected TM Emplacement - Enemy fairly active with his trench mortars. Mortar in Crab Crawl fired 10 rounds in retaliation to Rifle grenades, on enemy front line. Enemy activity in this portion of front - slight. Results of fire seemed good. Casualties - nil. [Remarks: MAP ZILLEBEKE Part of Sh. 28 1/10.000]
>
> **2 August** — 2.30 AM 15 rounds fired from Crab Crawl - enemy rifle grenades silenced - a proportion of air bursts obtained apparently with good results. Day 60 rounds fired from mortars in Mount Sorrel at request of infantry. 9.30 PM 16 " " " " Crab Crawl. 11.00 PM Opposite Mt Sorrel enemy fired 7 flares in quick succession from a point [uncertain] and a machine gun [uncertain] in front of his trench about opposite our trench 51 [uncertain]. Fire was directed on this point with Stokes - first shot made a direct hit - no more was heard from that quarter during the night. Casualties nil.
>
> **3 August** — 1.30 AM One of our patrols encountered an enemy patrol opp trenches 50/51 and a number of bombs were thrown. Stokes mortars in Mt Sorrel opened fire on trenches behind where encounter took place firing 60 rounds - Enemy in these trenches [uncertain] threw a large number of bombs into "No Man's Land" doing no damage. Following the blowing of a small mine near Hill 60 - mortars in Mt Sorrel fired 50 rounds on enemy front + support lines.

> Place: BELGIUM | Date: 1916, August 11–13
>
> **11 August** — 53 rounds fired by mortars in Hill 60 during the day and evening in retaliation to Enemy Trench Mortars + Rifle Grenades. 111 rounds fired by mortars in Mount Sorrel do do
>
> **12 August** — 8.30 AM Enemy opened heavy bombardment on Hill 60 sector + to right of it. 10.00 AM Enemy infantry attacked - During this bombardment and attack Mortars #1 + #2 in this sector fired 129 rounds and #2 37 rounds. On enemy front line and creating barrage in "No Man's Land". Corpl of 60th Can Battn. states he saw bombs exploding among enemy troops as they advanced across No Man's Land. Dug-outs + emplacements stood the bombardment well. Casualties - nil. Mortars in Mount Sorrel joined in retaliatory bombardment firing #1 - 35 rounds #2 - 25 rounds #3 - 25 rounds. - Bombing post opp trench 48 - registered on by #3 mortar - 8 rounds being fired.
>
> **13 August** — 2.30 AM / 1.00 AM [as written] 10 rounds fired on Bombing post [uncertain] destroying it. 9 " " " " opp trench 50 - destroying it. 1.00 AM [as written] Enemy bombarded vicinity #1 and #2 mortars in Mount Sorrel with 4.2" H.E. Obtaining a direct hit on #1 - exploding 142 rounds ammunition, completely destroying mortar and emplacement as well as 4 rifles and some other equipment. #2 emplacement was half filled with earth + debris burying the mortar and partially burying the crew. The mortar was quickly dug out and was again ready for action in a short time. Casualties NIL

## Page 30 — Diary text (August 1916, p. 7)

> Place: BELGIUM | Date: 1916, August 13–16
>
> **13 August (contd)** — Day Quiet. 11.00 PM Mortars in Hill 60 fired 39 rounds in retaliation to rifle grenades.
>
> **14 August** — 3.00 PM / 6.00 PM Enemy bombarded Hill 60 sector with trench mortars and Minenwerfer (heavy) doing considerable damage. About 5.00 PM a direct hit was obtained on Emplacement #2, completely destroying emplacement, mortar, 170 rounds ammunition, [struck through in original: one word] and rendering adjoining dug-outs unserviceable. Casualties 3 Killed - 6 wounded and shocked - 4 men were in dug-outs and door was sealed by earth from explosion, men were wounded by flying pieces and by partial collapse of dug-out and affected by gas. Owing to casualties and destruction of ammunition it is unknown what firing was done during the day. 2 of the casualties being from crew of mortar #1. - gassed. 7.00 PM Wired to billet for 2 NCOs + 6 men to replace casualties which arrived at Railway Dug-outs about 10.00 PM. New mortar set up in Old 60 [uncertain] TM emplacement in Allen St. 25 yds in rear of [uncertain] one destroyed.
>
> **15 August** — Quiet. No firing done. Corp. Casey left on Special Leave for England. 10.00 AM Pte. Jeacke [uncertain] buried in cemetery near Railway Dug-outs. Capt Masters held service
>
> **16 August** — Quiet. No firing done. Lieut MacDonald relieved Sergt. Maxwell in Mt. Sorrel and crews also relieved. Lieut Reade in Hill 60 relieved by Sergt. Sturrock. 12 AM [as written] Corpl A.F. Nelson buried beside Pte Jeacke - Capt Masters held service.

[Note: the 14 August bombardment killed three men; only two are named in the diary — Pte. Jeacke and Corpl. A. F. Nelson, buried 15 and 16 August. The third man killed is not named in the diary.]

>
> **18 August** — Quiet. Work proceeded with
>
> **19 August** — 2.00 PM Enemy bombarded Hill 60 sector during afternoon - Mortars in that sector fired 75 rounds. Emplacement #1 caved in from shells bursting in vicinity - sap leading to emplacement destroyed - mortar buried but was recovered later by crew. Casualties nil. Mortars in Mount Sorrel fired 37 rounds during afternoon in retaliation. Work on emplacements continued.
>
> **20 August** — Lieut. MacKenzie left for Special Course in Bombing for applicants for Bombing Instructors for Canada. Mortars [uncertain] in Hill 60 fired 13 rounds and in Mt Sorrel 26 rounds during the day - in retaliation to TM and Rifle Grenade fire. Work on emplacements continued.
>
> **21 August** — 2/Corpl. Rice [as written] left for Grenade School Terdeghem for course in Stokes. Mortars in Hill 60 fired 6 rounds only. Enemy active in Mount Sorrel sector with Artillery and Trench Mortars. Our mortars fired 45 rounds during our retaliatory Bombardment.
