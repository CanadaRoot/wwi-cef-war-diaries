# War Diary — 5th Canadian Divisional Signal Company (Artillery Detachment) — February 1918

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Divisional Signal Company (Artillery Detachment)
- **Month:** February 1918 (Volume 6)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004909 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5006, microfilm reel T-10854, files 694–695; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** February 1918, p. 1 – p. 6 (image files page-0032 – page-0037 of the 132-page diary)
- **February 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 32–37 of 132): cover, contents, diary text 1–28 February, including a monthly signal-traffic table, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-26.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Handwritten diary text throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"), in a third hand (different from both the August–December 1917 hand and the January 1918 hand). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. No map coordinates are given in the diary. "H.Q." = Headquarters; "Bdes." = Brigades (the 13th and 14th Brigades, C.F.A.); "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "C.D.A." = Canadian Divisional Artillery; "C.D.A.C." = Canadian Divisional Ammunition Column; "Divarty" = Divisional Artillery; "D.A." = Divisional Artillery; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Train [uncertain — as written]; "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "T.M.Bs." = Trench Mortar Batteries; "O.P." = Observation Post; "C.W." = Continuous Wave (wireless); "D.R.L.S." = Despatch Rider Letter Service; "N.C.Os." = Non-Commissioned Officers; "S.O.S." = call for artillery support. The repeated handwritten mark in the Hour column, read as "M.L.M." [uncertain] — apparently the diarist's initials — appears against most entries and is noted here rather than repeated against every entry.

## Pages 3–6 — Diary text (1–28 February 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY".

### Page 3 — 1–7 February 1918

> [Sheet heading: "5th Cdn. Div. [uncertain] (Artillery Detachments.)"]
>
> **Sains-en-Gohelle, Feb. 1** — Mild - situation quiet
>
> **2** — Fair - Lt. Laurie [uncertain spelling] & 2 N.C.Os. reported from 5th C.D.A.C. for instructors for the 5th C. Divarty. Sig. School to be run at this H.Q.
>
> **3.** — Dull and mild - 20 other ranks - 6 from 13th Bde. - 6 from 14th Bde. 3 from 5th C.D.A.C. 3 from C.D.A.C. and 2 from 5. C.D. T.M.Bs. for a Refresher Course in Signalling.
>
> **4.** — Mild & Cloudy - the 5th Cdn. Divarty. Sig. School, was opened to-day at this H.Q., for the further training and brushing up of the Battery Signallers. and will be run according to Syllabus on file. Direct lines from 13th & 14th Bdes. left. C.O. & 5th C.D. T.M.Bs. Control Station to Ryhe O.P. [uncertain spelling] which was used as Control Station for a raid. S.O.S. through to Btty. in 9 seconds.
>
> **5.** — Fair and mild - situation quiet.
>
> **6** — Dull - situation quiet.
>
> **7** — Dull with occasional showers - 2 C.W. wireless sets from Corps to 14th Bde. for General O.P. work for two days.

### Page 4 — 8–16 February 1918

> [Sheet heading: "5th Cdn. [uncertain] (Artillery Detachments.)"]
>
> **Sains-en-Gohelle, Feb. 8** — Light showers clearing in the afternoon - 14th Bde. had quite a successful shoot with C.W. wireless sets.
>
> **9** — Fair and mild. Lt. Cox goes to 13th Brigade Hdqrs to take over from Lieut. Rosewarn [uncertain spelling]. The Batteries of the 13th Brigade carried out successful trial shoots with the new C.W. Wireless Set.
>
> **10** — Lieut. Rosewarn left on leave to England
>
> **11** — Fair and mild - situation quiet.
>
> **12** — Fair and mild - situation quiet.
>
> **13** — Dull and showery.
>
> **14** — Drizzling rain, rear position reconnoitered with a view to establishing Visual communication if needed
>
> **15** — Cold and foggy, Arrangements made with 350th Bty Co R.E. [uncertain] for lighting hd qrs.
>
> **16** — Clear and cool, first course of 5th C.D.A. Signal School closed at 12.00 noon to day. [The words "Signal School" are inserted above the line.]

## Open questions / uncertain items (February 1918)
- 2 February: "Lt. Laurie" — surname spelling [uncertain]; "5th C.D.A.C." is written with a clear "D" here, supporting the reading of December's "C.7.A.C." as "C.D.A.C.".
- 3 February: "3 from 5th C.D.A.C. 3 from C.D.A.C. and 2 from 5. C.D. T.M.Bs." — the two C.D.A.C. figures are as written; the second may belong to a different C.D.A.C. unit.
- 4 February: "Direct lines from 13th & 14th Bdes. left. C.O. & 5th C.D. T.M.Bs. Control Station to Ryhe O.P." — "left. C.O." as written [uncertain]; "Ryhe O.P." [uncertain spelling].
- 9/10/26 February: "Lieut. Rosewarn" — surname spelling [uncertain]; Lt. A. H. Cox takes over from him at 13th Brigade H.Q. on 9 February, Rosewarn leaves for England 10 February and reports back 26 February.
- 15 February: "Arrangements made with 350th Bty Co R.E. for lighting hd qrs." — "350th Bty Co R.E." [uncertain].
- 22 February: "C.B. Airline blown down" — "C.B." [uncertain].
- 27 February: the date column on the last sheet reads 27, 26, 27, 28 in that order — the first "27" entry ("Fair and cold") is struck through, the "26" entry (Rosewarn's return) is out of order, and there are two entries dated 27 February; transcribed as written.
- Names read as: Lt. Laurie; Lt. A. H. Cox; Lieut. J. A. Rosewarn; Capt. M. L. Maitland (O.C., signatory).
- No casualties are recorded in February 1918.
