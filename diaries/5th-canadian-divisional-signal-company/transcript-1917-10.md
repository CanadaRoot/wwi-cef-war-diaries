# War Diary — 5th Canadian Divisional Signal Company (Artillery Detachment) — October 1917

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Divisional Signal Company (Artillery Detachment)
- **Month:** October 1917 (Volume 2)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004909 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5006, microfilm reel T-10854, files 694–695; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** October 1917, p. 1 – p. 5 (image files page-0009 – page-0013 of the 132-page diary)
- **October 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 9–13 of 132): cover, contents, diary text 1–31 October, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-26.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Handwritten diary text throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. No map coordinates are given in the diary except the map references noted as written (e.g. "34 C.25.65", "22 B.15.10 sheet 36 C S.W."). "Mackerel", "Newt", "Cream"/"Creep" are code names used in the diary for units/lines — transcribed as written. "H.Q." = Headquarters; "Bdes." = Brigades (the 13th and 14th Brigades, C.F.A.); "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "Divarty" = Divisional Artillery; "D.T." = Divisional Train; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Train [uncertain — as written]; "D.A." = Divisional Artillery; "R.A." = Royal Artillery; "Spr." = Sapper; "P.U.O." = Pyrexia of Unknown Origin; "CC Stn." = Casualty Clearing Station; "S.O.S." = call for artillery support. The repeated handwritten mark in the Hour column, read as "M.L.M." [uncertain] — apparently the diarist's initials (cf. the signature "M L Maitland" at 31 October) — appears against most entries and is noted here rather than repeated against every entry.

## Pages 3–5 — Diary text (1–31 October 1917)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY".

### Page 3 — 1–10 October 1917

> [Sheet heading: "5th Cdn. Divarty H.Q. 13th & 14th Bdes. Signal Sections."]
>
> **Souchez, Oct. 1st** — Fine and warm. Situation normal.
>
> **2.** — Fine and warm. 14th Bde. moved to new position in 34 C.25.65. new office opened at 10 A.M. Lines from Divarty H.Q. to Bdes started.
>
> **3** — Slight rainfall during night cleared up by 9 A.M. Lines started to 13th & 14th Bdes new H.Q.s. finished and tested through O.K.
>
> **4** — Cloudy & cool heavy rain in the evening. Work on alternate lines to Bdes. started. Line to 14th Bde. finished.
>
> **5.** — Strong winds and showery. Work on lines suspended owing to a change in operation Orders. Office of D. T. M. O. at wagon lines changed new line run in.
>
> **6** — Rain - situation normal. #67809 [uncertain — possibly #678049, cf. 11 October] Spr. Landsbury John admitted Field Amb. with P.U.O.
>
> **7** — Showery very heavy rain during the night - situation unchanged.
>
> **8** — Unsettled and showery - Received orders to move to Sains-en-Gohelle - Advance party left Souchez at 7 P.M.
>
> **Sains-en-Gohelle, 9** — Unsettled showery towards evening - at 11 A.M. new office opened at Sains-en-Gohelle old office closed at Souchez and rear party arrived at Sains-en-Gohelle at 4 P.M.
>
> **10** — Showery - new Office put in shape - remaining local lines run - also one to Newt Div. R.A.

### Page 4 — 11–24 October 1917

> [Sheet heading: "5th Cdn. Divnl Sig [uncertain] Artillery Detachments."]
>
> **Sains-en-Gohelle, Oct. 11** — Weather fine - at 12 noon Mackerel left forward Batt. H.Q. [uncertain — "Batt."?] and relieved Newt. Newt went out to wagon lines for rest. #678049 Spr Landsbury John evacuated to 18 CC Stn.
>
> **12** — Weather variable - situation normal.
>
> **13** — Weather unchanged - lines gone over & put in better shape.
>
> **14** — Weather rainy - situation normal.
>
> **15** — Weather fine but cloudy - Phone put in at D.T. Fosse 10 - two lines to Mackerel taken over (Sax 6-AXM 9th pr. 29 & Mechanics + pr 30 to Mapo [uncertain]) (SAX5-AXM 5th pr 4 on buried [uncertain] - 5th Divarty came under 6th D.A. at 12 noon.
>
> **16** — Weather fine - line to Newt SBJ [uncertain] 11th pr 18 TTA pr 13 to TTN pr 6 at Box 14.
>
> **17** — Weather fine - Newt took over from Mackerel at noon. - Mackerel
>
> **18** — Weather fine - some rear lines put out by bombs but soon repaired. Newt sent an S.O.S. to 3 batteries in 58 seconds.
>
> **19** — Weather unchanged - situation normal.
>
> **20** — Continued fine - situation unchanged.
>
> **21** — Fine and fair - Mackerel working on new positions.
>
> **22** — Cloudy & showery - situation normal.
>
> **23** — Unsettled - work on Mackerel position finished.
>
> **24** — Showery with high wind - Mackerel took over their position in 22 B.15.10 sheet 36 C S.W. at 12 noon.

### Page 5 — 25–31 October 1917

> [Sheet heading: "5th Cdn. Divnl [uncertain] Artillery Detachments."]
>
> **Sains-en-Gohelle, Oct. 25** — High winds and showery towards evening - line run into 5th C.D. T.M.O. at S 25 a 50.50 [uncertain] two lines put into Creep's group [uncertain — possibly "Cream's"] (in 28 to 30.15) at 10 A.M. when they took over. 14 horses turned into 5th Artillery in lieu of 14 bicycles received.
>
> **26** — Showery - situation normal.
>
> **27** — Unsettled - Mackerel lines cut several times through shelling - Communication good.
>
> **28.** — Fine and cool - lines to Cream cut but Communication was uninterrupted.
>
> **29** — Showery fine towards evening - situation quiet.
>
> **30** — Showery with high winds - situation normal.
>
> **31** — Fine and warm - lines to Cream cut but Communication uninterrupted.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *M L Maitland Capt C.E.*

- 25 October: "Creep's group" [uncertain — possibly "Cream's", cf. "Cream" on 28 and 31 October]; map reference "S 25 a 50.50" [uncertain].

- Casualty: Spr. John Landsbury, #67809/#678049, admitted to Field Ambulance with P.U.O. 6 October and evacuated to 18 Casualty Clearing Station 11 October — illness, not a battle casualty.
