# War Diary — 5th Canadian Divisional Signal Company (Artillery Detachment) — May 1918

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Divisional Signal Company (Artillery Detachment)
- **Month:** May 1918 (Volume 9)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004909 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5006, microfilm reel T-10854, files 694–695; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** May 1918, p. 1 – p. 7 (image files page-0053 – page-0059 of the 132-page diary)
- **May 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 53–59 of 132): cover, contents, diary text 1–31 May, monthly business summary. Transcribed 2026-10-26.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Handwritten diary text throughout. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The "Hour" column is struck through on every diary page. The heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through on the diary pages. No map coordinates are given in the diary; places are recorded as written. "C.D.A." = Canadian Divisional Artillery; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "R.F.A." = Royal Field Artillery; "D.A." = Divisional Artillery; "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "L.D." = limber driver (as written); "D.R." = despatch rider; "O.P." = observation post; "a/Cpl" = acting corporal; "Spr" = Sapper; "Sgt" = Sergeant.

## Pages 3–7 — Diary text (May 1918, pp. 3–7)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". Place: Aux-Rietz unless otherwise noted.

### Page 3

> **1** — Cool and foggy, received warning order of intended move.
>
> **2** — Clear and mild, two remounts received from Can. Corps. L.D.s for limber wagon, indented for Brigades.
>
> **3** — Clear and mild, preparations completed for impending move. all winter [inserted above the line: "Supplies"] stores turned in to Ordnance.
>
> **4** — Clear and mild, situation quiet.
>
> **5** — Dull and showery, 13th Brigade C.F.A. relieved by 24th R.F.A. Brigade, 14th Brigade relieved by 65th R.F.A. Bde, relief complete by 6.00 P.M. 52nd D.A. relieved 5 C.D.A. relief complete by 6 A.M. morning of 6th inst.
>
> **6** — Fair and mild, 5 Div, Arty., complete, moves to Houdain. H.Q. office opens at 9.00 A.M. except Brigades [uncertain] lines to all Units.
>
> **7** — Place: Houdain. Showery, clearing in afternoon, One pair to 14th Bde, C.F.A. cut through by 6.00 P.M., line laid from 13th Bde, C.F.A. to H.Q. by cable wagon. communication through by 6.00 P.M.

### Page 4

> **8** — Showery, both Bde, sections with H.Qs and signals from their batteries started a series of schemes in connection with their schedule of training.
>
> **9** — Fair and mild, Brigade and Battery signals carried out their second scheme. One Officer and one N.C.O. took charge of 5th D.T.M., Brigade signals for training.
>
> **10** — Fair and mild, training of Bdes, Batteries, and T.M. Signals carried out according to schedule, Two Wireless Operators and Sgt attached [inserted above the line: "from Can. Corps."] to T.M. Signals for duty in connection with a demonstration to be put on for the First Army Commander.
>
> **11** — Dull and mild, training of Div, Arty., Signals carried out according to schedule.
>
> **12** — Showery, clearing in afternoon, Bdes, and Battery Sigs, carried on according to schedule. T.M.'s rehearsal for demonstration carried out very successfully.

### Page 5

> **12** (continued) — No. 566 Spr Shergold S.C. appointed a/Cpl, without pay and posted to T.M. Bde, as N.C.O. in charge of Signals.
>
> **13** — Rain with high wind, 5th T.M. Bde, scheme showing mobility of T.M.'s carried out successfully.
>
> **14** — Clear and mild, an all day scheme carried out with the Bdes and battery signals of each Brigade. The battery signallers were pooled and concentrated on a single channel of communication for their respective Brigades. This plan proved to be very successful.
>
> **15** — Fine and mild, Units carried on their training separately.
>
> **16** — Fine and warm, drew from Ordnance two sets wheel harness, two Limbers R.E. and four L.D. from remounts for the two Brigades.
>
> **17** — Fine and warm, Two drivers reported from [uncertain] and are posted to each Brigade.

### Page 6

> **18** — Fine and warm, No. 5632 Sgt, Brown E. admitted to No 11 Can Field Ambulance, sick.
>
> **19** — Fine and warm.
>
> **20** — Fine and warm.
>
> **21** — Fine and warm.
>
> **22** — Place: Berles. Fine and warm, moved to Berles Chateau.
>
> **23** — Fine and warm, cloudy in afternoon.
>
> **24** — Rain with cool wind, advance party sent to 1st C.D.A. H.Q. near Maroeuil Station.
>
> **25** — Place: Maroeuil. Clear and cool, relieved 1st C.D.A. signals at 9.00 A.M. lines all working. Lines to 5 C.D.T.M.Bs and [uncertain] Div Train laid during the day.
>
> **26** — Fine and mild, [struck through in original] a third pair allotted to us from 5 C.D.A. to report Centre, One pair now cut direct through to each brigade with Fullerphone superimposed.
>
> **27** — Fine and warm, a third run put on to Bdes., by Mounted Orderly.

### Page 7

> **28** — Fine and warm. Line (direct) to 13th Bde out at 7.30 A.M. repaired by Area linemen at 1.30 P.M.
>
> **29** — Fine and warm, Line in to 5 Cav, Res, Trains detachment at their new location in Frevin-Capelle.
>
> **30** — Fine and warm, situation quiet.
>
> **31** — Fine and warm, the 4/5 Black Watch carried out a raid on enemy trenches covered by the 2nd D.A. and 5th C.D.A. all lines held good during and after the raid.

## Open questions / uncertain items (May)

- 6 May: one word between "except Brigades" and "lines to all Units" could not be read.
- 10 May: the words "from Can. Corps." are inserted above the line in the original.
- 12 May: the appointment entry for No. 566 Spr Shergold S.C. is written at the top of p. 5, immediately before the 13 May entry, with no separate date of its own; it is filed here under 12 May as written.
- 17 May: the place the two drivers reported from could not be read.
- 25 May: one word before "Div Train" could not be read.
- 26 May: one word at the start of the entry is struck through in the original and is illegible.
- Casualties: none killed or wounded are recorded in May 1918; Sgt Brown E. (No. 5632) was admitted sick to No. 11 Canadian Field Ambulance on 18 May.
- No map coordinates are given in the diary.
