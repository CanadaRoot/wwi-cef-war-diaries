# War Diary — 5th Canadian Divisional Signal Company (Artillery Detachment) — April 1919

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Divisional Signal Company (Artillery Detachment)
- **Month:** April 1919 (Volume 20)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004909 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5006; microfilm reel T-10854, files 694–695; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** April 1919, p. 1 – p. 4 (image files page-0126 – page-0129 of the 132-page diary)
- **April 1919 — COMPLETE:** cover, contents, diary text 1–30 April 1919. Transcribed 2026-10-26.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Typed diary text. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Struck-through original text is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`. The Hour column on each diary page carries a repeated check-like mark rather than times. No map coordinates are given in the diary; places are recorded as written. April entries are grouped into three ten-day blocks rather than written day by day. "C.D.A." = Canadian Divisional Artillery; "H.Q." = Headquarters; "Bde" = Brigade.

## Pages 3–4 — Diary text (April 1919, pp. 3–4)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — the "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is struck through. Header: "5Th Canadian Divisional Signal Co — Artillery Detachments".

Note: page 4 is a second copy of the same diary-text page as page 3, with the same typed text, signature and stamp but a different stamp number (see below). Both copies are transcribed; the text is identical.

### Page 3

> [Place: ORP-LE-PETIT, Belgium.]
>
> **April 1st to 10th** — Routine work carried out, consisting as last month in maintaining communication between units of 5th Canadian Divisional, Artillery and Canadian Corps Hq, and recreation. During this period documents re demobilization were prepared as far as possible. Shipments of horses made on the sixth.
>
> **April 10th to 20th** — Routine work carried out. Weather does not permit the carrying out of a sports program. Married men of this unit who have dependents in England with whom they desire to return to Canada, sent on fourteen days leave, on the expiration of which they report to Canadian Engineers Regimental Depot at Seaford. Wagons, harness and all equipment not actually in use turned in to Canadian Corps Troops Ordnance at Andenne. Shipment of horses made on the 12th.
>
> **April 20th to 30th** — Routine work carried out. Weather still unfavorable for sports. Report that this unit was to leave Belgium on the 26th was amended. Final shipment of horses made on the 28th. During this period especial attention was paid to the clothing and equipment of the men and all men fully equipped. All valuable stores turned in to Ordnance. Further preparation of papers for demobilization carried out. It being decided that this unit would proceed to England with Canadian Corps Signals.
>
> [Oval stamp: "CANADIAN SECTION No. 1776 [uncertain] — 4 MAY 1919 — 1st ECHELON, GENERAL HEADQUARTERS"]
>
> [Signed] *J.S. McPherson Captain C.E. O i/c 5th C.D. Signal Company, Arty Det*

## Open questions / uncertain items (April)
- April's diary text exists in duplicate (pages 3 and 4); both copies carry the Canadian Section / 1st Echelon General Headquarters stamp dated 4 May 1919, but the handwritten stamp numbers differ — read as 1776 on page 3 and 1750 on page 4 — both marked [uncertain].
- The cover (p. 1) and contents (p. 2) both omit "Divisional" from the unit name in places ("5th Divisional Signal Coy." on the cover; "5th Canadian Signal Company" on the contents page) — transcribed as written.
- No casualties are reported in April 1919.
