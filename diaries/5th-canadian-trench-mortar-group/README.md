## What the diary is

The group was organized at Witley Camp, England, in December 1916–January 1917 under Capt. W. H. Abbott (Divisional Trench Mortar Officer), from the 4th Canadian Division Artillery on the reorganization of divisional artillery. It comprised V (Heavy) Battery and X, Y and Z (Medium) Batteries. It trained at Witley, Cut Mill Common and Milford Camp through the summer of 1917 — for months with no guns and no authority for equipment — and embarked for France in August 1917, disembarking at Le Havre on 22 August. From September 1917 it served in the Lens sector (Nédonchelle, Barlin, Les Brebis, Hill 70), firing 6-inch and 9.45-inch trench mortars in support of the 13th and 14th Canadian Infantry Brigades. During the Hundred Days (August–November 1918) its batteries were detached in turn to the Canadian Independent Force and the Canadian Motor Machine Gun Brigade, advancing through Arras, Bourlon, Marquion, Denain and Valenciennes. The diary ends 16 November 1918 with the group broken up and its batteries distributed to the 13th and 14th Brigades, Canadian Field Artillery.

## Casualties (named)

- 21 Nov 1917: Gnr. Wain J.E. killed; buried at Cité St Pierre (Plymouth Road Cemetery).
- 19–20 Dec 1917: Gnr. Hill M.R. (No. 315583) wounded, died of wounds in No. 7 Casualty Clearing Station.
- 6 Feb 1918: Gnr. Greig J.W. (No. 336905) and Gnr. Walker A.C. (No. 319928) killed.
- 28 Apr 1918 (Givenchy): Bdr. Bland S.V. killed; Gnr. Foster T., Gnr. McNab J., Gnr. Saunders S.S. and Gnr. Slinn A.H. wounded. Gnr. Daly M.J. wounded 10 Apr.
- 23 May 1918 (Agnières): bombing raid, 7 men wounded by one bomb; Gnr. Talbot P. died of wounds at the C.C.S. Bdr. Smith A.B. wounded 28 May.
- 9 Jun 1918: Lieut. A. C. Magann and Gnr. McLeish W.R. wounded in a raid/barrage; Gnr. Slate J. wounded 8 June, Gnr. Anderson R.B. wounded 17 June, Lieut. J. E. de Sherwin and Cpl. Scott P.J. wounded 21 June.
- 20–23 Jul 1918 (Anzin): Gnr. Wallace J.A. wounded, died of wounds.
- 29 Aug 1918: Gnr. Shepherd G. wounded (appendix only; the diary text records only 1 mule killed and 1 wounded).
- 2 Sep 1918 (Villers, with the Canadian Independent Force): Gnr. Slaven (No. 320990) killed; Gnr. Nelson S. wounded, died of wounds 3 Sep; Lieuts P. A. Stairs and J. W. Kelly wounded; 6 more other ranks wounded (Bdr. Turner H. J., Gnr. Bradley T., Gnr. Wheeler L. G., Gnr. Rogers L. R., Gnr. Geary C. G., Gnr. Eaton L. J.). The diary, appendix and operations report give different casualty figures for this day — all transcribed as written.
- 7 Sep 1918 (Écourt-Saint-Quentin): Bdr. Jolley (No. 316022) killed; Gnr. Bennett W. J. and Gnr. McIlwain W. C. died of wounds 8 Sep; Gnr. Pattison L. M. and Gnr. King M. L. wounded.
- 15 Sep 1918: Gnr. Edgley wounded. 20 Sep 1918: Gnr. Banbury R. G. wounded.
- 4 Oct 1918: Capt. Stanley MacPherson, D.T.M.O., severely wounded by shrapnel from a bomb.
- 26 Oct 1918 (Mastaing): Bdr. Orr C. A. killed; Sgt. J. A. McPherson, Bdr. McKenzie and Gnr. Baxter R. H. wounded by a bomb. Orr buried 27 Oct at D 15 d 85.50.
- 1 Nov 1918: one man wounded (unnamed in the appendix). 3 Nov 1918: Sig. M. Macken T.F. [uncertain] wounded.
- Also gassed: 4 wounded 13 Jan 1918 (Cox, Maitland, Dumont, Saunders); Gnr. Glenn A.H. wounded 18 Jan 1918; Gnr. McIntosh T.J. and Gnr. Trent J.A. gassed 30–31 Jan 1918; 5 men gassed 11 Mar 1918. Gnr. W. E. Hutton (No. 1261189) was struck off strength as a deserter, 18 May 1917.

## Notable items

- 17 Dec 1917: 30 Germans came over to the group's lines and surrendered.
- 10 Nov 1917: V, X, Y and Z fired 169 rounds in a barrage covering a raid by the 32nd Infantry Brigade; a direct hit by a 6-inch trench mortar on an enemy dugout killed all occupants. 21 Nov 1917: 130 rounds covering a raid by the 33rd Infantry Brigade.
- 28 Aug–3 Sep 1918: the 5th C.D.T.M. Battery (Lieut. A. A. Magann) served with the Canadian Independent Force as lorry-mounted mortars; its Report of Operations is the September appendix and is indexed separately (same dates also appear in the main diary text).
- 20 Jun 1918: Ordnance told the group it had "no right to any bicycles" when ordered to turn its bicycles in to be scaled.
- 26 Oct 1917: Pte. Craig (No. 1251617) repaired the cut line to the O.P. twice under fire.
- Officers named in January 1917 on organization: Capt. W. H. Abbott (D.T.M.O.); Lieuts. J. Newton, J. F. P. Birnie, J. McEachern, M. Waddington, L. Marler, H. S. Robinson, S. L. McClenaghan, S. H. Short. Later officers include Lieut. R. A. Wood, Capt. J. H. McEachern, Lieut. A. A. Magann, Lieut. Williams, Lieut. A. R. Montgomery and Capt. Stanley MacPherson (D.T.M.O. from October 1918).
