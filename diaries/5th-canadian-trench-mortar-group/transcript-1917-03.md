# War Diary — 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group — March 1917

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group (Trench Mortar Batteries, 5th C.D.A.), 5th Canadian Division
- **Month:** March 1917
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004821 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, file 594, reel T-10809)
- **Pages:** March 1917, p. 1 – p. 8 (image files page-0018 – page-0025)
- **March 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–8): cover, contents, diary text 1–31 March, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-28.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or" with "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heavily struck through on every sheet). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Each day's entry is initialled "W.H.A." (Capt. W. H. Abbott, D.T.M.O.); the diary is signed by him at the foot of sheets 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6. The Place column reads "Witley Camp" throughout; the Hour column is not used; the "Remarks and references to Appendices" column is blank throughout. A circular stamp reading "TRENCH MORTAR BATTERIES MAR. 1917 4TH CAN. DIV." (Vol. III, Sheet 1–6) is struck on each diary sheet. The printed footer reads "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D. D. & L. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118." "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "C.D.A." = Canadian Divisional Artillery; "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Trench Mortar Officer; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "C.I." = Canadian Infantry; "O.R." = Other Ranks; "Bde." = Brigade; "Btn." = Battalion; "How." = Howitzer.

## Open questions / uncertain items (March 1917)
- 1 March: "On Parade 7 Officers 79 O.R. 11 O.R. in hospital" — the parade figure (79) is less than the 2 March total strength (90 O.R.), the difference being men in hospital and otherwise absent; transcribed as written.
- 3 March: the batteries began drawing rations and took over the men's mess; officers remained attached to the D.A.C. for rations and quarters. Retreat at 5.30 p.m.
- 5 March: one O.R. sent to Aldershot for a cookery course.
- 6 March: Lt. Waddington appointed "Quarantine Officer".
- 7 March: "Results of Telephone course shewn" — "shewn" is the spelling as written. The sentence is transcribed as written; no total number of men on the course is given.
- 11 March: the Bishop of London spoke at a meeting in "Tin Town".
- 12 March: "5 men sent on 11th Telephony course" — as written.
- 13 March: "X" "Y" and "Z" Batteries in quarantine for mumps, 35 O.R.; strength decreased by 21 O.R. to 15th Brigade, C.F.A., and increased by 1 O.R. from 14th Brigade, C.F.A.
- 14 March: 1 O.R. taken on from No. 9 Canadian Stationary Hospital.
- 21 March: Lt. J. F. P. Birnie struck off on transfer to 15th Brigade, C.F.A.; 1 O.R. taken on from 5th Pioneer Battalion; 32 O.R. attached from 15th Brigade for quarters, duty and discipline.
- 24 March: the word "confining" is struck through before "all ranks to camp" — orders were received to confine all ranks to camp and call back all ranks on leave or attending courses, to await important orders. Transcribed as a struck-through word.
- 25 March: preparing for mobilization; all ranks confined to camp.
- 27 March: "botth posted" is the spelling as written. Lts. C. T. Mee and A. J. Brice joined and were both posted to "V" Battery; orders to resume normal conditions had been received the previous night.
- 31 March: total strength is given as 7 officers and 92 O.R. (10 in hospital, 6 absent without leave, 35 in quarantine), with 33 O.R. attached — but the officer arithmetic (7 at 2 March, less Birnie on 21 March, plus Mee and Brice on 27 March) implies 8 officers. Transcribed as written.
- No killed, wounded or missing are recorded anywhere in this month. The only sickness noted is the mumps quarantine of 13 March (35 O.R.) and 10 O.R. in hospital on 31 March.
- Places read as: Witley Camp; Aldershot. No map references are given.
