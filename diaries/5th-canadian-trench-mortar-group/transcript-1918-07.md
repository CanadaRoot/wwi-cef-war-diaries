# War Diary — 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group — July 1918

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group (5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Brigade), 5th Canadian Division
- **Month:** July 1918
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004821 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, file 594, reel T-10809)
- **Pages:** July 1918, p. 1 – p. 11 (image files page-0128 – page-0138)
- **July 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–11): cover, contents, diary text 1–31 July, Appendices A (casualty 20–23 July) and B (promotion of officers). Transcribed 2026-10-28.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through on these sheets). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The July diary is written in a much more literary and humorous register than April–June, by Lieut. C. Lloyd, for D.T.M.O. 5 C.D.A., who signs every sheet; every July entry ends with the initials "C.A.B.". The Place column reads "Waverly Camp" 1–12 July and "Anzin" 13–31 July; the Date column on Sheets 3–8 gives the day number only, with "1918" at the head of the column. The Hour column is blank throughout. "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "C.D.T.M." = Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar; "C.D.A." = Canadian Divisional Artillery; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Trench Mortar Officer; "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "O.R." = Other Rank; "Bty" = Battery; "C.C.S." = Casualty Clearing Station; "Y.M.C.A." = Young Men's Christian Association; "E.F.C." = Expeditionary Force Canteen; "P.D.Q." = Pretty Damn Quick; "A/Captain" = Acting Captain; "L.G.S." = Limbered General Service (waggons); "Gnr." = Gunner. "Hun" is the diary's word for the enemy throughout. Place spellings are kept as written: "Tinques", "La Comte", "Beggin", "Gouare valley", "Anzin", "Aubigny", "Hazebrouck", "Newton trench".

## Pages 3–10 — Diary text (1–31 July 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed footer reads "A2990. Wt. W... 750,000. 1/17. D. D. & L., Ltd. Forms/C.2118/14."

### Page 3 — 1–4 July 1918 (Sheet 1)

Heading: "Trench Mortar Brigade 5th Canadian Division Artillery / July 1918."

> **Waverly Camp, 1918 1 July.** — The only event of importance in the war was the Can. Corps Sports held at Tinques. This Unit was forced to enter the long hopping contest. No official standing for this contest has been announced but this unit feels entitled to the rated Class A. Transport allotted did not report and all men got there safely and returned without walking. Canadian Women were at the gathering and gave tone to the whole proceedings. A number of staff cars served refreshing drinks but the E.F.C. was woefully inadequate. As a result those without pull or friends in the high places came home after. Even our D.T.M.O. — who did not enter the long hopping contest — was able to ride home. His groom now considers him a hero. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 2 July.** — This day was very quiet. Things settled down to normal and a few rounds were expended. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 3 July.** — Situation still normal though for some strange reason a consignment of ammunition was put in the dump for us. Our guns will look more businesslike with a little ammunition scattered carelessly around and in case the Hun comes over will impress him with our efficiency. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 4 July.** — Operations still continue to be nil but time does not hang on our hands. Two plans must be put in operation: first we must move mortars back P.D.Q. and place them so as to hit the Hun if he comes close enough. C.A.B.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *C. Lloyd Lieut / for D.T.M.O. 5 C.D.A.*

### Page 4 — 4–7 July 1918 (Sheet 2)

Heading: "5th Can. Div. Arty. / WAR DIARY / Trench Mortar Brigade. / July 1918."

> **Waverly Camp, 1918 4 July contd.** — and secondly we must make ourselves mobile enough to chase the Hun if he becomes frightened and begins to run. Can it be done? Time alone will tell but we are going to demonstrate the mobility some day in the near future to the edification of the powers that be and the honour and glory of the British Empire, we trust. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 5 July.** — Our O.C. mobility and his circus came back today — back from the cruel D.A.C. warfare. It now remains to see how mobile they are. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 6 July.** — Preliminary trials of our various platforms were held in the Gouare valley. Our own type proved fairly good but the type designed by Major Buttonshaw was in a different class altogether and will be used by us in demonstration referred to the other day. Capt Anderson, Corps Tramways illustrated the monorail idea and several cars were tried. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 7 July.** — The chief occupation in the waggon lines today was teaching the new bed to come to pieces easily. In the forward area reserve positions are being rushed. Mr Sweatman has designed still another mobile platform it is mounted on two wheels and bears in modest letters across the back the legend Anzin Fire Department. It must be mobile. C.A.B.

### Page 5 — 7–9 July 1918 (Sheet 3)

Heading: "Trench Mortar Brigade 5th Canadian Division Artillery / July 1918."

> **Waverly Camp, 1918 7 July contd.** — Our active positions were not idle today but expended one round and silenced an enemy T.M. according to intelligence summary from 7.0.0. [uncertain] The D.T.M.O. is sceptical. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 8 July.** — 3 new defensive guns put in action and ammunition put at positions. If the Hun comes where we can hit him he will get hurt. Mobile section moved to Beggin to be on hand for trials to take place July 9th. Personnel sufficient to demonstrate the mono rail was also taken. Our guests the 10th D.A. T.M. Bde and the Tramway people filled all available billets and we bivouaced in the lines of our old friends, the 14th Field Ambulance. Having no mess the officers visited Houdain and it is understood had a very nice dinner. A slight rain fell during the night. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 9 July.** — Demonstration took place near La Comte. It went off with military precision i.e. was scheduled to commence 12.45 and commenced about 1.10 while firing commenced even further behind schedule. Slight (flurries) rain hastened matters and the various speed artists demonstrated their mobility. A couple were almost volatile while a couple more were —
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *C. Lloyd Lt / for D.T.M.O. C.F.A.*

### Page 6 — 9–12 July 1918 (Sheet 4)

Heading: "Trench Mortar Brigade 5th Canadian Brigade / July 1918."

> **Waverly Camp, 9 July contd.** — almost stationary. The one used by our O.C. mobility attracted considerable attention and a certain general amused himself by using it to pot at the range party. In his youth there were no T.M's. The one rail railway did its little part and three guns were very quickly put into action. Return to camp was made without mishap. C.A.B. Our operations consisted of 35 rounds in harassing fire. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 10 July.** — 1st Army sent for our new bed and mule pack for investigating and perfecting. We shot 33 rounds at the Hun. The cause of our sudden fertility is that we are now allowed to shoot 30 rounds every day. For the first time since coming to this locality our canteen was really wet. The wet stock consisted of 3 doz. bottles of stout and lasted 10 minutes. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 11 July.** — The situation on our immediate front remains normal. Operations consisted of 38 rounds fired at hostile T.Ms. The chief event of the day was the arrival of a tennis set from the Canadian Field Comforts Association. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 12 July.** — No unusual celebration was held today. 42 rounds were fired at the enemy whilst he took in good part and did not attempt to return. Great interest is being taken in the building of a tennis court. It is now complete but showers of rain make it impossible to play. C.A.B.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *C. Lloyd Lt / for D.T.M.O. 5 C.D.A.*

### Page 7 — 12–16 July 1918 (Sheet 5)

Heading: "Trench Mortar Brigade 5th Canadian Division Artillery / July 1918."

> **Waverly Camp, 1918 12 July contd.** — The Canadians are comming in to day. Some friends called at the mess and we gather they have been living in a dry part of the country. — C.A.B. Congratulations to Perry A. Wood [uncertain] on his promotion. C.A.B. [Remarks column: "App. B."]
>
> **Anzin, 13 July.** — The day began quite peacefully with us still in Waverly Camp. The Town major and a staff officer paid us a visit and broke the news that we must move. There was only time to hold an exercise ride on the tennis court and pack up. We are now located in Anzin and beginning to feel at home. Operations 45 rounds at various targets. Weather warm and cloudy C.A.B.
>
> **,, 14 July.** — Weather still very warm. Operations 55 rounds. Enemy unusually quiet. Internal reorganization of the brigade is being carried out. Instead of the present system of one unit a system of three batteries properly brigaded is being tried. Each battery has now its own cook house. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 15 July.** — Our guns were today taken over [from — struck through] by the 1st C.D.T.M. Bde with the exception of two. At the same time we took over V6 from the 4th C.D.T.M. Bde. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 16 July.** — Operations were nil. Weather extremely hot and sultry following a heavy rain storm during the night 15th–16th. C.A.B.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *C. Lloyd Lt / for D.T.M.O. / 5 C.D.A.*

### Page 8 — 17–24 July 1918 (Sheet 6)

Heading: "Trench Mortar Brigade 5th Canadian Division Artillery / July 1918."

> **Anzin, 1918 17 July.** — New forward H.Q. established on left. Only one active gun left us. 37 rounds expended in harassing fire. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 18 July.** — Operations 40 rounds fired in destructive shoot on Newton trench. Position selected for new reserve positions and position for a Heavy T.M. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 19 July.** — Operations 45 rounds on Newton trench. A Heavy T.M. was drawn from Ordnance. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 20 July.** — Took over 8 guns from 1st Division making a total of 20 medium T.Ms in position. Operations 30 rounds. C.A.B. Gnr. Wallace wounded and evacuated C.A.B. [Remarks column: "App. B"]
>
> **,, 21 July.** — Heavy gun sent forward. Operations 20 rounds. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 22 July.** — Operations 80 rounds. 2 guns withdrawn from defensive positions and brought back to waggon lines to be made mobile. Reports of mobility test received. Our pattern was placed first. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 23 July.** — 78 rounds expended. Another defensive gun withdrawn. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 24 July.** — New T.M. group formed with Capt McEachern as O/C D.T.M.O. working under 277 Army Bde. 8 guns were handed over to Capt McEachern. Work progressing famously on Heavy. C.A.B.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *C. Lloyd Lt / for D.T.M.O. 5 C.D.A.*

### Page 9 — 25–28 July 1918 (Sheet 7)

Heading: "Trench Mortar Brigade 5th Canadian Division Artillery / July 1918."

> **Anzin, July 1918 25th.** — Operation orders received for two gas attacks and sent forward by ration waggon. Code messages cancelling these sent forward to Capt Wood reached him before the operation order which followed by ration waggon. Result was a solemn communication from Capt Wood stating that to him 'apple' and 'eggs' meant only something good to eat and as he was about to go on leave he preferred 'lobster' 'oyster' and 'gin' C.A.B.
>
> **,, 26.** — Front rather quiet. Our operations consisted of a few rounds of harassing fire. Received a gramophone from Y.M.C.A. and the morale has gone up considerably. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 27.** — Capt. P.A. Wood and 2 O.Rs proceeded on leave. This also had a splendid effect on the morale of all ranks though it was most noticeable on the one getting the leave. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 28.** — Day was normal i.e. the enemy tame and the weather agreeable 40 rounds expended in harassing fire. C.A.B.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *C. Lloyd Lt / for D.T.M.O. / 5 C.D.A.*

### Page 10 — 29–31 July 1918 (Sheet 8)

Heading: "Trench Mortar Brigade 5th Canadian Division Artillery / July 1918."

> **Anzin, 29.** — The heavy gun was put in action and fired 10 rounds. This had the usual effect and the Hun came right back with several varieties of artillery but without doing any material damage. C.A.B. [Remarks column: "App. B."]
>
> [Interlineation, written in smaller script between the 29 and 30 July entries:] Information received that Gnr. Wallace died of wounds at C.C.S.
>
> **,, 30.** — Orders received that we are to be relieved by 5-2nd T.M.B. Relief complete by noon. As we have been issued with Hazebrouck maps we suspect that we are off to the north country. C.A.B.
>
> **,, 31.** — To move everything we have 9 L.G.S. waggons. It was very difficult to load everything on but it was not until asked to explain why the waggons were overloaded that we fully appreciated the difficulty. However we got to Aubigny and entrained direction and destination still unknown C.A.B.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *C. Lloyd Lt / for D.T.M.O. / 5 C.D.A.*

## Open questions / uncertain items (July 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 12" struck through with "19." written above it; the contents page reads "Volume 19". Transcribed as written.
- 1 July: "came home after" — the word after "came home" is read as "after"; it may read "afoot", which would fit the sense (those without pull or friends in high places had to walk home). "feels entitled to the rated Class A" is read as written. "Transport allotted did not report and all men got there safely and returned without walking" is read as written, though it sits oddly against the transport not reporting.
- 5 July: "back from the cruel D.A.C. warfare" — "D.A.C." is read as written; the mobile section had been transferred to the D.A.C. on 27 June.
- 6 July: "Gouare valley" — read as "Gouare"; the spelling is not fully certain. "Major Buttonshaw" and "Capt Anderson, Corps Tramways" are read as written.
- 7 July (contd.): "according to intelligence summary from 7.0.0. [uncertain]" — the source reads as "7.0.0."; it may be "I.O.O." (Intelligence Officer). The D.T.M.O.'s scepticism about the claimed silencing of an enemy trench mortar is the diarist's own comment.
- 8 July: "Mobile section moved to Beggin" — read as "Beggin"; the spelling is not fully certain. "the 10th D.A. T.M. Bde" is read as written.
- 9 July: the entry breaks off mid-sentence at the foot of Sheet 3 ("while a couple more were —") and continues at the head of Sheet 4 ("almost stationary."). "Slight (flurries) rain" — the parenthetical word is read as "flurries" and is not fully certain.
- 9 July (contd.): "The one rail railway did its little part" — read as written.
- 10 July: "The cause of our sudden fertility is that we are now allowed to shoot 30 rounds every day" — "cause" is read as written; the word "is" falls across the line break and is read as "is". "mule pack" is read as written (the 1st Army sent for the brigade's new bed and mule pack).
- 12 July (contd.): "Congratulations to Perry A. Wood [uncertain] on his promotion" — the first name is read as "Perry" but is not fully certain; the 27 July entry gives the same officer as "Capt. P.A. Wood", and Appendix B records "Lieut P.A. Wood to be A/Captain eff. 1-5-18."
- 13 July: the Place column reads "Anzin" although the day began at Waverly Camp and the move to Anzin happened during the day; transcribed as written.
- 15 July: the word "from" before "by the 1st C.D.T.M. Bde" is struck through; transcribed as a struck-through word.
- 20 July: the Remarks column reads "App. B" although the casualty (Gnr. Wallace) is recorded in Appendix A; transcribed as written.
- 24 July: "New T.M. group formed with Capt McEachern as O/C D.T.M.O. working under 277 Army Bde" — "277 Army Bde" is read as written.
- 25 July: the code words 'apple' and 'eggs' were the cancellation codes for the two gas attacks; Capt. Wood's reply preferring 'lobster', 'oyster' and 'gin' is quoted as written.
- 29 July: the interlineation "Information received that Gnr. Wallace died of wounds at C.C.S." is written in smaller script between the 29 and 30 July entries; it is transcribed as an interlineation under 29 July. Appendix A dates Wallace's death 23-7-18. The Remarks column for 29 July reads "App. B."
- 30 July: "relieved by 5-2nd T.M.B." — read as written; the unit designation is not expanded.
- 31 July: the entry ends "direction and destination still unknown C.A.B." with no closing stop; the month ends there. The brigade entrained at Aubigny.
- Appendix B: "Lieut P.A. Wood to be A/Captain eff. 1-5-18." — the date is read as "1-5-18", but the contents page gives the promotion appendix as "1-8-18"; transcribed as written, not reconciled.
- The contents page gives the casualty appendix as "Casualties 22-7-18", but Appendix A itself records Gnr. Wallace wounded 20-7-18 and died of wounds 23-7-18; transcribed as written.
- The July diary names no killed or wounded other than Gnr. Wallace J.A. (wounded 20 July, died of wounds 23 July).
