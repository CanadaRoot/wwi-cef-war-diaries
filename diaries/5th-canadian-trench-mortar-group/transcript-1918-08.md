# War Diary — 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group — August 1918

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group (5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Brigade), 5th Canadian Division
- **Month:** August 1918
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004821 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, file 594, reel T-10809)
- **Pages:** August 1918, p. 1 – p. 8 (image files page-0139 – page-0146)
- **August 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–8): cover, contents, diary text 1–31 August (5 sheets), appendix (officers taken/struck off strength and casualties). Transcribed 2026-10-28.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through on the diary sheets and the heading is handwritten "Trench Mortar Brigade / 5th Canadian Division Artillery"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Each day's entry is initialled "C.B.S." at its end, and each sheet is signed "C.B. Boyd Lt" (5th T.M.B.). The Place column gives the unit's location (or "On the train" / "En Route"); the Hour column is blank throughout; the "Remarks and references to Appendices" column is blank throughout. "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "C.D.A.C." = Canadian Divisional Ammunition Column; "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column; "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer; "O.R's." = Other Ranks; "H.Qrs." = Headquarters; "H.V's." = high-velocity (shells); "A.A." = anti-aircraft; "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade; "C.M.Rs." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "D.S.O." = Distinguished Service Order; "S.O.S." = struck off strength; "Gnr." = Gunner; "Bdr." = Bombardier; "C.M.M.G." = Canadian Motor Machine Gun; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Trench Mortar Officer. The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "Wt. W.../M... 750,000. 1/17. D. D. & L., Ltd. Forms/C2118." (partly legible).

## Pages 3–7 — Diary text (1–31 August 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed footer reads "Wt. W.../M... 750,000. 1/17. D. D. & L., Ltd. Forms/C2118." (partly legible).

### Page 3 — Sheet 1 (1–7 August 1918)

Place: On the train.

> **1.** — Commenced to load last night as Hun aeroplanes overhead made the R.T.O. nervous and train was late in loading and leaving. After sunrise position of sun showed that we were travelling south not north as we were supposed to think. Early in the afternoon last T.M Battery pulled into Bacouel Station about noon and marched back along the route to Cagny. X battery lost itself on the way having only Hazebrouck maps. Billets were found in Boutillerie. C.B.S.

Place: Boutillerie.

> **2nd.** — A show is expected and T.M. batteries start on a hunt for methods [uncertain]. nothing is found except map locations of possible dumps and saw-mills. C.B.S.
>
> **3rd.** — The hunt continued but nothing found. C.B.S.
>
> **4th.** — Billetts moved to a more favorable location, and some timber located in Amiens. C.B.S.
>
> **5th.** — Timber salvaged and brought to our H. Qrs. If we had some tools we would now have sleds. C.B.S.
>
> **6th.** — A mill was located today but it had no authority to cut timber for us. C.B.S.
>
> **7th.** — Authority found but meanwhile the only saw large enough [continued on Page 4]

### Page 4 — Sheet 2 (7th continued – 10 August 1918)

Place: Boutillerie.

> **7th (continued).** — to do the work has been broken. Decided not to wait any longer for sleds but to hold the show without them. Capt McEachern Lieut Magann and Lieut Kelly and 24 O.R's. attached to Motor machine gun Brigade with two mortars. C.B.S.
>
> **8th.** — The show started early this morning and was a success. X & Y batteries moved forward with 4 guns in order to be ready if called upon to take part. Various rumours reached the rear and two large bodies of prisoners passed through this town. Orders received attaching us to No 3 Sec 5 C.D.A.C. C.B.S.
>
> **9th.** — moved with 3rd Sec 5 C.D.A.C after joining X & Y Batteries, to Domart. C.B.S.

Place: Domart.

> **10th.** — moved from Domart to a point between Le Quesnel and Beaufort and were attached to No 2 Sec. 5 C.D.A.C. Capt. McEachern's battery was in action yesterday in Le Quesnel woods and helped to clear the Hun out. The ground everywhere shows that the Hun was taken by surprise and left before he wished to go. cont'd C.B.S.

### Page 5 — Sheet 3 (10th continued – 18 August 1918)

Place: Beaufort.

> **10th (continued).** — This unit has gathered up 2 machine guns, the 08/15 pattern and has now an A.A. section. The way in which the Hun has cleaned up everything in the district including the crops contrasts very favorably with our own methods C.B.S.
>
> **11th.** — After the bombing of the previous night the chief order of the day is digging in. By night every man of the unit had a hole to sleep in. C.B.S.
>
> **12th.** — made a reconnaissance of Caix [uncertain] with a view to putting in mortars. C.B.S.
>
> **13th.** — Put in a mortar and fired 80 rounds into the village. C.B.S.
>
> **14th.** — Took out the mortar as it was now too far back to be of any use. C.B.S.
>
> **15th.** — Empty stores brought up from Boutillerie. C.B.S.
>
> **16th.** — Pay Parade. C.B.S.
>
> **17th.** — Capt J.W. McEachern [uncertain initials] was awarded the Croix de guerre for work in the advance. also 200 rounds of ammunition drawn. C.B.S.
>
> **18th.** — Battery commanders made a reconnaissance of the front C.B.S. Lieut P.A. Williams [uncertain initials] rejoined the unit C.B.S.

[Signed, bottom right:] *C.B. Boyd Lt / 5th T.M.B. [uncertain]*

### Page 6 — Sheet 4 (19–28 August 1918)

Place: Beaufort.

> **19th.** — Warning order received. Corps will be relieved. 200 rounds of ammunition returned to dump. C.B.S.
>
> **20th.** — Usual daily parades held. C.B.S.
>
> **21st.** — During afternoon moved to vicinity of 3rd Divty D.A.C. and during night moved to Domart. C.B.S.

Place: Domart.

> **22nd.** — During night moved to Saleux arriving 3.00 a.m. C.B.S.

Place: Saleux.

> **23rd.** — Orders received to entrain on night of 24th. C.B.S. Capt Wood went forward on detraining [uncertain].
>
> **24th.** — Entrained. X Hq [uncertain] and H.Q. on train no 12 at 9.30 and Y & V Bty on train no 18 at 12.30 C.B.S.

Place: En Route.

> **25th.** — Detrained at Aubigny 1.20 P.M. second train immediately afterwards. Proceeded to Montmerencourt [uncertain]. Capt Wood took 2 mortars and went forward to 13th Bde lines to follow up and support advance of infantry. C.B.S.

Place: Montmerencourt [uncertain].

> **26th.** — Show opened at 3:30 a.m. Progress was very favorable. Received orders to move forward with No 3 Sec. D.A.C. Place selected was unsuitable but located lines at Blangy. C.B.S.

Place: Blangy.

> **27th.** — Sent back waggons for balance of stores which we were compelled to leave behind under guard. C.B.S.
>
> **28th.** — Orders received for 2 additional mortars to be ready to report to Canadian Independent Force. Lieut Stains [uncertain — Stairs on Page 7], Lieut Blondell [uncertain — Blundell in the September operations report] and 21 O.R's. selected. New billets located in [uncertain]

### Page 7 — Sheet 5 (28th continued – 31 August 1918)

Place: Blangy.

> **28th (continued).** — Arras [uncertain] and personnel moved. Lieut Stairs and party leave to join Independent force. C.B.S.

Place: Arras.

> **29th.** — moved waggon lines to a more convenient location. Capt Wood and his party were also attached to the Independent force. His battery was in action in vicinity of [uncertain] having a mortar firing from the village almost [uncertain] the Hun was pushed out. In Arras the war is quite peaceable as only a few stray H.V's. are heard. C.B.S.
>
> **30th.** — Capt. Wood and his party rejoined the unit. The only casualties suffered by his battery were 1 mule killed and one wounded. C.B.S.
>
> **31st.** — Day quiet except for a few long range shell. C.B.S.

[Signed, bottom right:] *C. Boyd Lt / for O.C. [uncertain] / 5th T.M.B.*

## Open questions / uncertain items (August 1918)
- The cover reads "VOLUME 13" (circled) with "20." written above it; the contents page gives "Volume 20". Both as written.
- The contents page dates Appendix A "Casualties 28-8-17" and Appendix B "Officers taken or struck off strength 28-7-18", but the appendix itself dates the casualty 29-8-18 and Magann's striking off strength 29-7-18. Both as written.
- 1 August: "late in loading an[d] leaving" — the word is written "an"; read as "and".
- 2 August: "start on a hunt for methods [uncertain]" — the word reads "methods", but the entries of 5–7 August show the hunt was for sleds (timber salvaged, "If we had some tools we would now have sleds", "Decided not to wait any longer for sleds"); the 2 August word may in fact read "sleds". Recorded as written with the flag.
- 7 August: the entry breaks off at the foot of Sheet 1 ("the only saw large enough") and continues at the top of Sheet 2 ("to do the work has been broken").
- 10 August: the entry breaks off at the foot of Sheet 2 ("left before he wished to go. cont'd") and continues on Sheet 3.
- 12 August: the village reconnoitred reads "Caix" but the hand is cramped; "Cavillers"/"Caillers" are possible alternative readings.
- 17 August: Capt McEachern's initials read "J.W." here, but the September operations report (September p. 8) gives "Capt J. H. McEachern". The initials are flagged; the award of the Croix de guerre is as written.
- 18 August: Lieut Williams's initials read "P.A." but are not fully certain.
- 23 August: "Capt Wood went forward on detraining [uncertain]" — the word after "detraining" could not be read.
- 24 August: "X Hq [uncertain] and H.Q. on train no 12" — the word after "X" reads "Hq" but may be "Bty".
- 25 August: "Proceeded to Montmerencourt [uncertain]" — the place name is read as written; it is not verified. The same spelling is used in the Place column on 26 August.
- 28 August: the entry breaks off at the foot of Sheet 4 ("New billets located in [uncertain]") and continues on Sheet 5.
- 28 August: "Lieut Stains" on Sheet 4 and "Lieut Stairs" on Sheet 5 appear to be the same officer; the September appendix gives "Lieut A. B. Stairs". "Lieut Blondell" on Sheet 4 is spelled "Blundell" in the September operations report and appendix.
- 28 August (Sheet 5): the first word of the continuation reads "Arras" but is not fully certain.
- 29 August: two words could not be read — the place "in vicinity of [uncertain]" and the word after "almost" in "almost [uncertain] the Hun was pushed out".
- 30 August: "The only casualties suffered by his battery were 1 mule killed and one wounded" — but the appendix records Gnr. Shepherd G. wounded 29-8-18, a human casualty the diary text does not mention. Both as written.
- The appendix's "S.O.S to R.A.F." for Lieut A. A. Magann is read as written; Magann reappears in the September operations report commanding the 5th C.D.T.M. Battery.
- The diarist signs the sheets "C.B. Boyd Lt" (5th T.M.B.) while every entry is initialled "C.B.S."; both as written.
- Places read as: Boutillerie (spelled "Boutillerie"); Bacouel Station; Cagny; Hazebrouck; Amiens; Domart; Le Quesnel; Beaufort; Caix [uncertain]; Saleux; Aubigny; Montmerencourt [uncertain]; Blangy; Arras. No map references are given this month.
- No human casualties are recorded in the diary text itself this month (only 1 mule killed and 1 mule wounded, 30 August); the appendix adds Gnr. Shepherd G. wounded 29-8-18.
