# War Diary — 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group — September 1918

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group (5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Brigade), 5th Canadian Division

- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004821 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, file 594, reel T-10809)
- **Pages:** September 1918, p. 1 – p. 8 (image files page-0147 – page-0154; the manifest labels this month "september" lowercase)
- **September 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–8): cover, contents, diary text 1–30 September (4 sheets), appendices (officers taken/struck off strength and casualties, plus a report of operations of the 5th C.D.T.M. Battery attached to the Canadian Independent Force, 28 August–3 September 1918). Transcribed 2026-10-28.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — neither heading is struck through on these sheets, and the heading is handwritten "Trench Mortar Brigade 5th Can Div Artillery"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. This month is in a different, smaller and more cramped hand than August, and several words are hard to read; see the open questions. The Remarks column carries "Mess." after most entries (reading uncertain — see open questions); a few entries instead cite "Appendix "A"" or "Appendix "B"". The Place column is filled only for 1 September ("Arras"), 9 September ("Vis-en-Artois") and 21 September ("Hendecourt"), 28 September ("Cagnicourt") and 29 September ("Bourlon"); other days carry ditto marks or are blank. The Hour column is blank throughout. "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "C.D.A.C." = Canadian Divisional Ammunition Column; "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column; "O.R's." = Other Ranks; "H.V's." = high-velocity (shells); "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade; "C.M.Rs." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "C.M.M.G." = Canadian Motor Machine Gun; "G.S." = General Service (wagons); "M.G." = machine gun; "S.O.S." = struck off strength; "Gnr." = Gunner; "Bdr." = Bombardier; "Sgt." = Sergeant; "D.S.O." = Distinguished Service Order; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Trench Mortar Officer. The printed footer on the diary sheets reads "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D.D.&L. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118."

## Page 8 — Report of Operations, 5th C.D.T.M. Battery, 28 August–3 September 1918 (September 1918, p. 8)

> Report of Operations of 5th C.D.T.M. Battery attached Canadian Independent Force 28th August to 3rd September 1918.
>
> **28th August.** — At 11.30 p.m. Capt J. H. McEachern received orders from O.C. 2nd C.M.M.G. Bde., to place 2 mortars in action to cover I. 29 and 35. (Ref. 51 B). It was found impossible to take lorries past Monchy. From here the mortars had to be carried. One mortar with 80 Rounds was placed in action at 2.30 p.m. the 29th.
>
> **29th August.** — No firing was done as our Machine Gunners and Cyclists continued advancing in front of us.
>
> **30th August.** — At about 1.00 A.M orders were received to withdraw the mortars and proceed to CHAPEL Road. Here Lieut Stairs and Lieut. Blundell reported with two more mortars mounted on lorries. At 4.00 p.m. the Battery moved to the CITADEL - ARRAS. The total casualties were:- Wounded - 1 other rank.
>
> **31st August and 1st September.** — Spent in Rest.
>
> **2nd September.** — Attached to the 1st C.M.M.G Bde., the Battery moved off with the Brigade to Wancourt at about 6.00 P.M. The Brigade then moved its lines to Q.25 on the Cambrai Road. At about 1.30 p.m. Capt. McEachern made a reconnaissance and brought the lorry under Lieut. Magann into action on the CAMBRAI Road. at Q.33 b.9.1. and fired 45 Rounds into Villers with effective results. At 2.30 p.m. Lieut. Stairs mortar was brought into action at Q.34 c.4.9. and engaged concentrating enemy infantry to the left of Villers, firing about 25 rounds, before the lorry and mortar were put out of action by enemy artillery fire. Though these mortars were under constant artillery fire and Machine Gun fire, all ranks behaved with the utmost coolness and bravery. Lieut. Magann took command when Capt. McEachern and Lieut. Stairs became casualties. The Battery moved at about 4.30 p.m. to Brigade lines. Here one lorry was hit and the driver killed. About 9.00 p.m. the Brigade moved to Wancourt. The total casualties in this action were:- Killed — 2 other Ranks. Wounded — 8 other Ranks. 3 officers. Total 13 All Ranks. (Note:- this included 1 M.T. driver killed and 1 wounded. 1 lorry destroyed by shell fire. 1 mortar complete.
>
> **3rd September.** — Lieut. Magann received orders to proceed to ARRAS with the Battery and reorganize. All casualties were replaced with the exception of 1 officer, unavailable, and two lorries unavailable.
>
> Signed. A.A. Magann. Lieutenant 5th C.D. Trench Mortar Battery.

## Open questions / uncertain items (September 1918)
- The contents page dates the operations report "22-8-18 to 3-9-18", but the report itself is headed "28th August to 3rd September 1918". Both as written.
- The Remarks column carries "Mess." after most entries from September onward (and the appendix on this page is signed with a similar-looking scribble). The reading "Mess." is uncertain — it may be the diarist's name or initials — and it is transcribed as written wherever it appears.
- 2 September: the diary's casualty line reads "Killed 1 O.R. Wounded 3 officers & O.Rs [uncertain]" — the figure and wording are cramped and not fully certain. It does not match the appendix (which lists for 2-9-18: Gnr. Slaven killed; Lieuts Stairs and Kelly and 8 other ranks wounded, with Gnr. Nelson dying of wounds 3-9-18) or the operations report (2 killed, 8 other ranks wounded, 3 officers wounded, total 13). All three sources are transcribed as written.
- 2 September: "Capt. Woods detachment" — the possessive is as written (Capt Wood's detachment).
- 7 September: "East of Ecort St Quentin [uncertain] tonight" — the place name is read as written (Écourt-Saint-Quentin is the likely place, but the spelling is not certain).
- 7 September: the diary records "Killed 1 Wounded 4, two of the wounded died after being taken to the dressing station"; the appendix lists for 7-9-18: Bdr. Jolley killed, and Gnr. Bennett and McIlwain dying of wounds 8-9-18, plus Pattison and King wounded — i.e. 1 killed and 4 wounded, two of whom died, which matches the diary.
- 9 September: the cause of the beds sinking reads "[uncertain]" — the word after "owing to" could not be read with confidence.
- 11 September: the map reference before "19 d 50.80" could not be read.
- 12 September: the place where three reserve guns were put in could not be read.
- 13 September: the last word after "77 cm rounds" could not be read.
- 15 September: the diary's round counts use ditto marks repeating "Fired 10 rounds on Palluel"; the words around "with light mortar rounds" and the note of one man wounded are partly illegible and are flagged.
- 16 September: "Two of our men met [uncertain] patrol of 4" — the word before "patrol" could not be read.
- 18 September: the sentence after "Harassing fire." could not be read.
- 19/20 September: the "X Battery relieves detachment of V and Y Batteries…" paragraph sits between the 19 and 20 September entries with no date of its own; it is placed under 19 September here, but it may belong to 20 September. Two words in it ("Can. Motor [uncertain]") could not be read.
- 24 September: "the making of "Buttershaw" [uncertain] beds" — the quoted word is read as written and is not certain.
- 27 September: "His runner [uncertain]" — the word before "runner" reads "His" but may be "This".
- 28 September: "Mobile Trench Mortars [uncertain] in ready to follow up when required" — the word before "in ready" (possibly "held") could not be read.
- 29 September: "to engage Bourlon [uncertain] at A 9 d 55.90" — the word after "Bourlon" (possibly "Wood") could not be read.
- Appendix: the surname "Slaven" (320990, killed 2-9-18), "Jolley" (316022, killed 7-9-18), "McIlwain" (316943) and "Edgley" (1257855) are flagged as not fully certain; the initials of "Lieut J. F. MacKenzie" are likewise uncertain.
- The operations report gives Capt McEachern's initials as "J. H.", against "J.W." in the August diary (17 August); both as written.
- The operations report's casualty total for 2 September (2 killed, 8 other ranks wounded, 3 officers wounded = 13) is internally consistent, but the diary entry for 2 September and the appendix give different figures; see above.
- Places read as: Arras; Dury; Cambrai Road; Vis-en-Artois; Palluel; Chérisy; Hendecourt (Place column spells it "Hendicourt"); Quarry Wood; Cagnicourt; Bourlon; Monchy; Wancourt; Villers; Chapel Road; the Citadel, Arras. Map references are given as written (O 21 d 10.80; Q 10 c 60.70; Q 10 c 20.45; 19 d 50.80; E 9 d 2.0; F 1 q 7.7; F 9 c 4.0; A 9 d 55.90; A 8 b 40.50; Q.25; Q.33 b.9.1; Q.34 c.4.9) and are not verified.
- Casualties this month (appendix): 2 September — Gnr. Slaven killed in action; Gnr. Nelson S. wounded, died of wounds 3-9-18; Lieut P. A. Stairs and Lieut J. W. Kelly wounded; Bdr. Turner H. J., Gnr. Bradley T., Gnr. Wheeler L. G., Gnr. Rogers L. R., Gnr. Geary C. G., Gnr. Eaton L. J. wounded. 7 September — Bdr. Jolley killed in action; Gnr. Bennett W. J. and McIlwain W. C. wounded, died of wounds 8-9-18; Gnr. Pattison L. M. and Gnr. King M. L. wounded. 15 September — Gnr. Edgley wounded. 20 September — Gnr. Banbury R. G. wounded.
