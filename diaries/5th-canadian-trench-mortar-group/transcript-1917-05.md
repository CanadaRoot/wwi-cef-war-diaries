# War Diary — 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group — May 1917

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group (Trench Mortar Batteries, 5th C.D.A.), 5th Canadian Division
- **Month:** May 1917
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004821 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, file 594, reel T-10809)
- **Pages:** May 1917, p. 1 – p. 7 (image files page-0034 – page-0040)
- **May 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–7): cover, contents, diary text 1–31 May, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-28.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or" with "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heavily struck through on every sheet). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Each day's entry is initialled "W.H.A." (Capt. W. H. Abbott, D.T.M.O.); the diary is signed by him at the foot of sheet 5. The Place column reads "Witley Camp" 1–20 May and "Cut Mill Common" ("do.") 21–31 May; the Hour column is not used; the "Remarks and references to Appendices" column is blank throughout. A circular stamp reading "TRENCH MORTAR BATTERIES - MAY. 1917 5TH CAN. DIV." (Vol. IV, Sheet 1–5) is struck on each diary sheet — the stamp reads "Vol. IV" although the cover and contents give Volume 5. The printed footer on all five sheets reads "A5834 Wt. W4973/M687 750,000 8/16 D. D. & L. Ltd. Forms/C.2118/13." From 15 May the Date column gives the month as "6" (15-6 to 31-6) — a month slip; all such dates are treated as 15–31 May 1917 and are transcribed as written. "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "C.D.A." = Canadian Divisional Artillery; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Trench Mortar Officer; "O.R." = Other Ranks; "G.S." = General Service; "Gnr" = Gunner; "C.M.G." = Companion of the Order of St Michael and St George.

## Open questions / uncertain items (May 1917)
- Dates 15–31 May are written in the Date column with the month as "6" (15-6 to 31-6) — a month slip; they follow 14-5 on the May sheets (Volume V) and are treated as 15–31 May 1917. All dates are transcribed as written.
- 1 May: strength is given as 5 officers, 148 O.R. — one fewer officer than the April arithmetic implies (7 officers at 12 April, less Waddington and McLenaghan on 16 April, plus Wood on 17 April = 6). Transcribed as written.
- 5 May: franking of letters to Canada and allied countries by orderly room stamp was permitted.
- 13 May: Divisional Artillery Parade; Col. J. M. Almond, C.M.G., Director of Chaplain Services, preached.
- 14 May: all ranks had now been put through gas, with the exception of 7 men in hospital and 1 newly joined man.
- 15 May [written 15-6]: collection of 2 shillings per man for barrack damages.
- 18 May [written 18-6]: one O.R., No. 1261189 Gnr W. E. Hutton, struck off strength as a deserter — the only deserter recorded in January–May 1917.
- 21 May [written 21-6]: the T.M. Batteries went into bivouac on Cut Mill Common; "X" Battery was the advance party; they left Milford Camp at 8.30 a.m. with 3 G.S. wagons and 1 cook cart, and 7 miles of telephone wire were put out. The Place column changes from Witley Camp to Cut Mill Common on this date.
- 28 May [written 28-6]: material for bridging and 2 G.S. wagons received.
- 30 May [written 30-6]: fitting and issue of box respirators.
- 31 May [written 31-6]: filling up of National Service cards.
- No killed or wounded are recorded anywhere in this month.
- Places read as: Witley Camp; Cut Mill Common; Milford Camp. No map references are given.
