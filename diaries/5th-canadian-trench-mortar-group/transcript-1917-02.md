# War Diary — 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group — February 1917

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group (Trench Mortar Batteries, 5th C.D.A.), 5th Canadian Division
- **Month:** February 1917
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004821 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, file 594, reel T-10809)
- **Pages:** February 1917, p. 1 – p. 11 (image files page-0007 – page-0017)
- **February 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–11): cover, contents, diary text 1–28 February, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-28.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118). On sheets I–II the heading reads "WAR DIARY or" with the alternative heading heavily struck through; on sheet III it reads "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" with neither heading struck through; on sheets IV–IX it reads "WAR DIARY or" with "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Each day's entry is initialled "W.H.A." (Capt. W. H. Abbott, D.T.M.O.); the diary is signed by him at the foot of sheets I, II, III, IV, V, VI, VIII and IX. The Place column reads "Witley Camp" throughout (spelled "Witley Camp" on sheets I–II and "WITLEY CAMP" on sheets III–IX); the Hour column is used only on 20 February (2 p.m.) and 22 February (2.00 p.m. and 2.30 p.m.); the "Remarks and references to Appendices" column is blank throughout. A circular stamp reading "TRENCH MORTAR BATTERIES 17 FEB. 1917 4th CAN. DIV." is struck on sheets I–VIII; sheet IX is stamped "28 FEB. 1917". The printed footer on sheets I–VI reads "A5834 Wt. W4973/M687 750,000 8/16 D. D. & L. Ltd. Forms/C.2118/13."; on sheets VII–IX it reads "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D. D. & L. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118." "T.M.B." = Trench Mortar Battery; "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "C.D.A." = Canadian Divisional Artillery; "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Trench Mortar Officer; "C.R.A." = Commander Royal Artillery; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "C.I." = Canadian Infantry; "O.R." = Other Ranks; "Bgde." = Brigade; "Btn." = Battalion; "R.O." = Routine Order; "M.O." = Medical Officer; "N.C.O." = Non-Commissioned Officer; "C.A.S.C." = Canadian Army Service Corps; "A.S.C." = Army Service Corps.

> **13/2/17** — Strength. No Increase or Decrease this date. Total 8 Subalterns - 86 O.R.

## Open questions / uncertain items (February 1917)
- 1 February: the batteries are organized as V (Heavy), X, Y and Z (Medium). Lt. Short, who commands Y Battery with Lt. McClenaghan, was not named in January; Lt. Wilson, taken on 31 January, does not appear in the battery organization. The surname is spelled "McCLENAGHAN" here against "McClanaghan" on 25 January — transcribed as written in each case.
- 6 February: Z Battery is shown with 1 officer, the other three batteries with 2 each, totalling 7 officers.
- 8 February: the battery table sums to 79 O.R. (V 19 + X 20 + Y 20 + Z 20) but the running strength (78 + 3 − 11 − 1) gives 69; the 9 February total of 81 O.R. reconciles with 69 (69 − 3 + 15 = 81), not with 79 — the 8 February table appears to be in error. All figures transcribed as written.
- 11 February: the battery table sums to 82 O.R. (V 22 + X 20 + Y 20 + Z 20) but the running strength (81 − 1 on 10 February + 1) gives 81. Transcribed as written.
- 19 February: the total is given as 90 O.R. after a decrease of 4, but the running strength (94 on 17 February − 1 on 18 February − 4) gives 89. Transcribed as written.
- 26 February: strength is given as 7 officers, 80 O.R., although the running total (90 on 19 February + 1 on 20 February) gives 91, and the 22–25 February entries all record "No increase or decrease in strength"; no decrease of 11 O.R. is recorded. The 28 February total of 90 O.R. (80 + 10) is consistent with the 26 February figure. Transcribed as written.
- 12 February: the readjusted battery table sums correctly to 86 O.R., and the hospital figures (4 + 2 + 2 + 3) to 11 O.R.
- 13 February: remark that infantry men are more efficient than men transferred from artillery brigades, the latter being mostly "surplus details" from the re-organization of 4th Canadian Division Artillery while the infantry were volunteers.
- 14 February: remark that the lack of equipment, more particularly guns, is holding up training to a great extent.
- 16 February: Lt. S. H. Short is attached to the Royal Flying Corps; Lts. Wilson and Robinson transfer to 15th Brigade; Lts. D. S. Stayner (from D.A.C.) and D. McCutcheon (from 14th Brigade) join.
- 23 February: remark that other units have done "a great deal of harm" to T.M. batteries by sending men to them as a punishment, and that four volunteers (two of them sergeants wishing to revert) have applied to transfer in.
- 26 February: a divisional scheme is arranged to start the next day, with the T.Ms to take part in the second phase about 2–3 March.
- Hospital figures rise through the month: 2 O.R. admitted 1 February, 10 in hospital 9–10 February, 11 on 11–13 February, 12 on 20 February, 15 on 21 February. No killed, wounded or missing are recorded anywhere in this month.
- Places read as: Witley Camp (spelled "Witley Camp" / "WITLEY CAMP"); Thursley Common; Milford Camp; Bramshott Hospital. No map references are given.
