# War Diary — 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group — November 1917

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Group (5th Canadian Divisional Trench Mortar Batteries), 5th Canadian Division
- **Month:** November 1917
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004821 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4980, file 594, reel T-10809)
- **Pages:** November 1917, p. 1 – p. 12 (image files page-0073 – page-0084)
- **November 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–12): cover, contents, diary text 1–30 November (no entry for 27 November), appendix (casualties and courses), Operation Order No. 2 with barrage table, Operation Order No. 3 with barrage table. Transcribed 2026-10-28.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is handwritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through on sheets 1–5; on sheet 6 and on the appendix sheet the heading reads "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", with "WAR DIARY" apparently struck through). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. The Place column reads "Les Brebis" on every diary sheet; the Hour column is used only on 4 November (2 P.M. parade). The "Remarks and references to Appendices" column carries a large initialled scrawl against each day's entry; it is the same mark throughout and is not read with confidence, so it is not transcribed entry by entry. Each diary sheet is signed at the foot by the D.T.M.O., Capt., 5th Canadian Div'n; the signature is transcribed as [signature] because the surname is not read with confidence. "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "T.M.G." = Trench Mortar Group; "C.D.A." = Canadian Divisional Artillery; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "D.T.M.O." = Divisional Trench Mortar Officer; "D.A." = Divisional Artillery; "O.R." = Other Ranks; "N.C.O." = Non-Commissioned Officer; "C.A.R.D." = as written; "S.O.S." = as written; "H.Q." = Headquarters; "M.G." = Machine Gun; "R.E." = Royal Engineers. Map references (N3c, N8b, N2d, N3a, N2b, H33c) are read as written and are not verified.

## Open questions / uncertain items (November 1917)
- Cover reads "VOLUME 11" with a struck-through character before the "11"; contents page agrees on Volume 11. A "4" appears at the top right of the first diary sheet (page 3); it is not used as a page number.
- Contents page says "Diary Text 7 Pages" but the diary text is 6 sheets (pages 3–8); the appendix casualties/courses sheet (page 9) may be counted as the seventh. Contents also says "Orders issued by 5th C.D.T.M.G. 29-11-17", but the two operation orders in the file are dated 26/11 as read; the discrepancy is as written.
- No entry for 27 November 1917: the diary jumps from 26 to 28 November.
- 23 November appears twice: sheet 4 carries a 23 November entry (Visibility: Fair; 15 rounds on enemy T.M. emplacements) and sheet 5 opens with a second 23 November entry (Visibility: Good; 50 rounds at a suspected enemy T.M. emplacement). Both are transcribed as written.
- 1 November: "(sick)" is written in the Remarks column against the strength-decrease line; the officer evacuated to England is not named.
- 2 and 4 November: the battery lines are grouped by a brace to a shared note; transcribed with "}" at the end of each grouped line.
- 5 November: "5th C.D.A.C." as written.
- 8 November: "NIZAM Trench" as written.
- 10 November: the raid is by the "32nd Infantry Brigade"; 21 November's raid is by the "33rd Infantry Brigade" — both as written. "destroyed" in the 10 November entry is written over a struck-through word.
- 11 November: "Lieut. J.P. Vokes" — the surname is read as "Vokes" but the handwriting is small and the reading is not fully certain; flagged [uncertain].
- 12 November: "2 Other Ranks evacuated to C.A.R.D. England and S.O.S" — "S.O.S" as written (presumably struck off strength, not verified).
- 16 November: the "V" Battery aeroplane-observation shoot was attempted but proved unsuccessful owing to low cloud.

- The D.T.M.O.'s signature on the November sheets appears to begin "D.W." followed by a surname that is not read with confidence (it may read "Duchas"); it is transcribed as [signature] throughout. The same signature signs the operation orders as "Capt. C.F.A. D.T.M.O."
