## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written. Typewritten on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed. No item in this month required an `[uncertain]` mark; two overtyped words (the 17 December admissions figure and "elapsed" in the Agenda) are readable and are noted in the open questions below.
- The Remarks column throughout is the handwritten initials "H.S.B." (Colonel H. S. Birkett).
- Unlike September, on the December diary-text pages the sub-heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is NOT struck through — the heading reads "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", with "(Erase heading not required.)" beneath it.
- The printed footer on all three diary-text pages reads "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D. D. & L. A.D.S.S. Forms/C 2118." (a different print run from September's "T2124. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J.C. & S.") and the form carries the printed instructions block at top left ("Instructions regarding War Diaries and Intelligence Summaries are contained in F. S. Regs., Part II. and the Staff Manual respectively. Title pages will be prepared in manuscript.").
- The oval Office of Officer Commanding stamp ("OFFICE OF OFFICER COMMANDING / 31 DEC 1916 / No. 2 F 12/16 / No. 3 Canadian General Hospital—McGill") appears on the cover and on all three diary-text pages.
- No hours are recorded in December — the Hour column is blank throughout. The diary is kept as daily admission and discharge totals; the word used for patients leaving is "discharged" throughout (no "evacuated" figures this month — "evacuated" is used only for individuals sent to England).
- As in September, ditto marks (") in the Place and Date columns are expanded to the repeated value in the transcription below.

## Page 5 — Diary text, p. 3 — AGENDA (December 1916, p. 5)

> — Page three — WAR DIARY — Army Form C. 2118
>
> AGENDA
>
> 1. High winds and incessant rains have prevailed throughout the month, and the drainage system has proved adequate to meet the situation.
>
> 2. As a result of the visits of Surgeon-General T.W.Woodhouse,C.B., D.M.S., L of C., and Colonel,Sir Wm.Leishman,C.B., Adviser in Pathology,the P.U.O situation has been very much improved under the methods which have been adopted which are as follows:-
>
> (a) All cases which, in the opinion of the O.,i/c Medical Division belong clinically to the Enteric Group are evacuated immediately to No.14. Stationary Hospital.
>
> (b) Where the symptoms are not marked, one agglutination test is made, and the patient is then evacuated to No.14 Stationary Hospital, the work which has been done in the laboratory of this hospital being recognized by No.14 Stationary Hospital.
>
> (c) Patients who have had a moderate degree of temperature are segregated into one hut,and three stool examinations are made, which, when found negative are evacuated to Convalescent Depot or Base Details.
>
> 3. Owing to the shortage of officers due to various causes,the work of the Hospital under my command has been carried on under very trying circumstances. Lt.Colonel J.M.Elder,CAMC, was away for over five weeks on a commission in London,Lt.Colonel John McCrae,CAMC was ill for five weeks,and the following Officers have been struck off strength owing to illness,Major J.C. Browne and Captain C.K.Wallace;Captain T.A.Malloch is away on 3 week's sick leave. Captains W.T.Ewing, F.W.Tidmarsh, and A.D.Campbell were sent to the front to the 1st Canadian Division, no exchange being made. Captain H.M.Little resigned. This left this Unit with a shortage of nine officers,and the number of patients in hospital has varied from 1100 to 1300. Application has been duly made for six reinforcements,and although these have been authorized,and one month has elapsed since such authorization,they have not yet arrived. It is regrettable to think that such a situation as this exists.
>
> [Handwritten signature:] H.S. Birkett Col. A.M.S.
>
> O.C. No. 3 Canadian General Hospital (McGill)
>

## Open questions / uncertain items (December 1916)
- Cover volume numbering: the cover reads "VOLUME XIX" while the contents page reads "Volume 16-12" (September: cover "VOLUME XVI", contents "Volume 16-9") — both are transcribed as written.
- 7 December: "Mists and heavy rains" appears at the end of the 7 December entry, after "I extremely regret his resignation." (verified by zoom crop `zoom-dec16-dailyorders.jpg`).
- 7 December: Captain H.M.Little's entry gives the authority as "War Office letter 121/L/624 (A.M.D.1) d/25-11-16 and D.G.M.S. G.233"; the same authority is cited again on 15 December as "War Office 121/L/624(A.M.D.1) dated 25-11-16 & D.G.M.S., G.233".
- 9 December: the Daily Orders reference reads "No.644 Part; 2,655(4)" as typed (verified by zoom crop `zoom-dec16-dailyorders.jpg`).
- 10 December: "Capt.C.D.Walt" — the surname is typed "Walt" (verified by zoom crop `zoom-dec16-p3-bot.jpg`).
- 13 December: "Received advice from A.A.G., Canadian Section, 3rd Echelon, GHQ." — "A.A.G." as typed (verified by zoom crop `zoom-dec16-brand11.jpg`).
- 14 and 17 December: the convalescent home is spelled "Chateau Maurician"; on 20 December it is spelled "Chateau Mauricien" — both spellings are as typed (verified by zoom crops `zoom-dec16-ewing.jpg` and `zoom-dec16-mauricien.jpg`).
- 15 December: Lt-Colonel John McCrae "returned from Sick Leave at Cap Martin" and "relieved the tension of the acute situation" — both phrases as typed (verified by zoom crop `zoom-dec16-mccrae.jpg`). Captain G.E.Learmonth's surname is spelled "Learmonth" as typed.
- 17 December: the admissions figure "84" is overtyped (a second digit is struck over the first) but reads as 84 (verified by zoom crop `zoom-dec16-ewing.jpg`).
- 17 December: "Captains W.T.Ewing, F.W.Tidmarsh, and A.D.Campbell" — "Tidmarsh" as typed (verified by zoom crop `zoom-dec16-ewing.jpg`).

- Patient totals for December: 2,751 admitted and 2,925 discharged (31 daily figures each; every date 1–31 has figures). The heaviest day was 21 December (212 admitted); the lightest was 25 December (11 admitted). No casualties are named in this volume — patients are recorded only as daily numbers.
- No appendices are listed for this volume and none are filed in it.
