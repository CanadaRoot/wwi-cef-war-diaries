## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written. Typewritten on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or passage that could not be read with confidence — never guessed. Two `[uncertain]` marks were needed this month: a captain's name struck out on diary-text p. 1 (5 July), and one overtyped word in the post-mortem report on Agenda p. 5; see the open questions below.
- As in March and June, the sub-heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" IS struck through, with "or" handwritten above it — the heading reads "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", with "(Erase heading not required.)" beneath it. This holds on the diary-text, Appendix and Agenda sheets alike.
- The Remarks column carries the handwritten initials "H.S.B." (Colonel H. S. Birkett) against most entries; no initials appear against 1, 8, 11 and 24 July. The Agenda pages 4, 5 and 7 and Appendix pages 1 and 2 carry no Remarks initials; Appendix p. 3 and Agenda p. 6 do.
- The oval Office of Officer Commanding stamp ("OFFICE OF OFFICER COMMANDING / 31 JUL. 1917 / No. 2-WD-7-17 / No. 3 Canadian General Hospital—McGill") appears on the cover and on all diary-text, Appendix and Agenda pages; on the diary-text pages the date line of the stamp is faint and "2 WD 7-17" is overtyped in bold type.
- No hours are recorded in July — the Hour column is blank throughout.
- Two print runs are mixed in this volume. Diary-text pp. 1, 2 and 4 and Agenda pp. 4 and 7 use the A2908 run, footer "A2908. Wt. W12569/M1298 750,000. 1/17. D.D & L., Ltd. Forms/C2118/14." — the "Wt." contract reference, too faint to read on the June sheets, is legible on the July sheets as W12569/M1298. Diary-text p. 3, Appendix pp. 1–3 and Agenda pp. 5–6 use the older 2449 run, footer "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12." (the same run as March). The form carries the printed instructions block at top left ("Instructions regarding War Diaries and Intelligence Summaries are contained in F. S. Regs., Part II. and the Staff Manual respectively. Title pages will be prepared in manuscript.").
- The Place column is blank throughout, except for the first entry on each diary-text page — 1, 8, 19 and 24 July — where "Boulogne" is entered; the hospital remained at Boulogne all month. A small dot or mark stands in the Place column against 25 July.
- 17 July: ditto marks (") in the Summary column repeat the previous day's "Nothing of any moment to report"; they are expanded in the transcription below.
- The Agenda is numbered pages four to seven — there are no Agenda pages one to three in this volume; the three Appendix pages carry the numbers 1–3 instead. The 27 July entry refers to "Agenda, pages four, five, and six" for the new gas-shell cases.
- The diary is signed by Colonel Birkett at the end of diary-text p. 4 (31 July); the Agenda is signed again at the end of Agenda p. 6 and a third time at the end of Agenda p. 7 (Statistics).
- The Appendix (pp. 1–3) and Agenda (pp. 4–7) are transcribed as continuous text, not as tables, except that the Appendix tables (chest-case synopsis, missile/infection table, fatal-cases table) and the Agenda statistics are rendered as tables to preserve their figures. Words split by a hyphen at a line break are joined without the hyphen.
- The word "actual" (29 July) and the "Total Savings £1510:6:10½d" line (Agenda, p. 6) are underlined in the original.

> Hereunder, I beg to submit Pathological findings made by Major Lawrence J.Rhea, CAMC, O.,i/c Pathology in the hospital under my command, of a patient who recently died in the hospital from the effects of what I am informed is a new type of gas shell used by the enemy.
>
> CASE ONE.   No.44492 Br.NASH, Wm, R.F.A.   17th Bde.   26th By. Admitted to No.3.Canadian General Hospital (McGill) by convoy 24-7-17.   Died 28-7-17.
>
> CLINICAL HISTORY:
>
> Complaint:—   Unconsciousness, coughing, eyes closed, very short of breath, vomiting, pain in chest, throat, and headache.
>
> History:—   On 23-7-17, while sleeping in a gun pit, a gas shell came over. He had on his helmet for about a quarter of an hour, when it was removed. A gas shell burst near him, and he became unconscious. He thinks he was unconscious for about five hours. On admission to the hospital, his chief symptoms were pain and tightness in the throat, causing a choking sensation, with complete loss of voice.
>
> Physical Examination:—   There is marked cyanosis, breathing is heavy and difficult. There is a purulent conjunctivitis; exoriation and vesiculation about the side of the face, the left ear and the mastoid region. Marked flushing, lips are dry, tongue is coated and dry, the tonsils red. There is a profuse secretion (purulent) from the nose, and on coughing. The sputum is not blood stained. Heart:—   Rate is increased; otherwise negative. Lungs:—   Dulness and diminished breathing at the right base; scattered moist rales. Abdomen is negative. Extremities are negative. He is very stuporous and complains of some tenderness over his larynx. On 24-7-17, fifteen ounces of blood withdrawn from the vein. His breathing was somewhat easier after this. On the evening of the same day, the dulness of the lungs had increased. Vocal resonance and vocal fremitus present; voice distinctly nasal. There is bronchial breathing and crepitant rales. Colonel H.S.Birkett.., AMS, Commanding Officer, Specialist for this area of the Nose, Throat and Ear, saw the case to-day and removed from one side of the nose a large fibrinous cast. On 27-7-17, there is dulness over the lower and the middle lobes of the right lung with tubular breathing. The patient is still cyanosed. The breathing is somewhat easier. On the evening of the same day, ten ounces of blood was withdrawn. Patient became worse and died the next day.
