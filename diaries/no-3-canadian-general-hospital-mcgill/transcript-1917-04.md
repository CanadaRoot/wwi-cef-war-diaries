## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written. Typewritten on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or passage that could not be read with confidence — never guessed. No `[uncertain]` marks were needed in the diary text this month; the one faint handwritten annotation (diary-text p. 4) is noted in the open questions below.
- As in March, on the diary-text pages the sub-heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" IS struck through, with "or" handwritten above it — the heading reads "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", with "(Erase heading not required.)" beneath it.
- The Remarks column throughout is the handwritten initials "H.S.B." (Colonel H. S. Birkett).
- The printed footer on all diary-text pages reads "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12." (the same print run as March) and the form carries the printed instructions block at top left ("Instructions regarding War Diaries and Intelligence Summaries are contained in F. S. Regs., Part II. and the Staff Manual respectively. Title Pages will be prepared in manuscript.").
- The oval Office of Officer Commanding stamp ("OFFICE OF OFFICER COMMANDING / 1 - MAY. 1917 / No. 2 WD-4-17 / No. 3 Canadian General Hospital—McGill") appears on the cover and on all diary-text pages.
- No hours are recorded in April — the Hour column is blank throughout, except for a handwritten "1525" with "H.S.B." beneath it in the Hour column of diary-text p. 5, opposite Agenda item (2). The diary is kept as daily admission and discharge totals; the word used for patients leaving is "discharged" throughout (once, on 27 April, written with a lower-case "d").
- As in March, ditto marks (") in the Place and Date columns are expanded to the repeated value in the transcription below.
- The 10 April entry runs on past the foot of diary-text p. 1 and is continued at the top of diary-text p. 2; the 26 April entry likewise runs on and is continued at the top of diary-text p. 4. Both continuations are shown in place below.
- The Agenda begins on diary-text p. 4, after the 30 April entry, and continues over diary-text p. 5. The passage of Agenda item (1) recording the protest against the removal of the hospital's transport is underlined in the original, from "I place on record" to "for this purpose".
- The 1 April entry opens with the figures for 31 March (75 admitted, 55 discharged), which were not included in last month's Diary; those are March figures and are NOT counted in April's monthly totals below.

> > (1). Under authority of D.A.D.T, Boulogne Base 1908,dated 26-3-17,it is very much regretted that the transport which was a special gift to the Hospital under my command has been removed. This transport consisted of four Motor Ambulances, two Ford Cars, all of which were the gifts of private individuals in Montreal and the city of Quebec. This transport was brought to France, and during the period from June 1915 to November 1917, has carried out the work of convoying and evacuating patients to and from the various hospitals, both whilst we were at Dannes-Camiers and Boulogne Base. One of these ambulances was converted into a light lorry, because, owing to the fact that this hospital covers an area of 26 acres of ground, it was found impossible to transport various Ordnance and Army Service Stores by the personnel throughout this extensive area inasmuch as without it, it led to positive fatigue of the men who day by day did this long and heavy carrying. Moreover, this light lorry also carried a great many Stores from the Depots of this Base to our hospital, because the transport detailed to this Unit was not equal to the occasion, and it was only by the use of this transport that greater efficiency in our work has been obtained.
