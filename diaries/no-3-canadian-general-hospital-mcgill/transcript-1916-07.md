## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written. Typewritten on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed. No word in this month required an `[uncertain]` mark; every entry was legible (verified against zoom crops where noted).
- The Remarks column throughout is the handwritten initials "H.S.B." (Colonel H. S. Birkett).
- On all four diary-text pages the sub-heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through (the heading reads "WAR DIARY" with "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" crossed out); "(Erase heading not required.)" appears beneath it.
- The printed footer reads "T2124. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J.C. & S." — the same print run as June.
- The night-canteen note under 11 July is ringed in ink on the page; it has no date or hour entry of its own.
- The X-Ray report from Captain A. H. Pirie has no date or hour entries of its own; it is embodied in the diary under 16 July and reports X-Ray figures for June 1916.
- July entries give daily "patients admitted and evacuated" totals rather than the timed convoys of June; no "Nothing worthy of comment occurred" entry appears this month.

> | " | 11. | | During the recent activity on the British front, the work of this Unit necessitated our placing men on convoy duty, which called for their being at their posts throughout the night, and in order to make provision for them, in accordance with I.G.C. Order 102, 19-6-16, the Nursing Sisters of my Staff very kindly volunteered to meet this situation by providing a night canteen. According to the old system, which was done through the Hospital Kitchen, I wish to show what happened from the 15th to 30th June last (15 days) when 1156 patients were received, there were used according to this system 304 loaves of bread and 36 lbs of butter. As the result of the Nursing Sisters doing this work themselves for a period of 7 days, from the 3rd to 10th July, when 3481 patients were received, only 135 loaves of bread and 18 lbs of butter were used. This splendid saving is due entirely to the magnificent work done by my Nursing Sisters to whom I wish to express my deepest appreciation......... | H.S.B. |

> PAGE THREE. — WAR DIARY — Army Form C. 2118
>
> | Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks |
> | BOULOGNE | JULY. 1916. | | | |
> | " | 16. | | 187 patients admitted and 198 evacuated......... | H.S.B. |
> | " | 16. | | The following report has been handed to me by Captain A.H.Pirie, O.i/c X-Ray Department, of the Unit under my Command:- "During June, 1916, there were 1097 X-Ray photographs made; of these 1079 were of patients in our Hospital, 13 of patients in No. 1 Convalescent Depot, 1 in Base Details, 2 in No. 1 Canadian General Hospital and 2 of a patient from Headquarters Staff. During the first two weeks of June more than 700 radiographs were made. This number has been about our average for a month and consequently we had to indent for more plates before the end of the month. The machinery, tubes, chemicals and other necessities in the Department are in good condition and in thorough working order. The armature which was sent to London, has been received back repaired. It will remain in its packing until it is required as both the other armatures are in good condition. The orderlies of the Department continue to give satisfaction. For carrying the patients to and from the Wards, four men from Base Details have been supplied and they have performed the work to my satisfaction. The use of the electro-vibrator continues to reduce the necessity for localization of foreign bodies by X-Rays and this increases efficiency and economy...." | H.S.B. |
