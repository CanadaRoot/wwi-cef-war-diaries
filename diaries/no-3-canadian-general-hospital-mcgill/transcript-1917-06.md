## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written. Typewritten on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or passage that could not be read with confidence — never guessed. One `[uncertain]` mark was needed this month, in the printed footer on all five diary-text and Agenda sheets, where the "Wt." contract reference is too faint to read; see the open questions below.
- As in March, the sub-heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" IS struck through, with "or" handwritten above it — the heading reads "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", with "(Erase heading not required.)" beneath it.
- The Remarks column throughout is the handwritten initials "H.S.B." (Colonel H. S. Birkett).
- The printed footer on all diary-text and Agenda pages reads "A2908. Wt. [uncertain] 750,000. 1/17. D.D & L., Ltd. Forms/C2118/14." (a different print run from March's "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12.") and the form carries the printed instructions block at top left ("Instructions regarding War Diaries and Intelligence Summaries are contained in F. S. Regs., Part II. and the Staff Manual respectively. Title pages will be prepared in manuscript.").
- The oval Office of Officer Commanding stamp ("OFFICE OF OFFICER COMMANDING / 30 JUN. 1917 / No. 2 WD. 6-17 / No. 3 Canadian General Hospital—McGill") appears on the cover and on all diary-text and Agenda pages.
- No hours are recorded in June — the Hour column is blank throughout.
- Unlike March, this month gives NO daily admission or discharge figures. The diary-text entries record visits, postings, weather and hospital business only; the 30 June entry refers the reader to "agenda, page three" for the month's admissions and discharges. The Agenda (p. 3) gives the monthly admissions total (4,204 patients), with deaths and operations, but no monthly discharge total is stated anywhere in the volume.
- The Place column is blank throughout, except for 15 June, where "Boulogne" is entered (typed close against the date, "Boulogne15"); the hospital remained at Boulogne all month.
- 24 June: ditto marks (") in the Summary column repeat the previous day's "Nothing to report"; they are expanded in the transcription below.
- 15 June has two entries: the suspected scarlet fever case on diary-text p. 1, and the officers' movements on diary-text p. 2; both are transcribed under their own page.
- The Agenda runs over three pages (June 1917, p. 5–7) and is transcribed as continuous text, not as a table. Quoted passages are as typed: Captain Law's report opens each paragraph with a quotation mark and never closes one; the O., i/c Surgery's report opens with a quotation mark before "sometime ago" and closes after the "l'eau Monciere" formula. Words split by a hyphen at a line break are joined without the hyphen.
- The sentence "The output of dripping has been doubled since January of this year." (Agenda, p. 1) is underlined in the original.

> Some notes on the work of the O.,i/c Provisions, Captain H.E.Law, and his department are worthy of record:- He reports as follows:-
>
> "There were not any shelves in the Bread Room when I came; it was left in sacks and piled on the floor: since then, shelves have been put in, and the room stained. By the use of shelves, a great saving has been effected, the old bread being issued before the new, as it is prevented from getting mouldy and crushed. 480,700 lbs of bread have been handled during the past year.
>
> "The former meat room is now being used as a Store Room, and an excellent Refrigerator has been built at a cost of £36:-:-. This room was designed by Major L.J.Rhea (of the Unit under my command) and this room has reduced the cost of the ice used by half,besides keeping everything in perfect condition. During the whole year, since it has been in use,there has not been any meat destroyed, and the total amount of supplies stored in the Cooling Room during the past twelve months is 154,150 lbs of meat. Fowls,9,648; Fish, 2,974 lbs; Butter, 45,787 lbs; Eggs, 227,355. The amount of ice used during the same period is 13,459 Kilos.
>
> "Formerly, the vegetables were stored in the same room as the groceries,but it was found necessary to have them removed to a cooler place,owing to the rapid decay during the hot weather,causing very disagreeable odours. Various devices were tried to remedy this unsatisfactory state of affairs, but it was found best to build a shed with a cement floor. This has resulted in complete satisfaction.
>
> "Inasmuch as this hospital covers 25 acres of ground, with a bed capacity of 2,000 beds it was found expedient to have two Diet Kitchens, No 1 and No 2. This greatly relieved the pressure on Diet Kitchen No 1, and at the same time enabled the cooks to serve the meals hot and promptly to the wards.
>
> "The amount realized by the sale of dripping before orders were given for the same to be shipped to England, March 1916 to November 1916, amounted to 795.50 (Fr). From December to March inclusive, the amount realized was 836.05 (Fr) being considerably less per lb than was formerly obtained from the French buyers. The output of dripping has been doubled since January of this year. [The sentence "The output of dripping has been doubled since January of this year." is underlined in the original.]
>
> "To my knowledge, this is the only hospital that is selling meat bones. During December 1916 to May 1917, £32.50 (Fr) was realized by the sale of 3,876 Kilos.

> "The total amount saved on foodstuffs issued to wards and Diet Kitchens amounts to £12,251.13.7d and for personal rations,amounts to £2421:1:0d making a grand total of £14,672:14:7½d

> The O.,i/c Surgery reports that "sometime ago, a small amount of Flavine ('acriflavine') was sent to our hospital from America. I used it, in weak solutions (5%) as a dressing for septic wounds, and found it superior to Eusol, or Solution of Brilliant Green, in that blood serum did not inhibit its action as a germicide, and I thought I got a higher degree of leucocytosis from its use. However, the quantity at my disposal was so small, that I could not give it more than an experimental trial,and shall be glad of an opportunity for giving it a further trial. "Brilliant Green" solution, I have had more experience with, but both as a germicide, and as a regenerator of new tissue, I find a 2% solution of Ether in boiled (not boiling) water, used either as a bath, or by irrigation,superior to anything I have tried for cleansing foul wounds,and replacing dirty sloughs by healthy granulation tissue. To render the field operation aseptic, I think the application of 5% Picric Acid solution is best, provided it is applied twelve hours before operating, and the skin kept dry afterwards. If moist dressings are subsequently put on over this,irritation (almost eczema) of the skin is very likely to follow. The germicidal action of Picric Acid solution I find is slow,and therefore, I hold it should be applied several hours before operation. Cleansing of the skin for immediate operation I think can best be done by brisk rubbing with Ether following by swabbing lightly with "l'eau Monciere" (Benzoic acid 1 gramme, guaiacol 5 grammes, alcohol 4 grammes, Alcohol boiling water 1 litre)".
