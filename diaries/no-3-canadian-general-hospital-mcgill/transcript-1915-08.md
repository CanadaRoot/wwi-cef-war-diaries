> | Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks |
> | Camiers. | August. 9 | 2.00 p.m. | The first operation performed, followed by thirteen others many of which were of a very serious nature, no less than four of them being compound fractures of the skull. The arrangement of the Operating Hut, as suggested by the Chief of the Surgical Division, Lt. Colonel J. M. Elder, was to-day put to a very thorough test. The arrangement consisted of occupying that portion of the hut, set apart for beds, by a series of (4) cubicles, so that it allowed of four surgeons operating simultaneously and thus facilitating and expediting the heavy work to a marked and satisfactory degree. I wish also to draw attention to the localization of foreign bodies (pieces of shrapnel and bullets) by means of X-Rays. This work is under the direction of Capt. A. Howard Pirie, who has associated with him Capt. W. Wilkins. The work is so accurately carried out that in every case it is found that the situation of the foreign body is determined to the fractional part of an inch. The work reflects great credit upon those working in this Department. The Gas-Ether apparatus, which was presented to this Unit by Mrs. Danforth of Boston, Mass., U.S.A., has been used under the able direction of Capt. W. B. Howell, Chief Anaesthetist to the Unit, and this method of Anaesthesia has been found especially useful in penetrating wounds of the Thorax. | H.S.B. |

## Page 6 — Diary text, p. 4 (August 1915, p. 6)

> PAGE FOUR. — WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY — Army Form C. 2118
>
> | Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks |
> | Camiers. | August 27 | Cont. | two main lines coming north and south are clear. This, on every occasion which I have visited the siding of each convoy which has come into Camiers, has occupied 45 minutes. A possible further delay is caused by the Ambulances having to cross and recross the low level of the railway line and are frequently held up by passing trains. All these delays could have been avoided had the siding which existed close to the camp been utilized. This siding would then have been well within the camp lines, into which the train could have passed at once without the delay of waiting to cross to the other side. Further, the delays to the Ambulances would also have been avoided as their work would be carried on altogether within the camp lines. | H.S.B. |
> | " | 28 | | Nothing worthy of comment occurred. | H.S.B. |
> | " | 29 | | " " " " | H.S.B. |
> | " | 30 | 11.30 p.m. | Received notification that convoy would probably arrive at this time. It was not, however, in the east siding until 12.15 a.m. and it was 1.10 a.m. when the first patient was moved from the train after having taken all this time to reach the siding on the west side. | H.S.B. |
> | " | 31 | | The inconvenience of keeping Officers and men up expecting arrival at any moment was minimized considerably through an arrangement of the D. M. O. whereby the telephone message was sent to one Hospital in this area and that information conveyed to the various other hospitals by orderly. This information was conveyed about twenty minutes before the detraining of patients was expected to take place. This information on its receipt was conveyed to such Officers and orderlies as were needed to be on duty. The result was that through this arrangement very considerable loss of time and sleep were avoided. | H.S.B. |
