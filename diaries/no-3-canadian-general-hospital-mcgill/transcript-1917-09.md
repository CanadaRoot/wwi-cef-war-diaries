## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written. Typewritten on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or passage that could not be read with confidence — never guessed. Two `[uncertain]` marks were needed this month: a faint handwritten mark near the top centre of the cover, and the overtyped percentage in the Agenda's August statistics (see the open questions below).
- As in March and June, the sub-heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" IS struck through, with "or" handwritten above it — the heading reads "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", with "(Erase heading not required.)" beneath it.
- The Remarks column throughout is the handwritten initials "H.S.B." (Colonel H. S. Birkett).
- The printed footer on diary-text pp. 1–2 and on the Agenda page reads "D. D. & L., London, E.C. (A8001) Wt. W1771/M2031 750,000 5/17 Sch. 88 Forms/C2118/14" (the same print run as July and August; June's was "A2908. Wt. [uncertain] 750,000. 1/17. D.D & L., Ltd. Forms/C2118/14."). Diary-text p. 3 is on the older print run, "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12." (the March run), and the form carries the printed instructions block at top left ("Instructions regarding War Diaries and Intelligence Summaries are contained in F. S. Regs., Part II. and the Staff Manual respectively. Title pages will be prepared in manuscript.").
- The oval Office of Officer Commanding stamp ("OFFICE OF OFFICER COMMANDING / 1-OCT.1917 / No. 2-WD-9-17 / No. 3 Canadian General Hospital-McGill") appears on the cover and on all diary-text and Agenda pages. On the cover the stamp is double-struck, the "1-OCT.1917" and "2-WD-9-17" impressions overlapping.
- No hours are recorded in September — the Hour column is blank throughout. The Place column is also blank throughout; the hospital remained at Boulogne all month.
- The 13 September entry is split across diary-text pp. 1–2: it begins at the foot of p. 1 ("Warned all ranks that disciplinary measures will be taken against any member of the") and continues at the head of p. 2 ("Unit who may be reported for gambling............"); both parts are transcribed under their own page.
- The 21 September entry has no new date number for its final paragraph (the six nursing sisters taken on strength and N/Sister Stewart, E.J, returning from leave); it is transcribed as part of the 21 September entry. Likewise the "Seventeen Nursing Sisters attached for temporary duty" sentence at the foot of diary-text p. 2 has no date number and is transcribed as part of the 22 September entry. The Remarks column shows what appear to be extra "H.S.B." initials at the foot of diary-text p. 2, whose alignment with these unnumbered passages is unclear.
- In the 21 September Boulogne Base Medical Society list, the number "(3)" is used twice, as typed: "(3) Method of examining direct smears from haemothorax cases" and "(3) Types of Streptococci in wounds"; the list as typed runs (1), (2), (3), (3), (4), (5).
- The Agenda is a single page (September 1917, p. 6), headed "AGENDA — Page one. September" and marked "APPENDIX 1." in handwriting at the top right; the cover is likewise marked "with appendix 1" in handwriting at the bottom left. It is transcribed as continuous text, not as a table. Words split by a hyphen at a line break are joined without the hyphen.
- Unlike June, this month's Agenda gives NO September deaths or operations totals — only the admissions breakdown (walkers and stretcher cases, surgical and medical, plus detained cases) totalling 4,192 patients received. August's figures are quoted for comparison in the same Agenda: 4,216 admissions, 32 deaths, 363 operations, 11 deaths after operation, "three per cent" post-operative mortality, and a total mortality percentage that is overtyped in the typescript and corrected by hand in the Remarks column (see the open questions below).
- The September admissions figures add up exactly: 984 + 1,049 + 409 + 520 + 1,230 = 4,192.

> [Handwritten at top right, over "Original":] APPENDIX 1.
>
> During the month of September, there have been several air-raids in this vicinity, the most noteworthy having occurred on the 4th instant. At about 11.0 p.m the droning of the enemy engines could be heard distinctly above the hospital. The sound was so audible that many came to the conclusion that Zeppelins were approaching. This was quite excusable for the raiders were undoubtedly big machines, probably of the Gotha type. This droning became louder and filled the air to such an extent that it became impossible to tell by ear from which direction the sound came; now and again, it seemed as if the machines were immediately overhead. When the first plane showed up in the beam, the anti-aircraft guns came into action immediately. That the targets were very high was evident, for it was possible to count five or six seconds between the report of the gun and the time when the flash of the bursting shells could be seen among the clouds. Bombs began to drop, some of which made a sharp whistling sound. One piece of shell fell at the Officers' quarters doing no damage; tiles from some of the wards near the Administrative Hut fell down, but no-one was injured. Immediately outside the hospital, there was much damage to civilian property and also to a French hospital close by. Many windows were broken by descending trains of shell............
>

>
> [The heading "STATISTICS" is underlined.]

[Handwritten in the Remarks column beside this paragraph:] 0.69%

## Open questions / uncertain items (September 1917)
- Cover volume numbering: the cover reads "(VOLUME XXVIII)" (underlined) while the contents page reads "Volume 17-9" (June: cover "VOL. XXV", contents "Volume 17-6") — both are transcribed as written.
- Cover: a faint handwritten mark near the top centre could not be read; marked [uncertain] (verified by re-reading page 214). Below the stamp is a handwritten "7."; at the bottom left, "with appendix 1" is handwritten and underlined.
- Cover stamp: the oval Office of Officer Commanding stamp is double-struck on the cover, so that "1-OCT.1917" and "No. 2-WD-9-17" overlap and at first glance read "5-OCT.1917" (verified by zoom crop `zoom-sep17-cover-stamp2.jpg`). The same stamp on the diary-text and Agenda pages reads clearly "1-OCT.1917 / No. 2-WD-9-17".
- Contents page count: the contents page reads "Diary Text 4 Pages", but only three diary-text pages are present (September 1917, p. 3–5); the fourth sheet is the single Agenda page (p. 6). (June: contents counted 4 pages, five sheets present.)
- 1 September: "Lt.Colonel W. Burton Fanning, RAMC" — the initial "W." is as typed; the same officer is "Lt.Colonel F.W.Burton-Fanning, RAMC, T.F." on 10 September.
- 8 September: "reposing from No.1 Canadian Casualty Clearing Station" is as typed (presumably a typist's error for "reposting"); "Nursing Sister F. MacKeen" as typed.
- 9 September: "meningococci" is split "meningo- / cocci" across lines and joined in the transcription. Two cerebro-spinal meningitis cases were sent to No. 14 Stationary Hospital; the contacts showed no meningococci. No deaths are named in this volume.
- 13 September: the entry is split across diary-text pp. 1–2 (see conventions). Sir Henry Burdett is described as "Author and Statist" as typed.
- 14 September: "Lieut. McKenan" as typed (verified by zoom crops `zoom-sep17-p2-mckenan.jpg` and `zoom-sep17-p2-mckenan3.jpg`).
- 15 September: "Mr. Irving Bacheller, Writer, Riverside, Conn., U.S.A." and "Lieut Hon., Lionel Aubyn, K.R.R" as typed.
- 21 September: the Medical Society list numbers "(3)" twice, as typed (see conventions). "Major R.J. Rhea, O., i/c Pathology" as typed — June's Agenda gives "Major L.J. Rhea" for the officer who designed the refrigerator, so the initial here may be the same man with an unclear initial; transcribed as written. "Major J.J. Knea" as typed. The nursing-sisters paragraph at the end of the 21 September entry has no date number of its own; "N/Sister Stewart, E.J" is a different sister from "Nursing Sister E.A. Stewart" taken on strength on 1 September.
- 22 September: the "Seventeen Nursing Sisters attached for temporary duty" sentence has no date number of its own and is transcribed as part of the 22 September entry; the Remarks column shows extra "H.S.B." initials at the foot of the page whose alignment is unclear.
- 24 September: "Her Royal Highness Princess Louise" — "Highness" carries a small handwritten insertion/caret mark above it. The date is written "24." with a full stop, unlike the other dates. "Surgeon-General R.H.S. Sawyer, C.M.G., D.D.M.S, Boulogne Base" as typed. "Nursing Sister E.L. Dickie" as typed.
- 26 September: "Captain W.W. Francis, Registrar" proceeded to the G.O.C., 6th Division Imperial as a witness in a Field General Court Martial; he returned from special duty on 29 September. "Nursing Sisters E.R. Lordly and E.K. Cotter" as typed.
- 27 September: "Mr. Colmer, C.M.G., of the Canadian War Contingent Association" as typed.
- 28 September: the Destructor explosion happened "at midnight" — the entry is dated 28 September, so the explosion was on the night of 27–28 September. The Destructor destroyed is the one near the Red Cross Hut; the new Field Incinerator reported ready on 15 September is not mentioned as affected.
- Diary-text p. 3 (September 1917, p. 5) is printed on the older form run ("2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12."), while pp. 1–2 and the Agenda use the newer run ("Wt. W1771/M2031 750,000 5/17").
- Agenda, August statistics: the typed total-mortality percentage reads "0.75%" but is heavily overtyped, and the Remarks column carries a handwritten "0.69%" beside the paragraph (verified by zoom crops `zoom-sep17-ag-stats.jpg` and `zoom-sep17-ag-pct.jpg`). The post-operative figure checks out: 11 / 363 = 3.03% ("three per cent"). The typed figure is marked [uncertain] in the transcription.

- Patient totals for September: 4,192 patients received (984 walkers and 1,049 stretcher cases on the surgical side; 409 walkers and 520 stretcher cases on the medical side; 1,230 detained cases). For comparison, August: 4,216 admissions, 32 deaths, 363 operations, 11 post-operative deaths. No casualties are named in this volume.
