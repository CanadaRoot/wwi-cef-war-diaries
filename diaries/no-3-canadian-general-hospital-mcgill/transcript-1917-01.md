## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written. Typewritten on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed. No item in this month required an `[uncertain]` mark; the punctuation after "Birkett" in the 2 January Gazette quotation, the "A.A.Parker" overtype on 7 January, and the "No.3.Canadian Stationary Hospital" numeral on 17 January are readable and are noted in the open questions below.
- The Remarks column throughout is the handwritten initials "H.S.B." (Colonel H. S. Birkett).
- Unlike December, on the January diary-text pages the sub-heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" IS struck through — the heading reads "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", with "(Erase heading not required.)" beneath it.
- The printed footer on all five diary-text pages reads "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D. D. & L. A.D.S.S. Forms/C 2118." and the form carries the printed instructions block at top left ("Instructions regarding War Diaries and Intelligence Summaries are contained in F. S. Regs., Part II. and the Staff Manual respectively. Title pages will be prepared in manuscript.").
- The oval Office of Officer Commanding stamp ("OFFICE OF OFFICER COMMANDING / 31 JAN 1917 / No. 2 W.D. 1-17 / No. 3 Canadian General Hospital—McGill") appears on the cover and on all five diary-text pages.
- No hours are recorded in January — the Hour column is blank throughout. The diary is kept as daily admission and discharge totals; the word used for patients leaving is "discharged" throughout.
- The Place column gives "Boulogne" once at the head of each diary-text page and is otherwise blank (no ditto marks this month). The Date column gives "1917 Jan'y" at the head of each page, then the day numeral.
- 25 January has two entries, both dated 25: the first (93 admitted, 96 discharged) carries the cold-weather note, the second (79 admitted, 103 discharged) gives figures only.

## Page 4 — Diary text, p. 2 (January 1917, p. 4)

> — Page two — WAR DIARY — Army Form C. 2118
>
> | Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks |
> | Boulogne | 1917 Jan'y 11 | | Nursing Sister G.S.Usborne,granted three weeks Sick Leave to England. Thirteen Ward utility huts are under construction. Slight increase of P.U.O cases during the week. The main drain of the hospital became partially blocked for two days,but I had arrangements made at once for clearing the sewage away; all possible contaminated areas were disinfected. A number of cases of diarrhoea have been apparent among the patients & personnel, but happily none of them were serious. The cause could not be determined. A case of measles occurred, but all precautions were taken; the rest of the occupants of the tent in which the case was found were put under daily medical inspection, and the disease did not spread any further......... | H.S.B. |
> | | 12 | | Admissions 102; Discharges 36. Weather moderated, and atmosphere fine and dry. Nursing Sister M.F.Bliss admitted to Lady Giffard's Convalescent Home......... | H.S.B. |
> | | 13 | | Admissions 102; Discharges 91; Patient received by convoy said to have been a contact of cerebro-spinal meningitis. Cultures on special medium were made from the nose,pharynx & throat. No signs or symptoms developed. A member of the personnel developed nasal diphtheria with pharyngeal involvment. The source of the infection could not be ascertained, but the members of the tent were isolated and every precaution taken so that infection might not spread. Major A.L.Burch,Presbyterian Chaplain, is attached......... | H.S.B. |
> | | 14 | | Admissions 10; Discharges 80. Much snow and sleet turning to rain......... | H.S.B. |
> | | 15 | | Admissions 104; Discharges 33. Dry and very cold, particularly at night......... | H.S.B. |
> | | 16 | | Admissions 13; Discharges 126. . Nursing Sister M.J.Ross,admitted to Princess Louise Convalescent Home. Nursing Sister E.C.Anderson,CAMC,taken on strength on reporting from England(Auth., Telegram,Cefmed,CM-1564 dated 16-1-17)......... | H.S.B. |
> | | 17 | | Admissions 43; Discharges 36. Captain G.Shanks struck off strength on proceeding to England to take out a permanent commission in India Medical Service. (Auth.,D.M.S., L of C.,M3/16/17 dated 13-1-17). Captain H.C.Burgess,CAMC struck off strength on proceeding to No.3.Canadian Stationary Hospital for duty. I wish to record here the very efficient services of Captains Shanks and Burgess respectively. In their respective departments, they have done valuable work. Captain Shanks has carried on a good deal of Laboratory work under the direction of Major Rhea, and his services have been invaluable. Captain Burgess has also discharged his duties as M.O to my entire satisfaction. His ward has always been splendidly managed and his surgical work extremely good, and it is with very deep regret that I have parted with the services of these very excellent officers. One case of cerebro-spinal meningitis received. All the direct contacts were carefully examined for the question of carriers, but no symptoms suggesting the disease were found......... | H.S.B. |

> During the month of January 1917, the matter of precaution [against fire — interlineated above "precaution"] has received my particular attention. The Unit being divided into three sections for the reception and evacuation of wounded, the convoy Group for the day has acted as Fire Picquet. ## Open questions / uncertain items (January 1917)
- Cover volume numbering: the cover reads "VOLUME XX" while the contents page reads "Volume 17-1" (December: cover "VOLUME XIX", contents "Volume 16-12") — both are transcribed as written.
- 2 January: the punctuation after "Birkett" at the end of the first Gazette quotation reads closing quote followed by a full stop, "Birkett"." (verified by zoom crop `zoom-jan17-birkett.jpg`).
- 2 January: "for promotion in,and appointment" and "para;7 d/1-1-17" are as typed. Colonel Birkett's appointment is to the Order of the Bath (Companion, Third Class); the entry quotes the London Gazette No.29886 dated 1-1-17 and Corps Orders No.1, para 7.
- 3 January: the convalescent home is spelled "Chateau Mauricean" — a third spelling after December's "Chateau Maurician" (14 and 17 December) and "Chateau Mauricien" (20 December) — all as typed (verified by zoom crop `zoom-jan17-p127-top.jpg`).
- 7 January: "A.A.Parker" in the list of captains taken on strength looks slightly overtyped, but reads as A.A.Parker and appears clearly in the 8 January entry (verified by zoom crop `zoom-jan17-p127-mid.jpg`). The authority reads "D.G.M.S.,D.G., G.306 dated 12-12-16".
- 8 January: Captain A. Blais is taken on strength and struck off strength on the same day (to the 4th Canadian Division). Captains F.G.Logie, J.K.Mossman and A.A.Parker go to the Second Canadian Division and Captain D.R.Dunlop to the 3rd Canadian Division; the entry notes that six officer reinforcements were sent but six officers were ordered to the front within a week, leaving the situation as before.
- 9 January: "Captain C.K.Wallace,CAMC, (formerly Sanitary Office to this Unit)" — "Sanitary Office" as typed.
- 11 January: the entry ends with an unmatched closing parenthesis, "telegram dated 10-1-17)" — as typed.
- 13 January: "with pharyngeal involvment" — "involvment" as typed (missing "e").
- 16 January: a stray full stop follows "Discharges 126." before "Nursing Sister M.J.Ross" — transcribed as written.
- 17 January: Captain H.C.Burgess proceeds to "No.3.Canadian Stationary Hospital" — the numeral is 3, verified by zoom crop `zoom-jan17-burgess.jpg`.
- 18 January: the Part Two Orders reference reads "No.684,para;2,868" as typed (verified by zoom crop `zoom-jan17-honours.jpg`).
- 24 January: "Surgeon-General T.P.Woodhouse" as typed; the December Agenda gave the same officer as "T.W.Woodhouse". "Lt.Colonel Y.Inowy" and "Major S.Golo" as typed.
- 25 January: there are two entries dated 25 January (93 admitted / 96 discharged, with the cold-weather note; and 79 admitted / 103 discharged, figures only). "The material wa disinfected" — "wa" as typed.
- Agenda (p. 4): "we discharged to England,U.C.,Base Depots Hospitals &c., 35,349." — "U.C." as typed. The 1916 totals check: 18,946 sick + 17,195 wounded = 36,141 admissions; 18,217 sick + 17,132 wounded = 35,349 discharged.
- Agenda (p. 5): "against fire" is interlineated by hand above "precaution" in the fire-precautions paragraph. The X-ray breakdown (16 + 8 + 4 + 1 + 1 + 3 + 1 + 1 = 35) accounts for only 35 of the 366 photographs made in December 1916; the remainder were presumably made for this hospital itself.
- Patient totals for January: 2,479 admitted and 2,049 discharged (32 daily figures; two dated 25 January). The heaviest day was 31 January (391 admitted); the lightest was 6 January (8 admitted). No casualties are named in this volume — patients are recorded only as daily numbers, and the only deaths recorded are the 151 in the 1916 Agenda statistics.
- No appendices are listed for this volume and none are filed in it.
- Contents page count matches the pages present: "Diary Text 5 Pages" and five diary-text pages are in the volume.
- Colonel H. S. Birkett remains O.C.; the diary is signed by him as "H.S. Birkett. Col. A.M.S., O.C. No. 3 Canadian General Hospital (McGill)".
