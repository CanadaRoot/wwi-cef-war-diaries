> | Camiers. | Sept. 19. | Continued. | assist the clinician in making an early diagnosis of cases infected with these organisms in order that such cases may be sent as soon as possible to Hospital established especially for the treatment of them. The laboratory work being done in this connection consists of two methods of study—The Bacteriological and the Serological. The Bacteriological study consists of cultures made from the blood and the stools and urine. As the cases usually reach this Hospital too late in the course of the disease for cultures to be obtained from the blood, the greater number of positive results are obtained by the more difficult method of examination — that of the stools and urine. As stools normally contain so many organisms, and the affecting organisms may be so few in number, special Bacteriological methods are employed. These methods consist of the collection of a portion of the stools in distilled water. The stool is thoroughly mixed in the water and the grosser particles allowed to settle to the bottom. From the supernatant fluid, cultures are made into a fluid medium which contains a very weak solution of an aniline dye (brilliant green). This fluid medium is allowed to incubate for twenty hours, when stroke cultures are made on to a specially prepared Agar plate. This Agar contains two colouring matters — Litmus and Methyl Violet, as well as Lactose. The action of the colon bacillus upon the lactose medium produces a red colony, while the members of the Typhoid group of organisms do not show this reddening of the colonies.
> | | | | In studying the Serological actions of the organisms, isolated specific sera of Typhoid, Paratyphoid A, and Paratyphoid B, are employed. This is done in order to test the agglutinability of the organisms against the known sera, so that a more rapid diagnosis may be made. We are also making a study of the agglutinating properties of the sera of patients suffering from infections with the Typhoid group of organisms. At the same time we are doing many cases which do not show the clinical picture of infection with the Typhoid group of organisms, and whose stools do not show members of this group. This latter work is being done for two reasons — 1. To determine whether or not certain obscure cases of fever are due to Paratyphoid infections. 2. To determine the range of agglutinating properties of serum from patients who have been inoculated against Typhoid fever. | H.S.B. |
> | " | 20 | 3.00 p.m. | To-day we had a distinguished honour of a visit from Her Majesty Queen Amelia of Portugal, who was accompanied by Lady Clifford. Her Majesty visited several of the wards, stopping to converse with many of the patients offering them words of comfort and distributing small bags of Lavender. At the conclusion of the visit Her Majesty expressed great appreciation of all that she had seen. | H.S.B. |

> | | | | [Month-end surgical summary, in the Summary column below the 30 September entries:] The amount of Surgical work carried on under the direction of Lt. Col. J. M. Elder, with his able Staff, has consisted of a very large number of fractures. There have been 280 cases requiring major operations and six of anaerobic infection, which, till last week, the majority of infected wounds were due to shrapnel or shell but during the last week bullet wounds have almost completely replaced shell wounds. This means a very great increase in the number of cases of fracture of the long bones and of perforating wounds of the thorax and abdomen. There have been a fair number of cases of secondary hemorrhage, none of which has proved fatal. There have been but three amputations performed, two of the lower extremity and one of the upper, all being cases of gas gangrene. The Surgical Department owe a debt of gratitude to the efficient work of the X Ray Department and to the Pathological Department for their prompt returns as to the precise nature of infections, also to the Anaesthetic Department for their very excellent work. Total number of operations 280; post operative mortality 5, (nearly 2%). | H.S.B. |
