- **Unit:** No. 3 Canadian General Hospital (McGill), Canadian Army Medical Corps

- **LAC Collection Search:** Item ID 2005099 — "War diaries - 3rd Canadian General Hospital, Basingstoke", 1915/03/05–1919/06/24

## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written. Typewritten on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed. No item in this month required an `[uncertain]` mark; one handwritten stamp number is read as written but is hard to read (see open questions).
- The Remarks column throughout is the handwritten initials "H.S.B." (Colonel H. S. Birkett). The 1 November block carries three sets of initials, one against each paragraph of the entry.
- On all three diary-text pages the sub-heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is struck through (the heading reads "WAR DIARY" with "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" crossed out); "(Erase heading not required.)" appears beneath it.
- The printed footer on the diary-text pages reads "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D.D.& L. A.D.S.S.Forms/C 2118." (a different print run of Army Form C. 2118 from September's "T2124. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J.C. & S.") and the form carries the printed instructions block at top left ("Instructions regarding War Diaries and Intelligence Summaries are contained in F. S. Regs., Part II. and the Staff Manual respectively. Title pages will be prepared in manuscript."). On page 5 the final typed lines run over the printed footer, partly obscuring it.
- No hours are recorded in the Hour column this month — it is blank throughout. The only time given is "At 5.45 p.m." in the text of the 27 November entry, for the visit of the members of Parliament. The word used for patients leaving is "discharged" throughout; "evacuated" is not used for the daily figures this month.
- As in September, ditto marks (") in the Place and Date columns are expanded to the repeated value in the transcription below. The Date column heads "1916 Nov" with the day on the first entry row (1 November).
- An oval office stamp, "OFFICE OF OFFICER COMMANDING, No. 3 Canadian General Hospital—McGill", dated "1- DEC.1916", No. "2 F 11/16", is stamped on the cover and on all three diary-text pages, over the top right of the Summary column.

> | " | 6 | | 36 Admissions; 48 Discharges. | H.S.B. |

> | " | 8 | | 27 Admissions; 127 Discharges. The weather during the month so far has been comparatively dry and cold; sudden change in temperature and heavy downfalls of rain. The drainage system has proved adequate to meet the situation, leaving the ground by the tents dry and clean. | H.S.B. |
> | " | 9 | | 69 Admissions; 103 Discharges. One hundred detained cases admitted. Bright sunshine, fair and dry. | H.S.B. |
> | " | 10 | | 12 Admissions; 46 Discharges. The hundred detained cases sent to hospital ship, but returned. His Royal Highness was received by a Guard of Honour which he inspected; then the Officers of the Unit were presented individually after which His Royal Highness made a careful tour of the hospital, visiting many of the Wards, where he had frequent conversations with patients. At 5.45 p.m. the following members of Parliament visited the hospital:- Lt.Colonel G.H.Bradbury, Lt.Colonel A.C.Pratt, Lt.Colonel W.H.Sharpe, Lt.Colonel Sam Sharpe, Lt.Colonel Geo.W.Fowler, Lt.Colonel J.W.Aylen, Colonel Thomas C.Treadwell, U.S., M.C., Major E.N.Lewis, Captain J.H.Burnham, Captain Edward Bassett. These members of the Canadian House of Commons made a tour of the hospital and expressed themselves as being profoundly impressed by what they were pleased to term the organization of the various departments visited. Heavy downfalls of rain continued throughout the day. | H.S.B. |
> | " | 28 | | 23 Admissions; 80 Discharges. The weather has suddenly turned to be very cold, particularly at night, and in the morning several degrees of frost were noted. | H.S.B. |

Captains L.H.Thornton, C.A.D.C, and W.G.McNevin, C.A.D.C struck off strength on proceeding to England from No.13 Stationary Hospital, where they had been doing temporary duty as Dental Surgeons. | H.S.B. |
> | " | 30 | | Morning Convoy; admissions 57; discharges 30. The highest number of P.U.O's during the month of November reached 382 on the 3rd inst., It is also to be noted that a very large number of the N.C.O's and men, viz., 23, have been admitted to hospital with colds. This has no doubt been largely due to the frequent sudden changes in temperature which have been particularly marked during the month. Notwithstanding the extra strain, the work has continued to be performed to my entire satisfaction. The work at times has been very heavy, convoys and evacuations taking place at very early hours. | H.S.B. |

## Open questions / uncertain items (November 1916)
- 1 November: "Major J.G.Brown" is as typed here; the same officer is written "Major J.G.Browne" in the 24 September entry. Both spellings are transcribed as written; no correction has been made (verified by zoom crop `zoom-nov-p3-top.jpg`).
- 1 November: "Nursing Sister Constance M Stuart" — no stops or comma between "M" and "Stuart" as typed (verified by zoom crop `zoom-nov-p3-top.jpg`).
- 1 November: the 1 November block carries three sets of "H.S.B." initials in the Remarks column, one against each paragraph of the entry (verified by zoom crop `zoom-nov-p3-top.jpg`).
- 7 November: the authority reference reads "(Auth., DMS., Canadians,10-11-2,d/6-11-16)" — "10-11-2" is as typed, though it looks like a truncated file reference (verified by zoom crop `zoom-nov-p3-mid.jpg`).
- 9–10 November: "One hundred detained cases admitted" (9th) and "The hundred detained cases sent to hospital ship, but returned" (10th) — "detained" is as typed both times; no reason for the detention or the return is given in the diary (verified by zoom crops `zoom-nov-p3-mid.jpg` and `zoom-nov-p3-bot.jpg`).
- 10 November: "Lt.Col;Stanton" — a semicolon is typed where a comma would be expected (verified by zoom crop `zoom-nov-p3-bot.jpg`). "Lt.Col.J.V.Forrest,C.M.G" is as typed.
- 10 November: the Duke of Connaught entry begins on diary-text page 1 and continues at the top of page 2 ("pleased with all that he had seen..."); the continuation is transcribed under 10 November on Page 4 (verified by zoom crop `zoom-nov-p4-top.jpg`).
- 14 November: "Lt.Colonel John McCrae, O.,i/c Medicine" — "O.,i/c" is as typed (verified by zoom crop `zoom-nov-p4-top.jpg`). The same form is used for Lt-Colonel J.M.Elder, "O.,i/c Surgery", on 21 November.
- 21 November: the authority reference reads "(Auth., M.C.P.2081. 19, DDMS, BB., M.1821/16, d/19-11-16.)" — "M.C.P.2081. 19" is as typed, with a space before "19" (verified by zoom crop `zoom-nov-p4-mid.jpg`).
- 22 November: the Keeley attachment entry precedes the day's patient figures on the same line ("Capt.J.A.Keeley... 90 Admissions; 80 Discharges.") — transcribed in that order (verified by zoom crop `zoom-nov-p4-bot.jpg`).
- 25 November: "No.3 London General Hosp'l" — "Hosp'l" is as typed (verified by zoom crop `zoom-nov-p5-top.jpg`).
- 27 November: "Lt.Colonel Sam Sharpe" and "Lt.Colonel Geo.W.Fowler" are as typed — "Sam" and "Geo." are not expanded (verified by zoom crop `zoom-nov-p5-mid.jpg`).
- 30 November: "The highest number of P.U.O's during the month of November reached 382 on the 3rd inst." — "382" is as typed; the figure is high but is clearly typed (verified by zoom crop `zoom-nov-p5-bot.jpg`). "viz., 23" N.C.O's and men admitted with colds is also as typed.
- 30 November: the last two typed lines run over the printed footer, so the footer on page 5 is only partly legible; it is the same "2353 Wt. W2544/1454 700,000 5/15 D.D.& L. A.D.S.S.Forms/C 2118." run as on pages 3 and 4 (verified by zoom crop `zoom-nov-p5-bot.jpg`).

- Office stamp: the oval "OFFICE OF OFFICER COMMANDING" stamp, dated "1- DEC.1916", appears on the cover and on all three diary-text pages. Its handwritten number is read as "2 F 11/16" but the handwriting is crowded; it may be "2 F 1/1/16" (verified by zoom crops `zoom-nov-cover-stamp.jpg`, `zoom-nov-p4-top.jpg` and `zoom-nov-p5-top.jpg`).
- Patient totals for November: 2,750 admitted and 2,867 discharged (30 daily figures each; every date 1–30 November has figures). The heaviest admissions day was 21 November (242 admitted); the lightest was 5 November (9 admitted); the heaviest discharges day was 3 November (253 discharged). No casualties are named in this volume — patients are recorded only as daily numbers.
- No appendices are listed for this volume and none are filed in it.
- Colonel H. S. Birkett remains O.C.; the diary is signed by him as "H.S. Birkett. Col. A.M.S., O.C. No. 3 Canadian General Hospital (McGill)".
