Heading: WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY [the words "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" are struck through] — MAY 1916 — 5th CANADIAN BRIGADE MACHINE GUN COY. "ORIGINAL — CONFIDENTIAL".

Remarks column: each entry is initialled "S.W. Capt."

### 1.5.16 — FIELD

> REF. MAP. SHEET 28 OF BELGIUM SCALE 1:40.000 — TRENCH MAP. VOORMEZEELE. PART OF SHEET 28 SCALE 1:10 000.
>
> Two Artillery positions. AP1. (I33A31) and AP. 2 (I33B3.6) each occupied by a gun and crew of the 5th CDN Bde MG Co'y were damaged by hostile artillery fire between the hours of 2 PM and 4 PM. At AP. 1 the gun was put out of action and a number of spare parts destroyed. At AP. 2 the front of the emplacement was blown in and the spare machine gun barrel damaged. The emplacement was repaired immediately the shelling stopped. Indirect fire was used from I 32 D3.6, I 32 C 4.3 and the sunken road at O1 A 2.1. covering HIELE FARM. STELOI ROAD MESSINES - ST. ELOI ROAD and between MARTINS FARM. The machine gun at SARDINE BOX (I 32 A.2.1.) manned by the BORDENS M.G BATTERY was slightly damaged by fire which broke out at 10 PM. An alternative position was manned by a gun from VOORMEZEELE RESERVE. LIEUT. F.H. DUCK and two O.R. reported at the CAMIERES MACHINE GUN SCHOOL for a course of instruction. Casualties Killed - one O.R. Wounded two O.R. Total strength 9. OFFICERS. 147 O.R.

### 2.5.16 — (")

> Indirect fire was again used covering HIELE FARM. STELOI ROAD MESSINES ST. ELOI ROAD and the area between MARTINS FARM inclusive. 6000 rounds were fired. The enemy used indirect fire through out the night on the road between the BRASSERIE and VOORMEZEELE

## Pages 14–15 — Appendix D — Reconnaissance report, 19 May 1916

> APPENDIX D
>
> FROM: 5th CDN Bde MACHINE GUN COMPANY
>
> TO. 5th CON INF. Bde — 19.5.16.
>
> Sir:
>
> RECONNAISANCE
>
> I have the honour to report on a reconnaissance which I carried out this morning at ARUNDEL HOUSE S.P. BURNHAM & UPPER OOSTHOEK FARM
>
> ARUNDEL HOUSE from any point of view is hopeless as a strong point as there is nothing to make it one. From a machine gun point of view we never had any field of fire to speak of and now of course it is much it is much worse with the undergrowth and thickening of the hedges. The building itself is in a very dilapidated condition as all the work that has been done on it at one time has begun to fall away with decay and I do not consider it worth spending any more time on it unless the whole plan of defence were to be remodelled and even then of course it could not make a better vantage point for a machine gun
>
> S.P. BURNHAM. Also in my estimation is a very poor strong point and would be more of a support trench than anything else if it were properly revetted and good dug outs put in. The so called machine gun emplacement is just a hole in the ground where the gun is kept in the day time and at night it is moved back of the parapet behind a low mound. A garrison of ten men is kept in the trench but they are not near the machine gunners.
>
> UPPER OOSTHOEK FARM has quite a lot of advantages over both the previously named S.Ps. For one thing the site is better for an S.P. even if it is a little exposed if there is at least one cellar in the building which if it was properly drained and cleaned out could be made good use of as a dug out. At present it is in a filthy condition. The field of fire for a machine gun is good for a S.P. and covers the ground for about 100 yds in front and to the right behind behind LOWER OOSTHOEK FARM and S.P. BURNHAM. There is a little dome or mound over at present filled with debris about 15 yds in advance of the farm proper which could I think be made into an emplacement. If the regular farm buildings were thoroughly cleaned out and a line of trenches or breastwork put right through the centre of the buildings and good strong dug outs constructed. Of course all this work would have to be most carefully done and a lot of men required to rush it through in a few nights so as it would not lose its greatest value which is surprise effect and concealment. At present there are parties at work demolishing the buildings for the purpose of obtaining bricks for revetment even in dug outs - I reported this to the O.C. 26th Bn on my return.
>
> To sum up the whole reconnaissance I suggest that Arundel House and S.P. Burnham be abandoned as S.Ps and that all these small garrisons which have no strength be embodied into one at a point such as UPPER OOSTHOEK FARM i.e. if it is possible to endeavour to make a real S.P. out of it with good dug outs and have it thoroughly well wired all round. From this point a machine gun or machine guns would have a good chance of holding the enemy off without running the risks of being crept up on as in the case of ARUNDEL HOUSE.
>
> I have the honour to be
>
> Sir.
>
> Your obedient Servant -
>
> S. W. WATSON CAPT.
>
> O.C. 5th Bde M. G. Coy.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 May 1916 with no date gaps. Diary text is 6 pages (Sheets 1–6); appendices A–D add 7 pages (Operation Orders Nos. 11, 12 and 13, plus the reconnaissance report of 19 May). Total 15 pages transcribed.
- **Officers named:** Capt. S. W. Watson (O.C. 5th Bde M.G. Coy; signs the diary sheets "S.W. Capt." and signs Appendices C and D); Lieut. F. H. Duck (sent to the Camiers Machine Gun School, 1 May); Lieut. J. E. McCorkell (signs Appendices A and B "for O.C."); Lieut. Bowles, Lieut. Duthie, Lieut. Thomas and Lieut. Bertram (group commanders, Operation Order No. 11); Lieut. Ketcheson (Right Group, Operation Order No. 13).
- **Other ranks named:** None by name. Two sappers attached from the 25th Canadians (13 May) are unnamed; the one O.R. killed and two O.R. wounded on 1 May are unnamed.
- **Equipment:** Machine guns (Colt machine guns in use alongside Vickers-pattern guns); one Colt machine gun received 5 May to replace the gun put out of action on 1 May; two Lewis guns relieved at Trenches 15 and 16 by Colt guns (17 May); spare machine gun barrel damaged 1 May; limbers for moving guns and equipment; 6,000 rounds fired in indirect fire on 2 May; horses — 43 draft and 10 riding horses on 23 May, rising to 44 draft and 11 riding by 28 May.
- **Movements:** In the line at Voormezeele (front and subsidiary trenches) at the start of May; relieved by the 6th Bde M.G. Coy on the night of 3/4 May and moved to rest billets at Reninghelst, with two sections in reserve at Voormezeele; reserve sections relieved by the 4th Bde M.G. Coy on the night of 9/10 May and returned to Reninghelst; back into the line relieving the 4th Bde M.G. Coy on the night of 15/16 May; in reserve again from 23 May; relieving the 6th Bde M.G. Coy in the subsidiary and front line trenches on the night of 31 May/1 June.
- **Discipline:** None recorded in May 1916.
- **Casualties:** 1 May — one O.R. killed and two O.R. wounded by hostile artillery fire on the A.P. positions. No other casualties recorded in May.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The 16 May indirect-fire times include "12 midnight - to 1 PM", which appears to be a slip for 1 AM; transcribed as written. (2) The 17 May entry begins on Sheet 3 and continues onto Sheet 4 without a new date line. (3) On Sheet 4 the heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is not struck through (unlike Sheets 1, 2, 3 and 6); on Sheet 5 it is also not struck through. (4) The unit heading on Sheet 6 reads "5th CDN INF BDE M.G. COY." (with "INF" added). (5) The 28 May entry's "one h. dr aft horse" is transcribed as written; it most likely means one heavy draught horse. (6) The contents sheet describes Appendix D as orders, but Appendix D is in fact the reconnaissance report of 19 May. (7) Appendix A, para. 2(A), lists the guide for Moated Grange and then, without a new sub-heading, a second guide list for Convent School No. 1; transcribed as written. (8) The 12 May entry reads "The lining and giving over of the machine guns completed" — transcribed as written. (9) A small superscript annotation above "BURNHAM" in the 17 May entry is illegible; flagged [uncertain]. (10) The diary spells the place "STELOI" (for St Eloi) and "CAMIERES" throughout; both are transcribed as written.
