### 13/1/18 — Field

> The company was inspected by the O.C. at 11 A.M. the turn out was good, [uncertain — word struck through] No. 406517 Pte. Edison R. being the cleanest man on parade was granted special leave.
>
> The Transport Section under Lieut. Robson paraded at 1.00 P.M. and proceeded to NEDONCHELLE [uncertain — word struck through] an inspection was held of the Battn. Transport, by Lieut. Colonel Boak.
>
> The condition of the horses, limbers and harness was very good. The inspecting officer expressed his appreciation of the efficiency of the section and awarded it the [uncertain — word struck through] 1st prize. The Transport Sergt. No. 68001 Sgt. Christie J. was granted leave. No. 117544 Pte. Spraggen W.G. was congratulated by the inspecting officer for the care and attention he had given to his horses and harness, and was granted leave as having the best turnout in the battalion.

### 14/1/18

> The company paraded at 1.00 P.M. and marched to NEDONCHELLE where the Battalion under Major Goodfellow, M.C. was inspected by Brigadier General Ketchen, at 2.00 P.M. The appearance of the men was very good; the equipment was clean and well fitted.
>
> The Brigadier gave the men a very thorough inspection; arms and ammunition receiving special attention. After the inspection of the company had been completed a demonstration of gas drill was given, which was carried out under the supervision of Lieut. Colonel McIntyre.
>
> Several movements in company drill were carried out. After the inspection the inspecting officer congratulated the commanding officer on the efficiency of his company and awarded him the first place in the battalion.

### 14/1/18 (continued) — Field

> The company returned to NEDON at 4.00 P.M. where a company inspection was held by the O.C. "A" Section was chosen as being the cleanest and most soldier like section in the Coy.

### 15/1/18

> The company paraded at 12.30 P.M. with full marching order and proceeded to PERNES station where they entrained at 2. P.M FOR ECOURIE. The company detrained at 7.00 P.M. and marched to AUX REITZ Camp through pouring rain. Owing to the occupation of the camp 78th Can. Battn. Hqrs. Sub Section and "A" Section were billeted for the night in the 16th Can. M.G. Coy's Area. B.C. & D Sections were billeted at FORT GEORGE.

### 16/1/18

> The company paraded at 9.00 A.M. and fatigues parties were detailed. The cleaning and reconstruction of the camp was commenced.

### 17/1/18

> The Commanding Officer accompanied by the Section Officers made a reconnaisance of the forward area.
>
> The company paraded to the Baths at 10. A.M.
>
> The guns were cleaned, [uncertain — word struck through] refilled and limbers re-packed.

### 18/1/18

> In accordance with the 2nd Cdn. Divl. M.G. Battn. O.O. No. 71 the company relieved the 10th Cdn. M.G. Coy. as per O.O. No. 1 attached.
>
> The company paraded for the trenches at 1. P.M. and moved off at 1.15. P.M. relief being completed by 6.30 P.M. Owing to the heavy rains the trenches and emplacements were in a bad condition. Hostile artillery normal. Fired a few 4.1 around QUEBEC Trench from 9 P.M. to 12 MIDNIGHT.

> 2. INFORMATION TO THE ENEMY.
>
> From captured German Documents evidence has been obtained that certain British prisoners have given valuable information to the enemy.
>
> All ranks are again reminded that, if they have the misfortune to be captured by the enemy, it is their plain duty to their country and their comrades, to refuse to disclose any information whatsoever, except their names, rank, and regimental number. It may be impossible for a prisoner to conceal his regiment, but he should not disclose the number of his battalion.
>
> Every prisoner captured by the Germans is immediately put through a rigorous Cross-Examination by expert intelligence officers. The latter are sometimes dressed as British Officers in order to deceive our men, and it is probable that bogus prisoners also, speaking perfect English, are regularly mixed with the real prisoners in order to extract [uncertain — word struck through] information, so that all strangers dressed in British uniforms should be treated with the greatest suspicion.
>
> REFUSE TO ANSWER ALL QUESTIONS.
>
> Any man who allows himself to be drawn into conversation, even if seems to him to be perfectly harmless, or any man who imagines that by giving information he may obtain good treatment, is a traitor to his country and perhaps the murderer of his comrades.
>
> J. McCorbell Lieut. for Captain. Officer Commanding, 5th Cdn. M.G. Coy.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Diary entries run 1–31 January 1918 with no date gaps. The diary text is 11 pages (sheets 1–10), matching the contents sheet's "Diary Text — 11 Pages". The month divides into two parts: 1–17 January in the field at Nédon/Nédonchel (training, inspections and parades), and 18–30 January in the Mericourt sector trenches, with the company relieved by the 6th Canadian Machine Gun Company on the night of 30/31 January and going into reserve in the Vimy area.
- **Officers named:** Capt. J. E. McCorbell (O.C. 5th Cdn. M.G. Coy; signs the diary and all orders); Lieut. G. Robson (Military Cross; commanded the Transport Section and the A Section relief positions); Lieut. G. E. Harley (B Section; proceeded on a gas course 20 January, reported back 27 January); Lieut. H. M. Potticary (C Section; lectured on range finding); Lieut. F. L. Much (lectured on indirect fire; responsible for the ammunition dumps in the defence scheme); Lieut. McBurney (D Section); Lieut. D. Coy. on the night of 18/19 January → relieved by the 6th Cdn. M.G. Coy. on the night of 30/31 January → reserve positions in the Vimy area (Operation Order No. 3). - **Casualties:** Nil on every day of the month except 27 January, when Sgt. Street J., No. 160958, was accidentally wounded (1 accidental casualty). No enemy-inflicted casualties are recorded in January 1918.
- **Ammunition expenditure:** Indirect and harassing fire in the trenches expended 10,000 rounds on most days from 21 January (8,000 rounds on 24 January; 9,500 rounds by "C" Section on 24 January; 20,000 rounds on 26 and 27 January; 29,00 rounds on 28 January — see anomaly below). Anti-aircraft fire: 250 rounds at Keane on 22 January, 250 rounds each from Keane and Bedford on 25 January, 1,250 rounds from Keane and Pictou on 26 January.
- **Anomalies:**
  1. The diary-text sheet numbering repeats "Sheet 6": both page 8 (19–21 January) and page 9 (21–23 January) are headed "Sheet 6." The contents sheet's "Diary Text — 11 Pages" is still correct (11 diary pages, sheets 1–10).
  2. Finlayson's regimental number differs between documents: the diary (2 January) reads 1006866, while the routine orders of 2 January give 1003686.
  3. The diary (2 January) calls the range-finding lecture "Methods and Imports of Range Finding"; the routine orders for 9 January call it "Methods and importance of Range finding".
  4. The award recommendation for Pte. J. A. Anderson, No. 1004095, is a different man from Pte. A. H. Anderson, No. 1000566, who received the guard stick on 2 January.
  5. The 28 January S.A.A. expenditure is written "29,00 rds." — the final zero appears to be missing in the original (compare 20,000 and 10,000 rounds on adjacent days).
  6. Operation Order No. 3, paragraph 6, gives Reserve Company Headquarters as "S.25.b.60.5p." — the final character appears to be a misprint for "0" (60.50, as in paragraph 2(a)).
  7. The routine orders for 12–23 January are missing from this set: the orders jump from 11 January (page 31) to 24 January (page 32). The contents sheet says appendices 1–3 cover orders issued 1–30 January 1918, so the intervening daily orders are not among the surviving pages.
  8. The diary entry for 30 January appears in two parts: the day's firing on Sheet 9 and the relief by the 6th Cdn. M.G. Coy. on Sheet 10.
  9. The "Honour or Reward" column is blank on all seven award recommendation forms (Army Form W. 3121); the diary's 1 January entry names the honours for Robson (Military Cross), Chamberland (D.C.M.) and Sillers (D.C.M.) only.
  10. Page 26 (routine orders, 6 January) is very faint and the examination subjects under item 11 are only partly legible.
  11. The 2 January routine orders are signed by Lieut. J. H. McLean [uncertain] "for Captain" — the only routine orders in the month not signed by McCorbell himself.
