### 1/10/16 — Field

> Operation orders were received during the night covering the operations due to take place in the afternoon. In accordance with these orders the four guns in SUGAR TRENCH were distributed as follows during the morning One gun to 22nd Bn. Two guns to the 24th Bn. and one gun to the 25th Bn. This distribution was no sooner made than orders were received to alter it so that the guns were then distributed as follows. Two to 22nd Bn. six to 24th Bn and 5 to the 25th Bn. This redistribution was carried out immediately. During the attack in the afternoon these guns were used for indirect firing under the M.G.O. 24th Bn. The remainder of the guns were held in reserve with the exception of the two attached to the 22nd Bn. and the two in SUDBURY TRENCH under the 25th Bn. The two guns of 'C' Section which were put out of action on the night of the 28th-29th September were salvaged by a party in the morning and taken down to Hdqs. in BAILIFF WOOD. A ration party was [uncertain] at Hdqs at night which delivered the rations to the different Battalion Hdqs for the sections. [uncertain] Sgt Ross. ### 2/10/16 — Field

> Information was received that Lieut. Campbell had been shell shocked the evening of the 1st also that one of his guns in the Sunken Road at R 24.C.8.8 (EAST MIRAILMONT ROAD) had been put out of action during the night and two of the crew killed, a third member of the crew being missing. The gun and spare parts were salvaged during the day and the two men buried. On the 5th Cdn. Inf. Brigade being relieved in the night by the 6th Cdn Inf Brigade, orders were received by the 5th Cdn. Machine Gun Co. that they were under the orders of the 6th Cdn Inf Brigade until ordered to withdraw. Orders were received at 11.35 A.M. and all gun crews were notified as quickly as possible. At 5.00 P.M. the company having all been withdrawn as far as BAILIFF WOOD and the limbers packed, it moved off for the BRICKFIELDS ALBERT. reaching there at 7.30 P.M. Orders were then received for the company to move into Billets at BOUZINCOURT for the night and this move was made after the men had had a meal. The transport did not move. The health of the company is very good.

### 3/10/16 — Field (continued)

> WARLOY - VADENCOURT - CONTAY - HERISSART - RUBEMPRE - TALMAS - NAOURS - CANAPLES HALLOY - BERTEAUCOURT. BOUZINCOURT was cleared at 6.30 A.M. and two halts were made enroute to rest and feed the horses. ## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 October 1916 with no date gaps. 13 pages in total: cover, typed contents sheet, and 11 diary sheets (Sheets No. 1–11), matching the contents sheet's "Diary Text — 11 Pages".
- **Officers named:** Capt. J. H. McCorkell (O.C., signs the diary as Captain — promoted during October; he signed September as Lieutenant); Lt Roy (attached to the 22nd Bn. as Machine Gun Officer 6 Oct); Lt Campbell (shell shocked 1 Oct); Lt Benjamin Anderson Taylor (joined from the Cdn. M. G. Base, Shorncliffe, 8 Oct); Lt Kitchener and Lt Creighton (reinforcements from the Cdn. M. G. Depot, England, 20 Oct); the M.G.O. of the 24th Bn. (unnamed); the G.O.C. 2nd Cdn. Division (unnamed, inspected the company 9 Oct).
- **Other ranks named:** Sgt Ross (A Section; killed with his guns in Sudbury Trench 1 Oct, per the entry — the sentence breaks off mid-sentence); Sgt Hobson (awarded the Military Medal, presented by the G.O.C. 9 Oct); five unnamed old company men returned from hospital 8 Oct; six unnamed men returned from Ault 16 Oct.
- **Equipment:** Gun No. 718 put out of action by a "Rum jar" fragment 16 Oct; "fish tail" bombs and "Rum jars" (German trench-mortar bombs) falling on Morrow Trench; gas gongs erected at all positions 21 Oct; new gun platforms and emplacements built at Link Trench, Link Alley, Bajolle Line, Cap de Pont and Spinney Trench; 10,000 rounds S.A.A., 24 Mills bombs, 40 iron rations and 4 gallons of water placed in each emplacement 23 Oct; horse standings built at Fosse 10.
- **Movements:** Out of the line at Courcelette/Sugar Trench 2 Oct; Brickfields, Albert, then billets at Bouzincourt; transport marched via Warloy, Vadencourt, Contay, Hérissart, Rubempré, Talmas, Naours, Canaples, Halloy to Berteaucourt 3 Oct; company by bus to Berteaucourt-les-Dames 3–4 Oct; billets changed in town 7 Oct; march via Halloy, Canaples, Montrelet, Fienvillers to Harenval 10 Oct; Outrebois and Barly to Beauvoir-Bonnières 11 Oct; Frévent, Noncq, Petit Houvin to Buneville 12 Oct; Maisnil, St Pol, St Michel-sur-Ternoise to Osterville 13 Oct; Barlin 14 Oct; relieved the 112th Bde M.G. Coy in the Angres sector 15 Oct (transport lines and Q.M. stores at Fosse 10, Sains-en-Gohelle).
- **Discipline:** None recorded in October 1916.
- **Casualties:** 1 Oct — Sgt Ross killed in Sudbury Trench (entry breaks off mid-sentence); Lt Campbell shell shocked the evening of 1 Oct; one of Campbell's guns in the Sunken Road (R 24.C.8.8) put out of action, two of the crew killed and a third missing. 16 Oct — no casualties from the "Rum jar" that landed in the Morrow Trench dugout entrance, but rifles and equipment destroyed and gun No. 718 put out of action. No other casualties recorded in October.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The 1 Oct entry ends mid-sentence ("Sgt Ross. 'A' Section was killed while with his guns in SUDBURY TRENCH in") and the next sheet opens with the 2 Oct entry, so the sentence is never completed. (2) The 1 Oct gun redistribution numbers (2 to the 22nd Bn., six to the 24th Bn. and 5 to the 25th Bn. — 13 guns) do not match the four guns stated to be in Sugar Trench. (3) The 15 Oct entry says twelve guns were placed in the line, but the positions listed add up to ten guns (2 in Morrow Trench and one each in Park Lane, Car Dupont, Carons Daix Alley, Bully Alley, Lemco Alley, Reserve Line and the two ends of the Slag Pile); the 19 Oct entry then states 8 guns in position and 8 in reserve. (4) The 16 Oct entry opens with the odd phrase "Guns lived." (5) Several map-reference figures on 15 Oct are faint and are transcribed as read. (6) The O.C. signs as Captain on 31 Oct, having signed as Lieutenant at the end of September.
