## Page 7 — Appendix A (Report, page 1)

> APPENDIX A
>
> Headquarters
> 5th Canadian Infantry Brigade
> February 8th 1916.
>
> From
> O/C B.M.G.O.
> 5th Canadian Infantry Brigade
> To
> 5th Canadian Infantry Brigade
> Re — Farm Buildings in 5th Brigade Area — M.G. Emplacements.
> Ref. Map Sheet 28 Series B of Belgium, France etc 1/40000
>
> Sir
>
> I beg to submit the following report:—
>
> A personal reconnaissance was made by myself and 1 Cpl. [uncertain] yesterday of the buildings in the 5th Brigade Area east of VIERSTRAAT–KEMMEL ROAD.
>
> First of all the buildings opposite the LAITERIE ESTAMINET were inspected. These are at N.16.d.2.4. and are used as a dressing station by the 22nd Bn. There would be no use having a M.G. emplacement there, as one at FARM DESINET (N.16.d.5.4) which is just nearby would be more suitable in every way. There has been a M.G. emplacement at the farm but it was shelled and burnt, and a great deal of earth has fallen in. This could easily be remedied however, and there is a very good field of fire from that position.
>
> Next GOETHALS FARM at N.22.b.1.9. was visited. There is a M.G. emplacement there, and the farm is also used as an observation post by the Artillery. There was a certain amount of shelling going on and the Germans appear to have the range on it very accurately. The present emplacement is in the cellar and there is a good field of fire.

## Page 8 — Appendix A (Report, page 2)

> The ROSSIGNOL ESTAMINET at N.22 a 3.2. came next. It might be possible to build an emplacement there, and from such a position the lower end of the V.C. Road could be controlled and also the ROSSIGNOL WOOD just in front.
>
> The next building visited was a Farm House at N.22. b.1.4. This would be a very good position for a Machine Gun. There is a good field of fire in front, and on the V.C. ROAD to the right, and it has the added advantage of not having been shelled so much as the other buildings in the locality.
>
> Of the Farm House marked on the map as N.22 b.8.8. No trace remains.
>
> ALSTON HOUSE (N.22. b.6.1.) was next looked at. It is on the other side of the V.C. ROAD and although shelled a good deal, might be a very useful place for a Machine Gun. There is a cellar and a certain amount of cover so that it would be quite possible to build an emplacement there. If this house and the one at N.22. b.1.4. on the other side of the road were used, they should form a very effective obstacle in case of a German advance.
>
> Another farm at N.22. b.8.6. was visited. This has been heavily shelled and would not be suitable for a Machine Gun as there is not much of a field of fire.
>
> COLT COTTAGE at N.23. b.4.6. was the next building. There is an emplacement there already,

## Page 9 — Appendix A (Report, page 3)

> and a gun might be placed there instead of the farm at N.22.b.1.4. In combination with ALSTON HOUSE should provide a good defence. As it stands at present it might be made into an indirect fire position to get the German support line from opposite K.1. to H.2. It could also fire into WYTSCHAETE about 2500 yards away, and the VIERSTRAAT-WYTSCHAETE ROAD.
>
> The farm at N.17.c.4.2. was next visited. It was a former Artillery O.P. and would not be suitable for a Machine Gun emplacement. It is very much exposed, and any doubts as to suitability were quickly dispelled, for no sooner had we left the building than the Germans sent about 25 Whizz Bangs almost right on top of it. We had evidently been observed and this certainly convinced us that it was not a suitable place.
>
> STINK COTTAGE, at N.17.c.6.6. was the next on the list. It is just to the left of the FOSSE and although it has been shelled pretty heavily a good Machine Gun emplacement might be built there, to give a proper field of fire a couple of the sides of the walls would have to be partly broken down, and there would have to be good overhead cover built.
>
> WILTSHIRE HOUSE, marked on the map as N.17.C.8.7. was level with the ground, and the next position visited was FARM BERGHE at N.17.C.9.9. Like STINK COTTAGE and all the other farms along this road, FARM BERGHE is very much exposed to shelling, but there is a good field of fire from that position, and it

## Page 10 — Appendix A (Report, page 4)

> would be quite possible to build an emplacement there.
>
> FARM VANDAMME at N.17.d.6.1. was next visited. It would not be suitable for an emplacement. There is no field of fire.
>
> We next went to the old Barn on the V.C. ROAD at N.23.a.7.5. There is no cellar or cover in this position, only a great quantity of straw, and this would be no protection for a Machine Gun.
>
> R.E. advanced dumps at N.23.b.6.6. would not be a suitable place; nor would the house farther up the V.C. ROAD at N.25.a.5.1. as there are Machine Guns in trenches J.1. and H.3. which cover that area.
>
> The house in front of trench H.5. at N.24.a.0.2. is too much exposed to shell fire to be a good M.G. position.
>
> FARM BRYKERIE at N.17. b.1.5. was next visited. The farm itself is all in ruins, and there are three M.G. positions in a trench system in front. Nothing could be done as regards Machine Guns in the actual buildings.
>
> The next place was N.17.C.H.9. This is a possible position, but not particularly good one as there is not enough overhead cover. It has a good field of fire and might be made useful with a good deal of work. All the cover would have to be built.
>
> We then came to N.16.d.8.9. The farm at the top of the slope just to the left of the FOSSE. This seems a particularly good position, and a Machine Gun emplacement might easily be constructed there.

## Page 11 — Appendix A (Report, page 5)

> It would have an extremely good field of fire. The approach is somewhat exposed and caution would have to be exercised so as not to give the place away.
>
> FARM PARRET or MAXIM FARM came next. This is at N.17.a.4.4. and there are three old M.G. emplacements filled with water. Judging from the number of shell holes, the Germans seem to have the range of this place very accurately, but the position would be a useful one aside from this drawback. A good deal of work would be necessary to fix up a really strong emplacement there.
>
> Next came the farm just on the west side of the road near VIERSTRAAT at N.11.C.6.5. This is an old Artillery O.P. which has been heavily shelled. Like PARRET FARM it would be a good position. It is simply a question as to which of the two would be the least exposed. They seem to be almost equal in the number of shell holes round about.
>
> The last three buildings, FARM VANDEN BERGHE at N.18.C.2.7. the O.P. at N.18.a.3.4. and the ruins at N.18.a.6.6. could only be visited after dark. VANDEN BERGHE is used as an O.P. but an emplacement might possibly be built there. The O.P. at N.18.a.3.4. is also possible with a good deal of work. It would have a field of fire to the left around HOLLANDESCHUR FARM. This is behind trench L.6. The other position at N.18.a.6.6. behind L.7. left, was practically level with the ground and nothing could be done there.
>
> It was noticeable all the way through that the Germans seem to have the range

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–29 February 1916 with no date gaps (1916 was a leap year). The contents sheet states "Diary Text — 4 Pages", matching the four diary-text pages (pages 3–6). The month also carries three appendices: Appendix A (6 pages, the 8 February reconnaissance report), Appendix B (1 page, Operation Order No. 1, 20 February) and Appendix C (2 pages, Operation Order No. 2, 27 February).
- **Officers named:** Lt A. J. S. Mills (Adjutant; signs the diary 29 February and authors Appendix A as A/B M.G.O.); Lt S. W. Watson (B.M.G.O., 5th Canadian Infantry Brigade; author of Operation Orders No. 1 and No. 2); Lieut Grantham (ordered to relieve two machine guns at Maxim Farm, Operation Order No. 1); Brigadier-General D. Watson, C.B. (inspected the company on 12 February).
- **Other ranks named:** Sgt Duffett (to supervise bringing emplacements to completion, Operation Order No. 2); 1 Cpl. [uncertain] (accompanied Lt Mills on the 7 February reconnaissance — the name is not clearly legible). Otherwise other ranks are unnamed: a sergeant and twelve men with Lt Grantham; a corporal and six men to Machine Gun Farm.
- **Equipment:** Four Colt machine guns received from the Royal Canadian Dragoons on 9 February, bringing the company total to 8 guns. Four guns were sent to a battalion in brigade reserve at R.E. Farm on 19 February; two guns went up to the subsidiary line on 20 February; three 4th Brigade guns were relieved on 28 February. Armstrong huts erected (3 and 14 February), four tents erected (5 February), a 20x16 hut erected (26 February). Gas helmets practised with on 29 February; two N.C.Os. completed a Divisional Gas School course on 23 February; a German "Flammenwerfer" was demonstrated to the company on 24 February.
- **Movements:** Company headquarters at M.23 d 68. Subsidiary-line emplacements at N.16 b 6.7 were occupied from 20 February, with reliefs on 27–28 February. Guns were relieved at Maxim Farm (20 February) and at Fort Toronto (N.11 a 33) and positions right of Vierstraat (28 February). The Locre / Westoutre / Scherpenberg routine continued: baths at Westoutre, church parades at Locre, instructional firing at Scherpenberg.
- **Strength:** 9 officers and 59 O.R. on 1 February; 10 O.R. reinforcements from England on 16 February brought the total to 69 O.R.; 1 O.R. transferred to No. 8 Company, 2nd Divisional Train A.S.C. on 27 February (68 O.R.); 1 O.R. transferred in from the 24th Battalion on 28 February (69 O.R.); 1 O.R. transferred to the 22nd Battalion on 29 February (68 O.R.). Two attached C.A.M.C. men are included in all these figures.
- **Discipline:** None recorded in February 1916.
- **Casualties:** None recorded in February 1916.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The contents sheet types the Operation Order dates as "20-2-17 and 27-2-16"; "20-2-17" is evidently a slip for 20-2-16, since Appendix B is dated 20-2-1916. (2) The contents sheet numbers the appendices 1, 2 and 3, while the documents themselves are headed Appendix A, B and C. (3) The 20 February entry is garbled as written: "from N.16 b 6.7. was occupied by this officer as HEADQUARTERS" — the word "from" is inserted above the line and the sentence has no clear subject. (4) The 22 February entry ends abruptly at the foot of the diary sheet ("…Tactical scheme") with no continuation on the next sheet. (5) On 28 February a line about a transfer from the 24th Battalion is struck through and the shorter note "1 O.R. transferred from 24th BN." is inserted above the line instead. (6) In Operation Order No. 2, items 1 and 2, the word "present" is struck through in both cases ("at [present] at M.G. FARM" / "at [present] at M.G. HQRS."). (7) Item 6 of Operation Order No. 2 reads as if a word is missing ("will be used primarily to each emplacement to completion"); transcribed as written. (8) The sketch map Appendix A says is attached is not among the February pages. (9) The diary's first entry writes "MACHINE GUN COMPANYS HEADQUATERS" (no apostrophe, "HEADQUATERS" misspelt), as written.
