## Page 11 — Appendix A (Report, page 1)

> APPENDIX A
>
> From Lt. Duck Mar. 4th 1916
> To O.C. 5th Brigade Machine Gun Co.
>
> Sir,
>
> I have the honor to report that I reconnoitred our Brigade area to the east and south of Vierstraat. On the way I visited building in Vierstraat at N.11 e 88 which presents a splendid field of fire across the valley, and on the German trenches, from Vierstraat-Wytschaete Road, south beyond the Petit Bois an emplacement there could be made very strong with little trouble by knocking down the remainder of the bricks on top of emplacement and filling in with sand bags. I would strongly recommend that this should be looked into. Mr. Thomas with 4th Brigade officers took me to look at same, and Mr. Thomas is reporting on it to-night.
>
> With reference to emplacement south of Casse Farm It was very hard to approach on account of snow being on the ground. However I was in the Chicory Patch at N.11 b 33. and found that any place there presented a splendid field of fire, covering the German Trenches on the whole of our Brigade Area. The Chicory itself gives good cover from view, and a gun could easily be placed any place in the patch. There is an old trench in front of the spot which also presents the same frontage, and if advisable an emplacement could be built in it. This may be part of the Poppy Lane communication. I could not find out exactly if it is, but I doubt it. It is just on the right of barricade and seems to be a continuation of the barricade. I went up Poppy Lane as far as possible and found that they were draining it and fixing it up. They plan to run it down to the front line trenches. The emplacement in Poppy

## Page 12 — Appendix A (Report, page 2)

> Lane appears to be in bad shape. The roof is too low and should be raised about eighteen inches there is no gun platform in the emplacement. I then went down the front line trenches going from the H. Trenches over to the K. Trenches stopping from there to bear out the statement that the Chicory Patch or the trench is the best place for the emplacement. It would be advisable to build a shell proof emplacement in the trench which could be used in case they shelled the Chicory.
>
> On the right of our area I do not think our positions could be improved upon. The emplacement at Pine Briqueterie, at N.17 a 36 and at N.11 e 22 1/2 seem to cover the required area. The rest of the area is too open to build emplacements and in a pinch guns could be placed in the open with artificial cover constructed for them or taking advantage of any hedge or isolated cover. In fact our whole area presents the same possibilities, and a thorough knowledge of the area would be of more service than many more emplacements.
>
> I have the honor to be,
> Sir.
> Your obedient servant.
>
> F. H. Duck
> Lieut.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 March 1916 with no date gaps. The contents sheet states "Diary Text — 8 Pages", matching the eight diary-text pages (pages 3–10). The month also carries two appendices: Appendix A (2 pages, Lt Duck's reconnaissance report of 4 March) and Appendix B (1 page, Operation Order No. 3 of 13 March). A new hand takes over the diary in March; the remarks column is initialled "I. H. B. Lt." throughout.
- **Officers named:** Lt F. H. Duck (author of Appendix A; took command of the three guns at S.P. 12, S.P. 13 and Mount Royal under Operation Order No. 3); Lt S. W. Watson (O.C. 5th Can. Bde M.G. Coy; author of Operation Order No. 3); Lt McCorkell (reserve guns at Machine Gun Farm; rations); Lt Bowles (returned to headquarters on completion of the 13 March relief); Major General R. E. W. Turner, V.C., C.B., D.S.O., and Brigadier General D. Watson, C.B. (inspected the unit on 23 March); Brigadier General D. Watson, C.B., also inspected the new draft on 25 March. Mr. Thomas (with 4th Brigade officers) is named in Appendix A.
- **Other ranks named:** None named in March. One N.C.O. and 5 men from the 2nd Pioneer Bn Machine Gun Section were attached for instruction from 14 March and returned on 31 March; 1 officer and 23 O.R. from the Brigade Trench Mortar Co. were attached for discipline and rations on 31 March.
- **Equipment:** A signallers' hut (10'x10') to be built (1 March); a 20'x16' hut completed (5 March); wicker revetment and wire entanglements at Fort Morro (10 March); bomb-proof dugout excavation started at Vierstraat No. 1 (10 March); belt-filling machines in regular use; indirect fire used from N.17 G.14 covering the Poperinghe Road (21 March); gas and gas-helmet lectures to the new drafts.
- **Movements:** On 13 March Borden's Machine Gun Battery relieved the company at Fort Morro, Fort Toronto, Vierstraat No. 2 and Vierstraat No. 1, and the company took up new positions in the right sector at S.P. 12, S.P. 13 and Mount Royal (H2–H.N. trenches). A gun and crew moved from the open emplacement at N.17 A 19 to the new emplacement at N.17 A.36 on 2 March; a detachment was posted to the Brickerie S.P. on 22 March and relieved by a 22nd Battalion detachment on 23 March. The Locre / Westoutre routine continued: baths at Westoutre, church parades at Locre, pay parade to Locre (14 March).
- **Strength:** 8 officers and 67 O.R. on 1 March (one officer and his servant having returned to the 24th Battalion); drafts of 15 O.R. (2 March, 82 O.R.), 4 O.R. (10 March, 84 O.R.), 14 O.R. (15 March, 98 O.R.) and 47 O.R. (24 March) arrived from England; 1 O.R. returned to his former unit (8 March, 81 O.R.) and 1 O.R. transferred to the 25th Battalion (10 March, 80 O.R.); total strength 144 O.R. on 24 March after the one casualty. Two attached C.A.M.C. men (water detail) are included in the figures.
- **Discipline:** None recorded in March 1916.
- **Casualties:** One casualty in the subsidiary lines on 24 March — unnamed, with no details given in the diary.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The contents sheet dates Appendix B 15-3-16, but Operation Order No. 3 itself is dated 13.3.16, and the diary's 13 March entry refers to it. (2) The 1 March entry gives the new gun emplacement as "M.17. A36" while the 2 March entry gives the same emplacement as "N.17 A.36". (3) The 4 March entry gives the Vierstraat position as "50x to the right of cross roads" — transcribed as written. (4) On Sheet 8 (31 March) the heading "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is not struck through, unlike every other March sheet. (5) No signature appears at the foot of the March diary sheets. (6) The diary's 13 March sector reference reads "(H2 – H.N.) trenches" while Operation Order No. 3 writes the same sector as "(H2-K1N trenches)". (7) The March diarist is a new hand; the remarks initials "I. H. B. Lt." are not otherwise identified in the March diary — Lt Bowles is named in Appendix B but the diary does not state that he kept the diary. (8) The 10 and 24 March entries run across two sheets; the continuation is marked in the transcript.
