### 1/3/17 — La Folie Section

> REF. MAPS. VIMY. 36.C.S.W.3. EDITION 8 A 1/10,000
> ROCLINCOURT. 51.B.N.W.1. EDITION 5 A 1/10,000
>
> The largest raid ever undertaken by the CANADIAN CORPS on the WESTERN FRONT was launched early this morning by the 4TH CANADIAN DIVISION. We used Gas in this operation and thus first established a precedent in the history of Canadian [uncertain] It is the first occasion on which we have had experience with this instrument of destruction. Our Unit employed 10 M.Gs in this operation with the Artillery throughout, and laid down an effective barrage on the enemy's main communication trenches. We expended in all some 50,000 rounds of Ammunition, and although German Field Batteries retaliated rather vehemently in the close proximity of our guns but our casualties were nil. The Vickers Gun operated throughout with entirely satisfactory rapidity and precision. The programme of the raid was as follows. At 3 A.M. ### 1/3/17 — La Folie Section (continued)

> 2 hours and 40 minutes after ZERO hour the final and concentrated bombardment was opened and the Infantry made their assault. By approximately 7.30 A.M. conditions had assumed normal. Our usual Indirect Fire programme was carried out to-night as follows Right Section guns fired 3000 rds on PETIT VIMY, between 5.30 and 9.30 P.M. & Left fired 3000 rds on THELUS. Reserve guns fired 1500 rds. on enemy kitchens at THELUS, between the hours 5.30 P.M. to 6.30 P.M. and 9 P.M. to 10 P.M. Retaliation. Enemy M.G. fire much more concentrated on NEUVILLE ST. VAAST. Work being rushed at Right Section Hqrs. At 5.05 P.M. our anti-aircraft gun stationed at position 10 fired 250 rounds on 5 German Aeroplanes approaching from East. They however did not cross further west than our front line.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 March 1917 with no date gaps. Diary text is 18 sheets (pages 3–20), followed by 7 appendix pages (pages 21–27): Appendix A (Operation Order No. 3, 9–10 March, and Operation Order No. 4, 22 March) and Appendix B (Instructions for the Offensive No. 1, Machine Gun Scheme, 27 March — the Vimy Ridge attack plan). The "5th Cdn M.G. Coy Instructions to the Officers No 1" mentioned in the 30 March entry as "see attached" is not among the scanned pages.
- **Officers named:** Major S. W. Watson (O.C. 5th Cdn M.G. Coy; signed the diary and all orders; away on a Map Reading Course at Pernes 13–21 March); Lieut. Grantham (assumed command during Watson's absence); Lieut. A. C. Bowles (F.S. Battery commander in the offensive scheme; lectured on Discipline and Battle values 28 March); Lieut. Sinclair (Map Reading Course at Pernes 12–20 March); Lieut. Hitcherson (returned from Pernes 4 March); Lt. Hobson (work at No. 2 dugout); Lieut. McBurney (returned from M.G. School, Camiers, 20 March); Lieut. Hodson (left at Neuville St. Vaast with a 35-man working party 23 March); Lieut. A. B. Eller (lectured on the Machine Gun 26 March); Lieut. Creighton (lectured on Overhead Fire 29 March); Lieut. Taylor (lectured on Machine Gun in Defence 31 March).
- **Other ranks named:** Pte West (wounded 5 March, died of wounds in the dressing station 6 March); one unnamed O.R. of "B" Section killed at Neuville St. Vaast 21 March while carrying S.A.A.; 2 O.R. seriously wounded at No. 10 position 5 March; one horse wounded at Mt. St. Eloy 24 March. A 50-man Brigade working party returned 13 March; 32 O.R. (eight from each battalion of the Brigade) attached as carriers the same day; 1 officer and 16 O.R. of the 14th Cdn M.G. Coy attached 11–21 March; a draft of 10 O.R. received 25 March; a 16-man fatigue party detailed for ammunition carrying.
- **Equipment:** Vickers machine guns; anti-aircraft guns (firing 150–3,000 rounds a day at hostile aircraft); a sniping gun emplaced 20 March covering the Lens–Arras road; indirect-fire positions built up to eight by 5 March; deep dugouts and new company headquarters in Watling Street; gas used in the 1 March raid — the diary claims the first use of gas by Canadian troops and the first Canadian experience of it.
- **Movements:** In the line at the La Folie Section 1–12 March; relieved by the 9th Cdn M.G. Coy on the night of 12–13 March (Operation Order No. 3) and moved to the Thelus Section, with transport at Mt. St. Eloy; relieved in the line by the 13th Cdn M.G. Coy on the evening of 23 March (Operation Order No. 4) and moved to billets at Petit Servins via Villers au Bois, transport and Q.M. Stores following on 25 March. Sections rotated between the line and reserve at Mt. St. Eloy throughout.
- **Discipline:** None recorded in March 1917.
- **Casualties:** 5 March — 2 O.R. seriously wounded at No. 10 position, Pte West died of those wounds 6 March. 21 March — 1 O.R. killed by H.E. at Neuville St. Vaast. 24 March — one horse wounded at Mt. St. Eloy; "a few casualties" among troops at Mt. St. Eloy the same day (not this company's men, as worded).
- **Anomalies:** (1) The diary sheets are numbered twice as "Sheet No. 6" — the sheets for 7–10 March and 11–12 March both carry that number, after which sheet numbers run one behind the printed page numbers. (2) The 1 March entry's final sentence breaks across sheets ("At 20 minutes to" at the foot of sheet 1, continuing "2 hours and 40 minutes after ZERO hour..." on sheet 2); transcribed as written. (3) Operation Order No. 4 (22 March) says the 6th Cdn M.G. Coy will relieve the company, but the diary entry for 23 March says the relief was carried out by the 13th Cdn M.G. Coy; both readings are as written. (4) The 12 March entry says Lieut. Sinclair left for a course the word for which appears to read "Deep" Reading Course; the 20 March entry records his return from the Map Reading Course at Pernes. (5) A phrase in the 15 March entry and a line at the end of the 21 March entry are struck through in the original. (6) A copy-distribution line is struck through in Operation Order No. 4. (7) The word "de LA FOORCHE" in Operation Order No. 3 is as typed; the diary elsewhere spells the place "DE LA FOURCHE". (8) The 21 March entry's sniping-gun observation sentence reads oddly as written ("owing to low visibility, [uncertain] observation could be obtained in the afternoon").
