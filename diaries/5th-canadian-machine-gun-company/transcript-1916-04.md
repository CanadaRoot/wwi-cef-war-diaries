## Page 14 — Appendix C, Reconnaissance Report, 9 April 1916 (Sheet 1)

> APPENDIX C
>
> 5th Canadian Infantry Brigade Machine Gun Co.
>
> Reconnaissance carried out 8th April 1916 by the Bde M.G. Officer
>
> To G.O.C. 5th Can Inf. Bde
>
> Sir,
>
> I have the honour to report as follows:—
>
> I proceeded from Bde Hqrs at Garden Wall via the trenches and Elzenwalle C.T. to Voormezeele, where I was met by the O.C. 6th Can Bde M.G. Co. I proceeded to September Post via Convent Lane, over the Canal to the Ruins, where rations are dumped for the M.G. Companies, up Kingsway, which was once lined with dug-outs and is now a complete wreck, owing to the accurate German artillery firing. Not a single dug-out being left untouched. To Spoil Bank which is an extremely good M.G. emplacement, reached by a shaft, with about 15 feet of overhead cover, with a field of fire to the right as far as Arundel Farm and to the left as far as the western edge of Triangular Wood. To Lock Post which is an excellent emplacement, with a field of fire from Oosthoek Farm to left of Pontoon Bridge on Canal. To Hill Top with a field of fire from west of Triangular Wood to trench 27. [continued on page 15]

## Page 15 — Appendix C, Reconnaissance Report, 9 April 1916 (Sheet 2)

> [continued from page 14] We tried a visit ARUNDEL FARM, but were stopped by a sentry, and were told that nobody was allowed to go there in day time. In fact, the last four named emplacements are never to be used, unless there is an attack. There are very strict rules about entering these emplacements in day time, and of course no cooking is allowed.
>
> We then proceeded to the front line, and visited the following emplacements 24. 26. 27. and 28. these latter are all open emplacements and are quite adequate to cover the frontage, which is laid out for them, we could not proceed further in day light, and had to retrace our steps the way we came, The positions I have seen appear very satisfactory, although it is very hard to size up the general situation on the first tour over the ground. Two Officers and two N.C.O's of the 7th. M.G. Co. are going up daily to make a thorough reconnaissance and one Officer and one N.C.O. spend the night with one of the M.G. Co's and accompanies him on his rounds.
>
> I trust by the middle of the week that all officers and senior N.C.O's of the M.G. Co. will have a good idea of the country.
>
> The present difficulty that confronts us appears to be that nobody available has a complete knowledge of the area. This of course, will cause delay at first, but in the end, will give us a better idea and more scope to use our initiative.
>
> I have the honour to be
>
> Sir,
>
> Your obedient servant
>
> Bde M.G. Officer, S. W WATSON Lieut.

## Notes

### Coverage

- Diary entries for every day 1–30 April 1916; no date gaps.
- Diary text: 7 pages (pp. 38–44, Sheets 1–7).
- Appendices A–F: Appendix A (pp. 45–46, Operation Order No. 5, undated); Appendix B (pp. 47–48, Operation Order No. 6, undated); Appendix C (pp. 49–50, reconnaissance report dated 9 April 1916); Appendix D (pp. 51–52, Operation Order No. 8, 12 April 1916); Appendix E (pp. 53–55, Operation Order No. 9, 17 April 1916); Appendix F (pp. 56–58, Operation Order No. 10, 26 April 1916).
- The 16 April entry runs across two diary sheets (pp. 41–42).

### Officers named

- Lieut. S. W. Watson — O.C., author of all six appendices (operation orders and the reconnaissance report).
- Lieut. W. H. J. Ketcheson — to return command of the company guns in the right sector (Operation Order No. 5); reported to the 25th Canadian Battalion for duty with the Brigade Machine Gun Section on 12 April.
- Lieut. Bowles, Lieut. McCorkell, Lieut. Duck, Lieut. Thomas — section/group commanders throughout the month.
- Lieut. Duthie — first appears in Operation Order No. 10 (26 April) commanding the Centre Group, No. 3 Section.
- One unnamed officer arrived as a reinforcement from the 23rd Reserve Battalion (16 April).

### Other ranks named

- Sgt Hobson — No. 4 Section (Operation Order No. 8, 12 April).

### Equipment

- Colt machine guns: 14 in the unit's possession on 10 April (two handed over from each of the 24th, 25th and 26th Battalions); 16 on 20 April (two received from the 22nd Battalion).
- Transport: 12 G.S. limbered wagons and 39 L.D. horses (13 April).
- Indirect fire: 1,500 rounds fired on the night of 16/17 April from I 32 d 7.8; 2,000 rounds fired on the night of 17/18 April; "15-00 rds" used from the O.1 A 2.1 position on 17 April.
- S.A.A.: 6 belt boxes to relieve an emplacement; 10,000 rounds S.A.A. per emplacement ordered for the future (Operation Order No. 10).
- Lewis respirators, gas helmets, goggles and respirators inspected (7 and 19 April); belt-filling machines examined (10 April); a 30-foot extension built on the 60-foot horse shelter (14 April).

### Movements

- 1 April: took over L 6 and S.P. 13 in the subsidiary lines from the 22nd Battalion.
- 2 April: relieved in the subsidiary lines by the 149th Brigade Machine Gun Company.
- 3 April: moved from M.23 d 68 to M.5 c 18 (completed 5 A.M.).
- 4 April: moved to billets at G 34 B19 (completed 3 P.M.).
- 6 April: stood to at H.31 D 6.9 with two sections and eight guns; one officer and 35 O.R. detailed for duty with the 6th Canadian Infantry Brigade.
- 7 April: returned to rest billets at G 34 b 19.
- 8 April: the officer and 35 O.R. attached to the 6th Brigade returned.
- 12 April: relieved the 4th Brigade Machine Gun Company in the subsidiary and front lines (Voormezeele sector).
- Night of 17/18 April: relieved by the 6th Brigade Machine Gun Company; rest billets at Reninghelst (Operation Order No. 9).
- 27 April: relieved the 4th Brigade Machine Gun Company again in the subsidiary and front lines.

### Strength

- 7 April: 8 officers and 151 O.R., including 2 attached C.A.M.C. (water detail).
- 21 April: 9 officers and 147 O.R., after a draft of 6 O.R. arrived.
- 28 April: 9 officers and 148 O.R., after 1 more O.R. arrived.

### Discipline

- None recorded in April 1916.

### Casualties

- None recorded in April 1916.

### Anomalies

1. The contents sheet dates Appendices D–F "12-9-16 to 26-9-16" (September); the orders themselves are dated 12, 17 and 26 April 1916.
2. The diary's strength figures do not reconcile: 151 O.R. on 7 April, yet after a draft of 6 O.R. the total is given as 147 O.R. on 21 April (a shortfall of 10, with no transfer recorded in the diary); 147 + 1 = 148 on 28 April does reconcile.
3. The 13 April transport figures do not reconcile either: two wagons from the 4th Brigade plus one from each of the 22nd, 24th, 25th and 26th Battalions is six wagons, but the total is given as 12 G.S. wagons; and 12 four-horse wagons would need 48 horses, but 39 L.D. horses are given.
4. Appendix B: the paragraph after (3)(B) is labelled "(K) (B)" — evidently a slip in the numbering.
5. Appendix D item (8) reads "as soon a completed" (the word "as" is omitted).
6. Appendix E: the order's own pagination runs 9, 10 and 12 — page 11 is skipped in the numbering, but the text runs on without a break; the item numbering jumps from (2) to (6).
7. Appendix F: the paragraph numbering jumps from II to 7, with paragraphs 3–6 unnumbered; and item 7(D) gives Lieut. Duck's section as "No 2 SECTION" although paragraph II assigns him No. 4 Section.
8. No signature at the foot of the April diary sheets.
9. The 2 April diary entry and Appendix B agree the relieving unit was the 149th Brigade Machine Gun Company (confirmed by zoom — the diary reads "149th", not "14th").
10. The cover spells the unit "5th Infantry Brigade Machine Gun Coy." without "Canadian" before "Infantry".
