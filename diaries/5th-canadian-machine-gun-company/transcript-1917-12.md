### 1/12/17 — Field

> Reinforcements of 4 O.R. were brought up from the horse lines for "B" Section and one man was borrowed from Battn "A" [uncertain] "B" Section and sent to "B" Section thus bringing "B" Section up to working strength and the men of the 14th Cdn. M.G. Coy were returned. The guns of "B" Section that were damaged were repaired and put into action again. A thorough the gun maintenance of the whole area was made by the C.O.

### 2/12/17 — Field

> Ammunition pits were started at each gun emplacement and 6 frames were built and Gas Blankets installed. Indirect fire was carried out by two guns of "A" Section on the enemy Cemetery at Mira Court.

### 3/12/17 — Field

> 12 Reinforcements reported to the Coy. Ammunition pits were completed at each emplacement. Inventory boards were made and placed at each gun. Indirect fire was fired in support of an Infantry patrol which went out to reconnoitre the dump at [uncertain] on Hull Road. The patrol reported that the fire of the guns was very accurate and gave them assistance.

### 6/12/17 — Field

> Indirect fire was carried out by 2 guns of "D" section on following enemy targets. 1000 rounds on T.5. c. 50.30. 1000 " " " T.5. a. 00.50. Both our own and hostile artillery were exceptionally quiet. Our planes were active during forenoon, and were engaged by enemy M.G.s and A.A. We carried out the following indirect fire on selected targets. 4,000 rds on T.5. b. 05.15. 4,000 rds on T.12. A. 15.85. ### 10/12/17 — Field

> Day Fine. Hostile Artillery above Normal around BOIS & BLUENOSE positions using mostly 5.9" and gas shells. Enemy aircraft very active all day flying very low over our front & support lines on two occasions they were successfully driven back by fire from our M.G. guns which fired 2,400 rounds on E.A. during the day. During the night our guns fired 10,000 rounds on enemy targets. 31st C.I. Bn, report that enemy surrounded one of the Patrols in No Mans Land & 8 O.Rs are missing. S.A.A. expended 12400 rounds. Casualties Nil.

### 11/12/17 — Field

> Day cool. Enemy artillery quiet. E.A. were inactive to day but at 8.00 p.m. a hostile bombing machine dropped 7 bombs between BADGER (T.20. b. 15.85) and BULLDOG (T.20. a. 10.95) no damage was done. During night 2 guns of "D" Section carried out harassing fire on following:— 7.00 — 11.00 p.m. 4000 rds on T.4. a. 85.05. 7.00 — 11.00 p.m. 4000 rds on N.34. c. 55.15. S.A.A. expended 12,000 rounds. Casualties Nil.

### 15/12/17 — Field

> Day fine & clear, observation being exceptionally good. During night enemy fired a considerable number of gas shells. At 9.30 a.m. 9 enemy planes crossed our lines at a low altitude apparently to take photographs as they did not fire on our trenches. During the day guns of "A" & "B" sects fired 3,500 rds on E.A. While firing at E.A. Lt Hobsons fire apparently damaged one E.A. which was seen to plane down behind enemy's lines. During the night 2 guns of "D" section fired on following targets. 4,000 rds on T.4. d. 95.45 (enemy M.G.) 5.00 till 9.00 p.m. 2,500 " " T.10. b. 93.67 2.00 " 4.00 a.m. 10,500 " " T.5. a. 26.29. 5.00 till 10.00 p.m. & 5.00 till 6.00 a.m. S.A.A. expended 20,500 rds. Casualties Nil.

### 16/12/17 — Field

> Both our own & enemy artillery were quiet during the day. Four E.A. were over our lines during day two were engaged by guns of "A" & "B" sections which fired 750 rds & 500 rds respectively. During night 2 guns of "D" section fired 5,000 rds each on the following targets:— Mericourt Square T.5. b. 05.15. & Cross Roads T.5. a. 15.85. Fourteen reinforcements reported at Transport lines this afternoon. S.A.A. expended 11,500 rounds. Casualties Nil.

### 17/12/17 — Field

> Snowed during night, very cold & sharp all day & night. Hostile artillery was very active the left of BOIS (T.6. c. 30.90). Owing to low clouds & mist there was very little air activity during the day. At night enemy M.G. Guns were active & appeared to be searching for our night firing guns. Casualties Nil.

### 18/12/17 — Field

> Hostile artillery very active. At 1.30 a.m. enemy laid down a heavy barrage on our front line & support trench (Teddie [uncertain] Gerrard), enemy also shelled Vimy Station, Bois de Chaudiere & area in rear of Railway Embankment with 5.9". During the day two E.A. crossed our lines too high to be engaged by M.G. Gun fire. During the night two guns of "D" section fired on the following targets 4,500 rds on T.5. a. 15.85. 6.00 — 10.00 p.m. & 4,500 rds on T.5. b. 05.15. 6.00 to 10 p.m. S.A.A. expended 9,000 rds. > 826 CLOTHING, UNAUTHORIZED ALTERATIONS TO. It has come to notice that certain units have been in the habit of wearing shorts as part of the recognized S.D. uniform and that the trouser issued to the men have been cut down for the purpose. The practice of cutting trousers down into shorts is to cease. A certain number of service dress garments returned by the troops are found to be in a condition which them unfit for re-issue, although the material itself is frequently quite serviceable. Such clothing has been improperly altered in pattern. Especially is the case with trousers which have been either reduced in length or taken in to fit the shape of an individual soldier's leg, presumably to give neatness in folding the puttees. This practice which is causing waste at a time when the rigid economy is essential must cease. (Authority D.R.O. 3110.)
>
> 827 SEPARATION ALLOWANCE. It is published for the information of all ranks that an increase of five dollars per month in Separation Allowance, payable to all ranks below sergeants, has been approved, and became effective on first Dec. 1917. "The lowest Separation Allowance payable, therefore, to dependents of either ranks will, in future, be twenty five dollars per month". (Authority D.R.O. 3921.)
>
> 828 LEAVE. All officers and other ranks granted permission to proceed on leave to the Dominions, Colonies, or Allied countries will wear uniform at all times. Those to whom leave is granted to proceed to Neutral countries will wear plain clothes. A report, in writing, stating address, period of leave and authority will be rendered on arrival in the country, to visit which permission has been granted. (1) In the Dominions and Colonies — To the local Military Hqrs. (2) In Allied and Neutral Countries — To the British Attache, or to a British Consul. (Authority 1st Army R.O. 2178.)
>
> [Signed] J. McCorkell Capt. Officer Commanding, 5th Cdn. M.G.Coy.

> 833 Method of Registration by officers in Hotel Visitors Books in France and Belguim. An officer when entering his name in the Visitor's Book in an Hotel in France or Belguim will not specify the battalion or other unit to which he belongs. He will, if an infantry officer, state his regiment, and in other cases state the branch of the service to which he belongs. No indication is to be given as to the officers location in the country other than an entry that he belongs to the B.E.F. (Authority G.R.O. 2986.)

> 843 CONTENTS AND DESPATCH OF LETTERS AND PARCELS, ETC. (a. Forbidden information.) All ranks will be warned that in all letters written by them they must avoid giving any information which might be useful to the enemy. In no circumstances is specific reference to be made on post cards, in letters, on matter posted in parcels, or in private diaries to:— (i) The place from which they are written or despatched. Picture postcards, which give dangerous information, are included under this heading. It is not sufficient to erase or cut off the name. (ii) Plans of future operations, whether rumoured, surmised, or known (this includes details of mining and bridging operations, railway construction, particulars of new inventions.) (iii) Organization, numbers and movements of troops. Under this heading are included hours, dates and system of reliefs; positions of batteries, machine guns, and observing stations; positions and description of billets; reinforcements; situation of headquarters of brigades, divisions, etc. (iv) The armaments of troops or fortresses. (v) Defensive works. (vi) Condition of the troops, moral and physical. (vii) Casualties, previous to the publication of official lists, or giving particulars not obtainable from those lists. (viii) The service of maintenance, including positions of railways heads and supply columns, condition of roads and railways, reserves of supplies and ammunition, shortage of supplies or ammunition. (ix) The effects of hostile fire. Criticism of operations, of other branches, of allied troops or of superiors are forbidden, as are statements calculated to bring the Army or individuals into disrepute. ## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–19, 24–29 and 31 December 1917. There are no diary entries for 20–23 December or for 30 December. Diary text is 11 handwritten sheets (pages 3–13); Appendix 1 is Operation Order No. 58 (pages 14–17); Appendix 2 is seven Daily Routine Orders (pages 18–24). Two separate Daily Routine Orders are dated 31 December: the one on page 23 (orders 847–848, hours for 1 January 1918) and the one on page 24 (orders 844–846, hours for 31 December). Orders 834–835 do not appear in the appendix — the order numbers jump from 833 (24 December) to 836 (26 December).
- **Officers named:** Capt. J. E. McCorkell (O.C. 5th Cdn. M.G. Coy.; the O.C. changed from Capt. J. McCahill in November); Lieut. G. W. H. Milligan (signs most Daily Routine Orders "for Captain"); Lt. H. A. McBurney; Lt. F. J. Inch; Lt. I. G. MacLaren; Lt. H. J. Petticary; Lt. G. E. Harley; Lt. G. Hobson (Military Cross, 31 December); Lt. Creighton (Operation Order No. 58, para. 9); Major Samson (16th Cdn. M.G. Coy., takes over company headquarters at Badger, 19 December).
- **Other ranks named:** No. 61221 Sgt. Chamberland E. (Distinguished Conduct Medal); No. 721050 B/Cpl. Sillers R.W. (Distinguished Conduct Medal); No. 68001 Sgt. Christie J. (Military Medal); No. 69194 Pte. Caldwell L. (bar to the Military Medal); No. 1004095 Pte. Anderson J.A. (Military Medal); No. 910115 Pte. Duxbury C.B. (Military Medal); Pte. Rigby A.R. (817964) and Pte. Curran W.G. (163810), guard stick 26 December; Pte. Vincent A. (175297), guard stick 28 December; Pte. Anderson (1000859) and Pte. White W.E. (907718), guard stick 29 December; Pte. Fox T. (415528) and Pte. Ross G. (288786), guard stick 31 December. Orderly sergeants named in the Daily Routine Orders: Sgts. Street J., Perry A., Brown A., MacLean J. and Ronayne C.
- **Equipment:** Vickers machine guns (16 guns in four sections); gas blankets and ammunition pits at each gun emplacement; inventory boards; trench stores (48 tins iron rations, 4 gallons water, 24 Mills bombs, 1 copy standing orders per emplacement); limbers; 5.9-inch H.E. and gas shells; S.A.A. (small arms ammunition) expended — 9,000 rds. on 5 Dec., 2,000 on 6 Dec., 19,000 on 7 Dec., 8,500 on 8 Dec., 15,000 on 9 Dec., 12,400 on 10 Dec., 12,000 on 11 Dec., 12,000 on 12 Dec., 13,200 on 13 Dec., 18,000 on 14 Dec., 20,500 on 15 Dec., 11,500 on 16 Dec., 13,500 on 17 Dec., 9,000 on 18 Dec.
- **Movements:** In the line in the Chaudiere sector (Vimy) 1–19 December; relieved by the 10th and 16th Cdn. M.G. Coys. on the night of 19/20 December under Operation Order No. 58, and marched back to billets at Aux Reitz (spelled "Aux Rietz" in the diary entry of 19 December); in billets at Aux Reitz 24–31 December.
- **Discipline:** None recorded in December 1917. Training in billets: physical training, squad drill, mechanism, cleaning of guns and equipment, repacking of limbers; guard duties and guard instruction.
- **Casualties:** None in the company all month — every diary entry closes "Casualties Nil". The only missing men noted are 8 O.Rs of the 31st C.I. Bn, reported on 10 December as surrounded by the enemy in No Man's Land; they are not company casualties.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The December diary text is handwritten throughout, whereas November's diary sheets were typed. (2) The 1 Dec. entry reads "A thorough the gun maintenance of the whole area was made by the C.O.", transcribed as written; the sentence is garbled. (3) The 4 Dec. entry reads "were used Indirect Fire" (the word "for" is omitted), transcribed as written. (4) The 5 Dec. entry contains a fragmentary sentence beginning "of H.E. (H.1) fired a few rounds at BLUENOSE & BOIS positions during the day — no damage done", transcribed as written. (5) The 7 Dec. entry reads "During night scattered his M.G. fire over our front", transcribed as written; the subject (the enemy) is not stated. (6) The 9 Dec. barrage sentence is garbled — "firing 2 rds [uncertain] every 5 minutes until the Railway embankment on which he concentrated until 3.20 a.m." — transcribed as written. (7) The 15 Dec. entry reads "Lt Hobsons fire apparently damaged one E.A. which was seen to plane down behind enemy's lines", transcribed as written. (8) The 19 Dec. position list repeats "Auk" and omits "Bess", whereas Operation Order No. 58 lists BESS (and AUK once) among the guns relieved by the 16th Cdn. M.G. Coy.; transcribed as written. (9) The 27 and 29 Dec. entries give the midday hour as "12.00 a.m." (the 31 Dec. entry gives "12 noon"), transcribed as written. (10) The diary's guard-stick entry for 29 Dec. gives Pte. Anderson's initials as "J.A.", while the typed Daily Routine Orders of 28 Dec. give the same number (1000859) as "Anderson A.H."; transcribed as written in each place. (11) The diary writes the second guard-stick man's section on 26 Dec. as a ditto mark (") under "C Sec"; it is the "A" Section, as the typed Daily Routine Orders of 26 Dec. confirm. (12) Operation Order No. 58 spells the sector "CHAUDIERE"; this confirms the sector name that November's Operation Order No. 57 rendered as "CHAUDEE" [uncertain]. (13) In Daily Routine Orders 831 (24 Dec.), a word before "or Gas Helmets" is heavily blacked out; only an initial "R" and final "r" are legible, suggesting "Respirator". (14) In Daily Routine Orders 842 (28 Dec.), the date on which the guard was warned for duty is overtyped and reads as either 29th or 30th; it is transcribed as 30th because the diary's 29 Dec. entry records the guard for the 30th parading for instruction on the 29th. (16) S.A.A. totals: on several days the stated S.A.A. expended does not equal the sum of the rounds itemised in the entry — 7 Dec. (8,000 itemised vs 19,000 stated), 9 Dec.
