### 1/9/16 — Field

> Reference Map - HAZELBROUCK 85A of France Scale 1/100,000
>
> Owing to rain in the early morning, physical training was not carried on with. The company proceeded to the training area at 8.00 A.M. arriving there at 9.45 A.M. Advanced training was carried on with. Returned to billets at 4.00 P.M.

### 2/9/16 — (ditto)

> The company paraded for the ranges at 7.00 A.M. and spent the day there trying out the [word crossed out] guns. Practising the new men in firing & in trying out & accustoming the men in the use of the new Lee Enfield rifles. The result of the firing was very satisfactory. The company returned to billets at 4.00 P.M.

### 3/9/16 — (ditto)

> The company paraded from 6.0 A.M. until 6.45 A.M. for physical training. It paraded again at 7.45 A.M. and moved off to the training area at 8.0 A.M. The day was spent in advanced work for the old men and in gun drill & mechanism for the new men. The company returned to billets at 4.0 P.M. Church service was held in the billets by Capt. Bennett after the return.

### 4/9/16 — (ditto)

> The company left Billets at 10.0 A.M. and marched to ARQUES. Entrained at 2.20 P.M. The train pulled out at 5.30 P.M. Officers men & horses all entrained in first class condition.

### 5/9/16 — (ditto)

> Reference Map: - LENS. II. of FRANCE. SCALE 1/100,000
>
> The company detrained at CONTEVILLE at 2.0 A.M. All the horses arrived safely & suffered no harm on the train. The company marched to COULONVILLERS, and billeted there for the day. Time of arrival 5.0 A.M.

### 6/9/16 — (ditto)

> The Company left billets in COULONVILLERS at 7.45 A.M. Arrived at starting point at DOMQUEUR at 9.31 A.M. Proceeded by DOMART - ST LEGER road to PERNOIS. Billeted in HALLOY. Reached billets at 3.00 P.M. Billets very poor. Health of men very good.
>
> [uncertain] on the line of march.

### 7/9/16 — Field

> The company left HALLOY and arrived at starting point (road junction VIGNECOURT MONTELET-HABERNAS roads at 9.10 A.M.), proceeded by WAOURS-TALMAS road to billets at LAYICOGNE. Arrived in billets at 12.20 P.M. Accommodation very poor & part of the company bivouaced. Lt. McCorkell & Roy paid a visit to the [uncertain] line in front of ALBERT. The health of the company is of the best.

### 8/9/16 — (ditto)

> The company left LAYICOGNE at 7.45 A.M. and passed starting point at cross roads in TALMAS at 9.0 A.M. It proceeded to billets at RUBEMPRE, arriving in billets at 11.0 A.M. The billeting accommodation is very good, the company being on a large farm. The health of the company is very good. Lts DUTHIE & CAMPBELL went up to the line during the day on a reconnaissance.

### 9/9/16 — (ditto)

> The company left RUBEMPRE at 8.45 A.M. & passed the starting point (at crossroads S.E. of HERISSART at 9.40 A.M.) proceeded by HERISSART-CONTAY road to VADENCOURT. Arrived in billets at VADENCOURT at 12.0 Noon. Billets very poor. Health of the company very good. No casualties on the line of march.

### 10/9/16 — (ditto)

> Church service was held for the company at 9.00 A.M. The R.Cs. paraded to church under Lt Roy. The remainder of the morning was spent in cleaning the guns, S.A.A. etc. The company left VADENCOURT at 2. P.M. and arrived at starting point (on VADENCOURT-WARLOY road 1 mile east of VADENCOURT) at 2. P.M. Proceeded by SENLIS-BOUZINCOURT road to ALBERT and bivouaced in allotted area on the ALBERT-BOUZINCOURT road to the north of ALBERT. There were no casualties and the health of the company is very good.

### 11/9/16 — Field

> The day was spent quietly in training on the camp area, and in fixing up the camp. Orders were expected for the company to move to another area, but they did not arrive. All guns, S.A.A. and equipment were thoroughly overhauled and cleaned.

### 12/9/16 — (ditto)

> Lieuts. McCorkell & Grantham made a reconnaissance of the positions on the COURCELETTE front occupied by the 4th & 6th Cdn. M. G. Coys. Lieuts. Bowles and Sinclair, accompanied by two N.C.O's each, also paid a visit to the same areas. The Company carried on with elementary & advanced training between the hours of 9.00 A.M. & 4.00 P.M.

### 13/9/16 — (ditto)

> Lieuts. Duthie & Campbell, accompanied by two N.C.O's each, made a reconnaissance of the areas visited by Lieuts. Bowles and Sinclair on the preceding day. The Company started on a days syllabus of elementary and advanced training at 9.00 A.M., but were interrupted by the camp area being shelled, between the hours of 10.00 A.M. & 12.00 noon, by about 30 H.E. shells. Our area was in immediate proximity to the shelled area so our horses and men were removed to another part of the camp while the shelling was taking place. At 5.00 P.M. Lieuts. Grantham, Bowles & Duthie made a reconnaissance of the area the Company was to occupy while in Divisional Reserve to the 4th & 6th Bdes. during the operations due to take place on Sept 15th.

### 14/9/16 — (ditto)

> The Company paraded at 3.00 P.M. for the trenches & moved from the BRICKFIELDS at ALBERT to Hdqs. of the 6th Cdn. M. G. Coy in BAILIFF WOOD (MAP LOCATION - X.16 a 4.2) Company Hdqs. were established at that point and the Company was sheltered in dugouts along the road to the west of Hdqs. for a distance of 360 yards, and in the adjacent [uncertain].

> Lieut. Campbell reported that all was well with his two guns. All of the men under the above two officers did good work sniping. Rations were sent up to the gun crews in the line during the early morning.

### 17/9/16 — (ditto)

> During the night a message was sent to Lieut Roy at the Transport lines to report at once to the O.C. The two gun crews are deserving of the highest praise as they were almost continuously engaged with the enemy from the time of reaching their position. One party of the enemy succeeded in creeping around to their flank but luckily were put out of action by a shell. His casualties were five.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–30 September 1916 with no date gaps. 15 pages in total: cover, typed contents sheet, and 13 diary sheets (Sheets No. 1–13), matching the contents sheet's "Diary Text — 13 Pages".
- **Officers named:** Lt J. H. McCorkell (O.C., signs the diary and initials every entry "J.H.McC."); Capt. Bennett (held church service 3 Sep); Lt Roy; Lt W. A. Duthie (killed 15 Sep); Lt H. B. Sinclair (wounded 15 Sep); Lt Bowles; Lt Campbell; Lt Grantham; Lt Welch (recalled to the 26th Bn. 22 Sep); Lts O. B. Ellis and H. A. McBurney (joined from the Canadian Machine Gun Base, Shorncliffe, 24 Sep); Lt.-Col. Brutenell (1st Div. Hdqs., Tara Valley, 27 Sep).
- **Other ranks named:** Sgt Hobson (D Section, whose two guns held an advanced position 16–17 Sep); Sgt Chamberland (A Section); Sgt Ross (put in papers for a commission, took command of A Section 23 Sep); Sgt Hearn (A Section, 30 Sep); Lt Bowles' unnamed sergeant (shot and killed 16 Sep); the unnamed sergeant of C Section (killed 29 Sep).
- **Equipment:** New Lee Enfield rifles issued and tried on the ranges 2 Sep; machine guns, S.A.A., limbers and harness overhauled and cleaned; one gun and tripod destroyed by a direct hit 28 Sep; two C Section guns buried by shells and put out of action 29 Sep; company transport horses used as pack animals 28–30 Sep.
- **Movements:** Training near Hazebrouck 1–3 Sep; march to Arques and entrain 4 Sep; detrain Conteville, march to Coulonvillers 5 Sep; Domqueur, Domart–St Léger, Pernois, Halloy 6 Sep; Vignecourt, Talmas, Layicogne 7 Sep; Rubempré 8 Sep; Vadencourt 9 Sep; Albert 10 Sep; Brickfields, Albert, and Bailiff Wood 14 Sep; in the line at Courcelette / Gun Pit Trench / Sunken Road 15–18 Sep; relief completed 18 Sep, motor bus to Warloy; Rubempré 19 Sep; Brickfields, Albert 25–26 Sep; back into the line at Courcelette, Sugar Trench and the Sunken Road 27–30 Sep.
- **Discipline:** None recorded in September 1916.
- **Casualties:** 15 Sep — Lt W. A. Duthie killed, Lt H. B. Sinclair and 3 O.R. wounded. 16 Sep — Lt Bowles' sergeant shot and killed; Sgt Hobson's two gun crews suffered five casualties. 18 Sep totals for the 15–18 Sep tour: 2 officers and 16 O.R. (1 officer and 3 O.R. killed; 1 officer and 10 O.R. wounded; 2 O.R. missing [uncertain] wounded; 1 O.R. missing). 28 Sep — 2 men killed and 2 wounded when a direct hit destroyed an A Section gun. 29 Sep to noon — 7 O.R. killed and 5 O.R. wounded and shell shocked, including the C Section sergeant killed. 30 Sep — 2 O.R. wounded.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The 17 Sep entry says the 1st Cdn. M. G. Coy would relieve the company, but the officers who arrived at 6.00 P.M. to arrange the relief were from the 6th Cdn. M. G. Coy. (2) The 27 Sep entry gives the destination as "USAN VALLEY" with a marginal query mark "? USAN A" at the foot of the sheet; the same area is called TARA VALLEY on the next sheet. (3) Several passages in the 17–18 Sep entries are heavily overwritten or crossed out and partly illegible. (4) The 30 Sep entry refers to "(See entry Sept 23rd)" for the exchange involving Sgt Hearn, but the 23 Sep entry names Sgt Ross, not Hearn. (5) The 6 Sep entry ends with a partly legible line about the line of march. (6) The 29 Sep entry's sentence about the buried C Section crews is partly illegible ("[uncertain] four been killed").
