>
> (a) Prisoners of War will be sent by section arrangements to Company Advanced hqrs. and will be [uncertain] Hqrs Sub. Staff to Divisional Cage R. 11 c. 5.5
>
> (b) ARMS AND EQUIPMENT. All arms and equipment captured from the enemy are the property of the Government and should be immediately turned into Coy., Advanced Hqrs., for despatch to Ordnance.
>
> (c) DOCUMENTS. Prisoners should be given no opportunity to destroy or throw away DOCUMENTS in their possession at the time of Capture. In the case of officers prisoners, all their papers and correspondence will be placed in sand bags as soon as possible after capture. This bag will be sent to Company Hqrs., with the prisoner, O.W., prisoners will retain their personal effects. DOCUMENTS will be collected from dug-outs and dead if possible and will be placed in sand bags. Labels will be attached indicating the approximate locality in which the documents were found. These sand bags will be forward to Coy., Hqrs., with the least possible delay.
>
> (d) INTERROGATION. Any interrogation of, or conversation with prisoners by officers or other ranks, not belonging to the Intelligence is forbidden. Intelligence officers who are instructed by the Corps or by a Division to interrogate prisoners, will be provided with a pass to that effect.
>
> P.T.O.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 August 1917 with no date gaps. Diary text is 15 sheets (Sheets 1–15); the month also contains one appendix (Appendix A) in 8 pages: Administrative Arrangements issued with Operation Order No. 18 (2 pages), Operation Order No. 18 itself (4 pages), and Operation Order No. 21 (2 pages).
- **Officers named:** Lieut. J. E. McCorkill (O.C. and diarist); Lieut. MacLaren (Battery Commander of "K" Battery, the barrage sections); Lieut. Much (O.C. "C" Section); Lieut. Creighton (O.C. "D" Section); Lieut. H. A. Mc. Burney (reported sick and evacuated with P.U.O., 8 August); Lieut. Perks (O/O "D" Section, 22 August order; sent forward 17 August); Lieut. Reid (sent forward 17 August, remained 2nd in Command of "C" Section); Major Bassevi (O.C. 14th Cdn. M.G. Coy., Group Commander of the barrage guns); Major Goodfellow (took command of the new 2nd Canadian Divisional Machine Gun Battalion, 29 August); Lieut. McLean (15th Cdn. M.G. Coy., relieving "D" Section).
- **Other ranks named:** Sgt. Marson [uncertain] (wounded 5 August); the No. 1's of gun crews (unnamed).
- **Equipment:** 16 machine guns in four sections; 8 guns formed "K" Battery (barrage guns) and 8 the mobile guns for the Hill 70 attack; gun No. L 4946 of "B" Section knocked out by a 5.9-inch shell 12 August and replaced from Ordnance; one "D" Section gun and crew knocked out by a direct hit 15 August and replaced from Ordnance; "C" Section's four guns mounted for anti-aircraft work 16 August; S.A.A. expenditure ran from 2,000 to 75,000 rounds a day.
- **Movements:** In the Lens sector trenches at the start of August (Company Hqrs. M. 11. B. 8.3); "K" Battery withdrawn 3 August; company under command of G.O.C. 4th Cdn. Inf. Bde. 4 August, then 6th Cdn. Inf. Bde. 9 August, then 5th Cdn. Inf. Bde. 13 August; the Hill 70 attack 15 August (Zero Hour 4.25 A.M.) with "A" & "B" Sections as barrage guns and "C" & "D" as mobile guns going over the parapet; "C" & "D" relieved by 15th Cdn. M.G. Coy. 22/23 August and back to billets at Aix-Noulette; company moved to Gouy Servins 24 August; "A" & "B" relieved by 8th Cdn. M.G. Coy. 28 August; whole company to new billets at Camblain l'Abbé 29 August.
- **Discipline:** None recorded in August 1917.
- **Casualties:** 5 August — 1 O.R. wounded (Sgt. Marson). 15 August (Hill 70) — 3 O.R. killed, 7 O.R. wounded; one "D" Section gun and crew knocked out. 16 August — 2 O.R. wounded. 18 August — 3 O.R. wounded (gas). 21 August — 3 O.R. wounded (gas). All other days "NIL" or no casualty figure given. Three prisoners captured 16 August.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The 8 August entry cites "Operation order # 20 attached", but no copy of Order No. 20 is included in this month's appendix (Appendix A contains Orders No. 18 and No. 21 only). (2) The 14 August entry has a sentence about the eight barrage guns crossed out in the original and partly illegible. (3) The 24 August entry says the company moved less "'C' & 'B' Section which are still in the line", although the 22–23 August entries show "A" & "B" Sections were the ones remaining in barrage positions; transcribed as written. (4) The 27 August entry says word has come that "'C' & 'D' Sections will be relieved tonight by the 8th Cdn. M.G. Coy.", although "C" & "D" had already been relieved on 22/23 August and the 28 August entry records "A" & "B" Sections being relieved; transcribed as written. (5) The 15 August entry's "Lieut. [uncertain]" — the officer commanding "A" Section alongside Lieut. MacLaren — is illegible; the name appears to read "Paton". (6) In Operation Order No. 21, paragraph 1(a), the word "possible" appears struck through before "is safe"; transcribed as "as soon after dusk as is safe". (7) The 2 August and 8 August entries both run to the bottom edge of their sheets and their final lines are partly cut off. (8) Numbers in the diary are written variously as "33.000", "20.000", "43.875", "25.625", "54.000" (with a point) and as plain figures ("32000", "75000"); transcribed as written. (9) Pages 23 and 25 are largely show-through from the reverse of the sheets; only the copy lists are legible. (10) The 17 August entry reads "Lieut. Perks" — the same name appears as O/O "D" Section in Operation Order No. 21.
