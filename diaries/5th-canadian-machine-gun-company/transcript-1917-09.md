### 1/9/17 — Field

> Was carried out with training. P.T., Squad Drill, Gun Drill, Gas and revolver drill.

### 2/9/17 — (ditto)

> Divine service was held in Cinema. Company Attended.

### 3/9/17 — (ditto)

> Training was carried on at 9.30. A.M. O.Cs inspection was held. The remainder of the morning, stoppages tests were held.

### 4/9/17 — (ditto)

> Training carried on. P.T., Mechanism, stoppages, range card and revolver drill.

### 5/9/17 — (ditto)

> Was carried out training. P.T., Indirect fire, map reading and use of compass.

### 6/9/17 — (ditto)

> Training carried out till 9.30. A.M. when the whole company went to baths.

### 7/9/17 — (ditto)

> An O.Cs inspections was held at 9.30 A.M lasting until 11 A.M. The remainder of A.M. spent in Gas and revolver drill. At 3 P.M. 5th Can. M.G. Coy. met the 14th Can. M.G. Coy., at Football on the Chateau Ground. The 14th Coy. winning four goals to one.

### 8/9/17 — (ditto)

> The days was spent in training. P.T., Use of compass, map reading and indirect fire occupied all morning. A gun team was picked out to represent this unit in the 2nd Divl. M.G. Battn. Sports. In the Gun Mounting competition. This unit played the 18th M.G. Coy. at Baseball and losing by 11 to 7.

### 9/9/17 — (ditto)

> The company attended Divine service in the Cinema. The men picked for sports carrying on with their respective work.

### 10/9/17 — (ditto)

> Training carried out, limber drill, P.T., squad drill, and gas drill. The men picked for sports were let off all parades for to train.

### 11/9/17 — (ditto)

> Lieut. McCorkell appointed actg. D.A.M.G.C., 2nd Cdn. Div. while Major Goodfellow was on leave. Lieut. Hobson took over command of this company. Lieut. McBurney as second in Command. The O.Cs Companys 2nd Cdn. Divl. M.G. Battn. went up to inspect the new line, to be taken over by the 2nd. Cdn. Div.

## Page 8 — Appendix 1: Letter of appreciation

> COPY.
>
> Canadian Corps Headquarters, France, 30th, August, 1917.
>
> The Corps Machine Gun Officer.
>
> Now that the battle of HILL 70 is practically over, I desire to convey to you my appreciation of the most excellent work done by you personally, and by the Machine Gun Companies under your control in connection with these most successful operations.
>
> I have heard nothing but the very highest praise concerning the co-operation by the machine guns in the attack, and in the repelling of the numerous hostile counter-attacks. The liaison seem to have been very good indeed. I also know that the machine gun fire was largely responsible in preventing the enemy from repairing the damage caused by our preliminary bombardment.
>
> Success such as we have had is only possible when each branch of the service pulls it's own weight and all pull together. I would be glad if you would convey to the Machine Gunners under you command and control my appreciation of the manner in which they performed the tasks allotted to them.
>
> (Sgd.) A. W. Currie.
>
> Lieut.-General,
>
> Commanding, Canadian Corps.

[The contents sheet dates this appendix 20-9-17; the letter itself is dated 30th August, 1917.]
