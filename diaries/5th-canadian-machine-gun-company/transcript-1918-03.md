### 1/3/18 — Carency

> Weather ... Clear and Cold with snow flurries at intervals.
>
> Lieut. F.J.Luch visited 2nd Can.M.G.Bn.School at Souchez Corner.
>
> The usual training syllabus was carried out.
>
> Effective Strength of Company at this date was 184.

### 2/3/18 — (ditto)

> Weather .... Dull.
>
> Training was carried on as usual except in the morning when a Bath Parade was held.
>
> Lieut. W.J.Scougal reported for duty from D.M.G.Crs. Office.

### 3/3/18 — (ditto)

> Weather... Dull and Damp.
>
> Church Parade was held. No training was carried out.

### 4/3/18 — (ditto)

> Weather.... Dull with Mist and Rain at intervals.
>
> The enemy put on an unsuccessful operation on the LIEVIN front at 6.00 A.M.
>
> The 5th Cdn.M.G.Coy. relieved the 4th Cdn.M.G.Coy. in the Reserve positions of the LIEVIN sector on the afternoon of the 4th March, 1918.
>
> Relief was complete at 6.35 P.M.

### 5/3/18 — (ditto)

> Situation normal. One shell landed on the house separating J6 and J7 positions of the PETER GROUP.

## Page 4 — War Diary (Army Form C. 2118), Sheet 2

### 6/3/18 — Carency

> At 2.15 A.M. a raid was made by our troops supported by Artillery and Machine Guns. 1000 rounds were fired from 6a and J5 positions.
>
> During the day the situation was normal. At 4.30 P.M. an air fight took place when one hostile ALBATROS crashed in our area M.18 c.90.60.

### 7/3/18 — (ditto)

> A number of Gas shells fell at M.24c between 1.30 and 3.30 P.M.
>
> All Reserve positions were visited and found in order.
>
> A reconnaissance of the LEFT SECTION was made by the Actg. Officer Commanding, and one Section Officer.

### 8/3/18 — (ditto)

> Nothing unusual to report. Hostile artillery continued scattered shelling of the Support area. A large fire was observed at 2.00 P.M. from M.17c.70.60 position.

### 9/3/18 — (ditto)

> Enemy artillery showed a slight increase in activity and scattered shelling of the town of LIEVIN took place from 4.00 to 6.00 P.M. and again at 11.00 P.M.
>
> Two Officers made a reconnaissance of the LEFT SECTION.

### 10/3/18 — (ditto)

> There was considerable shelling of the whole area by the enemy during the day. No. 112352 Pte. MCKAY J. was slightly wounded with shrapnel in the chin and was evacuated. Our artillery carried out usual harassing fire

### 23/3/18 — (ditto)

### 24/3/18 — Villers au Bois

> Lieut. F.J.Luch proceeded to Springvale Camp, ECOURIE, at 6.00 A.M. to arrange billets.
>
> The Company left VILLERS AU BOIS at 8.30 A.M. by Motor Lorries and proceeded to ECOURIE which was reached at 11 A.M. The Company stood-to, ready for immediate action.

### 25/3/18 — Ecourie

> The Company remained standing-to. A reconnaissance of the forward area OPPY was made by the Section Officers during the day.

### 26/3/18 — (ditto)

> The ROCLINCOURT RIDGE line was reconnoitred during the day by the Section Officers.
>
> The Company left ECOURIE at 10.00 P.M. and marched to BERLES AU BOIS where billets had been by Lieut. Luch.

### 27/3/18 — Berles au Bois

> The Company rested during the morning and in the afternoon preparations were made for a quick move.

### 28/3/18 — (ditto)

> The Company stood-to.
>
> At 8.00 P.M. orders were received that the Company would be attached to the 5th Cdn.I.Bde. at FICHEUX for immediate action.
>
> The Column left BERLES AU BOIS at 10.00 P.M. and proceeded to [uncertain] where Transport lines were established.
>
> Under the instructions of G.O.C. 5th C.I.B. the Sections moved forward and took up 16 Positions in advance of FICHEUX.

## Notes

- **Coverage:** Entries run 1–31 March 1918 with no date gaps. The diary text is 6 pages (Sheets 1–6); the remaining 15 pages are appendices (Daily Routine Orders and Operation Orders Nos. 6 and 7 with an appendix to No. 7).
- **Officers named:** Lieut. F. J. Luch (Acting Officer Commanding throughout the month; signs the diary orders); Lieut. W. J. Scougal (reported for duty 2 March; later Acting Captain, temporarily commanding the Right Wing of the 2nd Cdn. M.G. Bn. School from 18 March); Lieut. G. Hobson M.C. (Transport/limbers; appointed Battery Commander of "A" Battery on 23 March); Lieut. H. A. McBurney (appointed Commander of "B" Battery on 23 March); Lieut. I. C. MacLaren; Lieut. G. E. Harley; Lieut. G. W. Comstock; Lieut. White (Route March, 23 March, no initials given); Capt. J. BelleCorieli (surname uncertain) (returned from course, 17 March); Capt. J. E. McCorkell (commanding, 21 March orders); the D.M.G.C. (inspected huts 17 March; lecturer, unnamed).
- **Other ranks named:** Pte. J. McKay, No. 112352 (wounded 10 March); Cpl. W. H. Sutherland, No. 160440 (promoted Acting Sergeant, 1 March); Pte. A. E. Mitchell, No. 237039 (appointed Acting Lance Sergeant, 13 March); Sgt. Brown A.; Sgt. Caldwell L. (also shown as Caldwell H. on 12 March); Sgt. Smitheram T. (also shown as Smitheram T.H.); Pte. T. Hopkins, No. 307330, Lancashire Fusiliers (tried and executed — see Discipline; not a member of this company).
- **Equipment:** Machine guns (16 guns in position in the Mercatel sector on 29 March — 8 in the front line, 8 in support); limbers; S.B.R.s (Small Box Respirators) and gas helmets; 72,000 rounds of S.A.A. exchanged with the 11th Cdn. M.G. Coy.; 14 belts and boxes, fully loaded, per gun crew on relief; motor lorries used for the move to Ecourie on 24 March.
- **Movements:** Carency (1–4 March, training) → Lievin sector reserve positions (relief of the 4th Cdn. M.G. Coy., 4 March) → Marqueffles Farm (relieved by the 11th Cdn. M.G. Coy. — the diary text of 11 March says the 7th Cdn. M.G. Coy., see Anomalies — and marched to billets, 11 March) → Villers au Bois (23 March) → Ecourie (24 March, by motor lorry) → Berles au Bois (26 March) → Ficheux/Mercatel sector (28–29 March, attached to the 5th Cdn. Infantry Brigade) → Grosville (29–31 March).
- **Discipline:** The 1 March orders reproduce a Field General Court Martial sentence on Pte. T. Hopkins, Lancashire Fusiliers, for leaving his post — "To suffer death by being shot" — carried out at 6.20 a.m. on 15 February 1918. The company also published a censorship order (15 March), a cord-pantaloons collection order (21 March), and routine orders on gas, leave, and the theft of rifles/revolvers by men proceeding on leave to Ireland.
- **Casualties:** One wounded — Pte. J. McKay, slightly wounded by shrapnel in the chin and evacuated on 10 March. "Casualties..... Nil." is recorded for both 30 and 31 March. No casualties from the hostile bombing of the camp on 21 March.
- **Anomalies:** (1) The 11 March diary entry says the company was relieved by the 7th Cdn. M.G. Coy., but Operation Order No. 7 (pages 19–21) and the Contents sheet say the relief was by the 11th Cdn. M.G. Coy.; transcribed as written in each place. (2) The diary text of 26 March reads "where billets had been by Lieut. Luch" — a word appears to be missing; transcribed as written. (3) Operation Order No. 6 has no paragraph 9 (it jumps from 8 to 10). (4) The Daily Routine Orders jump in numbering: 68 (15 March) to 77 (18 March), and 80 (18 March) to 90 (21 March) — orders for the intervening days are not included in this appendix. (5) The typed Daily Routine Order headings render the commanding officer's name as "F. L. MUCH." while his signature throughout reads "F. J. Luch"; transcribed as printed/signed in each place. (6) The 19 March orders list "9.45 A.M. Company Parade" before "9.00 A.M. Battalion Parade"; transcribed as printed. (7) The 15 March orders (page 15) have a faded heading; the issuing officer's name there could not be read. (8) The place name where Transport lines were established on 28 March is heavily overwritten and could not be read. (9) The 17 March entry spells a captain's surname in a way that could not be read with confidence (flagged in the 17 March entry). (10) The Remarks-column initials throughout read as "C.W.T. Lt" but are not fully clear. (11) The 12 March orders show Sgt. Caldwell as "H." while other orders show "L."; transcribed as printed in each place.
