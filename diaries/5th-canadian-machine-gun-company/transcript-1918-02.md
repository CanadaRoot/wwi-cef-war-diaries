### 1/2/18 — Field

> Wind East. Foggy.
> Hostile Artillery .... very quiet.
> Hostile M.G's. .... Harassing fire was directed on Front Line during day.
> Hostile Aircraft .... very quiet.
> Work. Four yards of trench was revetted at BADGER.
> A complete reconnaissance of the defensive emplacements was made by the O.C.
> Transport lines lost Mr. Creighton who took command of "B" Section.

### 2/2/18 — Field

> Wind East. Mild.
> Enemy Artillery ..... quiet.
> Enemy M.G's. ..... Normal.
> Enemy Aircraft ....... Normal. 500 rounds was fired from Anti Aircraft gun "B" section and 250 rounds from Anti-Aircraft gun "C" section.
> Lieut. Harley took command of "A" section.

### 3/2/18 — Field

> Weather ...... Clear.
> Hostile Artillery ... quiet.
> Hostile Aircraft ..... active. 500 rounds were fired from Anti Aircraft gun "B" section.
> The open emplacements in course of construction, c13 and c14 were completed. The work of improving the trench at BADGER was continued. The parapet was revetted with corrugated iron.
> The company was paid at Reserve Company Headquarters.

### 4/2/18 — Field

> Lieut. Comstock took command of "C" Section vice Lieut. Potticary who was sent to the Rest Camp.
> Owing to visibility being good there was increased activity in Aeroplane work on both sides.
> Enemy artillery was below normal.

### 5/2/18 — Field

> Enemy Artillery ... normal.
> M.G's ......... more active against our aircraft during the day. No hostile aeroplanes came within range of our guns.
> Owing to the men proceeding to Aux Reitz for a bath there was very little work done on the emplacements.

### 6/2/18 — Field

> There was increased activity in hostile artillery. About 100 "heavies" were dropped in neighborhood of T.19.b.80.80. between 2.00 P.M. and 5.00 P.M. and again at 9.30 P.M. about 200 gas shells and about 50 "whizzbangs" fell in neighborhood of T.13.b.
> Visibility was good and our artillery aeroplanes were active. A few enemy aircraft were seen patrolling his own lines.
> The rebuilding of the defensive positions was carried on satisfactorily.

### 7/2/18 — Field

> Hostile artillery and M.G's. were quiet. Owing to the mist there was very little aeroplane work. No hostile aeroplanes were seen on this sector. All ammunition dumps were inspected and found in order.

### 8/2/18 — Field

> Day was cloudy. Observation being poor, there was no aeroplane work on either side. Enemy artillery was quiet during the greater part of the day. A few shells fell around VICTORIA DUMP and TEDDY GERRARD TRENCH between 4.00 P.M. and 6.00 P.M.

### 9/2/18 — Field

> Hostile artillery showed a marked increase in activity, especially on Back areas. Hostile M.G's. were active during the day against our aeroplanes, and at night with harassing fire. Our own planes carried out indirect fire when in the vicinity of T.17.c.80.00 on to enemy line in Mericourt front.

### 10/2/18 — Field

> Enemy artillery was very active all day. A number of 5.9's. dropped in the neighborhood of T.19.b.40.30. Enemy M.G's. were active against our aeroplanes. Owing to the day being clear, our planes were active on observation duty.

### 11/2/18 — Field

> Our aircraft were active on artillery work only.
> 1 gun "B" section fired 10000 rounds on target at T.12.d.00.70
> There was some artillery activity on the LOOS sector about 7.00 P.M.
> In accordance with 2nd Cdn. Divl. M.G. Battl. Operation Order No. 75 para. I, sub-para. "b" the 5th Cdn. M.G. Coy. relieved the 6th Cdn. M.G. Coy. in the Forward area of the MERICOURT section. The relief was complete at 8.00 P.M.

### 12/2/18 — Field

> Enemy artillery active in morning. A number of 5.9's. and shells of smaller calibre were dropped near "B" Section H.Q. in the Quarries.
> French Mortars were active during day at T.17.d.45.50.
> Our artillery was active in evening, growing in intensity of fire until 11.00 P.M. The same rate of fire was continued until 2.00 A.M. 13th, when the Division on our left carried out a Minor operation.
> 1 gun "C" section fired 3000 rounds on target at T.5c.90.50 between the hours of 10.00 P.M. and 11.00 P.M. 12th, and 2 A.M. to 2.30 A.M. 13th.
> 1 gun "B" section fired 10000 rounds on target at T.12a.00.70. between the hours of 6.00 P.M. and 12.00 midnight 12th, and 4.30 A.M. to 6.00 A.M. 13th.

### 13/2/18 — Field

> Enemy artillery was normal throughout day, 14 "4.1S." falling along narrow gauge railway between C4 and C5 positions, and a few rounds at the Quarries. Heavy T.M's. were active along the whole front throughout the night. Enemy M.G's. were very active between 6.30 P.M. and 9.30 P.M. on left and extreme right of frontage.
> Our M.G's. fired as follows:—
> 10000 rounds on T.13.d.25.80. between 6.00 P.M. and 12.00 midnight 13th/14th. 3000 rounds on T.11.b.75.05. between 9.05 P.M. and 10.05 P.M. 1100 rounds on H.34.d.45.15. between 6.45 P.M. and 5.00 A.M. 14th. At 5.40 A.M. a minor operation was carried out on the front of the Division on our left.

### 14/2/18 — Field

> Enemy artillery was normal throughout day and night. Enemy T.M's. normal, firing a number of bombs to our wire and front line. Enemy M.G's. were very active, sweeping the forward area until midnight.
> Our artillery was active as usual in harassing fire.
> Our M.G's. fired 30000 rounds on the following targets:— T.6c.65.40 and T.5b.80.25.
> Owing to low visibility there was no activity in the air.

### 15/2/18 — Field

> Weather ... frosty with a slight haze. Moonlight at night.
> Enemy artillery active along LENS-ARRAS railroad.
> Enemy M.G's. active during the first part of night on forward tracks and wire. Our M.G's. fired 26000 rounds on enemy targets. No air activity.

### 16/2/18 — Field

> Enemy artillery was normally active, sending 20 shells to junction of BETTY and KEANE trenches. Enemy T.M's. were active on the left and his M.G's. were active on the area between the front and support trenches during the early part of the night. Enemy aircraft were very active in the morning.
> Our aircraft were very active throughout the day, bringing down an enemy plane in front of AVION, and forcing his captive balloons down several times.
> Our M.G's. fired 15000 rounds on enemy targets and 750 rounds at hostile aircraft.

> 5TH CANADIAN MACHINE GUN COMPANY.

### 23. SOLDIERS RETURN FROM LEAVE DEFICIENT OF REGULATION BOOTS.

> It has been brought to notice that soldiers arriving in England on furlough are in the habit of buying civilian boots, leaving behind in the shops the regulation boots which they discard as no longer of use.
> This action is wasteful and represents a loss to the public, and steps will at once be taken to put a stop to this practice.
> Every soldier returning to this country from leave will, on rejoining his unit, be required to produce his regulation boots.
> If not forthcoming, disciplinary action will be taken and the value of the deficient boots charged for. (G.R.O. 3224)

### 24. WATER SUPPLY.

> A large amount of water is being wasted especially from pipe lines in forward areas. Water which is being pumped is intended for drinking, cooking and horse watering only.
> No water from piped supply is to be used for washing purposes, or for washing vehicles. (Auth. D.R.O. 4008)

### 25. REMOVAL OF BLANKETS FROM LEAVE TRAINS.

> It has been brought to notice that many blankets are missing from Leave Trains at Termini in Army Areas.
> These blankets are placed in the trains for the comfort of men proceeding on and returning from leave and should be left in the trains at the completion of the journey. (D.R.O. 4011)

### 26. FUEL.

> Attention is drawn to the to the danger of using braziers, burning coke and charcoal, or stoves without chimneys, in covered places where there is no proper ventilation.
> In view of the number of dugouts, cellars and other closed shelters which will be occupied by the troops this winter, it is important that the attention of all concerned should be drawn to the risk involved.
> Stoves with piping will be provided with by the R.E. where Medical Officers consider the use of braziers undesirable. (Auth. 1st Army. 2351)

### 27. DAMAGE TO CROPS.

> The attention of all ranks is again directed to the need for care and avoiding damage to newly sown field and young crops. Officers and men off duty whether on foot or on horseback are forbidden to leave recognised roads and paths to take short cuts across tilled fields. Games are not to be played in grass fields unless permission has been received from, or some arrangement has been made with the farmer to whom the field belongs.
> Troops on duty or undergoing training must take the greatest care to avoid crossing newly sown land and young crops. If, for the success of the manoeuvres or training, it is essential, arrangements must be made by the officer in command of the troops who do the damage, to report his action to the G.O.C. formation, who will at once take steps to recompense the farmer whose property has been damaged.
> "This order does not in any way affect the movement of troops in the face of the enemy." (Auth. G.R.O. 1639.)

### 30. HONORS AND REWARDS.

### 31. DISCIPLINE

> It has been reported that in some cases timber and other material is being removed from gun positions which are not occupied.
> Material must not be removed on any account from gun positions without authority from 2nd Canadian Division.
>
> (Authority: G.1/294 4/2/17 2nd Cdn. Div.)

### 32. DRESS OF WARRANT OFFICERS.

> The practice of permitting Warrant Officers to wear Service Dress of the pattern authorized for officers is contrary to regulations and steps must be taken to prevent a continuance of this irregularity.
>
> (Authority: G.R.O. 3344.)

### 33. DISCIPLINE—DRINK.

> (i) Soldiers are forbidden to buy or accept as gifts from inhabitants or any other persons (including soldiers), any alcoholic drinks.
> (ii) Soldiers are forbidden to have alcoholic drinks or alcohol in their possession.
> (iii) Wine and beer only may be purchased in Canteens, Estaminets, Cafes or Cabarets, but such wine or beer must be consumed upon the premises were purchased.
> (iv) Soldier are forbidden to have rum in their possession. The rum issue to be consumed by the soldier at the time of issue in the presence of the officer.
> (v) The above orders do not apply to beer and wine kept at an consumed on premises used as duly authorized messes.
>
> (Authority: 1st Army R.O. 2359.)

Signed: "F.L. Much, Lieut., for Captain, Officer Commanding, 5th Cdn. M.G. Coy."

(No. 179254, posted "A" to "B" Section); Pte. Caldwell L. (No. 69194, made Acting Corporal 22/2, then Acting Sergeant 27/2); Pte. Allison L. (No. 161045, made Acting Lance Corporal 24/2); Pte. - **Equipment:** Vickers machine guns; anti-aircraft machine guns (500 rounds fired 2 Feb, 500 rounds 3 Feb, 10,000 rounds on ground targets 11 Feb); small box respirators and gas helmets (frequent gas drill, respirator marking and eye-piece inspection orders); limbers and belt boxes; revolvers and rifles (drill 25/2).
- **Movements:** In the line in the forward area of the Mericourt sector 1–18 February; the company relieved the 6th Cdn. M.G. Coy. there on the night of 11th/12th February (Operation Order No. 4), and was itself relieved by the 8th Cdn. M.G. Coy. on the night of 18th/19th February (Operation Order No. 5), moving by train to billets in Carency. 19–28 February was spent in training at Carency/Souchez, including reconnaissance of the Lievin sector gun positions (20–21 Feb) and instruction at the 2nd Cdn. Divl. M.G. Bn. School at Souchez.
- **Discipline:** Orders cover drink (no alcohol in possession, rum to be consumed at issue), dress (Warrant Officers not to wear officers' Service Dress; men not regimentally dressed on parade), removal of timber/material from unoccupied gun positions, damage to crops, and the order to fire at once on enemy troops attempting fraternization (28, 19/2/18). No company disciplinary cases are recorded in the diary text itself.
- **Casualties:** None recorded for the company in February 1918. The 22/2/18 orders note a gas attack near Lens in which over 2,000 gas shells and 300 projectors were fired and fewer than 40 casualties, mainly slight, were incurred — this is reported as an Army-level appreciation, not a company casualty return.
