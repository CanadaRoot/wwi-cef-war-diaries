## What the diary is

The diary of a Vickers machine-gun company of the 2nd Canadian Division. It opens in January 1916 as a training record at Locre/Westoutre (instructional firing at Scherpenberg, horse lines, limbers), then follows the company into the line: Mount Sorrel and the St Eloi craters (spring 1916), the Somme (Courcelette, September–October 1916 — the company's bloodiest month), Vimy Ridge and the Arleux/Fresnoy fighting (April–May 1917, including the 3 May attack south of Acheville), Hill 70 and Lens (August 1917), Passchendaele (November 1917), and the winter in the Chaudiere sector at Vimy (December 1917 – March 1918), ending with the reorganization into the 2nd Battalion, Canadian Machine Gun Corps in March 1918. O.C.s: Lt S. W. Watson (Dec 1915 – 1916), Major Watson (1917), Capt. J. McCahill (to Nov 1917), Capt. J. E. McCorkell (Dec 1917 – Mar 1918). The diary mixes handwritten Army Form C.2118 sheets with typed sheets and long typed appendices (operation orders, daily routine orders, reports, medal citations, training syllabuses).

- **1916:** none recorded Jan–Aug. **September 1916 (Somme):** 15 Sep — Lt W. A. Duthie killed, Lt H. B. Sinclair and 3 O.R. wounded; 16 Sep — a sergeant killed, five casualties in Sgt Hobson's crews; 15–18 Sep tour totals 2 officers and 16 O.R. (1 officer and 3 O.R. killed; 1 officer and 10 O.R. wounded; 2 O.R. missing [uncertain] wounded; 1 O.R. missing); 28 Sep — 2 killed, 2 wounded; 29 Sep — 7 O.R. killed, 5 wounded/shell-shocked; 30 Sep — 2 O.R. wounded. **October 1916:** 1 Oct — Sgt Ross killed in Sudbury Trench, Lt Campbell shell-shocked, two of his gun crew killed and a third missing.
- **1917:** 5 Mar — 2 O.R. seriously wounded, Pte West died of wounds 6 Mar; 21 Mar — 1 O.R. killed at Neuville St. Vaast. **April (Vimy):** 5 Apr — 1 killed, 2 wounded; 9 Apr — 1 killed, 6 wounded; 11 Apr — 1 killed, 1 wounded; 13 Apr — 1 killed, 3 wounded; 28 Apr — 2 killed, 1 wounded when a "B" Section gun was buried by the counter-barrage. **May:** 1 May — Sgt F. G. Hopkins and Pte Apt killed; 3 May — Pte D. W. Graham killed, 2 O.R. wounded (one died at a Casualty Clearing Station); 14 May — 2 O.R. of "D" Section killed; 20 May — 1 O.R. of "B" Section killed. June: none. **July (Lens):** 3 Jul — Lance Sergt. Clark killed, Lt B. A. Taylor seriously wounded (died of wounds 4 Jul), 1 O.R. of "D" Section killed, 2 wounded; 7 Jul — Pte Pettit died of wounds; 21 Jul — 1 killed, 1 died of wounds, 2 wounded; 23 Jul — Lt D. H. Rose wounded. **August (Hill 70):** 15 Aug — 3 O.R. killed, 7 wounded; 16 Aug — 2 wounded; 18 and 21 Aug — 3 wounded (gas) each day. September: none in the diary text. October: one sentry trivially wounded in the foot (ricochet, 7 Oct). **November (Passchendaele):** 14 killed in total (3 Nov — 2; 5 Nov — 1 died of wounds; 6 Nov — 3; 7 Nov — 1; 8 Nov — 2; 9 Nov — 3; 30 Nov — 2 unnamed), including attached carriers Pte Douville G. and Pte Davies T.E. ## Notable items

- 3 May 1917: the 6th Brigade attacked south of Acheville at 3.45 a.m.; the company's "C" and "D" Sections worked as "7 Battery" on barrage work; a counter-attack expected at 8.15 a.m. came to naught when the guns opened up.
- 9 April 1917 (Vimy): the company's sections formed "F.1" Battery and other batteries under Major Watson's command of the barrage guns.
- 29 August 1917: the four companies of the division formed into the 2nd Canadian Divisional Machine Gun Battalion under Major Goodfellow; the company moved to billets at Camblain l'Abbé.
- 19 December 1917: relieved in the Chaudiere sector by the 10th and 16th Cdn. M.G. Coys. under Operation Order No. 58 and marched back to billets at Aux Rietz; the December appendix preserves the full relief order and seven Daily Routine Orders (22–31 Dec).
- 23 March 1918: the 5th and 14th Cdn. M.G. Companies amalgamated to form No. 1 Coy., 2nd Cdn. M.G. Battalion (Lieut. G. Hobson M.C. taking "A" Battery, Lieut. H. A. McBurney "B" Battery), followed by the rapid move south during the German spring offensive (Villers au Bois → Ecourie → Berles au Bois → Ficheux/Mercatel, 24–29 Mar).
- The 1 March 1918 orders reproduce a Field General Court Martial death sentence (by shooting) on Pte. T. Hopkins, Lancashire Fusiliers, carried out 15 February 1918.
- The diary's own arithmetic often does not reconcile (e.g. S.A.A. totals vs itemised rounds, December 1917; gun counts, October 1916) — transcribed as written, discrepancies noted in the transcripts.

## Uncertain / open items

- 523 occurrences of `[uncertain]` across the 27 transcripts (including the standard conventions line and Notes references; roughly 470 in the diary text itself). Heaviest: July 1917 (87), November 1917 (51), September 1916 (50), May 1917 (41).
- The guide ("Guide to Sources Relating to Units of the CEF — Machine Gun Units") cites the diary as RG 9 III-D-3, **vol. 4982, folder 604**; the LAC record page for the diary with the exact title and date range gives **vol. 4983, file 605**. The record page governs; the guide reference is noted here as a discrepancy.
- 3 May 1917 has two diary rows (a short fragment row and the main entry, both dated 3/5/17 on separate sheets) — both transcribed and indexed as separate entries.
- 13 May 1917 also has a "[continued]" row; it is the continuation of the same entry split across pages and is indexed once.
- The 1 October 1916 entry breaks off mid-sentence ("Sgt Ross. 'A' Section was killed while with his guns in SUDBURY TRENCH in") and is never completed on the next sheet.
- Operation Order No. 6 (May 1917) is dated "May 18th. 1915." as typed; Appendix 3 (April 1917) is dated "April 22nd 1914" — both evident typist's errors for 1917.
- The December 1917 Daily Routine Orders jump in numbering (833 → 836; 68 → 77; 80 → 90); orders 834–835 and others are not in the appendix. Two separate Routine Orders are dated 31 December 1917 (orders 844–846 and 847–848).
- The diary spells the rest camp "AUX REITZ" and "AUX RIETZ" interchangeably, and the sector "LIEVEN" in July 1917 while the operation orders spell it "Lens"; the December sector is "CHAUDIERE" (confirming November's "CHAUDEE" [uncertain]).
- The 29 December 1917 guard-stick entry gives Pte. Anderson's initials as "J.A." while the typed Routine Orders of 28 December give the same number (1000859) as "Anderson A.H."; transcribed as written in each place.
