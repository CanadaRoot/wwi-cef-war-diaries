# 3rd Canadian Infantry Works Company — War Diary

## Unit

3rd Canadian Infantry Works Company (3rd Can. Inf. Works Coy).

## Source

- Library and Archives Canada, CEF War Diaries, RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5008, reel T-10856, file 710, part 1
- LAC Collection Search Item ID 2004932
- Finding aid 9-52

## Coverage

- **Date range:** 14 September 1918 – 15 May 1919
- **Pages:** 51 (LAC image numbers 0001–0051: covers, title pages and diary sheets)
- **Monthly transcripts:** 9 (`transcript-1918-09.md` through `transcript-1919-05.md`)
- **Index entries added to `INDEX-D.md`:** 238

## Key content

- **Formation, 14 September 1918:** the Company came into existence at midnight 13/14 September on the breaking up of the 1st Canadian Infantry Works Battalion, formed from its Nos 3 & 4 Companies, with 5 officers (1 attached) and 397 other ranks under Capt W. J. Chandler.
- **Road work in France, September–October 1918:** employed by Royal Engineers road construction companies (317th, 314th, 319th, 321st R.C.C. R.E.) on roads around Fontaine, Croisilles (written "Crinville"), Cagnicourt, Inchy, Bucquoy, Marquion, Dury, Arleux and Abscon; stone-dump work at Croisilles and Marquion, the latter repeatedly shelled in early October.
- **Armistice:** 11 November 1918 is an ordinary working day in the diary — road work, a change of supervising R.E. unit — with no mention of the Armistice itself.
- **March into Belgium, November 1918:** the Company stood by at Abscon 14–19 November, then marched north 20–27 November: Rouvignies – St Saulve – Onnaing – Hornu – St Symphorien – Binche – Namur, ending the month clearing out the Cadet School at Namur/Jambes.
- **Germany, December 1918:** by train to Stavelot (5–6 Dec), route march to Weywertz, crossing into Germany 10 December, then Euskirchen (11 Dec) and Godesberg (16 Dec); Christmas Day 1918 was spent on a troop train back to France, arriving at Aubin St Vaast on 26 December after the train overshot to Camiers.
- **Death of the Commanding Officer:** Capt G. E. Jack fell sick 8–9 December, was sent to hospital at Spa, and died there on 22 December 1918 of broncho-pneumonia. Lt J. Bell took acting command on 9 December; Capt J. J. Chandler took over command on 28 December.
- **Demobilization, January–May 1919:** the Company waited at Aubin St Vaast, sending 275 O.Rs to Etaples on 8 January, then was rebuilt with drafts from the 1st and 2nd Divisional wings for burial duties; in February it worked at the 3rd Canadian Horse Demobilization Depot at Rennix (Ronse), Belgium, handling hundreds of horses; in March–April it was dispersed on guard, horse-conducting, ordnance and convoy duties around Huy, Andenne, Namur, Verviers and Auvelais; it left Andenne for Etaples on 7 May 1919 and was completely demobilized there on 15 May 1919, the final diary entry.

## Casualties summary

Very light, for a labour unit behind the lines. No men were killed in action:

- **1 O.R. found dead:** 690957 Pte [uncertain] (surname illegible), found dead on Rue Dave, Jambes, 1 December 1918; a Court of Inquiry was held 2 December and the body was taken away by the Casualty Clearing Station. The cause of death is not recorded in the diary.
- **1 officer died of disease:** Capt G. E. Jack, died in hospital at Spa, 22 December 1918 (broncho-pneumonia).
- **Accidental injuries:** Pte [Puleford? uncertain] (441925) accidentally burned, 3 October 1918, sent to a Field Ambulance; Pte E. E. Reynolds accidentally injured unloading a train of ammunition at Somain, 1 November 1918.
- **Sickness:** Lt J.E. Taylor to hospital with chest trouble, 4 February 1919; two cases of mumps at Rennix in February 1919 (one case led to 52 O.Rs being quarantined); 45 O.Rs evacuated to 66 Field Ambulance on 21 November 1918, unable to stand the march.
- **Shelling without Company casualties:** parties working at the Marquion stone dump were shelled on 2, 7 and 8 October 1918, and shells fell close to the camp on 27 September 1918 — no casualties in the Company on any occasion. Pte Dawson (273625, stretcher bearer) and another private were commended on 7 October for carrying wounded men of other units at the Marquion dump.

## Uncertain readings and gaps

- The handwriting is cursive and often cramped, especially in September–October 1918; words that could not be read with confidence are marked [uncertain] in the transcripts and carried through into the index. Uncertain flags were never resolved into certain readings.
- Place-name spellings follow the diarist: Croisilles is written "Crinville"/"Crinilles", Rennix for Renaix/Ronse, and "Godesburg" for Godesberg.
- The officer named Lt F. N. Buchan on 4 November 1918 is written Lt J. N. Buchan on 10 November; the officer written Lt F. N. Lang on 16 December 1918 is written Lt Lange on 29 December — both readings are transcribed as written.
- The 11 November 1918 entry is written "319th I.C.C. R.E." taking over from "307th I.C.C. R.E.s"; the abbreviation is transcribed as written.
- The 13 October 1918 entry is partly cut off at the foot of the sheet in the scan; the 3 December 1918 entry is very faint and partly legible; the 21 January 1919 entry begins "Sunday" although 20 January 1919 was a Monday (19 January was the Sunday) — transcribed as written.
- The May 1919 title page reads "From 1st May to 31st May, 1918" — the year 1918 and the 31st are errors in the original; the diary text is dated May 1919 and ends 15 May 1919.
- The diary begins 14 September 1918 because the Company did not exist before then, and ends 15 May 1919 with demobilization; there are no missing days within that range. No appendices accompany the diary in the file.
