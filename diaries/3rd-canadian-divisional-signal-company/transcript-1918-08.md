# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company — August 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company, C.E.
- **Month:** August 1918 — Volume 33 (this volume covers 1–31 August 1918)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004905 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5005, microfilm reel T-10853, file 692; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** August 1918, p. 1 – p. 9 (image files page-0228 – page-0236 of the 307-page diary)
- **August 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–9 of 307): cover, contents, manuscript title page, diary text 1–31 August, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-26.
- **Note on the 11 August gap:** there is no entry for 11 August — the diary jumps from 10 August (page 6) to 12 August. The 11th is not recorded anywhere in this volume.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Typewritten diary text throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. "[word struck out]" marks words the typist struck out with x's or a heavy line — the struck words are not readable. No map coordinates are given in the diary; places are recorded as written. "C.I." = Canadian Infantry; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "Bde." = Brigade; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "O.R." = Other Rank; "D.R." = Despatch Rider; "D.R.L.S." = Despatch Rider Letter Service; "G." Staff = General Staff; "E.C." = a test point / cable head, as written; "CYCR" = the Advanced Signal Office, as written. A repeated handwritten mark in the Remarks column, read as "K.M.C." [uncertain] — apparently the O.C.'s initials (cf. the signature "K.M. Campbell" at 31 August) — appears against most entries; it is noted here rather than repeated against every entry. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets reads "3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Co." (one sheet reads "3rd Cdn. Div'l. Signal Co'y.").

## Pages 4–9 — Diary text (1–31 August 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". Heading on each sheet: "3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Co."

### Page 4 — 1–7 August 1918

> **Hornoy, 1st** — Arrived at Hornoy at 9.30 p.m. from SALEUX, where we detrained. Hornoy is about 33 Kilometres from AMIENS, S.W. Office opened in Barn. Divisional Artillery at PONT de METZ, and moved to DURY. 9th and 10th Bdes. CFA., at BACOUEL and VERS. All Artillery moves taking place after dusk. Infantry Bdes in HEBECOURT AREA. Resting after many moves.
>
> **2nd** — Still at Hornoy orders received to move to Dury by lorry on the 3rd instant. This is the first time Canadians have ever been in this area. Rained heavily all day. No change in Artillery and Infantry Bde dispositions.
>
> **Dury, 3rd, 10.30am** — Arrived at Dury; Area lately occupied by a French Division. Office located in quarry dugouts on outskirts of town. Raining very hard all day. No change in Infantry Dispositions. Artillery Bdes move to Bois de Boves in Vicinity of the village of the same name.
>
> **4th** — Continuous rain. Infantry Bdes in St.FUCIEN and DURY areas. Rifle Inspections and Gas mask inspections to-day, and equipment generally overhauled.
>
> **5th** — Tanks proceeding towards the line in the rear areas break down all lines of communication to Corps and Army; Lines down all night. Infantry Brigade Officers reconnoitre new front in view of pending operations. Cable Wagons go forward under Lieut. Gibson, to lay some lines in the area to be taken up by Divisional Headquarters in a few days, (BOVES). Lines pitched along Paris—Amiens Railway embankment.
>
> **6th** — Major and 2nd in Command reconnoitre new front. Units now under Cover of woods in communication by wire. Date of pending Operations not yet made public.
>
> **Boves, 7th** — Brigade Section inspected by O.C., Major Leavitt, M.C., and Lieut. Auld, proceeded to Quarries in DOMART on the River Luce at 6.00 p.m. with Office relief and linemen preparatory to Operations commencing to-morrow, zero hour not yet known. After reaching the Barrier on the Amiens—Roye Road and walking in advance of party, an enemy shell burst on right side of road instantly killing Lt. Auld and wounding Major Leavitt who was immediately carried to the nearest Aid Post, in the Gentelles Woods. Command of Company passed to Capt. J.H.Leeson. A visual scheme arranged to cover Operations commencing 8th. 1 Visual Signaller detailed by Divisional Signal Company attached to each infantry Bde. This Signaller to know the

### Page 5 — 7 (continued) – 10 August 1918

> **Boves (7th, continued)** — the location of Divisional receiving station, and approximate locations of stations to be established forward of that point if the assault should prove successful. The Divisional Signal receiving station is located for the commencement of the Battle about 100 yards N.E., of Advanced Divisional Headquarters in the Quarries at DOMART. It is the duty of this station to keep in touch with Brigades as they move forward in the attack. Everything placed in readiness for the commencement of the show.
>
> **Domart, 8th, 4.20 a.m.** — Battle commenced with heavy, short bombardment, many tanks and Aeroplanes. Telephonic communication was lost with brigades from the commencement. Attack pressed forward very rapidly and telephonic communication was out of the question, owing to the heavy traffic of Field Artillery, supply tanks, [uncertain — "etc."?] Visual Signalling became effective after the settling of the dust and the clearing of heavy smoke. Brigade and Divisional G. Staffs went forward and many different positions were taken up, and communication for the first part of the battle was by means of Motor Cycle despatch riders, and a troop of Canadian Light Horse. Cable wagons followed brigades where possible and laid lines but again the heavy traffic on the roads and cross country tracks in the enemy territory made the maintenance of them next to impossible. Towards evening with Divisional Headquarters and Brigade Headquarters grouped in Valley Wood good communication by telephone [word struck out] was maintained. Later in the evening Divisional and Brigade G. Staffs moved to Hamel Wood. Motor Cycle despatch riders used to carry dispatches to the rear. Casualties for the day 2 O.Rs. Brigades report good communication to forward Battalion Headquarters at all times by visual and Loop sets. Capt. K.M. Campbell takes over Command.
>
> **Valley Wood, 9th** — Infantry still going forward and Divisional Headquarters advancing in the direction of Beaucourt. Motor Cyclists working very hard but giving an adequate service to all Units. Telephone communication has been estblished for passed 12 hours to Brigades and ix lines [uncertain — "six"?] are used entirely by "G" Staff for conversations, leaving us with Motor Cyclists to deliver Administrative dispatches and Operation Orders. Headquarters taken up for the night in Beaucourt Chateau. Casualties Nil.
>
> **LeQuesnel, 10th** — Headquarters opened at Le Quesnel at 9.a.m. captured from the enemy at noon-day 9th instant. Advanced Signals 1,000 yards East of Village in French cement pill-box, rear Office in Basement of Chateau, which is badly damaged. The courtyard of Chateau Grounds is a big "RED-CROSS" indicating that the building was used by the British Troops as a military Hospital

### Page 6 — 10 (continued) – 18 August 1918

> **LeQuesnel (10th, continued)** — before the area was evacuated in the operations of March of this year. 7th and 9th C.I. Bdes., were relieved by two Brigades of the 32nd Imperial Division. Considerable enemy aircraft over the rear areas last night, bombing. Casualties 4 horses by [word struck out] enemy bombs and 1 horse by enemy shell fire. [word struck out] Lieut. J.D. Baker reported for duty to fill vacancy caused by the death of Lieut. R.W. Auld [uncertain — initials appear overstruck]. Lt. Baker took No. 1, detachment forward to lay line to 9th Bde, in vicinity of LeFOLIES Lines reported OK at 11.30 p.m. All lines forward and rear now in good shape providing adequate communications.
>
> **12th** — 3rd Canadian Division now in Corps reserve. Everything running satisfactorily. Salvaging German Signalling Equipment; Switch boards, telephones etc. Wire drums and disused wire. Considerable enemy bombing last night causing damage to lines.
>
> **13th** — Ideal weather has prevailed since commencement of operations. No change in dispositions. Good communication to All Brigades and Brigades report that all Battalions in front line linked up with telephones. 7th Brigade Section made experiments with German Homing Pigeons captured near the Village of [uncertain — word struck out, appears to read "LeFOLIES"] The experiments were quite successful, the birds Homing in Good time.
>
> **14th** — Parvillers Captured [the Village of Parvill struck out] by the 49th, 42nd and PPCLI Bn's. Lines laid to the advanced Headquarters of these Battalions and communication good. Weather extremely hot and roads very dusty.
>
> **15th** — Orders received to close at Le Quesnel on 16th and open at Beaucourt same day at 10 a.m. 7th C.I. Brigade relieved by the 3rd C. I. Bde., after relief 7th C.I. Bde., marched to Valley Wood, accupying old German dugouts.
>
> **Beaucourt, 16th** — Division opened at Beaucourt Chateau 10 a.m. 12 noon communication good to all Units, traffic on time. 2 pairs strung on old 5th Army Air Line route from Beaucourt to Le Quesnel;
>
> **17th** — Inspections of Rifles, Small Box Respirators and Harness and Equipment.
>
> **18th** — Captured German Signal material shipped to rear until such times as tactical situation permits thorough experiments.

### Page 7 — 19–25 August 1918

> **Beaucourt, 19th** — Move orders received. Dismounted party moved off at 6.10 p.m. under Lieut J.D. Baker to march to BOVES station on Paris—Amiens Railway, which was disused until the successful Canadian advance on 8th August. BOVES reached at 10—10 p.m. distance 19 kilometers. Train left at 4.35 a.m. cloudy night and heavy traffic to station and on platforms unhindered by enemy bombs on that account. Transport left at 7.30 p.m. by march route for REBREUVE in 3rd Army area. Lieut. P.S.McPherson with 1 lines Sergeant and 1 Office Supt. left by light car to prepare communications at REBREUVE. Train party detrained at BOUQUEMAISON at 10.10 a.m. 20th. 7th C.I.Brigade paraded for March past before M. Clemenceau, Field Marshall Sir Douglas Haig and Sir [word struck out] Henry Rawlinson. 7th Brigade Signal Section complimented by Brigadier Dyer on smart appearance and good march discipline.
>
> **Rebreuve, 20th** — Headquarters opened at this point at 10 a.m; and closed same hour at Beaucourt; Train party commenced march from BOUQUEMAISON at 12.30 p.m. arriving REBREUVE at 3.30 p.m. men in good shape after long march and tedious train journey. 1 Sounder line and 1 telephone line to 6th Corps, reaching 7th and 9th Brigades through 6th Corps exchange, communication to 8th Brigade by D.R.
>
> **la Cauroy, 21st** — Excessively hot, men bathing in the Canche River, which is a fast flowing stream and very refreshing after long dusty marches. Transport and dismounted party move off at 9.00 p.m. and march to La Cauroy. Office opened in Chateau at 10 p.m. communication to all Units.
>
> **22nd** — Muster parade. Inspection of Horses and Equipment; Rifles and Small Box Respirators.
>
> **23rd** — Excessively hot; Cleaning and overhauling Cable Wagons. Warning order to move into line. Dispatch Rider Weight severely injured in collision with 3 Ton Lorry while carrying special dispatches to Arras;
>
> **Duisans, 24th** — Headquarters opened at Duisans at 10 a.m. Transport and dismounted party march off at 7.30 a.m. arrived Duisans at 12.10 p.m. Occupying quarters just vacated by the XVII Corps.
>
> **25th** — Party went forward under Lieut Pryde and opened Battle Headquarters at 6.p.m. at G 29 central in LONDON CAVE, on the Arras—Cambrai Road

### Page 8 — 26–27 August 1918

> **London Cave, Advanced HQs, Arras, 26th** — 3rd Canadian Division attacked at 3.00 a.m. 8th Bde. attacking. 8th C. I. Bde. opened Advanced HQ in H. 25. b at 10.00 p.m. 25th instant. Lines to this HQ over buried system arranged [word struck out] from Division HQ at G. 29 Central to this Brigade via LA Cable head at H. 25 b. 8.4. Also pairs arranged in bury to cable head E.C. at H. 31. b. 4.1. Visual and Wireless also to 8th C. I. B. A party of 8th C. I. Brigade Signals went over soon after first wave and established a Report Centre at H. 34. d. (ORANGE HILL) taking a line with them. Shortly afterwards a party of Divisional linemen started to duplicate this line but were seriously handicapped in getting [word struck out] heavy cable over such rough country and did not arrive at ORANGE HILL until about 2.00 p.m. Another line laid from E.C. test point to 8th C. I. Bde, the 7th C. I. Bde had established at 10.00 p.m. at HQ near that of the 8th C. I. Bde on ORANGE HILL at H.34.b. and a cable wagon left Division at 10.00 a.m. to lay line via the ARRAS—CAMBRIA Road to them. Line OK at 3.00 p.m. Cpl. Ryder of this detachment did excellent work in laying this line, in spite of extreme difficulties, shell fire, rough country etc. 1 man wounded, and 1 horse wounded. Visual, Wireless, Loop sets and pigeons were all used quite satisfactorily, although the main bulk of traffic fell to the line telegraphy branch. It has been found that pigeons are useless after a rapid advance such as is the type of warfare now in existence. The Troops and Headquarters of staffs move so rapidly that they soon become out of reach of the pigeon service.
>
> **Rear Divisional HQs, Duisans, 27th** — A party of 50 from the P. P. C. L. I. 7th C. I. Bde under Lieut. Burgess, of the Canadian Corps with a party of the Sappers of this Company commenced at 8.00 a.m. to reclaim old bury system from E.C. to MONCY—LE—PREUX [uncertain — "MONCHY"?], every joint (440 yards) had been destroyed by the XVII British Corps before the evacuation of March and these had to be dug up and rejoined. In view of the difficulties encountered in this work, Lieut. Burgess was able to report by night that 4 pairs of this old buried system were in good working condition through to N. 3. b. 1.8. Lieut. R. B. Gibson of this Company with a party of linemen built and poled cable from N.3 b. 8.5. to N. 5. a. 8.6. making a "TEE IN" to the 7th C. I. Bde from N. 5. a. 2.6. These lines were connected into buried system at N. 3. b. 1.8 and route followed the old buried system mentioned above, the intention being to replace the poled cable built by Lieut Gibson by the bury as the latter was repaired and made good. A great [uncertain — "deal" struck out?] of trouble was experienced in maintaining the lines from cable heads to ORANGE HILL during the night of the 26/27th. So Lieut. H. Pryde was ordered out to repair and improve the lines. The work was very successful and by daylight the faulty lines had been greatly improved. 9th C. I. Bde occupying Hqs with 7th C. I. Bde. on ORANGE HILL night 27/28th.

### Page 9 — 27 (continued) – 31 August 1918

> **Advanced Headquarters, London Cave, Arras (27th, continued)** — Much good work was done by the Brigade sections during the 26th and 27th period, laying lines to their Battalions and keeping up communication, also visual to Battalions was carried out where the nature of the country would permit.
>
> **28th** — Notification was received at 6.00 a.m. that 8th and 9th C. I. Brigades were moving HQs to O. 7. b. 3.4. and O. 8. c. 3.5. respectively. One cable detachment was ordered forward from CYCR, (Advanced Signal Office), at 7.30 a.m. to lay a pair from EC testpoint along ARRAS—CAMBRIA Road to them. Shortly afterwards Lieut. Gibson left to continue his poled cable line from N5 a. 8.6. to these HQs. The latter complete at 10.00 a.m. Owing to the extreme roughness of the Country, and heavy transport movements and enemy shelling it has been found that Poled Cable where possible, and where sufficient time can be gained to permit this type of communication being built, that it gives much better service and warrants the extra work as compared with hastily and roughly laid (Over-land lines). A great deal of wire trouble increased the difficulties in communication in the afternoon, and it was not until 5.00 p.m. that this trouble was satisfactorily cleared to permit the disposal of heavily congested administrative business. During this period Visual and Wireless with the assistance of D.R.L.S., service did good work in disposing of all urgent Staff traffic. A party of 4th British Division linemen shown over lines starting at 5.30 a.m. Relieved by the above Division relief complete 6.30 p.m. All company personnel with exception of one Office Relief and Lieut. Pryde returned to rear Headquarters at DUISANS.
>
> **Rear Headquarters, Duisans, 29th** — Rained heavily during the morning. Inspection held in afternoon.
>
> **30th** — An Os. C. [uncertain — "O.C."?], inspection of All equipment held in morning. Holiday for men in afternoon.
>
> **31st** — Pay parade held at 9.30 a. m. Church parade at Corps Church Hut at 10.30 a.m.
>
