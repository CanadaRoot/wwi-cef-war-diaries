# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company, C.E. — February 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company, C.E. (Canadian Engineers)
- **Month:** February 1918 (Volume 27)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004905 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5005, microfilm reel T-10853, file 692; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** February 1918, p. 1 – p. 8 (image files page-0177 – page-0184 of the diary)
- **February 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–8): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–28 February, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-26.
- **Diary covers 5 December 1915 – 17 March 1919.**

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Handwritten diary text throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. No map coordinates are given in the diary; places are recorded as written. The diarist's initials "A.L." appear in the Remarks column against every entry and are not repeated against each entry below; the sheets are signed "A. Leavitt Maj" (O.C.). "H.Q." = Headquarters; "Hqrs" = Headquarters; "Bde" = Brigade; "Div" = Division; "Cdn" = Canadian; "Sigs" = Signals; "S.S.O." = Senior Signal Officer; "G.S.O.2" = General Staff Officer, 2nd grade; "O.P." = Observation Post; "C.C.R.C." = Canadian Corps Reinforcement Camp [uncertain — expansion not given in the diary]; "M.G." = Machine Gun; "O.R." = Other Ranks. "Auchel" is the place written in the Place column for 1–20 February; "Chateau D'Acq" for 21–28 February. "Ferfay" is a village near Auchel.

## Pages 4–8 — Diary text (1–28 February 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". Heading on each sheet: "3rd Canadian Div Sig Coy Cdn Sigs" (variant spellings "Cdn Signals", "Can Signals").

### Page 4 — 1–6 February 1918

> **Auchel, Feb 1st** — Training in all branches of signal work carried on with. Battalion and Battery classes, under the supervision of the Bde Infantry & Artillery Bde Signal Officers, making good progress. Men are keen & taking great interest in the work.
>
> **2nd** — Horse, harness & transport inspection of Hqrs & No 1 section by Maj Gen Lipsett Training continued. Weather fine
>
> **3rd** — Church parade 9.15 A.M. Office & necessary signal routine
>
> **4th** — Cable laying competition between different cable detachments. Lt Gibson posted to C.C.R.C Ferfay for the purpose of supervising the Signal training at the school. Other training continued.
>
> **5th** — Training & usual signal routine. Weather fine
>
> **6th** — Training & usual signal routine. Weather fine
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A. Leavitt Maj*

New B course at Ferfay commenced, this class consists of forty other ranks, who have but a slight knowledge of signalling and is mainly for the purpose of assisting [uncertain — "S. >

### Page 6 — 13–19 February 1918

> **Auchel, Feb 13th** — Training & usual signal office routine
>
> **14** — Training, gas helmet drill & usual routine. Warning order received that 3rd Cdn Div will relieve the 2nd Cdn Div in the line Feb 23rd

### Page 7 — 20–23 February 1918

> **Auchel, Feb 20** — Preparing for move. Weather dull & wet
>
> **Chateau D'Acq, 21** — Transport move off at 8.30 A.M Office closed at Auchel at 10 A.M & opened at new Hqrs Chateau D'Acq at the same hour. Relief completed without a hitch. All communications working well. Transport reaches new Hqrs at 3.30 P.M. Horses in good condition
>
> **22** — Repairing horse standings. Preparations made for ploughing plot of ground allotted. It is intended to plough at least two acres Patrol & maintenance of lines. Communications good. Enemy quiet. Weather fine
>
> **23** — New buried cable route commenced by 2nd Div Sigs taken over & work on same continued. Party of 200 [uncertain] employed. Digging at present very difficult as party are cutting through some old German trenches. Patrol & maintenance of lines & work continued on horse standings. Enemy quiet. Weather fine
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A. Leavitt Maj*

### Page 8 — 24–28 February 1918

> **Chateau D'Acq, Feb 24** — Work of ploughing commenced as per Divisional Agricultural scheme. Work on horse standings & buried cable continued. Selecting a point at which to establish a Central Div Visual Station. Patrol & maintenance of lines Daily business 1726 messages & despatches. Weather fine Enemy quiet
>
> **25** — Ploughing, horse standings & buried cable work continued. Patrol and maintenance of lines. Business for the day 1815 messages & despatches.
>
> **26** — Ground reconnoitred in company with G.S.O.2 to select suitable place for Bde Hqrs in the event of retirement. Central point selected and Div Visual Station established. Wireless station pushed forward to Batt Hqrs, & two Power Buzzer Stations installed at the two flank companies of the left Batt Right Bde. All tested out & working well Days business 1763. Weather fine Enemy quiet
>
> **27** — All work continued. Communications good. Days business 1759. Weather fine
>
> **28** — Finished ploughing & harrowing commenced. Good progress made with buried cable. Patrol & Maintenance of lines Days Business 1761. Weather fine
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A. Leavitt Maj*

## Open questions / uncertain items (February 1918)
- 7 February: "assisting [uncertain — 'S. In'?] Battalion M.G. Coys & Battery Signals" — the two words before "Battalion" are unclear.
- 16 February: "The 3rd Div to have relief completed by 10 A.M. Feb 21st" — read as written; the relief was in fact completed on 21 February (see 21 February entry).
- 19 February: "as 2nd Div Sigs are taking over from the 4th Div Sigs" — read as written.
- 23 February: "Party of 200 [uncertain] employed" — the figure is read as 200; the written figures are looped and could be read otherwise.
- Names read as: Maj Gen Lipsett; Lt Gibson; Lt Col Finlay (S.S.O., 3rd Cdn Div).
- No casualties are recorded in February 1918. The company spent 1–20 February in rest/training at Auchel, then relieved the 2nd Canadian Division's signals in the line at Chateau D'Acq on 21 February. Daily signal traffic at the front ran 1,726–1,815 messages and despatches a day (24–28 February).
