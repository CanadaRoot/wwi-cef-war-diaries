# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company, C.E. — April 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company, C.E. (Canadian Engineers)
- **Month:** April 1918 (Volume 29)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004905 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5005, microfilm reel T-10853, file 692; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** April 1918, p. 1 – p. 10 (image files page-0193 – page-0202 of the diary)
- **April 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–10): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–30 April, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-26.
- **Diary covers 5 December 1915 – 17 March 1919.**

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. The April diary is typewritten (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"), like March. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. No map coordinates are given in the diary except one map reference (M 9.d.6.6., 17 April); places are recorded as written. The diarist's initials "A.L." appear in the Remarks column against every entry and are not repeated against each entry below; the sheets are signed "A. Leavitt Maj" (O.C.). Typewriter conventions: the typewriter has no "1" key, so "1" is typed as "I" (e.g. "IIth" = 11th, "I9" = 19, "34I6" = 3416); "C.I.B." occasionally appears as "CiB" and is transcribed as "C.I.B.". Typos in the original ("attched", "Cheif", "movd", "stsaions", "accomodation", "vis" for "via", "routeing") are kept as typed. "H.Q." = Headquarters; "Hqrs" = Headquarters; "Bde" = Brigade; "Div" = Division; "Cdn" = Canadian; "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "Imp" = Imperial (British); "Sigs" = Signals; "Oi/c" = Officer in charge; "i/c" = in charge; "L.O." = Liaison Officer [uncertain — expansion not given in the diary]; "D.R.L.S." = Despatch Rider Letter Service; "P.B." = Power Buzzer; "Amp." = Amplifier; "S.O.S." = S.O.S. call; "D.T.M." = Divisional Trench Mortar [uncertain — expansion not given in the diary]; "T.M." = Trench Mortar; "O.P." = Observation Post; "C.S.R.D." = Canadian Signal Reinforcement Depot [uncertain — expansion not given in the diary]; "R.C.R." = Royal Canadian Regiment; "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "Bury" = buried cable (as used in this diary); "Fosse" = mine shaft (French coalfield); "Boche" = the Germans (as written). "Div'l H.Q's Chateau D'Acq Adv Corps Line (Fort George)" is the place written in the Place column for 1–11 April; "Adv HQ's Sains-en-Gohelle / Rear HQ's Chateau de la Haie" for 12–30 April.

Office found to be in bad shape and in need of rewiring. All buried lines in leaky condition and none registered. Business 3473 messages 1121 despatches 23 specials.

### Page 5 — 12–15 April 1918

> **12th** — Moved to Chateau de la Haie. Relief of Office at Chateau D'Acq and at Fort George made by 4th Cdn Div'n. Adv HQ's opened at Sains. Former Div'n located at Braquemont but office was not taken over their Artillery sub-office at Sains being converted into our Adv Signal Office. Trouble experienced with air-lines west of Les Brebis and Bully-Grenay. Wireless station established at 8th HQ's and a number of P.B. and Amplifiers installed on Centre and Right Bde fronts. Visual stations dismantled on old front and preparations made to install them on new. Lieut Lee attched from C.S.R.D. for duty with Company. Capt Griffith attched from R.C.R. for instruction proceeded on course at Army Signal School, Dunstable. 8th Bde Section experiencing trouble with bury. Lines registered and water tank which was in close proximity to Signal Office removed, thus reducing danger of induction. Communications somewhat difficult owing to large amount of air-line and enemy shelling in forward area. Reconnaissance made of all lines and bury. Weather good. Business 3260 messages 998 despatches 8 specials. L/Cpl McLaren awarded the M.M. for bravery in maintaining communications during raid by 5th C.M.R. Bn.
>
> **13th** — Lt Lee given charge of Cable Wagons and sections reformed. Arrangements made to complete bury into tunnel at Chateau. Enemy's attack advanced and every preparation made in case of forced retirement. Enemy now within short distance of Bethune. Difficulty still encountered with lines but beginning to improve. Business 3070 messages 1299 despatches 9 specials.
>
> **14th** — Major Leavitt, Capt McCready and Mr McPherson interviewed the Cheif Engineer of Mines, Les Brebis with reference to running cable through mine galleries from Fosse 10 to Fosse 2. Very encouraging interview and promised every assistance. After interview Major Leavitt proceeded to Corps HQ's to consult with A.D. Sigs re proposed bury and arrangements made to go ahead immediately with work. Great trouble in keeping up communications owing to enormous amount of air-line and faults in bury. Most bury in very bad shape. Communications in new area not up to usual standard. Reconnaissance made of new area with view to establishing visual stations at each Bde, owing to poor visibility. Lt Auld O i/c Visual could not establish all points. Cable wagons manned and preparations made to commence training under Lt Lee O i/c Cable Detachments. Usual routine maintained. Office at end of bury in old area closed and all personnel brought to Chateau de la Haie.
>
> **15th** — Arrangements made to have cable and messenger wire brought to head of Fosse 10. Gallery, at point reconnoitred by Capt McCready and Lt McPherson. Owing to a block being put in Gallery cable will have to be put down as soon as possible. Corps to loan 20 men to assist in work. Linesmen distributed at Les Brebis at rear office 8th CiB., also at Braquemont and Bully Grenay. Still struggling with difficulties in air-lines. Cable Dets under Lt Lee out for training and brush up in Cable drill. Reconnaissance of Visual stations completed and communications established from front to Div'l HQ's.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A. Leavitt Maj*

## Open questions / uncertain items (April 1918)
- 2 April appears TWICE in the Date column, with two different entries and two different business figures (2941 messages / 1037 despatches / 21 specials; and 2892 / 985 / 14). The duplicate is transcribed as written; one of the two entries was presumably meant for another date, but the following row is dated 3rd April.
- 6th–9th April are combined in a single entry, with a separate business figure for each day.
- 15 April is split across two pages ("15th" and "15th contd"); 18th and 21st and 24th are likewise continued onto the next page.
- 18 April: "3 pairs on Y route to Left Bde dis-Area Officer dug up all shell holes on route but without locating fault." — the text is garbled as typed; it probably reads "disconnected. Area Officer dug up all shell holes…", but the typed words are given as they stand.
- 19 April: "Germans attacked on nth of left Bde" — "nth" read as typed; presumably "north". "Cable in mines completed with exception of 7C. yds where block is to be built." — "7C." read as typed [uncertain]; it may be a figure such as 70 yards.
- 26 April: "11th Divn completed 30 pair bury from Braquemont to Mazingarbe and 61 pairs allotted to this Divn" — "61 pairs" read as typed; a 30-pair bury cannot supply 61 pairs, so the figure may be a typo for 6 pairs.
- "Mr McPherson" (14 April) and "Lt McPherson" (15 April onwards) are presumably the same man; both forms are kept as written. "Capt McCready" is spelled "McCready" throughout; "Major Cline" is named without initials.
- The place column for 1–11 April reads "Div'l H.Q's Chateau D'Acq Adv Corps Line (Fort George)"; the April 1918 volume is a continuation of the March front (Chateau D'Acq / Fort George) before the move to Sains-en-Gohelle on 12 April.
- Names read as: Lieut Lee; Capt H.F.E. Griffith; L/Cpl McLaren (awarded the M.M., 12 April, for the 15 March raid); Major Leavitt; Capt McCready; Mr/Lt McPherson; Lt Auld; Lt Hartley; Lt McGill; Lt Peebles; Lt Maxey; Major Cline; Major A.D. Signals Cdn Corps (unnamed).
- No casualties are recorded in April 1918. The month's main work was rebuilding the division's buried-cable system in the Lens–Loos coalfield, much of it run through mine galleries (Fosse 2, 5, 6, 10, 11) after taking over a neglected front from the 46th (Imperial) Division on 11 April, and preparing for relief by XVIII Corps (20th & 24th Divisions) on the night of 30 April – 3 May.
