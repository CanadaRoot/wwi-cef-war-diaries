## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. No map coordinates are given in the diary; places are recorded as written. "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "O.Rs." = Other Ranks; "N.C.O." = Non-Commissioned Officer; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade; "D.R.L.S." = Despatch Rider Letter Service; "W/T" = Wireless Telegraphy; "D.R." = Despatch Rider; "C.S.R.D." = Canadian Signal Reinforcement Depot [uncertain — expansion not given in the diary]; "C. L. H." = Canadian Light Horse; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "D.A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "C.R.E." = Commander Royal Engineers; "D.P.O." = Divisional Provost Officer [uncertain — expansion not given in the diary]; "A. D. Signals" = Assistant Director of Signals. A handwritten initialled mark, read as "K.H.M." [uncertain], appears in the Remarks column against most entries; it is noted here rather than repeated against every entry. The signature at 30 November is read as "H. M. Campbell" [uncertain] (Major K. M. Campbell was the O.C. during the month).

> The Company is sending a lorry every second day to BRUSSELS to take the men on sight-seeing excursions to the Capital. A convoy of Divisional lorries leaves here about 0700 hours and the trip can be made in two hours or less.
