- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company

- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004905 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5005, microfilm reel T-10853, file 692; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** September 1916, p. 1 – p. 6 (image files page-0057 – page-0062)
- **September 1916 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–6): cover, contents, a title sheet, diary text 1–30 September, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-26.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Handwritten diary text throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. No map coordinates are given in the diary; places are recorded as written. The diary is in the same hand as August, and the Remarks column initials read as "J.E.P."; they appear against most entries and are noted here rather than repeated against every entry. The diarist's spellings "STEENVOORDE", "Esquelbecq", "Rubempré", "Usna Hill", "Warley" and "Tara Hill" are kept as written. "H.Q." = Headquarters; "Bde." = Brigade; "Infy." = Infantry; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "O.R." = Other Ranks; "Div Arty" = Divisional Artillery.

## Pages 4–6 — Diary text (1–30 September 1916)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The sheets are not headed with the unit name, and are numbered, top right, 3, 4 and 5. Place: STEENVOORDE until 7 September, then Le Ploy, Rubempré, Albert / Usna Hill, and Warley / Tara Hill.

### Page 4 — 1–13 September 1916

> **STEENVOORDE, Sept 1st–5th** — Training of company continued.
>
> **6th** — Visual Stations called on, and all arrangements made for move.
>
> **7th** — Company move off at 7 A.M. for Esquelbecq when it entrained for Contay. Company were entrained at 11.30 A.M. and train pulled out at 1.30 P.M.
>
> **Le Ploy, 8th** — Reached Contay at 1.35 A.M. without mishap. Detrained and marched to Le Ploy reaching there at 4. A.M. Signal Office opened.
>
> **9th, 10th** — Company at rest usual routine carried out.
>
> **Rubempré, 11th** — Company left Le Ploy at 7. A.M. for Rubempré reaching there at 3.15 P.M. Signal Office closed 11 A.M. at Le Ploy and opened same hour at new Headquarters. Cable detachments sent forward to establish communication at Usna Hill.
>
> **Albert, 12th** — Company left Rubempré 7. A.M. for Albert arriving at Albert at 1.15 P.M. Signal Office at New Headquarters Usna Hill opened at 2.30 P.M. Branch Office also opened in Albert.
>
> **13th** — Working to forward brigade on Corps [uncertain — "new"?] buried cable. Cable very faulty and working party from company trying to locate and remedy faults. Shelling heavy and working party somewhat delayed with work. Office with 7th Bde opened at 7. P.M.

### Page 5 — 14–22 September 1916

> **Usna Hill, Sept 14th** — Party working on buried cable route. New line laid for Hqrs of 7th Bde.
>
> **15th** — All available linesmen posted at points along line to ensure that communication are maintained at all cost. Shelling very heavy, communication maintained throughout.
>
> **16th** — Difficulty in maintaining portion of line to 7th Bde, party of seven men on length of 300 yds of overland cable. Enemy shelling very heavy.
>
> **17th** — All available linesmen at work on buried cable and maintaining overland routes.
>
> **18th** — One cable detachment posted for duty with Lahore Artillery. New lines laid from Lahore Div Arty Hqrs to two forward Brigades. Communications good. Enemy shelling fairly heavy. Constant wires cut repeatedly by shell fire.
>
> **19th, 20th** — Hard at work maintaining communication. Enemy shelling heavy.
>
> **21st** — Enemy shelled horse lines. Several casualties. One officer and four O.R killed, three O.Rs seriously wounded, two O.Rs slightly wounded. Four horses killed and four wounded. Motor lorry also hit and put out of action.
>
> **22nd** — Company working hard maintaining lines.

### Page 6 — 23–30 September 1916

> **Warley, Sept 23rd** — Company relieved by 2nd Canadian Div Sig Coy. Office closed at Usna Hill at 10.30 A.M. and opened same hour at new Hqrs Warley. Company marched from Albert at 8 A.M. reached Warley 11.20 am.
>
> **24th** — Usual routine Company at rest. Visual training being carried out.
>
> **25th** — " [ditto]
>
> **26th, 27th** — " [ditto]
>
> **28th** — Office closed at Warley at 10 A.M. and opened at new Div Hqrs Tara Hill at the same hour relieving the 1st Canadian Div Sig Coy. Communication with forward Bde cut by shell fire during the afternoon.
>
> **29th** — Party repairing buried cable. Usual work of patrolling and maintenance carried out.
>
> **30th** — Wiring new Signal office and usual duties carried out.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *J. E. Powers Maj / O.C. 3rd Canadian Div Sig Coy*

## Open questions / uncertain items (September 1916)
- The contents page says "Diary Text 3 Pages"; the three diary sheets are numbered 3, 4 and 5 at the top right (the cover and contents are pages 1–2, the title sheet page 3).
- 7 September: "Company move off at 7 A.M. for Esquelbecq when it entrained for Contay." — "when" read as written; it may mean "where".
- 8 September: the place column reads "Le Ploy"; the company detrained at Contay and marched to Le Ploy, reaching there at 4 a.m.
- 13 September: "Working to forward brigade on Corps [uncertain — 'new'?] buried cable." — the word after "Corps" reads like "new".
- 18 September: "One cable detachment posted for duty with Lahore Artillery. New lines laid from Lahore Div Arty Hqrs to two forward Brigades." — "Lahore" read as written in both places.
- 21 September: the casualties are given only as totals — one officer and four other ranks killed, three other ranks seriously wounded, two slightly wounded, four horses killed and four wounded, one motor lorry put out of action. No names are given.
- 24th–27th: the entries are ditto marks under the 24th entry ("Usual routine Company at rest. Visual training being carried out.").
- The Remarks column initials read as "J.E.P."; they match the signature "J. E. Powers".
- No map coordinates are given.
- Names read as: Maj. J. E. Powers; no other personal names are given in this volume.
