# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company — June 1916

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company
- **Month:** June 1916 (Volume 7)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004905 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5005, microfilm reel T-10853, file 692; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** June 1916, p. 1 – p. 9 (image files page-0036 – page-0044)
- **June 1916 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–9): cover, contents, a title sheet, diary text 1–30 June, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-26.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Handwritten diary text throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. No map coordinates are given in the diary; places are recorded as written. The diarist's spellings "YEOMENRY", "RENINGHELST" and "STEENVOORDE" are kept as written. "H.Q." = Headquarters; "Bde." = Brigade; "Infy." = Infantry; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "D.R." = Despatch Rider; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "O.R." = Other Ranks; "C.Y.C.R." and "K.S." are test-station / battle-headquarters code names used in the diary. A repeated initialled mark in the Remarks column, read as "J.E.P." [uncertain], appears against most entries; it is noted here rather than repeated against every entry. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets reads "3rd Canadian Division Signal Coy."

## Pages 4–9 — Diary text (1–30 June 1916)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". Heading on each sheet: "3rd Canadian Division Signal Coy." Each sheet is marked "Original" at the top left. Place throughout: RENINGHELST.

### Page 4 — 1–4 June 1916

> **RENINGHELST, June 1st** — Several lines cut by shell-fire. Work resumed, between test station "K.S." and battle headquarters, on buried cable route. Captive balloon broke adrift on right our area.
>
> **2nd** — Heavy shelling occurred beginning at 8.30am, followed by German attack. Front line trenches in possession of enemy in right sector by four pm. Lines were maintained, but with difficulty, owing to the intense bombardment of certain areas.
>
> **3rd** — Bombardment and fighting continued. Forward lines left sector good. Right sector in from twelve noon until four pm. Divisional lines out forty minutes in afternoon, to right brigade. Lateral communication however kept divisional headquarters in touch with this brigade.
>
> **4th** — Mutual bombardment continued intermittently. Many lines cut. Lines laid for 3rd Imperial Divisional Artillery, who moved up this area.

### Page 5 — 5–7 June 1916

> **RENINGHELST, June 5th** — Communications in good condition. One telegraph and two telephone pairs working to each brigade. Left sector line to YEOMENRY POST destroyed.
>
> **6th** — Increased activity on both sides. Severe shelling. Practically every circuit except six foot buried route out several times during day. Largest days business since beginning of operations. Twelve hundred and eighteen telegrams and despatches handled.
>
> **7th** — Somewhat quiet. Business handled eleven hundred an sixty-six telegrams and despatches. Informed that this sector would come under 2nd Canadian Division Signal Company as the remnants of our two brigades have been withdrawn to be reinforced, and replaced by 2nd Division brigades. Casualties in this Company to date: One killed, thirteen wounded.

### Page 6 — 8–14 June 1916

> **RENINGHELST, June 8th, 9th, 10th, 11th** — All lines handed over to 2nd Canadian Division Signal Coy. We maintained same for them satisfactorily. Working party three hundred and fifty men supplied, and we completed in four nights our proposed buried route from DOLL'S HOUSE to RAMPARTS (YPRES). Nine casualties from shellfire. Arrangement of working under direction of another Company has proved very unsatisfactory to both.
>
> **12th** — 9th Canadian Infy. Brigade, of this Division, after being withdrawn and reinforced, went back into line and had successful attack, recovering most of the ground lost on second this month. All communications forward [uncertain — "dis"?] to Hdqrs. office. This Company still maintaining lines for Second Division.
>
> **13th, 14th** — Nothing to report. Quiet on front. Strengthening and repairing test stations and lines.

### Page 7 — 15–22 June 1916

> **RENINGHELST, June 15th, 16th, 17th** — Working party of one hundred and fifty men provided. Buried twenty "D5" cable lines from forward test station "K.S." back to battle headquarters "C.Y.C.R."
>
> **18th** — Office opened at STEENVOORDE, FRANCE, for "G" branch. Completed changes at TEST STATION "K.S." to strengthen route.
>
> **19th, 20th** — No forward communication as this Division had no brigade in the line, but all lines maintained. No working party.
>
> **21st** — 7th Canadian Infy. Brigade relieved Brigade of the Guards in LEFT sector. Established communication with that brigade hdqrs. in RAMPARTS (YPRES) at nine pm. Office relief completed 10 pm.
>
> **22nd** — Party of three hundred men provided to complete burying of cable to battle headquarters; "C.Y.C.R." Very quiet on front.

### Page 8 — 23–28 June 1916

> **RENINGHELST, June 23rd, 24th** — Office at STEENVOORDE, FRANCE closed. Wire buried back to within five hundred yards of battle hdqrs; "CYCR" (20 - "D5" cables.)
>
> **25th, 26th** — Second "Imperial" Cavalry Division, who have been supplying working parties for burying cable moved. Continued work with party of twenty men from Company. Situation normal in this sector during past week.
>
> **27th** — Working party ninth brigade practically completed cable burying on night 26/27th. Lines led into dugouts in daytime.
>
> **28th** — New fifteen line switch installed at battle hdqrs; "CYCR." Work started on burying of armoured cable through YPRES (LILLE GATE to RAMPARTS). Conference Signal O.Cs. of Canadian Corps held at 2nd Division hdqrs. Very beneficial. Many important details discussed.

### Page 9 — 29–30 June 1916

> **RENINGHELST, June 29th** — Sergts. McPHERSON and MASTERS, 3rd C.F.A. brigade granted temporary commissions and attached this Company as officers. Sent to England to outfit. Work on burying of armoured cable through YPRES continued. Court martial ordered on Lieut. C. A. McCREADY (of this Company) for July 1st 1916.
>
> **30th** — Working parties, for cable burying, forward discontinued. All lines working well.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *J. E. Powers Major / 3rd Can Div Signal Coy.*

## Open questions / uncertain items (June 1916)
- 1 June: "Captive balloon broke adrift on right our area." — read as written; "on right our area" may be short for "on [the] right [of] our area".
- 3 June: "Right sector in from twelve noon until four pm." — read as written; "in" may mean the right-sector lines were in (working) from noon to 4 p.m.
- 7 June: "Business handled eleven hundred an sixty-six telegrams and despatches." — "an" is the diarist's word for "and", kept as written. Company casualties to date: one killed, thirteen wounded — no names are given.
- 8th–11th: "Nine casualties from shellfire." — no names are given.
- 12 June: 'All communications forward [uncertain — "dis"?] to Hdqrs. office.' — the quoted word reads "dis"; it may be short for "disconnected" or "direct".
- The repeated mark in the Remarks column is read as "J.E.P." [uncertain] — apparently the diarist's initials; it matches the signature "J. E. Powers".
- No map coordinates are given.
- Names read as: Maj. J. E. Powers; Sergts. McPHERSON and MASTERS (3rd C.F.A. Brigade); Lieut. C. A. McCREADY.
