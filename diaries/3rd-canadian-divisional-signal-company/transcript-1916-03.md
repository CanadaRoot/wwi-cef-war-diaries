# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company — March 1916

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company, 3rd Canadian Division
- **Month:** March 1916 (Volume 4) — this volume covers 1 to 31 March 1916
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004905 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5005, microfilm reel T-10853, file 692; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** March 1916, p. 1 – p. 6 (image files page-0016 – page-0021 of the 307-page diary)
- **March 1916 — COMPLETE** (pages 16–21 of 307): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–31 March, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-26.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Handwritten diary text throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Map references are given as written (Sheet 28 YPRES); no latitude/longitude coordinates are given. "H.E." = high explosive; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "R.F.A." = Royal Field Artillery. The test station is written "K.5." in some entries and "K5." in others — both forms are kept as written. A repeated initialled mark appears in the Remarks column against most entries; it is noted in the open questions rather than repeated against every entry. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets is "3rd Canadian Divn. Signal Coy." (Sheets No. 7 and No. 9) and "3rd Canadian Divn. Signal Company." (Sheet No. 8), written in by hand at the top right. The "3" written beneath "Volume 4" on the cover is a microfilm/frame mark, not part of the volume number.

## Pages 4–6 — Diary text (1–31 March 1916)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". "Original" is written by hand at the top left of each sheet. The printed footer reads "T.2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."

### Page 4 — 1–22 March 1916 (Sheet No. 7)

> **MONTH OF MARCH 1916.**
>
> **FLETRE, Mar. 1st to Mar. 17th.** — Training of Company in airline and cable work, mounted and dismounted work and operating proceeded with. During this period one hundred and two men were taken on the strength of the Company.
>
> **FLETRE, Mar. 18th.** — One officer and ten men detached from the Company and attached to the 24th (Imperial) Divisional Artillery at RENINGHELST, BELGIUM (Map. location G34 central - sheet 28 YPRES) to take over artillery lines and to take care of same after the taking over by the LAHORE DIVISIONAL ARTILLERY, R.F.A. to take place on occupation of that area by 3rd Canadian Division.
>
> **FLETRE, Mar. 19th to Mar. 21st.** — Training and equipping of Company proceeded with. No event of importance happened during this period.
>
> **RENINGHELST (BELGIUM), Mar. 22nd.** — Company moved from FLETRE, FRANCE to RENINGHELST, BELGIUM (Map. ref. G34 central sheet 28 YPRES) and took over existing lines of 24th (Imperial) Division [entry continues on Sheet No. 8 — see Page 5].

### Page 5 — 23–27 March 1916 (Sheet No. 8)

> **MONTH OF MARCH 1916 — continued #2.**
>
> **RENINGHELST, Mar. 23rd.** — Work on lines taken over from 24th (Imperial) Division, to improve communication, begun. Situation quiet.
>
> **RENINGHELST, Mar. 24th.** — Shelling around test station known as "K.5." very heavy. All lines cut by shellfire. Communication restored within two hours after shelling ceased.
>
> **RENINGHELST, Mar. 25th.** — Area about "K.5." again heavily shelled between five and nine thirty P.M. All communication again cut off. Restored during night of 25th/26th.
>
> **RENINGHELST, Mar. 26th.** — Same occurrence with regard to test station "K5." One direct hit being made on dugout. Fortunately shell did not explode.
>
> **RENINGHELST, Mar. 27th.** — Test station "K5" again shelled. Decided to move this station to less shelled area.

### Page 6 — 28–31 March 1916 (Sheet No. 9)

> **MONTH OF MARCH 1916 continued #3.**
>
> **RENINGHELST, Mar. 28th.** — Today quiet. Little shelling. New position for test station found and reported on. Forward lines working well.
>
> **RENINGHELST, Mar. 29th.** — Heavy shelling. Decided to loop and ladder lines laid overland to replace buried route system during repairs to same. Find lines not laid deep enough to withstand H.E. shell.
>
> **RENINGHELST, Mar. 30th.** — Detailed parties to pick up wire to ladder surface metallic lines. Test station "K5." again shelled.
>
> **RENINGHELST, Mar. 31st.** — Fairly quiet during day. Lines out during night 30th/31st. Weather. Fine.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *S. E. Powers Major / O.C. 3rd Canadian Division Signals*

## Open questions / uncertain items (March 1916)
- The repeated mark in the Remarks column is the same initialled mark as in December 1915 – February 1916, read as "J.H." [uncertain].
- 22 March: the entry ends mid-sentence at the foot of Sheet No. 7 ("and took over existing lines of 24th (Imperial) Division") and the thought is completed by the 23 March entry on Sheet No. 8; transcribed as written on each sheet.
- The test station is written "K.5." (24 and 25 March) and "K5." / "K5" (26, 27 and 30 March) — both forms kept as written; it is the same station.
- Names read as: Major S. E. Powers.
- No casualties are recorded in this volume. The 26 March entry records a direct shell hit on a dugout at test station "K5" — the shell did not explode and no casualties are mentioned.
