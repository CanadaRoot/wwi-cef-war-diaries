# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company, C.E. — March 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company, C.E. (Canadian Engineers)
- **Month:** March 1918 (Volume 28)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004905 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5005, microfilm reel T-10853, file 692; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** March 1918, p. 1 – p. 8 (image files page-0185 – page-0192 of the diary)
- **March 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–8): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–31 March, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-26.
- **Diary covers 5 December 1915 – 17 March 1919.**

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. The March diary is typewritten (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"), unlike the handwritten January and February sheets. `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. No map coordinates are given in the diary; places are recorded as written. The diarist's initials "A.L." appear in the Remarks column against every entry and are not repeated against each entry below; the sheets are signed "A. Leavitt Maj" (O.C.). Typewriter conventions: the typewriter has no "1" key, so "1" is typed as "I" (e.g. "II Specials" = 11 Specials, "I9 Specials" = 19 Specials, "34I6" = 3416); "C.I.B." occasionally appears as "CiB"/"CiI.B." and is transcribed as "C.I.B.". "msges" is the diary's abbreviation for messages; the typo "msesgs" (4 March) is kept as typed. "H.Q." = Headquarters; "Hqrs" = Headquarters; "Bde" = Brigade; "Div" = Division; "Cdn" = Canadian; "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade; "C.M.R." = Canadian Mounted Rifles; "R.C.R." = Royal Canadian Regiment; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "Sigs" = Signals; "Oi/c" = Officer in charge; "i/c" = in charge; "L.O." = Liaison Officer [uncertain — expansion not given in the diary]; "C.C.S." = Casualty Clearing Station; "D.R.L.S." = Despatch Rider Letter Service; "P.B-A." = Power Buzzer-Amplifier; "G.H.Q." = General Headquarters; "Spr" = Sapper; "L/Cpl" = Lance Corporal; "Bury" = buried cable (as used in this diary); "sth" = south; "Boche" = the Germans (as written). "Div'l H.Q. Chateau D'Acq. Adv. H.Q's Fort George" is the place written in the Place column for the whole month.

## Pages 4–8 — Diary text (1–31 March 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". Heading on each sheet: "3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company" (variant "3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Coy.").

### Page 4 — 1–10 March 1918

> **March 1st** — Work continued on Agricultural scheme and ground harrowed. Good progress continued on Buried Cable. Usual patrolling and maintenance of Lines. Weather good. Business handled 7 Specials 3677 messages and despatches.
>
> **2nd** — Inspection of Horses, Harness and Transport. Work continued as outlined for previous day. Business handled 8 Specials, 3639 messages and despatches.
>
> **3rd** — Church Parade 9.15 a.m. Office and necessary routine carried on. Business handled 11 Specials 3744 messages and despatches.
>
> **[Date struck out]** — Lieut W.M. Maxey Oi/c Signals 9th C.I.B. granted 14 days leave to England and Lieut. C Skinner given charge of Section during his absence. Major A. Leavitt proceeded to Central Wireless School, Abbeville on 7 days course of instruction and inspection, Capt J.H. Leeson assuming charge of Company during his absence.
>
> **4th** — Harrowing on farm continued, Inspection of proposed Reserve Brigade H.Qs dugouts, Signal dugout chosen and suggested Buried Cable Route gone over. Business 6 Specials 3862 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **5th** — Work on farm continued. Progress maintained on Buried Cable from Left Brigade. Weather good. Usual patrol work and maintenance of lines. Business for day 1 Special 3711 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **6th** — Farm work continued. Construction of anti-bombing parapets for Horse-Standings commenced. Lieut H.W. Sharp, attached from 49th Cdn Inf. Battn transferred to Company and assumes charge of Wireless Section. Usual routine work continued. Relief of 9th C.I.B. by 7th C.I.B. on Avion Section commenced and lines taken over by No 2 Section.
>
> **7th** — Fine weather prolonged. Business for day 8 Specials 3634 msesgs and despatches. Relief of 9th C.I.B. completed and 9th C.I.B. H.Q's opened at Villers au Bois, Div'l Reserve. Work continued on farm, also routine patrolling of lines etc. Buried cable work continued.
>
> **8th** — Business for day 7 Specials 3414 msesgs and despatches. All work carried on, weather remaining good. Business for day 4 Specials 3553 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **9th** — In view of possible Enemy offensive, Position Calls were withdrawn and Station Code Calls put into use. Work on anti-bombing parapets for Horse-Standings completed. Steps taken to protect all buildings above ground. Usual routine work continued. Buried cable work maintained. Inspection of Horses, Harness and Transport. Business for day 8 Specials, 3588 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **10th** — Church Parade 9.15 a.m. Office and necessary routine work carried on. Major A. Leavitt rejoined from Central Wireless School, Capt J.H. Leeson re-assuming 2nd in Command of Company upon his return. Course at School tho' in nature only one of inspection, gave many valuable pointers on use of Wireless in Forward Areas. Business for day 10 Specials 3679 msesgs and despatches.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A. Leavitt Maj*

### Page 5 — 11–18 March 1918

> **March 11th** — Work on Farm continued. Patrolling of lines and usual Office routine. Enemy quiet and communication good. Lieut R.B. Gibson rejoined from leave and given charge of all lines. Business for day 7 Specials, 3416 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **12th** — Usual routine. Buried cable on Left Bde continued. All communications good. Business for day 37 Specials, 4037 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **13th** — Possibility of enemy offensive in near future. In order to safeguard communications, work commenced on reclaiming old Bury from Corps Line to Right Brigade by short extensions and re-burying where necessary. Party i/c of Lt R.B. Gibson. Buried cable on Left Bde continued. Usual routine maintained. Weather good. Business for day 6 Specials, 3819 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **14th** — Work on reclaiming old Bury and on Buried cable continued. Patrolling of lines and usual routine. Weather good and enemy quiet. Business for day 9 Specials, 3843 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **15th** — Routine work and Buried cable work carried on as outlined on previous day. Successful raid carried out by 5th C.M.R's Right Bde on enemy lines (mericourt front). Number of prisoners and casualties inflicted. L/Cpl McLaren, 10th Bde Sig Sub-section, patrolled line laid into our front line for L.O. Enemy barrage very heavy on our trenches. L/Cpl tho' warned to take cover, refused to do so and carried on with his work, maintaining communications and rendering valuable work. Recommended for Military Medal. Business for day 23 Specials, 3981 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **16th** — Work on Farm, Buried cable and usual Office routine continued. Weather good and communications in good shape. Business 10 Specials, 3510 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **17th** — Lieut H.W. Sharp proceeded on course at First Army School of Wireless (3 weeks special). Lieut C Skinner called in from No 2 Section to take charge of Wireless Section during his absence. Men from all Battns trained in Power Buzzer-Amplifier, to form Brigade Pool brought into H.Q's up to 50% of total laid down in G.H.Q. letter. Classes in P.B-A. and Forward Wireless Sets commenced. Two additional P.B-A's installed on Div'l Front Left Brigade. Total P.B-A's on Div'l Front-4. Usual routine and work on Buried cable carried on. Communications good and Enemy quiet. Business for day 7 Specials, 4377 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **18th** — Lieut W.M. Maxey rejoined from leave and re-assumed charge of No 4 Section 9th C.I.B. Wireless station at Bn H.Q's, Mont Foret Quarries dismantled and re-installed at 10th Bde C.F.A H.Q's. Buried cable from Left Brigade H.Q's to point on Old Bury completed. Party i/c Lt Christie completed work without incurring casualties. Route mapped out for spur from new bury to Reserve Brigade H.Q's dugouts. Work on re-claiming Old bury on Right Bde continued. Work on Farm and usual patrol and Office routine maintained. Weather good also communications. Business for day 9 Specials, 3780 msesgs and despatches.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A. Leavitt Maj*

### Page 6 — 19–25 March 1918

> **March 19th** — Work on spur from new Bury to Reserve Bde dugouts commenced. Party i/c Lt Christie attached C.C Sigs Re-claiming Old Bury continued. Usual routine patrolling lines and Office work maintained. Enemy quiet and weather continuing good. All communications in good shape. Business for day 9 Specials, 4318 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **20th** — Relief of 8th Bde (Right Bde front) by 9th Bde night of 20th/21st. Lines taken over by No 4 Section. Position Calls to be taken into use again at midnight. Usual routine and work on buried cable continued. Business for day 5 Specials, 3620 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **21st** — Relief completed by 9th Bde. 8th Bde located at Div'l reserve Villers au Bois. Work on buried cable and usual routine maintained. Enemy quiet and weather continues good. No trouble with communications. Business for day 18 Specials, 4033 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **22nd** — The much heralded enemy offensive commenced 5 a.m. from La Fere to Croisilles, sth of Arras. Assault delivered with large forces. Artillery active on our front. Communication remains good. Routine work and buried cable continued. Business for day 39 Specials, 5060 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **23rd** — Enemy continues to press offensive. 62nd Division on our right pulled out of line and 8th C.I.B. ordered to take over line. Inspection made of lines to be taken over. Artillery continues active. Necessary routine and buried cable continued. All communications maintained. Weather fine. Business for day 30 Specials, 4470 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **24th** — Relief by 8th C.I.B. successfully completed. Communication established and all lines cut through Fort George. 2nd Cdn Div'n moved to Fort George and H.Q's opened. All routine business over Wireless cut off and stations held in readiness to handle only important msesgs. In view of continued offensive, all men ordered to stand to, ready for all eventualities. Artillery very active on our right. Additional Wireless Station established at our new Right Bde H.Q's. Communications interfered owing to cut over of lines, but finally straightened out. Usual routine and work on buried cable carried out. Weather exceptionally fine. Business for day 26 Specials, 4706 msesgs and despatches. Business handled by Office staff during last 3 days establishes new record.
>
> **25th** — Considerable advance made by enemy Sth of Arras, battle still continues. In view of possible retirement on this front, brought about by enemy offensive, overland wires provided for from Bd. H.Q's (Reserve) to Test Point on new bury. Work on reclaiming old bury maintained, and in addition steps taken to bury lines into Div'l H.Q's, Chateau D'Acq. Work commenced on burying of lines from Right Bde H.Q's to Test Point on right bury. Necessary Office and patrolling of lines continued. Weather remains fine. All men on courses etc, called back to Units by Div'l H.Q's. Stand-to ordered by H.Q's owing to rumour of enemy attack at dawn 26th. Station Code Calls put on. Business for day 23 Specials, 4180 msesgs and despatches.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A. Leavitt Maj*

### Page 7 — 26–28 March 1918

> **March 26th** — Artillery remained very active during course of night. No enemy attack materialised and stand-to was removed by H.Q's during morning. Enemy offensive still continuing and advances made at certain points. Orders received to remove all surplus kits to rear. Work on reclaiming bury continued. Patrolling of lines and necessary Office routine carried on. 2nd Cdn Divn, Fort George, held as Corps Reserve ordered south, to proceed at once. Weather excellent and much aerial work carried out. Enemy planes dropped bombs at Div'l H.Q's Chateau D'Acq between 11 p.m. and Midnight. No casualties were incurred by our men, tho' Signal Offices were pierced at several points by fragments of bombs. A number of lines were blown out, but repairs were quickly made and communications re-established. Notification received from C.C.S of death of Spr Brasier who had been evacuated sick. This man rendered excellent work at Passchendaele for which he received the Military Medal, and his death is much regretted. Capt H.F.E Griffith (The R.C.R's) attached to Company for instruction in Signal work. Business for day 19 Specials, 4927 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **27th** — In accordance with orders, all surplus kits removed to Villers Chatel for storage. Move of 2nd Cdn Div'n units from this area completed during course of night. 13th Corps assumed command of Division from 12 noon. 8th CIB to be relieved by 168th Brigade and to move to Neuville St Vaast Area. H.Q's at Fort George, relief to be completed by tonight. No 2 Section opened Adv Signal Office at CYCR. Enemy shelling rear area and indications point to an attack on our right front. Communications difficult to maintain owing to constant changes, lines having to be frequently cut over. Important bury work continued and necessary Office routine. Orders issued by Division calling in all ranks on command at Courses etc. Lieut F W Auld Oi/c Signal School, Ferfay returned, upon receipt of Order, and School closed. Communication established to Div'l H.Q's with Lucas Lamp as emergency route, in the event of all lines being shot out. Attack expected by enemy in morning. Bury to Berthonval Farm inspected with view to opening Report Centre there, if enemy prove successful in attack. Business for day 20 Specials, 5000 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **28th** — Enemy artillery extremely active on rear areas and reported very heavy on our forward zone. La Targette shelled all morning with H.V. and all air-lines blown out. Attack developed on our right front, enemy putting down a heavy barrage. 8th CIB ordered to take up positions on defence line in support of 168th Brigade. Communication established on old bury and an exchange installed at Berthonval Wood end of bury. Enemy attacked with vigor during course of day, but was repulsed on each attempt. Touch maintained with all Brigades throughout day with exception of about 2 hours. D.R.L.S. hampered owing to heavy enemy shelling on roads to Brigades. The enemy attack which extended from a point sth of Arras to our right failed completely, heavy shelling continues. Business for day 18 Specials, 4706 msesgs and despatches. Lieut H.W. Sharp rejoined from First Army Wireless School.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A. Leavitt Maj*

### Page 8 — 29–31 March 1918

> **March 29th** — Lieut H.W. Sharp assumes charge of Signal Office, Chateau D'Acq. No further attempt has been made by the Boche tho' shelling of rear areas continues. A few casualties received by Company men during yesterdays battle, but none of a serious nature. Notice received that 4th Cdn Divn are to move to our right, to relieve the 56th Divn. 8th C.I.B. ordered to move back to Neuville St Vaast area with H.Q's at Machine Gun Fort, relief to be completed by tonight. All communications working well. Instructions issued that in the event of all air-lines to the rear being shot out Office reliefs will mann Visual Station at Div'l H.Q's in touch with CYCR. Weather changed and rain falling. Difficulty experienced in handling D.R.L.S owing to bad state of forward roads. Business for day 10 Specials, 4331 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **30th** — Relief by 4th Cdn Divn completed. 8th Bde H.Q's now located at M.G. Fort with all units in N. St. Vaast area. Preparations made to remove Report Centre to Bury end, Berthonval Wood, to obviate difficulties to communications caused by excessive length of air-line system. Bad weather continues. Chain of Visual Stations completed by installation of station at CYCR in touch with Station on Ridge. Communication now established by visual from front line to Div'l H.Q's Chateau D'Acq. Wireless station installed at CYCR in addition to stations already installed. Business for day 3 Specials, 3501 msesgs and despatches.
>
> **31st** — Nothing of moment to report. Enemy comparatively quiet and little shelling of rear areas. All communications working good. Weather changed for the better. Necessary routine such as extensions to bury and Office routine carried on. Business for day 3 Specials, 4112 msesgs and despatches.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A. Leavitt Maj*

## Open questions / uncertain items (March 1918)
- Undated entry between 3rd and 4th March: the date in the Date column is heavily struck out (it appears to read "4th,") and the entry — Lt W.M. Maxey's leave and Maj A. Leavitt's departure for the Central Wireless School, Abbeville — has no legible date. It is transcribed in its position between the 3rd and 4th entries. Leavitt's 7-day course fits a departure on 3rd March (he rejoined on 10th March), but the date is not asserted.
- 8 March: the entry contains two business figures — "Business for day 7 Specials 3414 msesgs" and, after "weather remaining good", "Business for day 4 Specials 3553 msesgs" — while the 9th entry also ends with its own figure (8 Specials, 3588). It is unclear whether the "4 Specials 3553" figure belongs to the 8th or the 9th; it is transcribed with the 8th entry, where it falls in the text block.
- 12 March: "Business for day 37 Specials, 4037 msesgs" — read as typed; 37 Specials is far above the usual 3–11 a day, but the figures are legible.
- 15 March: "Successful raid carried out by 5th C.M.R's Right Bde on enemy lines (mericourt front)" — "mericourt" is lower-case as typed. "L/Cpl McLaren" is given no first name or number; the same L/Cpl is referred to again as "L/Cpl tho' warned to take cover".
- 19 March: "Party i/c Lt Christie attached C.C Sigs" — "C.C Sigs" read as typed (presumably Corps Signals).
- 22 March: the diary dates the start of the German offensive to 22 March ("commenced 5 a.m. from La Fere to Croisilles, sth of Arras"); transcribed as written.
- 27 March: "CYCR" — a place or station code, written without stops; it recurs on 29–30 March ("Adv Signal Office at CYCR", "Visual Station at Div'l H.Q's in touch with CYCR", "station at CYCR"). Not expanded in the diary.
- "Mont Foret Quarries" (18 March) read as typed.
- Names read as: Lieut W.M. Maxey; Lieut C Skinner; Major A. Leavitt; Capt J.H. Leeson; Lieut H.W. Sharp; Lieut R.B. Gibson; L/Cpl McLaren; Lieut F W Auld; Capt H.F.E Griffith; Spr Brasier (deceased, 26 March — Military Medal, Passchendaele); Lt Christie.
- Casualties: Spr Brasier died (26 March, after evacuation sick — notified by C.C.S.); "a few casualties received by Company men during yesterdays battle, but none of a serious nature" (29 March, referring to the 28 March fighting); no names or numbers given for the 28–29 March casualties. Enemy bombs fell on Divisional H.Q. at Chateau D'Acq on the night of 26 March — no casualties, signal offices pierced by bomb fragments, lines blown out and quickly repaired.
- The German Spring Offensive dominates the second half of March: the diary records its opening (22 March), the 62nd Division pulling out on the company's right (23 March), the 2nd Canadian Division moving to Fort George (24 March), stand-to and retirement precautions (25–26 March), and the repulsed enemy attack south of Arras on 28 March. Daily signal traffic peaked at 5,060 messages on 22 March and 5,000 on 27 March.
