# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company — June 1918

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company
- **Month:** June 1918 (Volume 31)
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004905 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5005, microfilm reel T-10853, file 692; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** June 1918, p. 1 – p. 8 (image files page-0208 – page-0215 of the diary)
- **June 1918 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–8): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–30 June, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-26.
- **Note on the 17th/18th inserts:** the diary's main run covers 1–30 June in order, but two late-arriving entries for 17 and 18 June — the 9th Brigade C.F.A. sub-section's move into action — were added at the end of the month as "(INSERT 17th) data just to hand" (page 7) and a further continuation sheet (page 8, diary sheet No. 5). They are transcribed here in place at the end, as filed.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept, including the typist's errors ("bycycles", "staion"). The diary is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. No map coordinates are given in the diary; places are recorded as written. "H.Q." = Headquarters; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "C.D.A." = Canadian Divisional Artillery; "C.I.B." = Canadian Infantry Brigade; "D.R." = Despatch Rider; "Imp." = Imperial; "O. i/c" = Officer in Charge; "N.C.O." = Non-Commissioned Officer; "C.E.T.D." = Canadian Engineers Training Depot; "Os. I/c" = Officers in Charge; "Helio" = heliograph. A repeated handwritten monogram, read as "A.R." [uncertain], appears in the Remarks column against every entry; it is noted here rather than repeated against every entry. Each diary sheet is signed at bottom right "A Hewitt Maj" [uncertain — first initial] (Major A. Hewitt). The Place column on sheets No. 1–3 reads "NORRENT FONTES." for the whole month.

## Pages 4–8 — Diary text (1–30 June 1918)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". Heading on each sheet: "3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Co'y."

### Page 4 — 1–9 June 1918 (diary sheet No. 1)

> **NORRENT FONTES. — June 1st.** — Training of Wireless, Power Buzzer, and Visual Sections; and Cable detachments; Wireless; practicing: "procedure;", dismantling, and re-erection of stations and methods suitable for open warfare conditions; Power Buzzer, practicing with three brigade stations out working with a station representing Divisional Headquarters, considerable traffic being handled in this way. Visual section flag drill; and practical station work; four stations forming a line of communication between Divisional Hqrs and Artillery Hqrs; Lucas Lamp and Helio being used; Cable detachments laying lines to surrounding units beyond the reach of permanents;
>
> **2nd** — Sunday; Ideal weather; In morning rifle inspection and small box respirator inspection and gas drill; Half holiday in afternoon.
>
> **3rd** — Fine weather continued; All motor cycles and bycycles tagged by Ordnance with a view to prevent them being stolen; Police having strict orders to arrest anyone riding a government machine not tagged and sealed. Training continued; Wireless and Power Buzzer sections carrying out Divisional Signalling scheme, No. 1;
>
> **4th** — Training of all branches. Cable detachment all out on all day cable drill.
>
> **5th** — Training Continued; Long distance staion work by Visual section and Communication opened Between Div'l and Art'y Hqrs.
>
> **6th** — Training; Scheme No. 2, being carried out by Wireless section Cable detachments on all day cable drill; Despatch Rider Cpl McGruther collided with an Imperial D. R., in the Domy AREA receiving injuries to right arm and fingers; Since reported as being evacuated to England; Collision unavoidable, due to nature of roads and crossings.
>
> **7th** — Training; No's 1 and 2 cable detachments out under Lt. Lee; No's 3 and 4; cleaning and overhauling equipment; New material for Power Buzzer section receiving elementary training under Lt. Sharp.
>
> **8th** — Continuous fine weather of past few days broken by occasional showers throughout day; Wireless Section lectured by Lt. Sharp; topic, "Procedure"; Drivers cleaning harness; Wagonmen cleaning wagons;
>
> **9th** — Rifle; small box respirator; and harness inspections from 9 a.m to 10.30 a.m; bathing parade remainder of morning. Holiday in afternoon; baseball game; 3rd Signals 14 4th Signals 9;
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A Hewitt Maj*

### Page 5 — 10–14 June 1918 (diary sheet No. 2)

> **10th** — Manoeuvres were carried out by the Third Canadian Division in the Domy Area. Aeroplanes, Tanks, and Cavalry were used in the Operation. All, branches of the Signal Service were represented; each getting good training such as would be beneficial in a war of movement; Div'l Hqrs Established in the Domy Wood which afforded excellent cover from Enemy planes for troops operating in the vicinity; Lines were laid to the 7th and 8th Brigades by Cable wagons, and and a good service of telephonic and telegraphic communications were maintained throughout the scheme. In additionx to the aforementioned means of communication; Visual was very satisfactorily used between Brigades and formations in the forward area; Power Buzzer also being used in this connection; proving a very efficient means of communication in operations of much movement. Wireless was also Established from Divisional Headquarters to Brigades, but owing to the good working of telephone and telegraph lines; this branch did not have the opportunity to transmit a great deal of traffic. Throughout the whole operation the Signal Service was quite efficient and fulfilled all requirements. Rain during the latter part of the afternoon did not put a stop to the Operations.
>
> **11th** — Wireless and Power Buzzer sections training and reviewing tactical scheme of previous day. Cable detachments cleaning and overhauling equipment; drivers cleaning harness.
>
> **12th** — Training continued; Sgt. Bennett Divisional Signalmaster; Sgt. McCausland senior N.C.O., 9th Bde. Section; proceeded to England with a view to qualifying for a commission in the C. E.
>
> **13th** — Training for all branches; Lts. McPherson [uncertain] and Gibson out with Cable detachments; Wireless under Lt. Sharp; and Lt. Auld out on all day station work with Visual Section.
>
> **14th** — A tactical scheme was carried out, (Without Troops); Especially planned for the General Staff and Signals; with a view to improving the co-operation of the Signals Service with the General Staff in a War of movement. Officers of imaginary Units took up positions and received orders from the General Staff. The General idea of the scheme i.e., a force has supposedly broken the line and advanced approximately 15 miles; The 3rd Canadian Division being in the vicinity is ordered in conjunction with flanking divisions to counter-attack and regain line. During the operations much useful training was gained by personnel of the Company; in rapid opening of communication at numerous different stations. Much traffic was very smoothly handled; points found to be superfluous in framing messages in operations of this character, with a view to shortening; have been abolished; increasing the efficiency of the limited means of communication; such as those available in a War of movement.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A Hewitt Maj*

### Page 6 — 15–25 June 1918 (diary sheet No. 3)

> **15th** — Ideal weather. Wireless, Power Buzzer and Visual sections carrying out training detailed by their respective Os. I/c. Cable detachments cleaning and overhauling equipment;
>
> **16th** — Pay parade; Squad Drill for dismounted men and harness inspections for mounted men..
>
> **17th** — Much line trouble to-day to the 11th British Corps; caused by Enemy bombing activities in the vicinity of Aire. Training for all branches.
>
> **18th** — Enemy again bombing same vicinity; Training continued.
>
> **19th** — 3 men sne [uncertain — "sent"?] to 10th Bde. [and struck out] CFA., at Flechinelle; transported by Wireless Box Car; which car was badly damaged by colliding with a PORTUGUESE Ambulance very recklessly handled by its driver. Dr. Robinson [uncertain — probably "Dvr." (Driver)] of Box Car was thrown to the ground receiving injuries to his head; and becoming unconscious; after which his car swerved across the road being stopped by a tree; contact causing much damage to chassis; Box car and its driver now at Mechanical Transport workshops.
>
> **20th** — Training continued; Weather favorable for Helio on long distance stations between Divisional Hqrs and Artillery Hqrs.
>
> **21st** — Rain; No training and nothing of importance to report.
>
> **22nd** — Lt. Barber of 7th Bde. Section proceeds on leave: being relieved by Lt. Auld of Hqrs section. Training carried out by all branches.
>
> **23rd** — Capt. McCready O.i/c Signals, 3rd C.D.A., and Lt. E.W. Stenhouse O. i/c Signals, 10th Bde. CFA. proceeded to C.E.T.D., Seaford to take up Instructional duties at that Camp. Capt. McCready relieved by Lt. McPherson [uncertain]; Lt. Stenhouse by Lt. Lee. Church parade in a.m; holiday in afternoon.
>
> **24th** — Training continued. Word has been received that we are shortly to be ordered to move from this Area. Officers of the 74th Imp. Divisional Signal Co'y., visit and look over billets; signal office, horse lines etc.
>
> **25th** — A party of Operators, clerks, runners and a switchboard Operator leave by lorry for LE CAUROY when Divisional to open up Signal Office at that place at 3 O'clock in afternoon of 26th. Horse lines move by march route; stopping over night in the ST.POL area.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A Hewitt Maj*

### Page 7 — 26–30 June 1918, with 17th/18th insert (diary sheet No. 4)

> **26th** — Divisional Headquarters closed at NORRENT FONTES at 3 p.m. opening same hour at LE CAUROY; office established in Chateau; Division now under VI British Corps; 3rd Canadian Division being concentrated in the BERLENCOURT-SIMENCOURT area lately occupied by the 74th Imp. Div.
>
> **27th** — It is recorded that this Company stayed over one night at LE CAUROY on its way northwards after the SOMME operations of 1916. Brigade dispositions now are; 7th CIB., HARBAROQ-AVESNES-LE-COMTE area; 8th CIB., IZEL-LES-HAMEAU-VILLERS-SIR-SIMON area; 9th CIB., GRAND-RULLECOURT-LIGERUIL area.
>
> **28th** — Wireless Officer proceeded to new Divisional HEAD QUARTERS at BASSEUX, to go over wireless stations and methods of VI British Corps. Also Lines Sgt. and a party of linemen, to acquaint themselves with the routes, test-points etc.,
>
> **29th** — All wireless personnel proceeded by lorry to new area. 7th CIB., are to take over left sector 8th CIB., right sector; on MERCATEL DIVISIONAL FRONT [uncertain — "FRONT" overstruck].
>
> **30th** — Orders received to take over 2nd Canadian Divisional Signal Co'ys., lines at 10 a.m; Office at LE CAUROY closing at same hour. Making ready to move and despatching stores; rations etc., by lorry to new area; 2 complete office reliefs and signal-master proceeded to new area to take over Signal-office at 10 a. m. July 1st.
>
> ———— (INSERT 17th) data just to hand ————
>
> **(17th)** — 9th Bde.CFA., sub-section, Lt. L.R. Peebles O. i/c., left AMETTES rest area with Artillery brigade proceeding to wagon lines at BOESEGHEHEM [uncertain — probably Boeseghem], in XI British Corps area; The brigade is moving up to support forthcoming operation of V Imp.Div.
>
> **18th** — The Brigade marched into action along the route of the NIEPPE CANAL; Enemy opened a heavy fire on cross-roads ahead of column; necessitating it to change its route of march; The canal was crossed and another road running parallel to the one being shelled was reached. The column galloped at intervals under the line of fire; no casualties resulting; great excitement prevailed; horses were masterfully handled by drivers; although one driver was over-come by the excitement, having to be lifted from his mount after getting out of the danger zone.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A Hewitt Maj*

### Page 8 — 9th Bde. C.F.A. sub-section, 19–22 June (diary sheet No. 5)

> [Undated — continuation of the 17th/18th insert:] Brigade headquarters located in CHATEAU LE [MORTX struck out] MORTS [uncertain], lately vacated by a French nobleman; the Chateau had received three direct hits from enemy shells previous to the arrival of the 9th Bde. CFA. It is recorded that the rich decorations and ancient furniture have not been damaged to any great extent.
>
> **19th** — Lines laid to Batteries and good communication resulted.
>
> **20th** — One gun per battery registered.
>
> **21st** — Orders were received to go out of action without having taken part in the proposed operations of the V Imp. Div. Moved back at night to wagon lines at BOESEGHEHEM [uncertain — probably Boeseghem];
>
> **22nd** — Wagon lines now located at ROQUETOIRE.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A Hewitt Maj*
