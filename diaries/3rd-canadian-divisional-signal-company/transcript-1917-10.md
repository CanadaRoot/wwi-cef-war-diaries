- **Pages:** October 1917, p. 1 – p. 12 (image files page-0139 – page-0150 of the diary)
- **October 1917 — COMPLETE** (pages 139–150): cover, contents, title page, diary text 1–31 October, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-26.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Handwritten diary text throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. No map coordinates are given in the diary; places are recorded as written. "A.L." in the Remarks column is the initials of the officer commanding, Major A. Leavitt (signature "A. Leavitt Maj" at the foot of each sheet); it is noted here rather than repeated against every entry. "H.Q." = Headquarters; "Bde." = Brigade; "Cdn" = Canadian; "Div" = Division; "Sig Coy" = Signal Company; "Imp" = Imperial; "Inf Bde" = Infantry Brigade; "D.R." = Despatch Rider; "N.Z. Div" = New Zealand Division; "A.D. Sigs" = Assistant Director of Signals; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "S.N.S." = the wireless school. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets reads "3rd CANADIAN Div Sig Coy. CAN SIGS". This month covers the Passchendaele attacks of 26 and 30 October 1917.

## Pages 4–12 — Diary text (1–31 October 1917)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". Heading on each sheet: "3rd CANADIAN Div Sig Coy. CAN SIGS".

### Page 4 — 1–4 October 1917

> **Div Hqrs Corps Line, Oct 1st** — Buried cable work continued. Racks & test panels installed at various test dugouts Weather fine Enemy quiet.
>
> **2** — Buried contd. Working party of 300 supplied & good progress made in spite of the fact that trench has to be dug through solid chalk. Party were from 42nd Batt & P.P.C.L.I. Over 400 yds of trench dug, cable laid, & trench filled in. Enemy quiet
>
> **3rd** — Buried contd, good progress made, cable now laid to within 100 yds of test dugout in front line trench. A lateral bury also started from Quarries to connect with the Bde on our left. No 117095 Sgt N B McCausland awarded the Military Medal for conspicuous bravery & good work done in laying and maintaining communication in heavily shelled Area in Loos Sector
>
> **4th** — Buried contd & cable completed to front line dugout. A gas attack by the 31st Imp Div on our right at 9.30 P.M. Heavy enemy retaliation on our front & support lines & working party caught between the two barrages. By the coolness of Lt S. M. Smith, the officer in charge, party were withdrawn to a place of safety without a single casualty. Enemy quiet at 10.10 P.M and party resumed work. Other usual routine

### Page 5 — 5–8 October 1917

> **Div Hqrs Corps Line, Oct 5th** — Orders received that 2nd Cdn Div will relieve 3rd Cdn Div relief to be completed by 10 A.M. Oct 6th 3rd Cdn Div Hqrs to move to VILLERS CHATEL. Party sent to install Signal Office for new Hqrs. Officers of 2nd Sigs shown over lines & communications in present area & arrangements made for carrying on all work already in hand Working party on buried called off on account of heavy rain. Enemy quiet
>
> **Villers Chatel, 6th** — Office closed at Corps Line at 10 A.M. & opened at Villers Chatel at same hour. Orders recd from Cdn Corps that no communication to Bdes in rear area other than by D. R. as no lines are available. No 2 Section relieved by No 4 Section 2nd Sigs. Relief completed at 11.30 P.M. No 2 Section move to Neuville St Vaast for the night.
>
> **7th** — 7th Cdn Inf Bde move to CHELLERS and wireless communication established with them. Wireless working well & large number of messages handled. Inspection of all ranks not on duty. Arrangements made to commence training. Weather very wet.
>
> **8th** — Buzzer, Sounder, Wireless, & Power Buzzer classes commenced. Owing to continued bad weather training interfered with. Usual routine.

### Page 6 — 9–12 October 1917

> **Villers Chatel, Oct 9** — Training carried on as well as weather conditions permit. 9th Bde Ralstons Group move from line to GAUCHIN LEGAL. One team from Hqrs Section sent to assist the Sig Sub Section with move. As yet no transport is allowed for the Arty Sub Sections Wireless working well. Business normal
>
> **10** — Warning order received for further move. No. 329873 Sgt J. M. McQuigg No 304417 Sig T. G. Selby No 349103 Sig T W Aikens No 348977. H. W Forrester awarded the Military Medal for conspicuous bravery displayed during action at Hill 70. These men maintained telephone communication under constant and heavy shell fire. Training continued Weather wet & disagreeable
>
> **11** — Training carried on. Wireless station established at new 9th Cdn Inf Bde Hqrs. Signals exchanged & working well at 2.30 P.M. Weather fine
>
> **12** — Training continued. Capt Rees proceeds on leave to England. Weather still disagreeable

### Page 7 — 13–19 October 1917

> **Villers Chatel, Oct 13** — Training continued. Usual routine.
>
> **14** — Movement order received. Company to entrain but permission granted to move No 1 Section & all mounted men by road. Mounted detachment under Lt Smith move off at 5 P.M. Party proceed by lorry to make ready new Sig Office.
>
> **15th** — Arrangements made for movement of the remainder of Coy. Usual routine
>
> **Castre, 16** — Office closed at VILLERS CHATEL at 12. P.M and opened at CASTRE at the same time. Routine business as usual.
>
> **17** — Warning order received that Cdn Corps will relieve either the II ANZAC CORPS or the X Imperial CORPS. Inspection of all equipment and stores & preparations made for going into the line
>
> **18** — Party under Lt McPherson go forward to look over lines & communications of 1st New Zealand Division. Usual routine
>
> **19** — Orders received that the 3rd Cdn Div will relieve the 1st N.Z. Div and Linemen, instruments & equipment sent forward to be ready for the take over

### Page 8 — 20–22 October 1917

> **Castre, Oct 20** — All preparations completed for the move. Usual routine
>
> **21** — Lt Beckett who had gone in advance to install Wireless & power buzzer stations on our new front, while returning to Hqrs in company with Cpl Brooks, was killed by an aeroplane bomb. Cpl Brooks was severely wounded. Lt Beckett, though only with the company [inserted above the line: "but a short time"] had shown himself to be a most energetic & capable officer. By his death this unit suffers a severe loss.
>
> **22** — The Heavy Artillery wishing two lines for observation purposes from KRONPRINZ FARM to the CEMETERY & being unwilling to risk their own men in order to maintain same [inserted above the line: "same"] through it had been pointed out how important these lines were in order that the wire might be cut for the infantry before attacking, eight men were detailed from this company in order to carry on the work. In one party which were patrolling one man No 430856 Pte HUSON was killed & No 5311999 [uncertain] Spr MALOESCHE [uncertain] 624575 Spr WITTICOMBE 625174 Spr UPCHURCH wounded. No 497352 Corp GORDON Despatch Rider also severely wounded while carrying important despatches

### Page 9 — 23–24 October 1917

> **Canal Bank, Oct 23** — Patrol of all lines. Enemy aircraft active & both buried cable and air lines blown out in several places. Party busy repairing same. The lines laid & maintained for artillery patrolled by men of this company Line was laid overland & had to be maintained under constant and heavy shelling. Linemen reported one line as having had twenty nine breaks & the other thirty one. Communication was however scarcely interrupted & artillery did good shooting. Weather wet & enemy artillery active
>
> **24** — Everything ready for the attack. Wireless station installed at KOREK & one also at CAPITCH. Divisional [crossed-out word, illegible] visual station also established at KIREK. This station to be prepared to accept all messages from the battalions attacking & to pass some back to Bde Hqrs in the event of the buried cable being shot out. Power Buzzer & Amplifier Stas also installed at KIREK and WATERLOO. Complete inspection made of the entire system of communications

### Page 10 — 25–26 October 1917

> **Canal Bank, Oct 25th** — Aeroplane contact station established. Complete inspection of all means of communication. Everything working satisfactorily and all in readiness for the attack which takes place at 5.40 A.M. Oct 26th Enemy artillery active. Weather fine. 11. P.M Enemy aircraft active. Lines to Corps bombed out. One bomb dropped at end of Canal destroyed a number of lines leading into test dugout putting us out of communication with our forward Brigades. [uncertain — reads "Remains"?] out of communication established at 11.45 P.M.
>
> **26** — Attack launched at 5:40 A.M. Lines to Brigades frequently cut by shell fire but communication never lost as two alternative routes were provided. Communication from Bdes to Battalions very difficult to maintain as enemy shelling is very heavy No 3 Section under Lt McGill & No 4 Section under Lt McKenzie doing excellent work. Communication never out more than a few minutes at a time. Weather wet & disagreeable

### Page 11 — 27–28 October 1917

> **Canal Bank, Oct 27** — Orders received that offensive will be resumed on the 30th and all busy improving existing communications and laying new lines. Enemy shelling heavy
>
> **28** — Inspection of all means of communication prior to the attack. No 4 Section relieved by No 2 Section. All communications working well. No 2 Section under Lt Frampton have telephonic communication to advanced Battalion Hqrs at Bellevue which lines though frequently cut by shell fire are quickly repaired. The work of the linemen with the different Brigade Sections and with the Div Hqrs has been beyond praise. All ranks have done splendid work. In addition to telephone communication; wireless, power buzzer, visual and pigeons are being used. Wireless however is practically useless on account of number of stations in the area and the great amount of jamming. No 2 Section under Lt McGill not using power buzzer but relying on lines & visual. The work of No 113914 Cpl Flanigan of No 4 Section during the attack on the 26th was [uncertain — an inserted word, possibly "is"] worthy of special mention & he has been recommended for the Military Medal. Enemy shelling heavy

### Page 12 — 29–31 October 1917

> **Canal Bank, Oct 29** — Completion of the line & all in readiness for the attack.
>
> **30** — Attack launched at 5.50 A.M. Communication between Div Hqrs and Bde Hqrs never interrupted, between Bde & Batt Hqrs lines frequently cut by shell fire but quickly repaired. Wireless useless & no messages attempted. Power buzzer stations though working satisfactorily before the attack were, owing to the heavy shelling which destroyed the base lines etc practically useless. Lines were maintained and Visual & pigeons were made good use of. The Div Visual station at KOREK, Sgt Taylor in charge, did exceptionally good work and a large number of important messages were handled.
>
> **31** — Busy improving existing communication. Enemy shelling heavy Weather fine
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *A. Leavitt Maj*

## Open questions / uncertain items (October 1917)
- 3 October: Sgt McCausland's number is read as 117095 here, but the same award is recorded on 28 September with the number 117025 — one of the two readings must be wrong; both are given as written.
- 21 October: Lt Beckett killed by an aeroplane bomb; Cpl Brooks severely wounded. The phrase "but a short time" is inserted above the line.
- 22 October: the sentence about the Heavy Artillery lines is garbled as written — "in order to maintain same through it had been pointed out" — "through" likely means "though", and "same" is inserted above the line. The casualty list reads: Pte Huson (No 430856) killed; Spr Maloesche [uncertain] (No 5311999 [uncertain] — seven digits, possibly 531199), Spr Witticombe (624575) and Spr Upchurch (625174) wounded; Cpl Gordon (No 497352), Despatch Rider, severely wounded.
- 24 October: the visual station place is written both "KOREK" and "KIREK" on this page (and "KOREK" again on 30 October) — evidently the same place spelled two ways. "CAPITCH" is read as written. One word before "visual station" is crossed out and illegible.
- 25 October: the sentence about communication being re-established at 11.45 P.M. begins with a word read as "Remains" [uncertain] — the sense is that communication was restored at 11.45 P.M.
- 28 October: Cpl Flanigan's number is read as 113914 here, but the same man is recorded on 3 December with the number 113014 — one of the two readings must be wrong; both are given as written. An inserted word before "worthy" is unreadable [uncertain].
- Casualties in October: 2 killed (Lt Beckett, 21 October; Pte Huson, 22 October) and 5 wounded (Cpl Brooks severely, Spr Maloesche, Spr Witticombe, Spr Upchurch, Cpl Gordon severely). The working party on 4 October escaped a gas attack and barrage without a casualty.
- Military Medals in October: Sgt N. B. McCausland (3 October); Sgt J. M. McQuigg (No 329873), Sig T. G. Selby (No 304417), Sig T. W. Aikens (No 349103), Sig H. W. Forrester (No 348977) for Hill 70 (10 October); Cpl Flanigan recommended for the Military Medal (28 October).
- Names read as: Lt S. M. Smith; Lt Beckett; Cpl Brooks; Lt McGill; Lt McKenzie; Lt Frampton; Lt McPherson; Capt Rees; Sgt Taylor; Pte Huson; Spr Maloesche [uncertain]; Spr Witticombe; Spr Upchurch; Cpl Gordon; Cpl Flanigan.
- No map coordinates are given in the diary.
