# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company — April 1916

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company, 3rd Canadian Division
- **Month:** April 1916 (Volume 5) — this volume covers 1 to 30 April 1916
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2004905 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5005, microfilm reel T-10853, file 692; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** April 1916, p. 1 – p. 6 (image files page-0022 – page-0027 of the 307-page diary)
- **April 1916 — COMPLETE** (pages 22–27 of 307): cover, contents, diary text 1–30 April, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-26.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Handwritten diary text throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Map references are given as written (Sheet 28 YPRES); no latitude/longitude coordinates are given. "O.C." = Officer Commanding. A repeated initialled mark appears in the Remarks column against most entries; it is noted in the open questions rather than repeated against every entry. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets is "3rd Canadian Division Signal Coy.", written in by hand at the top right. The "4" written beneath "Volume 5" on the cover is a microfilm/frame mark, not part of the volume number. **Note:** unlike December 1915 – March 1916, this month's section has NO handwritten title page ("Month of April 1916 / Confidential / Original Copy") — the diary sheets begin directly after the typed contents page. Also note the diary-sheet numbering restarts at Sheet No. 1 for April, after running 1–9 across December – March.

## Pages 3–6 — Diary text (1–30 April 1916)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". "Original" is written by hand at the top left of each sheet. The printed footer reads "T.2134. Wt. W708—776. 500000. 4/15. Sir J. C. & S."

### Page 3 — 1–4 April 1916 (Sheet No. 1)

> **MONTH OF APRIL 1916.**
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 1st.** — Major S.E. Powers returned from leave. Appointed Officer Commanding 3rd Canadian Division Signal Company temporarily. Advanced report centre heavily shelled.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 2nd.** — Decided to change position of Advanced Report Centre on account of heavy shelling. Weather; Fine and warm.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 3rd.** — New position for advanced report centre approved and work on change begun. Location of new position H22 A 05 [uncertain — "H22 A 05"?] (sheet 28 Ypres, Belgium). Rain.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 4th.** — All lines to RIGHT BRIGADE and most to LEFT BRIGADE cut by shell fire. New metallic return line to Right Brigade looped. Rain.

### Page 4 — 5–16 April 1916 (Sheet No. 2)

> **MONTH OF APRIL 1916 — contd. #2.**
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 5th.** — Work of changing Report Centre completed. Looping of lines 25 and 27 continued. Rain.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 6th to 7th.** — Repaired permanent route and continued work on lines Nos. 26 and 27.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 8th.** — Began pegging all overland routes forward. Rain.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 9th.** — Pegging and looping of lines forward continued.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 10th to 15th.** — Pegging and looping of lines continued. Also renumbered lines and began labelling same. Rain every day.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 16th.** — Looping of telephone lines completed. Survey of country begun for new buried route to forward brigades.

### Page 5 — 17–26 April 1916 (Sheet No. 3)

> **MONTH OF APRIL 1916 — contd. #3.**
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 17th to 19th.** — Rest brigade position changed [uncertain — "Rest"?]. Location now is G11 C 6.4 (sheet 28, YPRES, BELGIUM). Lines laid to new camp. Rain.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 20th to 25th.** — Completed pegging up overland lines. Staked out buried route as far as YSER CANAL. Repaired trestle route lines for Artillery. Buried work from brigade headquarters to battalion headquarters staked out and begun. Also a buried system for artillery groups and batteries laid out as far as battery headquarters.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 25th.** — Began new buried route six to seven feet deep for all infantry and artillery lines. Greatly hampered by water. Work done during night.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 26th.** — Continued work on buried route. Weather, fine.

### Page 6 — 27–30 April 1916 (Sheet No. 4)

> **MONTH OF APRIL 1916. contd. #4.**
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 27th.** — Considerable progress made on new route YPRES-ZILLEBEKE-TRENCHES. Ground drying. Less water encountered.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 28th.** — Relief night. No working party. Heavy shelling during day in area between KRUSTRAAT ROAD and forward brigades.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 29th.** — Weather fine and very warm. Burying of cable resumed.
>
> **RENINGHELST, April 30th.** — Sunday. No duty parties. Lines patrolled. Seventyfive yards cable buried during night 30/1 May.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *S. E. Powers Major / O.C. 3rd Cdn Div Signals* — the signature line is partly cut off at the right edge of the scan.

## Open questions / uncertain items (April 1916)
- The repeated mark in the Remarks column is the same initialled mark as in December 1915 – March 1916, read as "J.H." [uncertain].
- 3 April: the map reference for the new Advanced Report Centre position is read as "H22 A 05" [uncertain] — the figures are written close together and could read "H22A05".
- 17–19 April: "Rest brigade position changed" — "Rest" [uncertain] is read as written; it likely means the brigade in rest (out of the line) rather than a numbered brigade.
- 28 April: "KRUSTRAAT ROAD" is read as written.
- The signature line at the foot of Sheet No. 4 is partly cut off at the right edge of the scan.
- 25 April appears twice: once inside the 20th–25th block and once as its own entry ("Began new buried route…"); both are transcribed as written.
- No casualties are recorded in this volume.
