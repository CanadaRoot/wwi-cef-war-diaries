## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Handwritten diary text throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY"). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. No map coordinates are given in the diary; places are recorded as written. The diarist's spelling "YEOMENRY" (June) and "RENINGHELST" (Reninghelst) are kept as written. "H.Q." = Headquarters; "Bde." = Brigade; "Infy." = Infantry; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "D.R." = Despatch Rider; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "O.R." = Other Ranks. A repeated initialled mark in the Remarks column, read as "J.E.P." [uncertain], appears against most entries; it is noted here rather than repeated against every entry. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets reads "3rd Canadian Division Signal Coy."

## Pages 3–8 — Diary text (1–31 May 1916)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". Heading on each sheet: "3rd Canadian Division Signal Coy." Each sheet is marked "Original" at the top left and numbered, top right, No. I to No. VI. Place throughout: RENINGHELST.

### Page 3 — 1–4 May 1916

> **RENINGHELST, May 1st** — Attack on front of 49th and 52nd battalions five pm and eight pm repulsed. Working party shelled on Dickebusch Road. Good progress made with buried work.
>
> **2nd** — Lines from Battle Headquarters to LILLE GATE (at YPRES) inspected. Considerable shelling. Burying of cable carried on with during day and night. Heavy electric storm during afternoon with much rain.
>
> **3rd** — Tests made with new "FULLER" phones, for use in advanced stations. Fairly quiet forward in shelled area. Cable burying work continued. Buried route between RIGHT and LEFT Infantry Brigades (for lateral communication) completed.
>
> **4th** — Arranged communication to new position of Gas Alarm system. Weather continued. [Remarks column: Weather, Hot.] *Continued on page 4.*

### Page 4 — 4–8 May 1916

> **RENINGHELST, May 4th (continued)** — Rearrangement of routes of telegraph and telephone lines and work on buried routes continued.
>
> **5th** — Arranged to change location of Signal Office at RENINGHELST on account of present position being dangerous. Progress in work on buried cable.
>
> **6th** — Forward lines to LEFT Infantry Brigade cut by shell fire. Progress on buried work good.
>
> **7th** — Secured hut for use as a new Signal Office. Visit from Director of Army Signals who reported on condition of communications. Poor progress on buried route. Unable to finish night's task on account of difficult nature of ground.
>
> **8th** — No work on buried route. Party not warned. Heavy rain. Quiet on our front.

### Page 5 — 9–15 May 1916

> **RENINGHELST, May 9th** — Commenced building of line to new camp of battalions in divisional reserve and [uncertain — "Claxton"?] from. Lines on permanent route cut by shrapnel at 6.30pm. Working again at 7.30pm. Work on burying of cable cancelled on account of heavy rain.
>
> **10th** — Hut for signal office erected at east end of village. Lines at BATTLE HEADQUARTERS cut by shellfire at four pm. Communication kept up by use of alternate "trestle" route. Heavy shelling around signal Battle Headquarters during evening.
>
> **11th, 12th, 13th** — Good progress made on buried routes, except in RIGHT infantry brigade area. Working parties too small and failed to find [uncertain — "relief"?] on 12th inst.
>
> **14th, 15th** — Relief nights. One party of seventy men only available on main buried route. Quiet on front. Communication good.

### Page 6 — 16–20 May 1916

> **RENINGHELST, May 16th** — Buried route on right sector has had work on it held up three nights on account shortage of "quad" cable. Progress on LEFT SECTOR and MAIN routes satisfactory. Eight lines cut by shell-fire on brigade route. Communication was not interrupted.
>
> **17th** — All lines working well. Reported poor communication to Field Companies (Engineer). Proved to be inattention on part of operator. Buried work proceeding.
>
> **18th, 19th** — All lines O.K. Quiet in this area. Buried work good.
>
> **20th** — Buried lines in LEFT SECTOR cut by grave-digging party. All other good. Fair progress on all buried routes, except in RIGHT "Infantry" and RIGHT "Artillery" groups, where work has been abandoned on account of shortage of men.

### Page 7 — 21–24 May 1916

> **RENINGHELST, May 21st** — Quiet on this front. Communications good. Work on burying of cable progressing, except no working party RIGHT GROUP "Artillery" and RIGHT SECTOR "Infantry".
>
> **22nd** — Working party RIGHT group "artillery" and RIGHT SECTOR "infantry" again failed to appear. Satisfactory progress made on other routes.
>
> **23rd** — Heavy hostile shelling in afternoon and evening. All lines cut by shellfire, except one sounder and one telephone pair to LEFT BRIGADE and lateral lines to RIGHT infantry brigade. Artillery lines O.K. Communication was never cut, though lines were crowded, nearly one thousand telegrams and despatches handled in Headquarters office.
>
> **24th** — All lines in good working order by early morning this date. Satisfactory progress on all buried lines. Work resumed on RIGHT "Artillery" group lines and RIGHT "Infantry" brigade.

### Page 8 — 25–31 May 1916

> **RENINGHELST, May 25th** — Heavy shelling on both sides. All lines working well. Rained heavily during evening. Work on buried route as usual.
>
> **26th** — Surveyed route for continuation of seven foot buried cable scheme as far as "LILLE" GATE (YPRES). Quiet in this area. Work on buried route as usual.
>
> **27th** — All lines working well. Improvements have been made in wireless and pigeon service. Buried work going well.
>
> **28th, 29th, 30th** — Work on buried cable route held up for lack of cable. Weather fine. Hostile shelling very heavy on 29th. All lines working well.
>
> **31st** — Weather fine & very warm. Heavy shelling at YPRES. No work on main buried route.
>
> [Signed, bottom right:] *J. E. Powers Major / O.C. 3rd Can. Div. Signals*

## Open questions / uncertain items (May 1916)
- 9 May: "divisional reserve and [uncertain — 'Claxton'?] from" — the word after "and" reads like "Claxton" but is not certain; the sentence as written does not parse cleanly.
- 11th–13th: "failed to find [uncertain — 'relief'?] on 12th inst." — the word after "find" reads like "relief".
- 4 May: the Remarks column for this entry reads "Weather, Hot."; the entry is continued on page 4 ("May 4th (CONTINUED)").
- The repeated mark in the Remarks column is read as "J.E.P." [uncertain] — apparently the diarist's initials; it matches the signature "J. E. Powers".
- No casualties are named in this volume; no map coordinates are given.
- Names read as: Maj. J. E. Powers, O.C. 3rd Canadian Divisional Signal Company; the Director of Army Signals (7 May, unnamed).
