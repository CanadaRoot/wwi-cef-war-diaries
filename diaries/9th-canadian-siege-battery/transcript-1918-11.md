## Notes

- **Date coverage:** Entries exist for 1–14, 17, 18 and 30 November 1918. **No entries for 15, 16 or 19–29 November.** The 9–10, 13–14 and 17–18 November entries are each a single entry covering two braced dates.
- **Casualties:** 4 November — No. 2001329 Gunner T. A. HARRIS seriously wounded by enemy shelling of the Valenciennes–Mons road in St. Saulve, and later died of wounds. Enemy gas shells into billets in St. Saulve the same day caused damage and casualties to the civilian population (number not given).
- **Armistice:** 11 November — the diary records only the ceasefire message received that morning: "Hostilities will cease at 11.00 hours on the 11th instant. Troops will stand fast on lines reached at that hour, which will be reported to Corps Headquarters. Censor precautions will be maintained. There will be no intercourse of any description with the enemy. Further instructions will follow. Prowse." No comment or celebration is recorded. The battery had been silent since 8 November, awaiting orders to proceed forward, and moved to Jemappes on 12 November.
- **Messages:** 2 November — message from G.O.C. 10th Canadian Infantry Brigade, signed T. M. ROSS, thanking the battery for its artillery support the previous day: "My people say they never saw anything finer."
- **Places and map references:** HERIN (D22.a.9.9); FAUBOURG DE PARIS, Valenciennes (E.8.c.30.20); ST. SAULVE (W30d.3.8); ONNAING (X16.c); JEMAPPES (Place de la Citadelle); VALENCIENNES; VALENCIENNES–MONS road; and numerous map references (F8.c.7.d, E11.a.1.b, E17c, E23b.7.9, E17.d.0.2, E17.d.0.0, E10.b.0.0, F.9.b.2.6, F15a-1-5, F15b.0.1, F.15d.1.3, F10 CENTRAL, F5a Central, X28a, X22c, X22d.6.0, S.21.a, S11.c, S.7.a, S11.a, S12.c, M22.c.00.20, M33a.20.80, M.15, M.21, M36a, M29d, M30d, M36b, M.30.c, M30.b+d, M9.c.2.8, M8d.7.5, M8c.9.05).
- **Oddities:** The cover is handwritten in ink on lined paper (unlike the printed covers of earlier months) and reads "Volume 21". Contents page states "Diary Text 4 Pages" — the diary text is indeed 4 pages (pp. 3–6 of this month's file). The 3 November entry runs over two pages. The Brigade Order number in the 3 November entry has been heavily crossed out and is illegible, and one word in the 11 November entry is crossed out. The 30 November entry is the only entry for the last twelve days of the month, and the rest of p. 6 is blank. The month-end signature is in faint pencil and the name cannot be read; the rank appears to be Captain — a change from Major J. A. Stewart, who signed the September 1917 diary.
