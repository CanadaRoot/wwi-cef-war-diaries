# War Diary Transcript — 9th Canadian Siege Battery, October 1917

- **Unit:** 9th Canadian Siege Battery
- **LAC Item ID:** 2004802, RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4977, file 576, reel T-10805
- **Month:** 1–31 October 1917
- **Pages transcribed:** 8 (diary pages 1–8; LAC images 56–63)
- **Volume:** 8 (cover reads "VOLUME 8", with "6" written below in pencil)
- **Commanding Officer / signatory:** Major S. A. Heward, R.C.A., O.C. No. 9 Can. Siege By. R.C.C.A. (signature at end of 31 October entry; the printed rank line reads "R.C.C.A."). Heward was on leave 8–22 October, with Captain H. R. N. Cobbett in temporary command; Heward resumed command on 22 October. (In September the battery was commanded by Major J. A. Stewart, so command changed between the two months.)
- **Place:** Cité de Caumont to 18 October, then on the move to Belgium, then Ypres from 20 October.

Transcription conventions: original spelling and punctuation kept as far as readable. Anything illegible or uncertain is marked [uncertain]. The "Remarks and references to Appendices" column on each diary page carries the initials "JAS" against most entries. The diarist writes the word "to" in map-reference ranges as a small superscript "tᵒ", and writes "½" before a target name where half a target is meant (e.g. "½ Hostile Battery", "½ CITE du NORD").

| Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cité de Caumont | 18-10-17 (Contd.) | | that at hour [uncertain] our Stores and kit were loaded on lorries in a heavy rain storm. The whole column proceeded by road through NOEUX-le-MINES and BETHUNE to HAZEBROUCK, where we arrived at about 4.15 pm. We left HAZE- |
| —"— | 19-10-17 | | Brouck at 9 pm and proceeded to WATOU on the BELGIAN border, where we arrived at about 1.45 am. We remained here for some time and left for YPRES at 11.45 am |
| —"— | 20-10-17 | | the 20th. We arrived at YPRES at 2.15 pm. and during the afternoon took over the gun stores etc from No. 112 SIEGE BATTERY. |
| YPRES. | 21-10-17 | | At dawn our gun detachments relieved the detachments of No. 112 S.B., and immediately went into action. The battery position is now C.30c 11.15 [uncertain] on the YPRES-ZONNEBEKE road, and the gun crews off duty occupy billets in YPRES. The vicinity of these billets were shelled throughout the day with enemy H.V. guns, and bombed in the evening by enemy airmen. One casualty was caused by the latter, Gunner C. WARING being wounded. ## Page 7 (October 1917, p. 7) — [diary text, Army Form C. 2118]

| Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information |
|---|---|---|---|
| YPRES. | 26-10-17 | | This has been our heaviest day since arriving in the area. In conjunction with an attack by the 3rd and 4th CANADIAN divisions, we fired an attack barrage from 6 am to 9 am. Throughout the day several concentration shoots and 3 calls on S.O.S lines were carried out. In all, 1031 rounds were fired, and the gun detachments carried out their tasks in an excellent manner. The battery position was lightly shelled during the period, without damage or casualties. |
| —"— | 27-10-17 | | A final protective barrage was fired at 5.20 am, 84 rounds being fired. PASSCHENDAELE CHURCH at D 6 d. 31.45 was fired on for Calibration, 30 rounds. A satisfactory shoot on trench D.12 a 75.50 to D 12 d 10.15 was made, and 165 rds were fired. Minor tasks were carried out from 5.35 to 7.00 pm, 35 rounds being expended. During this period the HAMBURG O.P and vicinity was heavily shelled by enemy artillery, and A/Cpl J. SMITH and Gunner L.C. ASTON were severely wounded, while keeping telephone communications in repair. Billets again attacked by enemy airmen, but no damage done. |
| —"— | 28-10-17 | | At 9 am this morning, hostile shelling commenced, and continued throughout the day becoming very intense between 5 pm and 6 pm. Many rounds burst in Battery position, but no damage was done. The detachments took what little cover was available, and there were no casualties. An aeroplane shoot had to be abandoned owing to poor visibility, but 143 rounds were fired on various targets and on S.O.S lines, also Concentrations (No 5 + 9). EVASIVE FARM, E 8 c. 88.90. was also shelled by us. Many improvements have been made to battery position by working parties, but much remains yet to be done. |
| —"— | 29-10-17 | | No firing till afternoon, but constructive work was carried on with the aid of an infantry working party. A barrage was laid down at 2.30 pm. No. 2 gun goes out of action. At 5.30 pm a shoot on wire in front of HAELLEN TRENCH was carried out, 30 rounds being fired. Night firing consisted of road searching on Roads to WRATH and EXERT FARMS. The battery position was shelled, and several 5.9" burst near the guns. The billets and vicinity received the usual attention from enemy airmen. |

## Notes

- **Date coverage:** Entries exist for 1–8, 19–31 October 1917. **No separate daily entries for 9–17 October** — the period 8–18 October is covered by a single summary entry (dated "8-10-17 to 18-10-17") recording that operations had practically ceased owing to the removal of most artillery from the sector and an apparent scarcity of ammunition, with the battery firing no more than about 50 rounds a day.
- **Move to the Ypres sector:** On the night of 17–18 October word was received to move to Belgium. The battery marched from Cité de Caumont on 18 October via Hersin, Noeux-les-Mines, Bethune and Hazebrouck to Watou on the Belgian border, then on to Ypres, arriving 20 October and relieving No. 112 Siege Battery on 21 October. The new battery position was C.30c 11.15 [uncertain] on the Ypres–Zonnebeke road.
- **Casualties:** 21 October — Gunner C. WARING wounded by enemy airmen bombing the billets at Ypres. 27 October — A/Cpl J. SMITH and Gunner L. C. ASTON severely wounded by shelling at the Hamburg O.P. while keeping telephone communications in repair. No deaths are recorded in October.
- **Decorations:** 30 October — a recommendation for the Military Cross has been forwarded to H.Q. for an officer of the battery, and recommendations for the Military Medal for one N.C.O. and one man, all in connection with duty at the Hamburg O.P. on 27 October (no names given).
- **Command:** Major S. A. Heward on leave to England 8–22 October; Captain H. R. N. Cobbett in temporary command. Heward resumed command 22 October and signed the month. Lieut E. S. Hoare commanded the rear party left behind at Cité de Caumont. Lieut M. A. Wilson joined for duty 25 October. Lieut Starr arrived 30 October with the advance party of the new (No. 3) section; the remainder joined 31 October, increasing battery strength by one officer and 54 other ranks.
- **Heaviest day:** 26 October — 1,031 rounds fired in support of the attack by the 3rd and 4th Canadian Divisions (attack barrage 6–9 am, plus concentration shoots and three S.O.S. calls). 30 October — a further attack by the 3rd and 4th Canadian Divisions; Crest Farm reported demolished.
- **Places and map references:** Cité de Caumont; NOYELLES; MERICOURT; CITE du NORD; LENS; CANADA ROAD; HERSIN; NOEUX-le-MINES; BETHUNE; HAZEBROUCK; WATOU; YPRES; YPRES-ZONNEBEKE road; WRATH FARM; EXERT FARMS; EVASIVE FARM; CREST FARM; PASSCHENDAELE CHURCH; HAELLEN TRENCH; HAMBURG O.P.; FLAME (target); and trench-map references N 28 a, N 36 c 20.15, N 22 c, T 6 a 15.65, N 17 d. 70.60, T.5.a 1.8, N 2.36 and 38, T.6.a.55.70, T 5 a 1.8, N 20 d 80.83, N 2 a, D 116.70.40 to 25.70, D 11 b 25.70, D 6 a 40.15, D.6.c 95.75 to d.0.0.22 [uncertain], D.6.b.50.35 [uncertain] to E1.c 40.60, D 6 d. 31.45, D.12 a 75.50 to D 12 d 10.15, E 8 c. 88.90.
- **Oddities:** Cover is stamped CONFIDENTIAL and marked "VOLUME 8" with a pencilled "6" beneath. Contents page states "Diary Text 6 Pages" — the diary text is indeed 6 pages (pp. 3–8 of this month's file). The 8–18 October entry is dated across three lines in the date column. The 18 October entry continues onto page 5 with the word "that" inserted above the line, and the 19 and 20 October entries both begin mid-sentence. The 24 October entry begins without a capital letter, and "LIEUT M.A. WILSON joins for duty." is written below the printed footer at the foot of page 6. The battery position reference "C.30c 11.15" is written with the two pairs of digits stacked one above the other; the first pair reads "11" but could be "14". The word after "at" in the 18 October continuation reads "hour" but makes no clear sense in context. The second map reference in the 24 October entry ("d.0.0.22") does not parse as a standard trench-map reference.
