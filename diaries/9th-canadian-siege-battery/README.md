# 9th Canadian Siege Battery — diary notes

- **LAC record:** Item ID 2004802, "War diaries - 9th Canadian Siege Battery", RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 4977, file 576, microfilm reel T-10805. 175 page images, 6 February 1917 – 28 February 1919, in 24 monthly volumes.
- **This diary (transcribed):** all 175 pages, 24 monthly transcripts (`transcript-1917-02.md` … `transcript-1919-02.md`), 560 dated index entries in `INDEX-F.md`. The February 1917 volume covers 6 Feb – 30 Mar 1917, so there is no separate March 1917 volume.
- **What the diary is:** handwritten Army Form C.2118 diary of a 6-inch 26 cwt siege battery (1st Brigade, Canadian Garrison Artillery). Mobilized at Halifax, sailed 27 Sep 1916 aboard LACONIA, arrived England 6 Oct 1916; the diary opens at Lydd, 6 Feb 1917, with mobilization orders for Codford. Arrived in France 22 Mar 1917 (Havre); took over guns from No. 3 Canadian Siege Battery 17–18 Apr 1917 and went into action in the Lens/Avion sector. Served through Vimy (Apr 1917), Hill 70 (Aug 1917), Passchendaele (Oct–Nov 1917, Ypres), the German spring offensive and the Hundred Days (Amiens sector, then the advance to Valenciennes–Mons, Nov 1918). After the Armistice the battery moved to Jemappes (12 Nov 1918); the diary ends 28 Feb 1919 at Jemappes with the single line "No events of importance." No demobilisation note.
- **Command:** Capt. H. R. Cobbett (Feb–May 1917); Maj. J. A. Stewart (Jun–Sep 1917); Maj. S. A. Heward (Oct 1917, on leave 8–22 Oct with Capt. Cobbett in temporary command); Maj. W. J. Stewart (Aug–Oct 1918 — a different signature/initials from J. A. Stewart; whether the two Stewarts are the same man is NOT resolved in the transcripts); Maj. W. G. Scully (assumed command 13 Jul 1918); Capt. A. T. Seaman (a/O.C. from 30 May 1918, vice A/Capt. J. E. Lean to 1st Can. Siege Bty). The Feb 1919 volume is signed by an illegible Captain.
- **Notable items:** 1 Jul 1917 — Canadian Corps Artillery fired 3 salvos to commemorate Dominion Day. 26 Oct 1917 — heaviest day at Passchendaele: 1,031 rounds fired in support of the 3rd and 4th Canadian Divisions. 6 Sep 1917 — at least 5,000 gas shells fired into the battery's village in one night; only 3 men gassed. 11 Nov 1918 — the Armistice message is copied verbatim; no comment or celebration recorded. 20 Dec 1917 — inspected by Lieut.-General Sir A. W. Currie; 23 Dec 1917 — honours presented by Brig.-General Massie, including the Military Medal to Bomb. H. W. Horwill.
- **Casualties:** 14 killed/died of wounds, ~46 wounded/gassed in the battery's own diary. Worst single day: 13 May 1917 — a shell burst in the battery cook house, 6 N.C.O.s and men killed and 7 wounded (none named). Worst day for named casualties: 15 Apr 1918 — Bombr. J. T. Wentzell killed; Gnrs. E. C. Parslow, C. G. Leroux and J. Barclay died of wounds; Lieut. E. A. MacKenzie and 5 other ranks wounded. The diary also records casualties of neighbouring units (No. 3 Canadian Siege Battery, 5 killed and 6 wounded, 18 and 28 Jun 1918; 2 A.S.C. drivers wounded 28 Sep 1918) and civilian casualties at St. Saulve, 4 Nov 1918.
- **Decorations:** Military Medals to Gnr. T. Makin (No. 1257620, presented 14 Jun 1917 by Gen. Massie), A/Cpl J. B. Troop (No. 1257633) and Bdr. H. W. Horwill (No. 1257518, Routine Order No. 46, 12 Sep 1917); Sgt. W. Rawling (No. 1257506, Routine Order No. 1550, for 25 Nov 1917).
- **Uncertain / open items:**
  - 135 [uncertain] flags across the 24 transcripts (84 in the index entries) — mostly map references and a few names. No names were guessed.
  - The Bombardier wounded 15 Aug 1917 is written "Norwill" on the casualty page and "Horwill" in the entry continuation; "Horwill" is used (confirmed by the M.M. award). The 27 Oct 1917 casualty is written "Aston" but the November M.M. entry writes "Acton" (Gnr L. C. Acton, No. 1257597); both spellings kept as written.
  - The battery's position is spelled "Cité de Gaumont" Apr–Jun 1917 and "Cité de Caumont" from July 1917, as written.
  - The June 1917 signature appears to read "J. A. Stewart" but could read "Steward" [uncertain]; the August–October 1918 signature reads "W. J. Stewart" with initials "W.J.S." — not reconciled with J. A. Stewart ("JAS").
  - 28 May 1917 entry is dated but its text space is blank. 31 Oct 1918 has a date line and signature only, no entry.
  - December 1918 and February 1919 are each covered by a single month-long summary entry; January 1919 by five grouped entries; no daily entries exist for those months.
  - No map coordinates are given in the diary (map references are sheet/grid references, e.g. S.24d, U.8.b.11.9, transcribed as written).
