## Page 3 — Diary text (Army Form C.2118)

### 1 June 1916 — St. Eloi Front

The frontage held by the 5th Canadian Infantry Brigade was split into two parts; the left half from the CANAL to the LILLE ROAD, and the right half from the LILLE ROAD to trench 9. The 5½ T.M. Battery covered the left half and 5½ [uncertain] the right half. In the left sector defensive positions were taken up with by our Stokes guns one in ESTAMET LANE C.T. at O.3.b.6.6, two in sap off SHELLY LANE C.T. at O.2.b.6.3. The 4" guns of this battery were left at H.Q. in VOORMEZELE as there was a shortage of ammunition for this type of mortar. The 4" R.M.L. for the right half. 5-9 [uncertain] R.M.L. mortars were used and [one word crossed out — illegible] [uncertain] Stokes were used. The 4" mortars were left in the front line and thus ready to retaliate for hostile bombing which was particularly active on this front. The Stokes during the day were kept at S.R.P [uncertain] or N.6.d.11 and during the night through special permission from the G.O.C. 3rd [uncertain] Bde. were set up in front line trenches on No 2 Crater or O.2.b.9.6. a point from which an attack might be launched into No 1 Crater.

### 2 June 1916 — ditto

Officers and N.C.O's spent considerable time reconnoitring their front. Under cover of artillery fire the following points were registered No 2 Crater, Piccadilly Fm., and the sap head at O.3.b.7.1.

### 3 June 1916 — ditto

A new system of relief tried out in the right sector. The battery was split into two parts, a day and night shift which allowed an N.C.O and four men for two guns. In this way the men stood to during their tour of duty and spent their time cleaning the bombs, and improving the bomb stores and emplacements.

### 4 June 1916 — ditto

Germans more active than usual with their trench mortars. By means of one Stokes mortar firing into crater 4 and artillery firing on their T.M. positions near Piccadilly Fm our retaliation in conjunction with that from the medium Trench mortars soon silenced them. Lt Johnstone [uncertain] 5½ Battery and two N.C.O's return from the Stokes gun course at TERDEGHEM.

### 5 June 1916 — ditto

Nil.

## Page 4 — Diary text (Army Form C.2118)

### 6 June 1916 — St. Eloi

The 4" mortars in right half did good work firing on the german T.M. which were active in the evening. Lt Wayfield [uncertain] from 3rd Bde T.M. Batteries reconnoitres part of line and takes over from us

### 7 June 1916 — RENNINGHELST CAMP

Relief complete by 8.30 A.M. Delay caused by the infantry relief jamming the trenches in ESTAMET LANE. Four new Stokes Mortars Mk II arrive to replace the 4" mortars. Lieut. Morrison and 4 N.C.O's go up to reconnoitre the new trenches in the neighbourhood of ZILLEBEKE.

### 8 June 1916 — Trenches near Zillebeke

Lieut. Morrison killed by shell in SQUARE WOOD. Men taken up in motor lorries as far as YPRES. 5½ [uncertain] battery takes up front including Hill 60 and 5½ [uncertain] the new system of trenches as far as MAPLE COPSE. Relief delayed by germans shelling the C.T.

### 9 June 1916 — ditto

In the left sector the guns were distributed as follows:— 2 in SQUARE WOOD covering ARMAGH Ho and our front line and 2 in support trench in rear of RUDKIN Ho covering our front line in case of an attack. Those in SQUARE WOOD were heavily shelled, two bomb stores being blown up. After reconnoitring the front well it was decided to sit [uncertain] them up in the front line behind ARMAGH WOOD.

### 10 June 1916 — ditto

The G.O.C objected to the Stokes being kept in front for fear of being captured so a position near KNOLL Fm was taken up. From this position part of our front line, SQUARE WOOD, and FOSS WAY C.T. could be bombarded in case of a german advance. Shelling during the night and day was very heavy in retaliation for our artillery activity.

### 11 June 1916 — ditto

Relieved by 2nd T.M. Battery (4 guns) and 3rd T.M. Battery (4 guns). All guns were left in the line and arrangements made to get 8 guns in return. Early relief. Buses [uncertain] transportation to RENNINGHELST Camp A (1st Cdn Div. Area)

## Page 5 — Diary text (Army Form C.2118)

### 12 June 1916 — Hill 60 & ZILLEBEKE

Canadians again lost trenches at ZILLEBEKE. Guns are parked on limber borrowed from the 5th Brigade M.G. Coy. and arrangements made to move off on 10 minutes notice.

### 14 June 1916 — ditto

5½ Bde T.M. Batteries relieve the 2½ T.M. Battery and the 3½ T.M. Battery. The guns are left in the line as usual and are distributed as follows. Hill 60: 3, Bde. H.Q.: 2, and Rudkin Ho.: 3, Total. 8. Those at Rudkin Ho being to far back and also being subject to heavy shell fire were moved to Valley Cottages until a more suitable location could be found. Work started deepening the emplacements at Hill 60.

### 15 June 1916 — ditto

at 2.30 pm 18 rounds were fired from Hill 60 in retaliation for german rifle grenades. The name 6th Trench Mortar Battery given in place of 5½ and 6½ Trench Mortar Battery. [uncertain — the exact wording of the renaming is unclear] Lieut. R.M. Fair appointed O.C. Defensive position chosen in german front line at I 2.4.d.2.3. where two guns were set up, a bomb store built and filled with 90 Stokes Bombs.

### 16 June 1916 — ditto

Shelling very heavy during afternoon and night during which time we fired 60 Stokes bombs on artillery O.P. on HILL 60. This prevented work being done in left sector, bombs being carried to HILL 60.

### 17 June 1916 — ditto

After reconnaissance it was decided not to place any more guns in the line until the trenches were built up better. 100 bombs carried to position in HILL 60. A few rounds fired in retaliation to rifle grenades.

### 18 June 1916 — ditto

100 more bombs carried to HILL 60. Bomb Store in old german support trench at I 2.4.d.0.1 was filled in case the Stokes guns were called upon for retaliation on that front. An emplacement in HILL 60 completed, having lowered it 2 ft. and added 4 ft of head cover.

## Page 6 — Diary text (Army Form C.2118)

[Page heading reads "6th Trench Mortar Battery" — see Notes.]

### 19 June 1916 — HILL 60 & ZILLEBEKE

Fired 6 rounds in retaliation for rifle grenades, one round blowing up a rifle grenade store. Germans retaliated with H.E.

### 20 June 1916 — ditto

Stopped Minenwerfer fire on our front & support lines on HILL 60 by firing 24 rds in the neighbourhood of its emplacement. Relieved by 2nd T.M. Battery which had only 4 guns. Four guns were taken out of the line leaving 3 at HILL 60. and one in the left sector. Taken to RENNINGHELST by bus.

### 21 June 1916 — RENNINGHELST

Nil.

### 22 June 1916 — ditto

Nil.

### 23 June 1916 — ditto

Battery moves into a camp separate from the 5th Bde M.G. Coy. This camp situated at G.27.d.8.2. was not in use having been occupied by Corp. troops.

### 24 June 1916 — ditto

Men kept very busy repairing and improving the new camp. Kitchen and oven built.

### 25 June 1916 — ditto

Church parade held with 5th Bde. M.G. Company. Brigade operation orders were cancelled. 1 N.C.O. detailed to Divisional School as instructor.

### 26 June 1916 — ditto

No. 65453 Cpl. Burke v. [uncertain] detailed to attend a course of instruction on Stokes Mortar at Army Corps School TERDEGHAM [uncertain]. He proceeded to the school 1 p.m. by bus.

## Notes

- **Date coverage:** The June volume covers 1–26 June 1916 only; 27–30 June are not in this volume (presumably in the next volume). **13 June has no entry** — the diary jumps from 12 June to 14 June with no explanation.
- **Casualty:** **Lieut. Morrison was killed by a shell in Square Wood on 8 June 1916** — the only fatality recorded in these two months. (Morrison had returned from leave on 6 May, when Lt Lefebre [uncertain] replaced him, and had gone up to reconnoitre the Zillebeke trenches on 7 June.) No other ranks casualties are recorded in June.
- **Officers named:** Lt R. M. Fair (O.C. from 15 June; signed every June entry as "R.M. Fair, Lieut., O.C."), Lt Morrison (killed 8 June), Lt L. H. Johnston (spelled "Johnstone" [uncertain] on 4 June; returned from the Stokes gun course at Terdeghem), Lt Wayfield [uncertain] (3rd Bde T.M. Batteries, 6 June).
- **Other ranks named:** Cpl. Burke, No. 65453 (detailed to the Stokes Mortar course at Army Corps School, Terdeghem, 26 June).
- **Movements:** St Eloi Front 1–6 June; relieved 7 June to Renninghelst Camp; trenches near Zillebeke / Hill 60 8–11 June; relieved 11 June to Renninghelst Camp A (1st Cdn Div. Area); back in the line at Hill 60 / Zillebeke 12–20 June; relieved 20 June to Renninghelst; separate camp at map reference G.27.d.8.2 from 23 June.
- **Renaming:** The 15 June entry records that the battery was given the name "6th Trench Mortar Battery" in place of the 5th and 6th batteries (wording unclear — see [uncertain] flag), and the diary heading on the final page changes from "5th Trench Mortar Battery" to "6th Trench Mortar Battery". Lieut. R. M. Fair was appointed O.C. the same day.
- **Other notable items:** Four new Stokes Mortars Mk II arrived 7 June to replace the 4-inch mortars. The 4-inch guns had been left at H.Q. in Voormezeele for lack of ammunition (1 June). Heavy fighting around Hill 60 and Zillebeke 8–20 June: two bomb stores blown up in Square Wood (9 June), 60 Stokes bombs fired 16 June, a German rifle-grenade store blown up 19 June, and Minenwerfer fire stopped by 24 rounds on 20 June. The Canadians "again lost trenches at Zillebeke" on 12 June.
- **Place names:** "Noormegule" in the May diary is spelled "VOORMEZELE" here (1 June), confirming the same place. "TERDEGHAM" (26 June) is the same place spelled "TERDEGHEM" on 4 June.
- **No appendices** are listed for June; the Remarks column carries only the officer's signature on each line.
