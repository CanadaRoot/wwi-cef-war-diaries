## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is not struck through on these pages.
- The Place column is written once per page; it is repeated in the tables below for each entry on that page. A ditto mark (") in the Place column is expanded to the repeated place.
- The remarks column is initialled throughout with a circled two-letter monogram that appears to read "P.S." — the reading is not certain; it matches the initials of Capt. P. Smallland, who signs the 28 February entry.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; map-sheet references (e.g. "Sheet LIEGE 7") are transcribed as written.
- "Admitted … Animals to M.V.S." is the section's daily intake of sick or wounded animals. "D.A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Veterinary Services. "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General. "A.A.+ Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General. "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column. "R.A.S.C." = Royal Army Service Corps.

## Notes — February 1919
- The section stayed at Huy, Belgium, all month. The work is now demobilisation and disposal: the diary records demobilisation questionnaires (8 February) and nominal rolls (18 February), the return of veterinary equipment, supplies, transport and the ambulance to the Ordnance Officer, 1st Canadian Division (23 and 27 February), and the unit's horses entraining for Brussels for disposal to the Belgian Government (28 February, authority Q 55-G dated 20 February 1919). Animals unfit for further service were destroyed for butchery almost daily: cases 114, 121 and 122 (3 February), case 160 (10 February), cases 174, 175 and 176 (13 February), cases 179, 182 and 180 (17 February), 6 animals (21 February), 3 (22 February), 4 (24 February), 3 (25 February), case 200 (26 February, sold for butchery) and 3 (28 February).
- Transfers to the C.C.V.E.S. continued: 34 horses and 12 mules on 7 February, 17 horses and 2 mules on 11 February, 4 horses on 17 February.
- The horse ambulance collected cases from Braives (No. 179, 4th Canadian Infantry Battalion, 13 February), Viemme (No. 182, 2nd Canadian Field Ambulance, 14 February) and Warnant (No. 204, 5th Canadian Infantry Battalion, 23 February). The case numbers now run into the 200s (case 204 on 23 February, case 200 on 26 February).
- Personnel: Dvr. H. Carpenter [uncertain] (No. 376765) reported with the motor horse ambulance to the C.C.V.E.S. on 5 February, and Pte. J. J. Walmsley [uncertain] (No. 1262820) rejoined the C.C.M.T. Company for duty the same day. Pte. W. Earl [uncertain] (No. 183972) was admitted to hospital on 10 February. Pte. J. Chase (No. 34722) and Pte. F. H. Unning [uncertain] (No. 513077) were granted 14 days' leave of absence to the U.K. on 28 February. Note: Pte. Earl's number 183972 is the same number given for Pte. W. Earl [uncertain] in September 1918 — the name is consistently uncertain.
- The cover is a different form from the earlier months: a printed "CONFIDENTIAL WAR DIARY OF" cover with the unit and dates handwritten, numbered "VOLUME No 48." (with "2." beneath). The contents page's "Diary Text 9 Pages" counts the 9 C. 2118 diary-text pages (pp. 4–12), not the cover, contents or title page.
- No monthly summary table of admissions/evacuations is given for February; the month closes with "Close of War Diary Month Ending Feby 28th 1919" and the O.C.'s signature.
- No appendices are listed or included for February.
