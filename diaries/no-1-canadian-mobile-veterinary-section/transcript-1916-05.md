## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Hour, Date, Place | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is struck through on the diary pages.
- The diary is written as continuous narrative prose; each day's entry is given in full, in the diarist's own words and order.
- Map references in the diary (e.g. "G.34 b 7.4. sheet 28", "G.11. a 8.8 sheet 28") are transcribed as written; they are British trench-map sheet and square references, not latitude/longitude.
- "M.V.S." = Mobile Veterinary Section; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "D.D.V.S." = Deputy Director of Veterinary Services; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "Bde." = Brigade; "Batty." = Battery; "Batt." = Battalion; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column; "R.G.A." = Royal Garrison Artillery; "R.F.A." = Royal Field Artillery; "A.S.C." = Army Service Corps; "A.P.M." = Assistant Provost Marshal; "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General; "A.A. + Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer; "G.H.Q." = General Headquarters; "C.A.V.C." = Canadian Army Veterinary Corps; "F.P." = Field Punishment; "B.R.O." = [uncertain — as written]; "G.R.O." = [uncertain — as written]; "M.F.O." = [uncertain — as written]; "R.S.P.C.A." = Royal Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals.
- "Received/admitted … sick horses" is the section's daily intake of sick or wounded horses; "Evacuated … to No. 13 Vet. Hospital, NEUFCHATEL" is transfer to the veterinary hospital at Neufchâtel; "cast" horses are horses condemned as unfit for further service; "N.H." after a wound is transcribed as written (probably "near hind").

## Notes — May 1916
- The unit stayed at Poperinghe (south of Poperinghe) for the whole month. On 12 May the men moved from their farm billet into their own huts; the camp was nearing completion by 20 May.
- The contents page's "Diary Text 16 Pages" counts the sixteen diary-text pages (pp. 4–19); p. 3 is the handwritten title page, making 19 pages in the month's volume.
- Remounts: on 2 May ten men fetched 20 remount horses from the Advanced Remount Depot; two were delivered to the Camp Commandant, 1st Canadian Division Headquarters, on 3 May, and 9 each were collected from the section by the 3rd Canadian Infantry Brigade (4 May, Lance Corporal Wheeler A.) and the 2nd Canadian Infantry Brigade (5 May, Sergeant Coy H.).
- The section's horse ambulance was obtained at Abbeville: Staff Sergeant Southern and Pte. Sutherland J. left on 5 May with a G.S. limber to exchange for it, and Staff Sergeant Southern returned with it on 11 May. Its first call was on 18 May (Case No. 1847, 3rd Canadian Field Ambulance). The monthly summary records that it handled 6 cases in May — 3 destroyed on arrival and 3 evacuated.
- Monthly summary (p. 19, the diary's own figures): 13 horses under treatment from April; 193 admitted during May (206 in total); 173 evacuated to Base Veterinary Hospital; 8 destroyed; 7 returned to unit; 18 remaining under treatment at 31 May. The evacuated total of 173 matches the daily entries exactly (16 + 16 + 21 + 21 + 46 + 13 + 40). The daily entries account for only 167 horses admitted, against the summary's 193 — the diary does not explain the difference; the 20 remounts received on 2 May and the 6 horse-ambulance cases may account for part of it. The 8 destroyed are accounted for as 2 on 3 May (Cases 1779 and 1783), 3 on 30 May (Cases 1904, 1944 and 1958), and the 3 destroyed on arrival by the horse ambulance.
- Horses destroyed in May: Case No. 1779 (lymphangitis, old age and debility, by order of the A.D.V.S.) and Case No. 1783 (shrapnel wound, N.H.) on 3 May; Case No. 1904 (1st Canadian Divisional Ammunition Column, injury to the bowels), Case No. 1944 (Headquarters Company, 1st Canadian Divisional Train, quittor, N.H.) and Case No. 1958 (2nd Canadian Heavy Battery, septicaemia) on 30 May.
- Skin disease was the largest single cause among the evacuated horses: 10 of the 21 evacuated on 19 May and 6 of the 13 evacuated on 26 May were skin-disease cases. On 20 May the section admitted 39 horses in one day — 12 cast horses, 25 surplus from the 1st Canadian Divisional Artillery, and 2 injured.
- Belgian units continued to use the section: a horse and a mule from the 7th Belgian Artillery Ammunition Column (26 May), and the horse ambulance collected Case No. 1893 from the 7th Belgian Artillery's waggon lines (22 May).
- Men: Pte. Houghty F.A. (No. 48541) was admitted to No. 2 Canadian Field Ambulance with a lacerated jaw on 6 May, returned to duty on 11 May, and was re-admitted on 13 May with a fractured jaw. Pte. Durand J. (No. 48731) returned to duty from hospital at 4 P.M. on 13 May. Pte. Hamilton J.C. (No. 16501) returned from leave five days overdue on 18 May, delayed at Boulogne; Pte. Young A.S. (No. 13696) also returned from leave detained at Boulogne on 25 May. Pte. Gauthier F.J. (No. 48750) was attached to Headquarters Signals, 1st Canadian Divisional Train, on 28 May to act as brakesman on the supply waggon. Four reinforcements arrived from Havre on 18 May.
- The Director of Veterinary Services and Remounts is named in full for the first time: Col. W. J. Neill (31 May).
- Uncertain readings: "Pte. Houghty F.A." — the letters "F.A." are now read as the man's initials (they recur consistently); "Sergeant Coy H." (5 May); "Viscount de Bremoul" (5 May — the interpreter at Headquarters whose horse, Case No. 1793, was returned cured); "M.F.O." and "G.R.O. 1547" (13 May and 8 May — references as written); the two men named Southern appear in different ranks on the same day (Corporal Southern returning from Neufchâtel and Staff Sergeant Southern returning from Abbeville, 11 May) — transcribed as written.
- No appendices are listed or included for May.
