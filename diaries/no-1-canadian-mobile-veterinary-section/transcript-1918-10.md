## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is not struck through on these pages.
- The Place column is written once per page; it is repeated in the tables below for each entry on that page. A ditto mark (") in the Place column is expanded to the repeated place.
- The remarks column is initialled throughout with a circled two-letter monogram that appears to read "P.S." — the reading is not certain; it matches the initials of Capt. P. Smallland, who signs the 31 October entry.
- The diary-text pages are numbered in pencil at the top right, 2618–2626 (pp. 4–12).
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; map-sheet references (e.g. "Sheet 51 B") are transcribed as written.
- "Admitted … horses to M.V.S." is the section's daily intake of sick or wounded horses; "Transferred … to C.C.V.E.S." is transfer onward to the Canadian Corps Veterinary Evacuating Station. "Horse Hides" in the entries are the hides of horses that died. "D.A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Veterinary Services. "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General. "A.A.+ Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General.

## Notes — October 1918
- The section followed the 1st Canadian Division's advance through the Pursuit to the Selle and the Battle of Valenciennes (1–2 November falls just after this month): Queant (to 7 October), Cherisy (7–14 October), Etaing (14–19 October), Guesnain (19–20 October) and Fenain (20 October to month's end). The diary records no battle detail — only veterinary work, movements and administration.
- Case numbers run from 751 (7 October) to 923 (29 October). The busiest transfer day was 4 October: 25 horses and 6 mules to the C.C.V.E.S.
- Horses that died this month: case 761 (9 October, 5th Battery, 2nd Brigade C.F.A. — debility, old age [uncertain]); case 808 (13 October, No. 1 Section, 20th Divisional Ammunition Column — tetanus [uncertain]); case 870 (25 October, No. 1 Section, 1st Canadian Divisional Ammunition Column — shrapnel wounds). Two horses with shrapnel wounds (cases 826 and 828) were collected from 1/49 Field Ambulance [uncertain] on 11 October.
- Personnel: Pte. F. H. Unning [uncertain] (No. 513077) rejoined from leave on 8 October; Pte. S. J. Bougen [uncertain] (No. 49652) was granted 14 days at First Army Rest Camp on 2 October and rejoined on 17 October. Dvr. E. Ellery [uncertain] (No. 220287) rejoined from leave on 15 October — the initial is given as "I." here but "E." on 28 September, so one of the two readings is wrong. Pte. E. J. MacDonald [uncertain] (No. 48785) was granted 14 days' leave to 4 November on 21 October. A/Sergt. J. Chase (No. 34722) and Pte. H. Navey [uncertain] (No. 48793) were granted 14 days' leave to the U.K. on 28 October.
- The cover's volume number reads "42" with "44." written above it. The contents page's "Diary Text 9 Pages" counts the 9 C. 2118 diary-text pages (pp. 4–12), not the cover, contents or title page.
- No monthly summary table of admissions/evacuations is given for October; the month closes with "Close of War Diary Month Ending October 31st/1918" and the O.C.'s signature.
- No appendices are listed or included for October.
