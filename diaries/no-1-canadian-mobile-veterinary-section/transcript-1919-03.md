## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is not struck through on these pages.
- The Place column is written once per page; it is repeated in the tables below for each entry on that page. A ditto mark (") in the Place column is expanded to the repeated place.
- The remarks column is initialled throughout with a circled two-letter monogram that appears to read "P.S." — the reading is not certain; it matches the initials of Capt. P. Smallland, who signs the 31 March entry.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; map-sheet references (e.g. "Sheet LIEGE 7") are transcribed as written.
- "D.A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Veterinary Services. "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General. "A.A.+ Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General. "O.R.s" = Other Ranks. "L.D." = Light Draught (animal). "A.F.B. 122" = Army Form B.122 (field service conduct sheet / casualty form).

## Notes — March 1919
- The diary ends with the section's return to England. It stayed at Huy, Belgium, until 23 March, when it received the order to move: it entrained at Huy Station at 7 a.m. on 24 March with No. 24 Group, travelled to Le Havre (arriving 4.15 p.m. on 26 March, No. 2 Rest Camp), embarked on the SS LORINA at 10 p.m. on 27 March, arrived at Weymouth at 9.30 a.m. on 28 March, entrained at 11 a.m., arrived at Liphook at 2 p.m. and marched to Bramshott Camp, where it was quartered in F Wing. The last three entries (29–31 March) read simply "Bramshott Camp", and the diary closes with "Close of War Diary for Month Ending March 31st 1919" and the O.C.'s signature. There is no closing summary, farewell or demobilisation statement — the diary simply stops at Bramshott Camp on 31 March 1919, the end of the diary's span (1 March 1915 – 31 March 1919).
- Demobilisation of the men is under way through the month: Pte. J. L. Gilbs [uncertain] (No. 48754) proceeded to England with Group 6 on 9 March, and Pte. E. W. Roy (No. 406920) with Group 19 on 16 March. Note the number for Gilbs here is 48754, while the July 1918 entry gave "Gilb [uncertain] J.L." as No. 48784 — one of the two readings is wrong. A.F.B. 122s were forwarded for 3 other ranks (4 March), for Pte. Wm R.A. Smith [uncertain] (No. 7228) to the O.C. Canadian Veterinary Hospital, Base (4 March), and for 14 other ranks to the O.C. Concentration Camp, Huy (23 March).
- The last animals were disposed of in March: cases 209 and 210 destroyed for butchery (1 March), cases 212, 213 and 214 (5 March), case 215 (6 March) and case 216 (10 March); one L.D. horse and one L.D. mule were entrained for Brussels on 15 March (authority Q.55-J, 1st Canadian Division), and two L.D. horses were transferred to the Camp Commandant, H.Q. 1st Canadian Division, on 23 March. One horse was admitted to the M.V.S. on 9 March — the last admission recorded in the diary.
- The cover is the same printed form as February's: "CONFIDENTIAL WAR DIARY OF" with the unit and dates handwritten, numbered "VOLUME No 49." (with "6." beneath). The contents page's "Diary Text 6 Pages" counts the 6 C. 2118 diary-text pages (pp. 3–8), not the cover or contents page. Unlike the earlier months there is no separate handwritten title page for March.
- No monthly summary table of admissions/evacuations is given for March.
- No appendices are listed or included for March.
