## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, except for the typed contents page).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Hour, Date, Place | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary.
- "Issued … cured" means a recovered horse was returned to its unit.

## Notes — May 1915
- No entries for 18, 20 or 21 May: the diary jumps from 17 to 19 May, and from 19 to 22 May.
- Places: Poperinghe until 2 May (billet required by the 28th Division); Proven 2–4 May; Nieppe 4–15 May (via a night at Locre); Busnes 15–22 May; Hinges from 22 May.
- Horses: 172 sick horses received during May; 131 evacuated to the Base Veterinary Hospital (22 + 21 + 13 + 16 + 40 + 3 + 16); 2 died, 3 horses and 1 mule destroyed; 16 issued cured.
- The 25 May receipt of 36 sick horses is the largest single day of the month; 40 horses were evacuated on 26 May.
- Other units named: 28th Division, No. 17 M.V.S., 29th Bde. R.F.A., No. 4 Mobile Veterinary Section, 2nd Infantry Brigade, Canadian Field Artillery.
- The diarist's initials "M.G.O'G." (M. G. O'Gogarty) appear at the end of most daily entries; the month is signed "M. G. O'Gogarty Capt., O.C. No. 1 Canadian Mobile Veterinary Section". June's typed title page gives the name as "Captain M. G. O' Gogarty", confirming the reading.
- The contents page's "Volume 15-5" is the LAC filing volume; the cover's "Volume 3." is the diary's own volume number.
- No appendices are listed or included for May.
