## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is not struck through on these pages.
- The Place column is written once per page; it is repeated in the tables below for each entry on that page. A ditto mark (") in the Place column is expanded to the repeated place.
- The remarks column is initialled throughout with a circled two-letter monogram that appears to read "P.S." — the reading is not certain; it matches the initials of Capt. P. Smallland, who signs the 31 December entry.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; map-sheet references (e.g. "Sheet Liege 7", "Sheet 1.M") are transcribed as written.
- "Admitted … horses to M.V.S." is the section's daily intake of sick or wounded horses. "D.A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Veterinary Services. "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General. "A.A.+ Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General.

## Notes — December 1918
- The section marched into Germany with the Army of Occupation: from Gives (Belgium) on 1 December through Modave Station, Borlon (2 December), Haute Bodeux (4 December) and Petit Thier (6 December), crossing the German frontier at 7.50 a.m. on 7 December and reaching Mirfeld at 4 p.m. It then moved through Murringen (8 December), Kirschseiffen (9 December), Wibkirchen (10 December), Pingsdorf (11 December) and Rodenkirchen (12 December) to cross the Rhine at Cologne: on 13 December the section passed through Cologne and crossed the Rhine by the Deutz Bridge at 10.45 a.m., in column with "C" Group, leading the division, while General Plumer, G.O.C. 2nd Army, inspected the division crossing the bridge. It located at Heumar at 3 p.m., then moved back into Cologne on 21 December, to the Artillery Barracks, Karinburg [uncertain] Strasse, where it remained to year's end.
- The Christmas entry is the only festive note in this chunk: "Xmas Dinner held at No. 337 Bonner Strasse" on 25 December. Pte. J. A. Stains [uncertain] (No. 48598) and Pte. E. W. Roy (No. 406920) were granted 14 days' leave to the U.K. the same day.
- Case numbers run from 997 (1 December) to 999 (28 December): case 999, belonging to 1st Canadian Divisional Ammunition Column, was destroyed on 28 December and sold to the butcher — the only horse destroyed in the month. Case 998 was collected from Herr Rath, No. 20 Bishop Strasse, Euskirchen, on 15 December.
- Personnel: Pte. L. James [uncertain] (No. 49754) was the subject of an A.F.B. 122 forwarded to the A.A.G., Base, on 14 December (he had been admitted to a Field Ambulance sick on 17 November). Dvr. L. Lee (No. 35462) and Pte. N. Gunnin [uncertain] (No. 49084) reported to No. 1 Company, 1st Canadian Divisional Train, with a supply wagon for duty on 22 December. Pte. S. J. Bougen [uncertain] (No. 49652) was granted 14 days' leave to the U.K. on 30 December; Sergt. T. H. Southern [uncertain] (No. 34703) likewise on 31 December.
- The cover's volume number reads "44" (circled) with "46." written above it. The contents page's "Diary Text 9 Pages" counts the 9 C. 2118 diary-text pages (pp. 4–12), not the cover, contents or title page.
- No monthly summary table of admissions/evacuations is given for December; the month closes with "Close of War Diary Month Ending December 31st 1918" and the O.C.'s signature.
- No appendices are listed or included for December.
