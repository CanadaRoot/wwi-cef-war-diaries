## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, except for the typed contents page).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Hour, Date, Place | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary.
- "Issued … cured" means a recovered horse was returned to its unit.
- Ditto marks (") in the Place and Summary columns are expanded as the repeated place or words in the tables below, following the diary's own shorthand.

## Notes — August 1915
- No entries for 4, 5, 14, 15, 16 or 29 August: the diary jumps from 3 to 6 August, from 13 to 17 August, and from 28 to 30 August.
- Places: Zwartemolenhoek until 1 August; Neuve Eglise (on the Belgian frontier) from 1 August for the rest of the month.
- Horses: 152 sick horses received during August, plus 4 horses received for the mallein test on 2 August; 131 evacuated to No. 10 Veterinary Hospital, Neufchatel; 4 destroyed; none recorded as died; 5 issued cured. Over 900 horses had been admitted to date, averaging 150 a month (Remarks, p. 4).
- Glanders / mallein testing: the 10 horses received for the mallein test showed no reaction and were evacuated as debilitated; they were tested by the subcutaneous method, not the intra-palpebral method demonstrated under the A.D.V.S., 2nd Army, who was unsure whether the mallein used was right for intra-palpebral injection. Horses admitted with a nasal discharge tested by mallein did not react and the discharge ceased within 24 hours — the same result reported from the Base Veterinary Hospitals.
- On 16 August O'Gogarty was instructed to indent for 610 smoke helmets for horses (10 per cent of the Division's horse strength); they were received on 21 August and distributed to the units — the first mention of gas protection for horses in this diary.
- The Remarks argue that an Orderly Room Clerk is essential for a Mobile Veterinary Section, to do the work of Orderly Room Clerk, Quarter Master Sergeant and Pay Sergeant.
- The contents page's "Volume 15-8" is the LAC filing volume; the cover's "Volume 6." is the diary's own volume number.
- No appendices are listed or included for August.
