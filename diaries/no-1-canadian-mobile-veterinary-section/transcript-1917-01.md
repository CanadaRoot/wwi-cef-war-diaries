## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word, name or number that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary. The section was at Bruay 1–17 January 1917, then moved to Barlin on 18 January.
- "M.V.S." = Mobile Veterinary Section. "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services. "D.D.V.S." = Deputy Director of Veterinary Services. "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer. "B.R.O." = Base Records Office. "A.A. + Q.M.G." = Adjutant-General and Quartermaster-General (branch). "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery. "U.A.C." = [uncertain — written as "U.A.C." throughout; probably Divisional Ammunition Column]. "F.P. No. 1" = Field Punishment No. 1.
- Ditto marks (") are transcribed as written.

## Notes — January 1917
- The section moved from Bruay to Barlin on 18 January 1917, relieving the 2nd Canadian M.V.S. there. Both are in the Pas-de-Calais coalfield, a few miles west of Vimy Ridge.
- Monthly totals as written: 39 horses on hand at 31 December 1916; 294 admitted in January; 213 evacuated to No. 13 Veterinary Hospital, Neufchatel; 8 returned to units; 14 destroyed at the section; 98 remaining under treatment at 31 January 1917. The diarist's arithmetic balances (39 + 294 = 333; 213 + 8 + 14 + 98 = 333).
- 63 horses admitted on 31 January alone — all mange cases — the largest single day's admissions in the month.
- Discipline: Pte. Gauthier F. J. (No. 48750) was tried 24 January for drunkenness and disorderly conduct on active service, sentenced to 28 days Field Punishment No. 1, and delivered to the Divisional Field Punishment station on 25 January. Pte. Fournier A. E. (No. 48514) was also delivered to the Divisional Field Punishment station on 26 January (no offence stated).
- Horses were destroyed for debility, broken wind, colic, lymphangitis, old age and mange; Case No. 4048 (lymphangitis) was sent to the abattoir on A.D.V.S. orders.
- Calcium sulphide (a mange dip) was issued in gallon quantities almost daily to infantry brigades, artillery batteries, the Divisional Train, the Cavalry Brigade and other units — evidence of a widespread mange problem in the Division's animals.
- The cover's volume number is ambiguous: "21" is written on the printed "Volume" line, with "23." written above it and "12" below it. The contents page says "Volume 17-1".
- No appendices are listed or included for January.
