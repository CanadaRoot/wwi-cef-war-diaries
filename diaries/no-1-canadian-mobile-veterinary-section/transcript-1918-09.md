## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is not struck through on these pages.
- The Place column is written once per page; it is repeated in the tables below for each entry on that page. A ditto mark (") in the Place column is expanded to the repeated place.
- The remarks column is initialled throughout with a circled two-letter monogram that appears to read "P.S." — the reading is not certain; it matches the initials of Capt. P. Smallland, who signs the 30 September entry.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; map-sheet references (e.g. "Sheet 51 B") are transcribed as written.
- "Admitted … horses to M.V.S." is the section's daily intake of sick or wounded horses; "Transferred … to C.C.V.E.S." is transfer onward to the Canadian Corps Veterinary Evacuating Station. "Horse Hides" in the entries are the hides of horses that died. "D.A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Veterinary Services. "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General. "A.A.+ Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General.

## Notes — September 1918
- The section was in the Arras sector for the whole month, shuttling between Agnez-lez-Duisans, Heninel, Hendecourt and Queant as the 1st Canadian Division advanced through the Hindenburg Line battles (the Battles of the Drocourt–Quéant Line, 2–3 September, and the Canal du Nord, 27 September–1 October, fall in this month). Moves: Arras area to Heninel on 2 September, to Hendecourt on 5 September, back to Agnez-lez-Duisans on 6 September, a cancelled move on 16 September (the section set out, halted en route, and returned to Agnez-lez-Duisans), to Hendecourt on 23 September, and to Queant on 30 September. The diary records no battle detail — only veterinary work, movements and administration.
- This was the heaviest month so far in this chunk: 14 September alone saw 35 horses and 4 mules transferred to the C.C.V.E.S. Case numbers run from 339 (4 September) to 624 (29 September).
- Horses destroyed this month: cases 339, 343 and 356 on 4 September (all shrapnel wounds); case 571 on 26 September (52 Battery, 13th Brigade C.F.A. — gas wound [uncertain]); case 590 on 27 September (D/59 Brigade R.F.A. — shell wounds). Case 277, a horse captured from the enemy, was forwarded to the O.C., No. 1 Field Remount Section, on 23 September.
- Unusual entry: 12 sheep were admitted to the M.V.S. on 11 September — the only sheep in the diary so far. An officer's charger sent in by the A.D.V.S., Canadian Corps, was forwarded to the C.C.V.E.S. on 22 September.
- Personnel: Corpl. G. Vosburgh (No. 48511) rejoined from the Canadian Veterinary Hospital, Base, on 13 September — confirming the July reading "Voshurgh" as Vosburgh. Reinforcements from the Canadian Veterinary Hospital, Base: Pte. J. King (No. 799552) and Pte. E. W. Roy (No. 406920) on 3 September; Pte. W. Earl [uncertain] (No. 183972) on 8 September. Dvr. L. Lee (No. 35462) and Dvr. N. Gunn [uncertain] (No. 49084) rejoined with a supply wagon from 1st Canadian Divisional Train on 4 September. Sergt. T. H. Southern [uncertain] (No. 34703) was granted 10 days' leave on 8 September and rejoined on 19 September. Pte. F. H. Unning [uncertain] (No. 513077) was granted 14 days' leave from 22 September to 6 October. Dvr. E. Ellery [uncertain] (No. 220287) was granted 14 days' leave to the U.K. on 28 September. Pte. J. A. Stains [uncertain] (No. 48578) claimed separation allowance on 20 September.
- The cover's volume number reads "41" with "43." written above it; the title page is numbered "41" bottom right. The contents page's "Diary Text 11 Pages" counts the 11 C. 2118 diary-text pages (pp. 4–14), not the cover, contents or title page.
- No monthly summary table of admissions/evacuations is given for September; the month closes with "Close of War Diary Month Ending September 30th 1918" and the O.C.'s signature.
- No appendices are listed or included for September.
