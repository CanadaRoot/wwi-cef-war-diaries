## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is not struck through on these pages.
- The Place column is written once per page; it is repeated in the tables below for each entry on that page. A ditto mark (") in the Place column is expanded to the repeated place.
- The remarks column is initialled throughout with what appears to be "A.E.C." — the hand is stylised and the reading is not certain; it matches the initials of Capt. A. E. Coulombe, the officer commanding.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary.
- "Admitted … horses to M.V.S." is the section's daily intake of sick or wounded horses; "Evacuated … to No. 7 Veterinary Hospital, Forges-les-Eaux" is transfer onward by rail; "Destroyed" is horses destroyed (put down). "D.V.S.+R." is the Director of Veterinary Services and Remounts.

## Notes — September 1916
- Monthly totals (as written on p. 17): 122 horses admitted during September; 111 evacuated to base hospitals, 4 destroyed at the unit, 2 transferred to the relieving unit, 1 cured and returned to unit, 4 remaining under treatment (122 accounted for). August had ended with none remaining under treatment.
- The section was on the Somme. It moved from Longueville to Rubempré on 1 September (via Havernas, Naours and Talmas), then to Albert on 5 September (via Hérissart, Contay, Vadencourt, Warloy-Baillon, Hénencourt and Millencourt), staying on the Albert–Bouzincourt road until 12 September, when it moved back to Rubempré (billet No. 1 Argyll Street South), and on 19 September to Harponville (via Hérissart and Toutencourt), where it remained to month's end. The 1st Canadian Division was in action on the Somme (the Battles of Flers–Courcelette, 15–22 September, fall in this month), but the diary records no battle detail — only veterinary work.
- Horses destroyed this month (4, per the monthly report; three are named in the daily entries): case 440 (6 September, tetanus, D Battery, 83rd Brigade RFA, 18th Division); a stray bay gelding of unknown unit (7 September, broken tibia after a kick to the near stifle); case 3028 (11 September, shrapnel wound to the stomach, D/11 Battery, 18th Division — perforation of the large intestine found post-mortem). The fourth destroyed horse is not named in the daily entries.
- Evacuations now go to No. 7 Veterinary Hospital, Forges-les-Eaux, by rail from Albert, Héricourt, Acheux or Vignacourt stations.
- On 5 September the section received 5 horses and 1 mule from the 4th Australian Mobile Veterinary Section, 4th Australian Division, at its Albert billets.
- On 16 September the diary lists six horses collected from French inhabitants at Vignacourt station (cases 3046–3051), with the owners' names largely unreadable; the copy of the list was forwarded to the A.D.V.S.
- Personnel: Sergt. E. King and Corpl. J. Duffie reported from No. 7 Veterinary Hospital for temporary duty on 6 September; A/Sgt King and 4 men left on 10 September to report to 50th M.V.S., 39th Division. Corpl. G. C. Hamilton went to No. 7 Veterinary Hospital on 20 September to remain temporarily for instruction in clipping machines and repairs. Corpl. McRae (first name not given) is in charge of several conducting parties this month. Two reinforcements arrived from No. 1 Canadian Veterinary Hospital, Havre, on 9 September (unnamed). Pte. J. Ulland (No. 48731) was forwarded to the Canadian Casualty Assembly Centre, Folkestone, on 16 September; Pte. U. Mead (No. 48779) likewise on 29 September. Pte. A. J. Hyatt (No. 48572) was given 5 days No. 1 Field Punishment by the O.C. on 25 September. Corpl. R. A. Muir had a change in assigned pay on 17 September; Pte. J. J. Murray (No. 18453) likewise on 29 September.
- The Reserve Army Letter No. 363 cited on 6–7 September is dated 8-9-16 — two days after the 6 September entry that cites it; the entries were presumably written up after the event.
- The diary-text pages carry printed numbers 3–16 in the top right corner (pp. 4–17); the title page on p. 3 is numbered "2". The contents page's "Diary Text 14 Pages" counts the 14 C. 2118 diary-text pages (pp. 4–17), not the title page.
- The billet address on 1 and 12 September, first read as "No. 1 [uncertain] st. South", is "No. 1 Argyll st. South" (12 September entry).
- No appendices are listed or included for September.
