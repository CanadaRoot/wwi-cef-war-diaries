## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Hour, Date, Place | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is struck through on the diary pages.
- The diary is written as continuous narrative prose; each day's entry is given in full, in the diarist's own words and order.
- Map references in the diary (e.g. "Q.14. a.9.9.", "Sheet 27 R.4- a-9.0.", "H.21- d.7.5-") are transcribed as written; they are British trench-map sheet and square references, not latitude/longitude.
- "M.V.S." = Mobile Veterinary Section; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "D.D.V.S." = Deputy Director of Veterinary Services; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "Bde." = Brigade; "Batty." = Battery; "Batt." = Battalion; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "R.G.A." = Royal Garrison Artillery; "R.F.A." = Royal Field Artillery; "A.S.C." = Army Service Corps; "A.P.M." = Assistant Provost Marshal; "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General; "A.A. + Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer; "G.H.Q." = General Headquarters; "C.A.V.C." = Canadian Army Veterinary Corps; "F.P." = Field Punishment; "B.R.O." = [uncertain — as written]; "W.R.O." = [uncertain — as written].
- "Received/admitted … sick horses" is the section's daily intake of sick or wounded horses; "Evacuated … to No. 13 Vet. Hospital, NEUFCHATEL" is transfer to the veterinary hospital at Neufchâtel; "cast" horses are horses condemned as unfit for further service; the Mallein test is the diagnostic test for glanders.

## Notes — April 1916
- The unit moved twice in April: on 1 April it marched from Nieppe via Bailleul, Meteren and Flêtre to Coq. de Paille (arriving 12 A.M. as written), and on 7 April it marched from Coq. de Paille via Berthen and Boeschepe to a location south of Poperinghe, where it remained for the rest of the month.
- The contents page's "Diary Text 16 Pages" counts the sixteen diary-text pages (pp. 4–19); p. 3 is the handwritten title page, making 19 pages in the month's volume.
- The diarist's place spelling varies: "Coq. de Paille" (also written "Coq.-de-Paille"), "Poperinghe" (also "Poperinghe S" for the location south of Poperinghe), "Meteren" (written "Meteen" in February), "Fletre", "Godwaersvelde" (also written "Godewaersvelde"), "Berthen", "Boeschepe".
- Capt. Wood, Veterinary Officer of the 2nd Infantry Brigade, died; the O.C. was informed on 10 April and attended his funeral at 2 P.M. that day. On 15 April Capt. Coulombe took over as Veterinary Officer to the horses of Divisional Headquarters, Headquarters Artillery, Engineers and Signal Headquarters.
- Monthly summary (p. 19, the diary's own figures): 2 horses under treatment at 31 March; 103 admitted during April (105 in total); 83 evacuated to Base Veterinary Hospital; 7 returned to unit cured; 1 died; 1 destroyed; 13 remaining under treatment at 30 April. The daily entries show 2 horses destroyed in April (one with shrapnel wounds on 8 April; Case No. 1720 on 17 April) against the summary's 1 — the difference is not explained in the diary. The evacuated total of 83 is reached if the officers' charger "Ricky" (collected 9 April) is counted in the 9 April shipment.
- Horses that died or were destroyed in April: one horse with shrapnel wounds to thigh, hoof and stomach destroyed 8 April; Case No. 1720 (debility, then general paralysis) destroyed 17 April; Case No. 1733 (chronic diarrhoea; post-mortem: enlarged heart and kidneys with inflammation of the intestines) died 22 April. The pleurisy case belonging to the 1st Australian Division Mounted Troops died on the night of 5–6 April — it was not the section's own horse.
- The section acted as Veterinary Officer to the 1st Australian Division Mounted Troops from 5 April (attending a pleurisy case with no chance of recovery) and handed over first-aid drugs (strychnine, a hypodermic syringe and carbonate of ammonia) to them on 6 April when leaving Coq. de Paille.
- Belgian units appear in the diary for the first time: 4 mules from the 7th Belgian Artillery Ammunition Column and 1 horse from the 71st Heavy Battery, R.G.A., admitted 25 April; Case No. 602 of the 3rd Belgian Battery was sought at a map reference on 26 April but had already been evacuated by the 2nd Division Mobile Veterinary Section.
- Pte. Ellison (No. 5304) completed a 90-day Field Punishment No. 1 sentence and returned to the unit for duty on 15 April, having been sent back to the Divisional Field Punishment Station to complete the sentence on 10 April. Pte. Cutts (No. 48538) was admitted to No. 3 Canadian General Hospital, Boulogne, with tonsillitis on 16 April, having fallen ill while evacuating horses to Neufchâtel. Pte. Durand (No. 48731) was admitted to No. 3 Canadian Field Ambulance on 25 April.
- Uncertain readings: "Pte. Houghty F.A." (2 April — the letters after the name); "Lieut. Puston" (9 April — the officer owning the charger "Ricky"); the first-aid drugs list of 6 April reads "822 — Strychnine — 1 — Hypo Syringe and Carb. Ammonia Bull" — the "822" and "Bull" are transcribed as written and their meaning is not certain.
- No appendices are listed or included for April.
