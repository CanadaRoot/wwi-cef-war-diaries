## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is not struck through on these pages.
- The Place column is written once per page; it is repeated in the tables below for each entry on that page. A ditto mark (") in the Place column is expanded to the repeated place.
- The remarks column is initialled throughout with what appears to be "M.G.O.T." — the hand is stylised and the reading is not certain; it matches the initials on the title page and the signature at the foot of 31 July.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary.
- "Admitted … horses to M.V.S." is the section's daily intake of sick or wounded horses; "Transferred … to C.C.V.E.S." is transfer onward to the Canadian Corps Veterinary Evacuating Station (earlier months used No. 17 V.E.S. and No. 7 Veterinary Hospital, Forges-les-Eaux). "Horse Hides" in the entries are the hides of horses that died. "D.A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Veterinary Services. "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General. "A.A.+ Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General.

## Notes — July 1918
- The section began the month at Tincquette and moved on 14 July to Agnez-lez-Duisans (billet 72), where it remained to month's end. The 1st Canadian Division was in the Arras sector in July 1918; the diary records no battle detail — only veterinary work and administration.
- Onward evacuations now go to the Canadian Corps Veterinary Evacuating Station (C.C.V.E.S.), replacing No. 17 V.E.S. (used 3–13 July) and, in earlier years, No. 7 Veterinary Hospital, Forges-les-Eaux.
- The heaviest casualty day was 21 July: 16 mules admitted from 5th Canadian Divisional Ammunition Column with bomb wounds, plus 7 horse hides (dead horses) received the same day — the only bomb-wound cases in the month. Case numbers run from 59 (1 July) to 136 (29 July).
- Horses died at the unit this month: one from B/277 Brigade (15 July) and one transferred to C.C.V.E.S. on 17 July is recorded as "1 Horse Died"; horse hides were also received on 22 July (1, from B/177 Brigade) and 23 July (1).
- Personnel: Sergt. J. Salmon (No. 34730) reported to the Remount Staging Camp on 12 July and proceeded to 1st Army Rest Camp on 22 July. Pte. O. F. Reed (No. 12466) reported for duty with the Remount Conducting Party on 27 July. Pte. L. E. Post [uncertain] (No. 195245) reported to the D.A.D.V.S., 4th Canadian Division, for temporary duty on 28 July. Dvr. H. Fountain [uncertain] (No. 18343) rejoined from leave on 28 July — this may be the same man recorded as "Dvr. Frankis [uncertain] H." (No. 18543) granted 10 days' leave on 17 July; the number and the name differ between the two entries and both readings are uncertain. Corpl. G. Voshurgh [uncertain] (No. 48511) was the subject of an A.F.B. 122 on 19 July. Pte. J. L. Gilb [uncertain] (No. 48784) had an assigned-pay change on 17 July.
- The cover's volume number reads "39" with "41." written above it; the title page is numbered "39." bottom right. The contents page's "Diary Text 11 Pages" counts the 11 C. 2118 diary-text pages (pp. 4–14), not the cover, contents or title page.
- No monthly summary table of admissions/evacuations is given for July (unlike September 1916); the month closes with the 31 July entry and the O.C.'s signature.
- No appendices are listed or included for July.
