## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". From June the diary uses a new printing of the form (Forms/C.2118/12) with separate Place, Date and Hour columns, instead of the single combined "Hour, Date, Place" column used in earlier months; the "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is not struck through on these pages. Each day's entry is given in full, in the diarist's own words and order.
- Map references in the diary are transcribed as written; they are British trench-map sheet and square references, not latitude/longitude.
- "M.V.S." = Mobile Veterinary Section; "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services; "D.V.S." = Director of Veterinary Services; "D.D.V.S." = Deputy Director of Veterinary Services; "V.O." = Veterinary Officer; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "Bde." = Brigade; "Batty." = Battery; "Batt." = Battalion; "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery; "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column; "R.G.A." = Royal Garrison Artillery; "R.F.A." = Royal Field Artillery; "A.S.C." = Army Service Corps; "A.P.M." = Assistant Provost Marshal; "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General; "A.A. + Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General; "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer; "G.H.Q." = General Headquarters; "G.O.C." = General Officer Commanding; "C.A.V.C." = Canadian Army Veterinary Corps; "F.P." = Field Punishment; "B.R.O." = [uncertain — as written]; "D.A.C." = Divisional Ammunition Column.
- "Received/admitted … sick horses" is the section's daily intake of sick or wounded horses; "Evacuated … to No. 13 Vet. Hospital, NEUFCHATEL" is transfer to the veterinary hospital at Neufchâtel; "cast" horses are horses condemned as unfit for further service; "N.H." and "O.H." after a wound are transcribed as written (probably "near hind" and "off hind"); "N. Hock" and "Off Hock" are the near and off hocks.

## Notes — June 1916
- The unit stayed at Poperinghe (south of Poperinghe) for the whole month. The cover is marked "Duplicate" in the top left corner, and the contents page counts "Diary Text 15 Pages" — the fifteen diary-text pages are pp. 5–19; p. 4 is a blank divider page headed "War Diary".
- From June the diary uses a new printing of Army Form C. 2118 (Forms/C.2118/12; imprint "2469 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A.") with separate Place, Date and Hour columns, and the "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is no longer struck through.
- Monthly report (p. 19, the diary's own figures): 18 cases remaining under treatment from May; 263 admitted during June (281 in total); 243 evacuated to Base Hospital; 7 destroyed at the section; 1 died; 4 returned to unit; 28 remaining under treatment. The four disposal figures as written sum to 255, not the 253 subtotal written in the diary, so the remaining figure of 28 follows from the written subtotal rather than from the components — the diary's arithmetic is inconsistent by 2. The daily entries account for about 200 horses admitted, against the summary's 263; the difference is not explained in the diary, though the 40 Belgian horses and 3 mules received on 5 June and the 20 horse-ambulance cases may account for part of it. The evacuated total of 243 matches the daily entries if the 3 mules evacuated on 6 June are counted with the 240 horses.
- The horse ambulance handled 20 cases in June (12 evacuated, 5 destroyed, 3 remaining under treatment), up from 6 in May.
- Evacuation by road began in June: on 21 June 23 horses were evacuated by road to St. Omer for the first time (Corpl. Ballinger in charge), and on 28 June Corpl. Ballinger took 66 more horses to St. Omer, 22 of them skin-disease cases. The road party returned from St. Omer on 24 June at 5 P.M.
- Skin disease remained the largest single cause: 11 of the 18 horses evacuated on 2 June, 24 of the 65 horses and 3 mules evacuated on 6 June, and 22 of the 66 horses sent to St. Omer on 28 June were skin-disease cases.
- The unit adopted Summer Time on 14 June: all clocks were advanced 60 minutes at 11 P.M. that night (11 P.M. to be 12 P.M.).
- Belgian horses: 40 horses and 3 mules were received from the 7th Belgian Artillery on 5 June and evacuated to Neufchâtel on 6 June (with 24 of them skin-disease cases). The section also collected and delivered horses for the 3rd Canadian Mobile Veterinary Section (near Steenvoorde, 21 June; a mule, 11 June; a horse from the 3rd Canadian M.V.S., 23 June).
- Horses destroyed or died in June: Case No. 2095 (paralysis, shrapnel wound N.H., shattered bone) destroyed 14 June; Case No. 2100 (7th Belgian Artillery) destroyed 15 June; a horse at the 32nd Reserve Park destroyed 16 June (fracture, off hind); an animal at the 71st Heavy Battery destroyed on the spot 25 June; Cases No. 2217 (shrapnel wound, shoulder), No. 2225 (injury, off hock) and No. 2236 (7th Belgian Artillery, 2nd Battery, Horse No. 1,611 — kick, near hock) destroyed 30 June. One horse at the 1st Canadian Divisional Ammunition Column died during the night of 17–18 June.
- Officers: Col. Watson, D.V.S., and Major Sutcliffe, A.D.V.S., inspected the section on 2 June (and again on 10 June). The A.D.V.S. is named for the first time: Major Sutcliffe.
- Men: Pte. McRae J. (No. 1278) joined the section by transfer from the 8th Battalion, 2nd Infantry Brigade, on 11 June; Pte. Black J.O. (No. 48528) reported to the 8th Battery, 2nd Artillery Brigade, for duty on 19 June; Pte. Wenham W. (No. 48512) was admitted to No. 2 Field Ambulance on 25 June and discharged on 29 June; Pte. Houghty F.A. (No. 48541) reported back for duty from the Canadian Base Depot on 14 June; Pte. Salmon J. (No. 34730) was granted 8 days' leave on 14 June and returned on 23 June; Pte. Parry H. (No. 48793) was granted 8 days' leave on 7 June and rejoined on 17 June.
- Uncertain readings: the parenthetical word after "Regimental Fund Report" on 6 June is illegible; the ambulance case number on 6 June is left blank in the original; "June Pass" (6 June) is transcribed as written; Pte. Bilot C.G. is given as No. 48578 on 9 June but No. 48518 on 16 June (and in February) — transcribed as written in each place; Pte. Wenham W. is given as No. 48512 in June but No. 48812 in February — transcribed as written in each place. On 9 June the diary repeats "admitted 10 horses to M.V.S. today" twice, with 24 halters received from the 1st Canadian Divisional Train between the two lines — transcribed as written.
- No appendices are listed or included for June.
