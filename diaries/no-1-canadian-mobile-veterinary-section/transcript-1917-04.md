## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word, name or number that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. From this month the form heading carries a handwritten "1st Canadian Mobile Veterinary Section / 1st Canadian Division" at the top right.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary. The section was at Estree Cauchie (Estrée-Cauchie) until 9 April 1917, when it moved to "Ang. Road" [uncertain] and was located on the "A.C.P. Road".
- "M.V.S." = Mobile Veterinary Section. "A.D.V.S." = Assistant Director of Veterinary Services. "R.T.O." = Railway Transport Officer. "B.R.O." = Base Records Office. "A.A. + Q.M.G." = Adjutant-General and Quartermaster-General (branch). "C.F.A." = Canadian Field Artillery. "R.F.A." = Royal Field Artillery. "R.H.A." = Royal Horse Artillery. "A.P.M." = Assistant Provost Marshal. "I.O.M." = Inspector of Ordnance Machinery [uncertain]. "M.M.P." = [uncertain]. "O.H." = [uncertain — "off hind"?]. "O/S" = [uncertain].

## Notes — April 1917
- The Battle of Vimy Ridge (9–12 April 1917) is not named in the diary, but the section's workload explodes from 9 April: it moved that day from Estrée-Cauchie to a new site on the "A.C.P. Road" (place written "Ang. Road" [uncertain] — possibly Angres Road), and admissions 9–21 April total exactly 575 horses (74 + 138 + 43 + 28 + 65 + 43 + 35 + 2 + 45 + 48 + 5 + 10 + 39), matching the figure cited from this diary for the Vimy period. Evacuations in the same thirteen days total 870 horses and 17 mules (139 + 5 mules on 11 Apr; 123 on 14 Apr; 160 on 16 Apr; 216 + 7 mules on 18 Apr; 232 + 5 mules on 21 Apr).
- Unlike January–March, April has NO monthly horse statement/summary table: the month closes on p. 14 with the 30 April entries and Capt. Coulombe's signature only.
- The week's died-or-destroyed report for the week ending 6 April shows 72 horses died and 53 destroyed — the heaviest weekly loss in the diary so far. On 13 April alone 18 horses died and 5 were destroyed.
- Reinforcements flooded in during the battle: detachments from the 1st Canadian Infantry Brigade, No. 1 Canadian Veterinary Hospital (Havre), the 2nd Canadian Divisional M.V.S., No. 5 Mobile Veterinary Section, No. 12 and No. 13 Veterinary Hospitals, and Capt. Shaw W.H. of the 49th (West Riding) Division Ammunition Column with 50 other ranks from the 245th and 246th Brigades, R.F.A.
- The horse ambulance was condemned by the I.O.M. on 26 April and delivered to the railhead at Frévent (written "Frevin Capelle" throughout — Frévent is the railhead town; "Capelle" is Frévent's neighbouring commune).
- The contents page says "Diary Text 11 Pages" but the volume as scanned has 12 diary-text pages (pp. 3–14); the cover/contents/title pages are pp. 1–3, so the diary-text count should be 12, not 11.
- The cover's volume number is ambiguous: "24" is written on the printed "Volume" line, with "26" written above it and "11." below it. The contents page says "Volume 17-4".
- The remarks-column initials change this month from the "M.G.O.T." flourish of January–March to a different flourish reading "W.E.C." [uncertain] — possibly a different diarist/initialler, or the same hand read differently.
- No appendices are listed or included for April.
- Uncertain readings in this month: the diarist's initials ("W.E.C."); the new place from 9 April ("Ang. Road" and "A.C.P. Road"); the sergeants' surnames (4, 8, 15, 22, 25, 26, 29 Apr); "Myer J." (4 Apr); the place supplying the fatigue party and the parcels received (5 Apr); "1 Can [uncertain]" (8 Apr); "M.M.P." and the case collected (30 Apr); the number of mules evacuated 18 April; the hospital/place for Sergt [uncertain] (22 Apr).
