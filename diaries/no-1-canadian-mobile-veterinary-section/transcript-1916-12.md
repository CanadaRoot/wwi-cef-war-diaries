## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is not struck through on these pages.
- The Place column is written once per page; it is repeated in the tables below for each entry on that page. The section was at Frévent until 22 December, then at Bruay.
- This month's diary-text pages are on the same print run of the form as November ("2353 Wt. W2514/1454 700,000 5/15 D. D. & L. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118."), and the Date column gives the day only, with "1916" and "Dec." written at the top of each page.
- The remarks column is initialled throughout with what appears to be "A.E.C." — the hand is stylised and the reading is not certain; it matches the initials of Capt. A. E. Coulombe, the officer commanding.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary.
- "Admitted … horses to M.V.S." is the section's daily intake of sick or wounded horses; "Evacuated … to No. 13 Veterinary Hospital, Neufchatel" is transfer onward by rail; "Destroyed" is horses destroyed (put down) as unfit for further service. "V.O." is Veterinary Officer; "D.V.S.+R." is the Director of Veterinary Services and Remounts; "A.P.M." is the Assistant Provost Marshal; "C.F.A." is Canadian Field Artillery; "R.F.A." is Royal Field Artillery; "R.H.A." is Royal Horse Artillery.

## Notes — December 1916
- Monthly totals (as written on p. 18): 13 horses remaining from November; 175 admitted during December (188 in total); 121 evacuated, 21 destroyed, 1 died, 6 returned to unit, 39 remaining under treatment (188 accounted for).
- The section stayed at Frévent until 22 December, then moved to Bruay (via Rebreuve and Houdain, 8–10 A.M.), relieving the 4th Canadian Mobile Veterinary Section there and taking over 3 sick horses it left behind. Evacuations went by rail from Barlin station (until 21 December) and then from Bruay station, to No. 13 Veterinary Hospital, Neufchatel.
- Horses destroyed this month (21, per the monthly report; 16 are named in the daily entries): cases 3677 (nail puncture), 3697 (sprained shoulder), 3701 (knee gall, near fore) and 3712 (kick, off hind), 4 December; cases 3719 and 3722 (both debility), 9 December; cases 3801 (lame near stifle, kick), 3802 (kick, off hock), 3809 (chronic laminitis), 3810 (debilitated) and 3813 (lame near hind, kick stifle), 21 December; cases 3814, 3829 (quittor, off hind), 3832, 3834 and 3841, 28 December. Five destroyed horses are not named in the daily entries.
- One horse died at the unit: case 3822 (25 December, Christmas Day, gangrenous dermatitis — collected from No. 4 Section, 1st Canadian D.A.C., on 23 December). No Christmas observance is recorded in the diary.
- Six horses were returned to their units: case 3706 (3 December, to No. 3 Coy., 1st Canadian Divisional Train, fit for duty); 2 light draught mules to the 13th Battalion and 2 light draught horses to the 1st Field Company, Canadian Engineers (13 December); 1 heavy draught stray horse to the 3rd Canadian Field Ambulance (24 December, by wire authority Q. 5346 of 18-12-16).
- Calcium sulphide (the mange treatment) was issued and collected throughout the month: 2 gallons delivered to 2nd Reserve Park (11 December); 4 gallons to No. 1 Machine Gun Company and 2 gallons to 3rd Canadian Infantry Brigade (16 December); 4 gallons to the 14th Battalion (18 December); 2 gallons collected by 3rd Canadian Infantry Brigade transport and 1 gallon delivered to the Veterinary Officer at Divisional Train Headquarters (13 December). Two cases of mange were admitted on 7 December.
- Collecting parties under Corpl. A. E. Coulombe worked the St. Pol and Monchy-Cayeux area (5–14 December) and Heuchin (23 December), bringing in sick horses; the horse ambulance accompanied the party on 8 December. Stray horses came in from the 1st Canadian Pioneer Battalion (2 December, 3 horses), the 1st Canadian Divisional Engineers (8 December, case 3730, by order of the A.P.M.), and the Town Major at Bruay (23 December, 2 horses, collected two hours later by the A.P.M.).
- Personnel: Sergt. P. W. Southern (No. 34729) rejoined from leave on 3 December. Corpl. J. H. Southern (No. 34763) is a different man from Sergt. P. W. Southern — this resolves the November 30 entry's "Corpl. Southern J.H." (see the November notes). Pte. D. W. McRae (No. 1277) was on leave 4–14 December, rejoined 16 December, and was admitted to No. 3 Field Ambulance on 19 December. Ptes. E. G. MacDonald (No. 48765) and A. J. Hyatt (No. 48572) were on leave 2–14 December. Pte. W. Wenham was granted 10 days' leave on 6 December (given as No. 48812), but the 24 December entry gives him as "No. 48572 Pte. Wenham W." and says he reported as an absentee — the number 48572 is Hyatt's number, so this is a diarist's error. Corpl. G. C. Hamilton (No. 16871) was temporarily attached for duty to the Railroad Postal Corps [uncertain], 1st Canadian Division, on 8 December. Pte. A. E. Fournier (No. 48814) was admitted to No. 3 Canadian General Hospital on 28 November — notice reached the unit by letter on 17 December. Pte. P. Papineau (No. 48583) was struck off strength by Part II Orders No. 52 of 23-12-16 (30 December). Driver J. Sutherland (No. 21093) and Pte. G. Chase (No. 34722) were granted 10 days' leave from 30 December to 9 January 1917 — Chase is given the rank of Private here, though he is a Corporal elsewhere in the diary.
