## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is not struck through on these pages.
- The Place column is written once per page (always "Poperinghe"); it is repeated in the tables below for each entry on that page.
- The remarks column is initialled throughout with what appears to be "A.E.C." — the hand is stylised and the reading is not certain; it matches the initials of Capt. A. E. Coulombe, the officer commanding.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; the section remained at Poperinghe throughout July 1916.
- "Admitted … horses to M.V.S." is the section's daily intake of sick or wounded horses; "Evacuated … to St. Omer / Neufchatel" is transfer onward (by road to St. Omer, or by rail from Wippenhoek Siding to No. 10 Veterinary Hospital, Neufchâtel); "Returned to Unit" is horses returned fit for duty; "Destroyed" is horses destroyed (put down). "Mange cases" and "skin disease cases" dominate the evacuations this month.

## Notes — July 1916
- Monthly totals (as written on p. 18): 26 horses remaining under treatment from June; 484 admitted during July (510 in total); 480 evacuated, 11 destroyed, 9 returned to unit (500 accounted for); 10 remaining under treatment.
- The month's admissions (484) are far higher than January's (211), and most evacuations are mange / skin-disease cases — 108 of the 139 evacuated by train on 20 July, and 121 of the 148 evacuated on 29 July. The section is evacuating by road to St. Omer and by rail from Wippenhoek Siding to No. 10 Veterinary Hospital, Neufchâtel.
- The section remained at Poperinghe (the diarist's spelling; modern Poperinge, Belgium) all month; Wippenhoek Siding, St. Omer, Neufchâtel and Godewaersvelde are the other places named.
- Personnel: Capt. A. E. Coulombe commands. Corpl. F. Ballinger (No. 34726) was promoted A/Sgt on 4 July and transferred to C.A.V.C. (General) the same day, leaving the unit on 20 July to report to 2nd Canadian Heavy Battery. Corpl. P. W. Southern (No. 34729) was promoted Sergeant and Pte. J. Salmon (No. 34730) promoted Corporal on 17 July; Salmon was admitted to No. 1 Field Ambulance on 27 July. Corpl. H. McEwen (No. 49458) arrived as a reinforcement from Canadian Base Details on 23 July. Pte. D. W. McRae (No. 1277) was admitted to No. 1 Field Ambulance on 23 July and discharged fit for duty on 26 July. Pte. W. Leonard [uncertain] (No. 48766) was admitted to No. 2 Field Ambulance on 15 July — the August diary (18 August) gives this man's name as Lessard, so "Leonard" was a misreading. Pte. J. Sutherland (No. 21093) had his pay book forwarded to the Canadian Pay Office, London, on 11 July. Pte. H. Parry (No. 48793) relieved Pte. Gauthier (No. 48750) in an unreadable role at 1st Canadian Divisional Train on 11 July. Pte. Wenham (No. 48812) delivered a stray horse to the Field Remount Section on 13 July.
- Senior officers visiting/inspecting: A.D.V.S. (Assistant Director of Veterinary Services) and Major Cutcliffe, A.D.V.S.; Col. Wilson, D.D.V.S.; Col. Taschereau; Major Muir of Divisional Train HQ; Col. Neill, D.V.S.
- Horses destroyed this month (11, per the monthly report): cases 2180 (broken leg, 1 July), 2218, 2249 and 2296 (5 July), 2237 (7 July), 2316 (8 July), 2311, 2344 and 2327 (13 July), 2370 (19 July), 2365 and 2573 (22 July). Note the daily destruction notes add up to more than 11 — the monthly report's figure of 11 is as written.
- Two horses were evacuated to St. Omer on 5 July as mules are also recorded (2 mules evacuated 5 July; the 81-horse receipt on 8 July also covers 2 mules).
- The 7th Belgian Artillery features heavily: its horses are admitted, evacuated and destroyed throughout the month, and its transport calls for a clipping machine and calcium sulphide.
- Calcium sulphide (a mange treatment) is collected repeatedly from Wippenhoek Siding and its manufacture at the unit began on 24 July.
- The "Muster Parade State" of 25 July was at first read as "Muscle Parade"; the August entries (24–25 August) show the word is "Muster".
- The role Pte. Parry relieved Pte. Gauthier in on 11 July is unreadable in the original (possibly "batman").
- The place name written "Abel station" on 12–13 July may be Abele (on the Belgian border near Poperinge).
- No appendices are listed or included for July; the contents page claims "Diary Text 16 Pages" for the 16 diary-text pages (pp. 3–18).
