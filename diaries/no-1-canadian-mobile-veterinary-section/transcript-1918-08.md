## Transcription conventions
- Faithful to the original: spelling, punctuation, abbreviations and capitalisation as written (the diary is handwritten throughout, on Army Form C. 2118).
- `[uncertain]` marks a word or name that could not be read with confidence — never guessed.
- Text struck through in the original is shown as `[struck through in original: "..."]`.
- The diary-text pages are Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY", handwritten in columns: Place | Date | Hour | Summary of Events and Information | Remarks and references to Appendices. The "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" heading is not struck through on these pages.
- The Place column is written once per page; it is repeated in the tables below for each entry on that page. A ditto mark (") in the Place column is expanded to the repeated place.
- The remarks column is initialled throughout with a circled two-letter monogram that appears to read "P.S." — the reading is not certain; it matches the initials of Capt. P. Smallland, who signs the 31 August entry. The same monogram appears from 1 August, although Smallland is only recorded as assuming command on 12 August.
- No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given in the diary; map-sheet references (e.g. "Sheet 62 D", "Sheet 51B") are transcribed as written.
- "Admitted … horses to M.V.S." is the section's daily intake of sick or wounded horses; "Transferred … to C.C.V.E.S." is transfer onward to the Canadian Corps Veterinary Evacuating Station. "Horse Hides" in the entries are the hides of horses that died. "D.A.D.V.S." = Deputy Assistant Director of Veterinary Services. "D.A.A.G." = Deputy Assistant Adjutant General. "A.A.+ Q.M.G." = Assistant Adjutant and Quartermaster General.

## Notes — August 1918
- The section followed the 1st Canadian Division south for the Battle of Amiens (8–11 August 1918): it left Agnez-lez-Duisans on 2 August, entrained at Ligny-St. Flochel on 4 August via St. Pol and Abbeville, reached Senarpont on 5 August, moved by road to Hornoy and Blanchemaison, then to Vers (near Amiens) on 6 August, St. Fluchien [sic — St. Fuscien] on 8 August, Gentelles Wood on 9 August, Caix on 11 August, Le Quesnel on 17 August, Aubercourt on 22 August and Boves Wood on 23 August. It then entrained at Bacouel on 25 August, reached Savy on 26 August and Eturn, and on 30 August moved back north to near Arras. The diary records no battle detail — only veterinary work, movements and administration.
- The busiest day was 12 August: 17 horses admitted to the M.V.S. (the Amiens fighting was at its height). Case numbers run from 157 (1 August) to 303 (31 August).
- Horses destroyed/died this month: cases 186 and 190 destroyed on 13 August (shrapnel wounds); case 218 died on 17 August (tetanus from shrapnel wounds). One mule hide was received on 1 August (from No. 1 Section, 16th Divisional Ammunition Column) and one mule hide was transferred to 2/1 W. Lancs M.V.S. on 2 August.
- The only human casualty recorded this month: Dvr. J. J. Murray (No. 18453) was admitted to No. 33 Casualty Clearing Station on 13 August; his A.F.B. 122 was forwarded to the A.A.G., Base, on 27 August. Murray had been granted leave in July (see transcript-1918-07.md).
- Personnel: Capt. P. Smallland [uncertain spelling] assumed command of the unit on 12 August. Sergt. J. Salmon (No. 34730) rejoined from 1st Army Rest Camp on 9 August. Pte. H. E. Brooks (No. 19994), 1st Canadian Division Employment Company, reported for duty on 12 August. Pte. G. C. Hamilton (No. 16571) reported to the C.C.R.C. on 13 August for transfer to the 7th Infantry Brigade [uncertain]. Pte. L. E. Post (No. 195248 on 14 August — given as No. 195245 on 28 July) ceased to be attached to the M.V.S. on transfer to the Canadian Corps Veterinary Evacuating Station, 12 August. Pte. A. J. Merritt [uncertain] (No. 48572) proceeded to the Canadian Corps Reinforcement Camp on 27 August for transfer to the 7th Canadian Infantry Battalion — the same regimental number was given for Pte. A. J. Hyatt in September 1916, so the surname reading here is uncertain.
- The cover's volume number reads "40" (circled) with "42." written above it; the title page is numbered "40" bottom right. The contents page's "Diary Text 10 Pages" counts the 10 C. 2118 diary-text pages (pp. 4–13), not the cover, contents or title page.
- No monthly summary table of admissions/evacuations is given for August; the month closes with "Close of War Diary Month Ending Aug. 31st/1918" and the O.C.'s signature.
- No appendices are listed or included for August.
