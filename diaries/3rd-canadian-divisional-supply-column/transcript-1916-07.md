# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Supply Column — July 1916

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Supply Column, 3rd Canadian Division (styled "3rd (Temp.) Canadian Divisional Supply Column" until 11 July 1916, then "3rd Canadian Divisional Supply Column")
- **Month:** July 1916 (Volume 6) — this volume covers 1 to 31 July 1916
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005012 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5021, microfilm reel T-10905, file 776; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** July 1916, p. 1 – p. 6 (image files page-0023 – page-0028 of the 102-page diary)
- **July 1916 — COMPLETE** (pages 23–28 of 102): cover, title page, contents, diary text 1–31 July on two sheets, and an appendix "Organization of 3rd Cdn. Divisional Supply Column". Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Ditto marks in the original are written out as the repeated text, with "(ditto)" noted. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. Map references are given as written (Sheet 27, Sheet 28); no latitude/longitude coordinates are given. "C.C.A.P." = Canadian Corps Ammunition Park (as written); "M.T." = Mechanical Transport (as written); "G.H.Q." = General Headquarters; "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "Cwt." = hundredweight. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets is the unit name written in by hand at the top right. The signature at the foot of the July sheets and appendix is read as [uncertain] (see open questions).

## Page 6 — Appendix: Organization of 3rd Cdn. Divisional Supply Column

> APPENDIX to War Diary.
>
> The 3rd. Canadian Divisional Supply Column, was formed from the Prov. Canadian Corps Troops Supply Column and drafts from the Canadian Training Depot, dating from the 1st. Feb. 1916.
>
> The Prov. Canadian Corps Troop Supply Column was formed on the 14th. Sept. 1915, at G.H.Q. from the Canadian Reserve Lorry Park it was then known as Canadian Corps Troop Supply Column, the Prov. being added when it was known that a Column had been formed in England and was on its way out.
>
> On arrival of the new Column the work of supplying the Canadian Corps and 3rd. Canadian Division, which was in process of formation, was [struck through in the original: "carried"] carried on by this Column. The work of the Corps was gradually taken over by the new column, and on February, 2nd., 1916, all motor lorries, light cars, car drivers, and attached motor omnibuses were struck off strength, the new column having taken over all the work of the Canadian Corps.
>
> The designation of this column was changed to 3rd. (Temporary) Canadian Divisional Supply Column. (Canadian Corps, R.O. 303, dated 30-1-16).
>
> On the morning of March the 23rd., under orders from the Corps the Supply lorries, after unloading at the different dumps, proceeded direct to Railhead, where, instead of drawing fresh rations, the reserve rations of a Standard Pack Train were drawn. The 3rd. Canadian Divisional Train drew fresh rations for the Division. At 10.30 a.m. this unit moved off in column from S.13.d.2.8, sheet 28, and proceeded direct to P.23.c.0.3, Sheet 27, where a new Park was formed. The Supply Details with six lorries moving direct to new Railhead at L.28.c.4.3., under Lieut. Cameron, from which point they issued direct to 3rd. Train.
>
> This Railhead shifted to C.21.a.1.6, sheet 28, on April, 12th., at which point they are now located.
>
> On April the 15th. the Ambulance Workshop joined this unit, and on June the 24th., 22 new Daimler lorries, with Canadian personnel, were received from C.A.S.C. Training Depot to replace Imperial personnel then with the column.
>
> Part 2 Orders No. 21, dated 7-7-16, changed the designation of this unit to the 3rd. Canadian Divisional Supply Column from the 1st. Feb., 1916.
>
> This column is now complete, and a satisfactory report has been received from the D.D.S.&T., Second Army as to the excellent condition of the lorries.
>
> On the 30th. July, General Byng inspected the column and found it to be very satisfactory.
>
> [Signed:] 31st. July, 1916, *[signature — [uncertain]] Major, / O.C. 3rd. Canadian Divisional Supply Col.*

## Open questions / uncertain items (July 1916)
- 1 July: the "5—3 ton lorries attached to C.C.A.P." line follows the feeding-strength lines of the 1 July entry and is transcribed as part of that entry; the 2 July entry is the single line "Supplies as usual."
- 20 July: the heading "Details showing work of the Column, not inc. lorries on road work or transport detailed by the Corps, repairs to vehicles etc." introduces the daily ration figures that run from 20 July to the end of the month; it is transcribed as the opening line of the 20 July entry.
- 23 July: the coal-clearance place is written "Goddersveldt" — transcribed as written; the spelling is not verified.
- 30 July: the diary entry spells the Corps Commander's name "General Bing"; the appendix (page 6) spells it "General Byng" — both as written.
- 11 July: Part 2 Orders No. 21, dated 7/7/16, changed the unit's designation from "3rd. (Temporary) Canadian Divisional Supply Column" to "3rd. Canadian Divisional Supply Column", effective from 1/2/16. The "(Temp.)" styling disappears from the title page from July onward.
- The appendix states the Prov. Canadian Corps Troop Supply Column was formed 14 September 1915 at G.H.Q. from the Canadian Reserve Lorry Park, and that the 3rd Canadian Divisional Supply Column was formed from it and drafts from the Canadian Training Depot, dating from 1 February 1916.
- The cover reads "Volume 6" but the contents page reads "Volume 16.— 7." — both as written.
- The O.C.'s signature at the foot of the July sheets and appendix is not read with confidence (it appears to read "J. O. Lawson" [uncertain]) and is recorded here as [uncertain].
- The handwritten "270" in the left margin of the title page is a file/frame mark, not part of the diary text.
- No casualties are recorded in this volume. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; dump and railhead locations are given as sheet references only.
