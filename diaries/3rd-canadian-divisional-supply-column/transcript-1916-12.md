# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Supply Column — December 1916

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Supply Column, Canadian Corps
- **Month:** December 1916 (Volume 11) — 1 to 31 December 1916
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005012 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5021, file 776, microfilm reel T-10905; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** December 1916, p. 1 – p. 3 (image files page-0044 – page-0046 of the 102-page diary)
- **December 1916 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–3 of 102): cover, contents, diary text 1–31 December on a single sheet, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" (the printed heading "or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is not struck through). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Entries are initialled in ink at the end of each line — "H.O.L." (Major H. O. Lawson, Officer Commanding) for 1–6 and 15–31 December, and "C.McK." (Capt. C. G. MacKinnon) for 7–14 December; the initials are not repeated after every entry below. Every entry this month is a ration issue with the weather only; the Column did not move and no other event is recorded. No map references are given this month; no latitude/longitude coordinates are given. The Place column reads "Tilloy-Les-Hermaville" throughout the month. The contents page drops "Supply" from the unit name ("3rd Canadian Divisional Column - Cdn. Corps."); it is transcribed as written. The printed footer on this month's diary sheet differs from earlier months: "1875 Wt. W593/826 1,000,000 4/15 J.B.C. & A. A.D.S.S./Forms/C. 2118."

## Open questions / uncertain items (December 1916)
- The initials change from "H.O.L." to "C.McK." on 7 December and back to "H.O.L." on 15 December, marking a week in which Capt. MacKinnon kept the diary.
- Horse ration figures vary sharply from day to day (e.g. 8,324 on 11 December against about 5,200–6,500 on neighbouring days, and 4,430 on 10 December); the figures are transcribed as written and the diary gives no explanation.
- No names other than the diarists' initials appear this month.
- No casualties are recorded in this month, and the Column did not move.
