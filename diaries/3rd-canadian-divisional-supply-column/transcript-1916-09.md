# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Supply Column — September 1916

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Supply Column, Canadian Corps
- **Month:** September 1916 (Volume 8) — 1 to 30 September 1916
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005012 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5021, file 776, microfilm reel T-10905; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** September 1916, p. 1 – p. 4 (image files page-0033 – page-0036 of the 102-page diary)
- **September 1916 — COMPLETE** (pages 1–4 of 102): cover, contents, diary text 1–30 September, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout on Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" (the printed heading "or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" is not struck through). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Ditto marks (") under "Issued Supplies as usual" and under weather notes are expanded to the repeated text. Every entry is initialled in ink at the end of its line — "H.O.L." (Major H. O. Lawson, Officer Commanding) throughout this month; the initials are not repeated after every entry below. The 12 September entry is split across the two diary sheets ("…loaded up supplies which were then" / "delivered to Refilling Points…"); it is transcribed here as one entry. Map references are given as written (Sheet 27, Sheet 28); no latitude/longitude coordinates are given. "A.Q.M.G." = Assistant Quartermaster General. The Place column reads "Steenvoorde" for 1–7 September and "Coulonvillers" for 8–12 September; the 12 September continuation sheet is headed "(Continued)" and "Rubempre" from 13 September. The figure "1/2" appears at the top right of the Summary of Events column on Sheet No. 1 and "3" on Sheet No. 2. The cover gives the volume as "Volume 8", while the contents page gives "Volume 16 - 9" — both are transcribed as written.

### Page 3 — 1–12 September 1916 (Sheet No. 1)

> **Steenvoorde, Sept. 1st.** — Issued 20345 men's and 5317 horses' rations. Weather conditions — fine.
>
> **Steenvoorde, Sept. 2nd.** — Issued Supplies as usual. Weather conditions — showery.
>
> **Steenvoorde, Sept. 3rd.** — Issued Supplies as usual. Weather conditions — slight showers.
>
> **Steenvoorde, Sept. 4th.** — Issued 19946 men's and 5297 horses' rations. Weather conditions — showery.
>
> **Steenvoorde, Sept. 5th.** — Issued Supplies as usual. Weather conditions — rain.
>
> **Steenvoorde, Sept. 6th.** — Issued 20275 men's and 5286 horses' rations at 6.30 a.m. and a like quantity at 2.30 p.m. Weather conditions — fine and warm.
>
> **Steenvoorde, Sept. 7th.** — The Headquarters and Workshops of Column left Steenvoorde at 6.00 a.m. via the following route through Hazebrouck, Aire, St. Hilaire, Bergueneuse, Anvin, St. Pol, Auxi le Chateau and Heirmont to Coulonvillers, in Reserve Army Area. (authority for move, A.Q.M.G., 3rd. Canadian Division, A.B.817, dated 5-9-16.) The weather conditions were fine and very warm, and Column arrived at destination at 6.00 p.m., making a total of 12 hours steady run without mishap. 21 Lorries under Lieut. Gibson, and 10 lorries under Lieut. McDowall proceeded with Supply Section and Details of Divisional Train to Railhead at Caestre. After loading Supplies Lieut. Gibson's Convoy proceeded direct to Coulonvillers via the route stated above. Lieut. McDowall's Convoy returned to Steenvoorde, and remained at Column's Steenvoorde location. Ordnance and Postal Lorries and Details proceeded to their respective new locations in same area.
>
> **Coulonvillers, Sept. 8th.** — Lieut. McDowall's Convoy arrived from Steenvoorde by same route as other Convoys at 4.30 p.m. without mishap. Weather conditions — fine and warm. Refilling Points are located on Branch Roads around this village. The Railhead is at St. Riquier. Postal Details are at Railhead, and Ordnance Details at St. Flury.
>
> **Coulonvillers, Sept. 9th.** — Issued 13590 men's and 1921 Horses' rations at Refilling Points at 8.30 a.m. Convoys proceeded to Railhead at 10 a.m. and loaded a like quantity of Rations which were delivered to Refilling Points at 4 p.m. Convoys then returned to Column. Weather — fine and very hot.
>
> **Coulonvillers, Sept. 10th.** — Lieut. Gibson with Convoy of 30 lorries, and Supply Details proceeded to Railhead at Vignacourt. Weather conditions — fine and warm.
>
> **Coulonvillers, Sept. 11th.** — Lieut. Gibson's convoy proceeded from Vignacourt to Rubempre. Issued 16536 men's and 1995 horses' rations. Weather conditions — fine and warm.
>
> **Coulonvillers, Sept. 12th.** — The Headquarters and Workshops Sections of Column proceeded at 7 a.m. via Longvillers, Bernaville, Candas, le Val de Maison to Rubempre, arriving at 11.30 a.m. Lieut. McDowall's Convoy left Coulonvillers at 6 a.m. and proceeded to Rubempre via same route, arriving at 10.15 a.m. They immediately unloaded lorries and proceeded to new Railhead at Belle Eglise via the following route Rubempre, Herrisart, Toutencourt, Lealvillers, and loaded up supplies which were then [continued on Sheet No. 2 — see Page 4].
>
> [Signed in the right margin, written vertically:] *H. O. Lawson, Major, / O.C. 3rd. Canadian D.S. Column.*

## Open questions / uncertain items (September 1916)
- 7 September: the route place "Heirmont" is transcribed as written; the spelling is the diary's own. "Bergueneuse" likewise.
- 8 September: "Ordnance Details at St. Flury" is transcribed as written.
- 16 September: 2 Drivers of the Advanced Park were reported wounded by shell fire — the first casualties recorded in this diary. No names are given, so none are recorded here.
- 17 September: "Lieut. Humphries of Headquarters Section" — first appearance of this officer in the diary.
- Names read as: Lieut. F. A. Gibson; Lieut. McDowall; Lieut. Humphries; Major H. O. Lawson.
- The Column moved twice this month: Headquarters and Workshops Steenvoorde to Coulonvillers on 7 September (12-hour run), then Coulonvillers to Rubempre on 12 September. The railhead moved Caestre to St. Riquier/Vignacourt, then to Belle Eglise. From 14 September a 20-lorry Advanced Park worked on Engineers' Work at Albert, with No. 1 and No. 2 Sections relieving each other on 17, 23 and 28 September.
