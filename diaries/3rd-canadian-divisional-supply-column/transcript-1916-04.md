# War Diary — 3rd Canadian Divisional Supply Column — April 1916

- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Supply Column, 3rd Canadian Division (styled "3rd (Temp.) Canadian Divisional Supply Column")
- **Month:** April 1916 (Volume 3) — this volume covers 1 to 30 April 1916
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005012 (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5021, microfilm reel T-10905, file 776; finding aid 9-52)
- **Pages:** April 1916, p. 1 – p. 4 (image files page-0011 – page-0014 of the 102-page diary)
- **April 1916 — COMPLETE** (pages 11–14 of 102): cover, title page, contents, diary text 1–30 April on a single sheet, no appendices. Transcribed 2026-10-27.

## Transcription conventions
Original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations kept. Diary text is typewritten throughout (Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" — "INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY" struck through on the printed heading). `[uncertain]` marks words I could not read with confidence — never guessed. Ditto marks in the original are written out as the repeated text, with "(ditto)" noted. A repeated handwritten initial mark appears at the end of most entries; it is not repeated against every entry here. Map references are given as written (Sheet 28); no latitude/longitude coordinates are given. "C.C.A.P." = Canadian Corps Ammunition Park (as written); "A.T.S. Col." = Army Transport and Supply Column (as written); "G.R.O." = General Routine Order (as written); "D.D.S.&T." = Deputy Director of Supplies and Transport (as written); "O.C." = Officer Commanding; "Cwt." = hundredweight. The diary's own heading on the diary sheets is the unit name written in by hand at the top right. The signature at the foot of the April sheet is read as [uncertain] (see open questions).

## Page 4 — Diary text (1–30 April 1916)

Army Form C. 2118, "WAR DIARY or INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY". The printed footer reads "2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12." The whole month is on this single sheet.

> **ST SYLVESTRE-CAPPEL, 1/4/16.** — Issued supplies at railhead as usual. Feeding strength, 19436 men and 4717 horses. Received one three ton lorry.
>
> **ST SYLVESTRE-CAPPEL, 2/4/16.** — Supplies as usual. Received one three ton lorry. Transferred off two three ton lorries.

> **ST SYLVESTRE-CAPPEL, 5/4/16.** — Owing to the number of lorries returning from C.C.A.P. where they are detached for road work, on account of worn out tyres, I [uncertain — "inspected"?] these lorries at their work. This work is exceptionally hard on motor vehicles, the tyres especially coming in for a great deal of wear. I issued orders to my Officer i/c of these lorries to see that all loads were properly distributed to prevent wear of load being heavier on rear tyres than on front.

## Open questions / uncertain items (April 1916)
- 5 April: one word in the typed entry is overtyped and not read with confidence — transcribed as [uncertain — "inspected"?]; the sense is that the O.C. inspected the lorries at their road work.
- 30 April: part of the entry's first line is obscured where the printed form footer ("2449 Wt. W14957/M90 750,000 1/16 J.B.C. & A. Forms/C.2118/12.") runs across the typed text; the feeding-strength figures that follow are legible and are transcribed as written.
- The O.C.'s signature, written vertically in the right margin, is not read with confidence (it appears to read "J. O. Lawson" [uncertain]) and is recorded here as [uncertain].
- The handwritten "264" in the left margin of the title page is a file/frame mark, not part of the diary text.
- Names read as: Lieut. Humphries (Ambulance Workshop Unit).
- No casualties are recorded in this volume. No map coordinates (latitude/longitude) are given; the new railhead is given as a sheet reference only.
