- **Unit:** 3rd Canadian Divisional Supply Column, Canadian Army Service Corps
- **Source:** Library and Archives Canada, Collection Search Item ID 2005012 — "War diaries - 3rd Canadian Divisional Supply Column" (RG 9 III-D-3, vol. 5021, file 776, microfilm reel T-10905, finding aid 9-52)
- **Dates:** 1 February 1916 – 14 April 1918
- **Pages:** 102 page images (27 monthly volumes, Feb 1916 – Apr 1918), all downloaded from the LAC IIIF manifest `https://digitalmanifest.bac-lac.gc.ca/DigitalManifest/1/2005012` (`manifest.json`, `download-pages.sh`)
- **Transcripts:** 27 monthly files, `transcript-1916-02.md` … `transcript-1918-04.md`
- **Index:** 750 dated entries in `INDEX-D.md` (merged from `index-supplycol-a.md` … `index-supplycol-d.md`), covering all 804 days 1916-02-01 – 1918-04-14 with no gaps or overlaps (28 range entries where the diary groups days).
- **Transcribed:** 2026-10-27, Eighth D run 3 (four parallel subagents, one per quarter of the diary; parent run verified index coverage and spot-checked February 1916 against the page image).

## What the diary is

The typewritten daily log (Army Form C.2118) of the 3rd Division's supply column — the lorries that hauled rations, forage, coal, ammunition and troops from railheads to divisional dumps. Almost every entry records the day's ration issue (men's and horses' rations, typically ~17,000–35,000 men and ~2,000–7,500 horses) and the weather, plus railhead and dump moves, lorry exchanges and vehicle casualties.

## Key findings

- 1 Feb 1916: the unit's designation changed to 3rd (Temporary) Canadian Divisional Supply Column (Canadian Corps R.O. No. 303, 30 Jan 1916), taking over the Canadian Corps Troops Supply Column's work at Bailleul.
- The column moved with the division: Bailleul → St Sylvestre-Cappel (Mar 1916) → Steenvoorde (Aug 1916) → Coulonvillers/Rubempre (Sep 1916, Somme) → Bernaville/Maisonleblond (Oct 1916) → Olhain (early 1917, Vimy sector) → Haillicourt/Berles (Apr 1918).
- 3 Aug 1916: Lieut. W. Stratton left to train as a pilot in the Royal Flying Corps.
- 16 Sep 1916 (Rubempre): first casualties recorded — 2 drivers of the Advanced Park wounded by shell fire.
- 4 May 1917: aerial bombardment of Ecurie Railhead — Lorry W.D. No. 12976 damaged and its driver wounded by bomb fragments.
- 14 Apr 1918: the diary ends. On the reorganization of Canadian Corps Mechanical Transport (C.H.Q. letter No. 6712 (QA1), 28 Mar 1918), the column was absorbed with the 3rd Canadian Divisional Ammunition Sub-Park into No. 3 Canadian Divisional Mechanical Transport Company under Major E. M. Harris. The cover carries the handwritten note: "This unit ceased to exist on 14–4–18."
- No battle deaths are recorded in this diary; the only personnel casualties are the three wounded men above. Most "casualties" in the diary are mechanical — lorries and light cars sent to base as unserviceable.

## Uncertain / open items

- 91 `[uncertain]` flags across the 27 transcripts (illegible names, map references, figures and a few struck-through entries).
- April 1918: the 3 April move destination is typed "AERLES" while the Place column from 4 April reads "BERLES" — transcribed as written, not corrected. The 11 April entry is struck through with no legible figures; the 13–14 April rations rows have struck/overwritten dates.
- May 1917: an undated ration-issue line sits between the 4 and 5 May entries; it is carried under 4 May in the index with a note in the transcript.
- The LAC guide's "Part 1 = 1916/07/22 – 1916/12/31; Part 2 = 1917/01/03 – 1919/08/13" lines do not match this record's 1916/02/01–1918/04/14 range — the record page is authoritative; the guide lines are noted, not relied on.
- The 4th Canadian Divisional Supply Column shares RG 9 III-D-3 vol. 5021 in the guide; it is a separate LAC record and a later QUEUE-D item (item 34) — not transcribed in this run.
