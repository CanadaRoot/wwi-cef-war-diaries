# 5th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Central Ontario) — diary README

- **Unit:** 5th Canadian Reserve Battalion (Central Ontario). Organized at Bramshott 5 January 1917 under Lt-Col. G. T. Chisholm (Canadian Routine Order 271, 20 January 1917). Formed by absorbing the 92nd, 95th and 169th Battalions; absorbed the 157th Battalion 10 February 1917, the 241st Battalion 7 May 1917 and the 253rd Battalion in May 1917. Reinforced the 15th, 20th and 134th Battalions. Moved to West Sandling before April 1917. Absorbed by the 12th Canadian Reserve Battalion in February 1918. (Background from the LAC "Guide to Sources Relating to Units of the Canadian Expeditionary Force — Reserve Battalions".)

## What the diary is

The typed war diary (Army Form C. 2118) of a reserve battalion training at West Sandling, Kent — not a front-line diary. Each month is a separate "Confidential / Original" volume (Volumes 1–7) with a cover and two diary-text sheets (three for April). Almost every entry is a strength movement: officers and other ranks taken on or struck off strength as drafts go to France (chiefly the 15th and 20th Battalions) and new drafts arrive from the 1st C.O.R.D., the C.C.D. Hastings, and other reserve battalions. The battalion absorbed the 241st Battalion (21 officers, 625 other ranks) and the 253rd Battalion (17 officers, 459 other ranks) in May 1917, reorganized from 8 to 6 companies (7 August) and then to 5 companies (14 September), sent 20 snipers to France (23 September), went on brigade manoeuvres 26–28 September, and sat under German air raids on six nights (29–30 September, 1, 2, 6 and 9 October) with no casualties. O.C.s: Lt-Col. G. T. Chisholm (April–May); a Major, then Lt-Col. (signature reads "J. D. McCrimmon" [uncertain]) June–August; a Major (signature reads "R. T. Lane" [uncertain]) in September; no signature in October.

## Casualties

None recorded in April–October 1917. The diary's "returned casualty" officers (24–25 April, 26 May) are men returning to the battalion after recovering, not new casualties. The September air raids (29–30 September, 1–2 and 6 and 9 October) are all recorded as "No casualties in this Bn:" / "No casualties in this Battalion."
